## Overview

Delivery

in person

Overview text

You are curious and creative. You know what’s going on in the world and you want to make a difference as a multi-media storyteller. Join BCIT’s Broadcast and Online Journalism program to take the first steps in your new career. Our reputation in industry means that more than 90% of our graduates are employed. From day one you’ll be working as a hands-on journalist in news, sports and current affairs. Students will be mentored by experienced faculty and connect with professionals in the field. Our emphasis on multi-platform production puts this program on the cutting edge of journalism schools across the country. Check out our student work on our BCIT News website . To see more specifics about how BCIT’s unique training prepares you for a fast-paced journalism career, visit our Program Details page. Every full-time diploma program has a practicum component where students spend time with an industry partner (TV and Radio stations; production, sports and entertainment companies). Practicums are typically four weeks in duration. In addition to practicum, students may have the opportunity to enter co-op in level four. This program was developed by industry professionals for people who have a strong curiosity about the world and want to develop critical thinking skills related to news and current affairs storytelling. You should also have strong written and oral communication skills. If this sounds like you, apply today through our Entrance Requirements page. There is always a strong demand for writers, reporters, video journalists, producers, and researchers who are skilled in producing radio, television, and online content. Our graduates work in newsrooms, sports stations, and media relations firms, including government and production houses. Some have established their own video production companies. Learn more about the career possibilities available to our graduates on the Graduating and Jobs page.



## Courses

Introduction

The hands-on experience of working in radio, television, and online makes your training at BCIT completely one-of-a-kind. In your two years in the program, you’ll practice the everyday skills needed for journalism and learn how to investigate original news stories, shoot and edit stories, produce documentaries and more. We have a strong focus on digital journalism and how to use social media for news and information reporting.

### Level 1 (15 weeks)

BCST 1108 - Videography

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst1108/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This course combines journalism critical thinking skills with the technical elements of shooting and editing visuals and audio for news and information programming as a solo Video Journalist. Students will explore both pre-production planning and field techniques including the basic elements of lighting. They will also use pacing, structure and sequencing for effective video storytelling.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

BCST 1118 - Video Editing

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst1118/

Credits: 2.0

Description: This course teaches students how to edit video footage, still images and audio using professional editing software to create a variety video content. It also introduces basic video graphics and some creative editing techniques.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

BCST 1128 - On-Air Performance

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst1128/

Credits: 3.0

Description: Announcing techniques, news reading skills and basic concepts of effective voice usage are introduced in this course. You will explore articulation, pronunciation, inflection, and breathing techniques to help you develop your voice to its maximum potential. During the course daily practice is required and coaching will be provided both individually and in groups.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

BCST 1130 - Introduction to News Reporting

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst1130/

Credits: 2.0

Description: Introduces the student to the basic principles of news gathering and production. The course will give students a grounding in the systems, issues, and practices of news reporting and will prepare them for the more detailed and involved aspects of the broadcast and online journalism industry.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

BCST 1132 - Introduction to Audio Production

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst1132/

Credits: 2.0

Description: This course explores the fundamentals of audio storytelling. Students will use broadcast equipment and editing software to produce engaging short and long-form content. Class time will be divided between classroom instruction and hands-on lab experience. Journalistic standards and principles apply for all content in this course.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

BCST 1134 - Introduction to Broadcast Writing

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst1134/

Credits: 2.0

Description: This course trains and prepares students to write stories for a variety of platforms. This course combines basic writing theory, broadcast formats, and writing content to deadline. This course relies heavily on in-class practice, writing analysis and critique.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

BCST 1139 - Visual Fundamentals for Journalists

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst1139/

Credits: 2.0

Description: Visual Fundamentals is a hands-on, creative course that introduces students to the aesthetic and technical principles of visual storytelling. Students will explore both photography and video production throughout the course. In this class, students will develop an understanding of how to incorporate visual storytelling techniques in order to engage an audience across multiple platforms. Through assignments, they will apply mobile production techniques to create a variety of visual stories with an emphasis on the concepts of composition and sequencing. All content will be explored using the principles of journalism.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

### Level 2 (16 weeks plus 4 week practicum)

BCST 1331 - Media Law

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst1331/

Credits: 2.0

Description: Trains journalists to function within the Canadian judicial system. This course explains the inner workings and the various levels of courts and familiarizes students with Canadian criminal law and the laws of libel and slander.

Prerequisites: Successful completion of all term 1 courses.

BCST 2230 - News Reporting

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst2230/

Credits: 2.0

Description: In this course, students will learn how to identify, research, and gather information for a variety of news beats. Topics include police, court, and political reporting. Students learn how to navigate these story types while using safe, inclusive, and trauma-informed reporting practices.

Prerequisites: Successful completion of all term 1 courses.

BCST 2233 - Television News and Video Journalism

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst2233/

Credits: 2.0

Description: Teaches the process by which a story idea is transformed into a television news story, how to gather visual materials that tell the story, to write a script that works with the visuals, and to edit visuals together with script to create a comprehensible television news story. Some attention is given to newscast make-up and presentation.

Prerequisites: Successful completion of all term 1 courses.

BCST 2234 - Audio Storytelling

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst2234/

Credits: 2.0

Description: This course introduces students to the elements of radio documentary production. During the semester students will work on researching, interviewing, writing, sound-gathering techniques and producing effective proposals. They will assess effective long-form productions. Students will produce a number of small assignments and work with a partner to produce an audio documentary.

Prerequisites: • Successful completion of all term 1 courses.

BCST 2235 - Journalism Practicum 1

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst2235/

Credits: 6.0

Description: In this course students are immersed in a multiplatform newsroom environment. Students will learn to function in a working environment across platforms with deadlines. They will incorporate industry ethical and legal standards in their work. Students will operate and file audio, video, and digital content to all BCIT Newsroom platforms. Students will work together to create television news content.

Prerequisites: Successful completion of all other Level 1 and Level 2 courses in the Journalism program or Program Head's approval.

BCST 2237 - Radio News Lab

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst2237/

Credits: 3.0

Description: Presents the first opportunity for students to work in a newsroom environment. The class is divided into small groups for personal instruction on operating newsroom equipment. This is followed by several weeks of practice where students function as reporters and anchors, gathering information, filing news stories, writing, and reading newscasts while honing their ability to use newsroom equipment.

Prerequisites: Successful completion of all term 1 courses.

BCST 2238 - Multiplatform Writing

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst2238/

Credits: 2.0

Description: This course is designed to build on the skills learned and practiced in the News Writing course in Level 1 (BCST 1134). Students will continue to improve their use of industry standard writing for television, radio and online sites with an emphasis on news. The focus is on effective communication using proper format, style and structure. This course is a lab in which students work, to deadline to produce news and information assignments.

Prerequisites: Successful completion of all term 1 courses

BCST 2240 - Digital Journalism

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst2240/

Credits: 2.0

Description: This course is a broad exploration of Digital Journalism. Throughout the term, students will explore the current digital landscape, with a focus on design, social media, and online story delivery. Classes will be a mix of topical lectures/discussions and hands-on learning. Through course work, students will explore a variety of digital tools and platforms currently being used in industry. The course will also investigate the important topic of verification and look at tools to combat misinformation in the digital landscape. At the culmination of this course, students will bring together everything they have learned to create an in-depth, cross-platform multimedia story.

Prerequisites: Successful completion of all term 1 courses

COMM 1112 - Media Relations Skills for Journalists

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/comm1112/

Credits: 3.0

Description: In this course, students prepare for work in a media relations role for an organization. It relies on the concepts of mass media to address the role of media relations in organizations, and explains the development of a media relations plan in the context of both old and new media. This course also assesses the contemporary issues and topics surrounding media relations, while providing students with effective writing, communication, audience identification & demographics, branding, and interview strategies. Students will learn how to build an effective media relations plan, how to evaluate media relations within a broader corporate public relations plan, and apply concepts using tools and procedures used in interactions with the media.

Prerequisites: Successful completion of all term 1 courses.

### Level 3 (15 weeks)

BCST 3332 - Radio Newsroom

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst3332/

Credits: 8.0

Description: In this hands-on course, students produce live and recorded content for a student run radio station. Assignments include reporting from the field as well as hosting daily news and sportscasts.). Evaluation is based on performance, writing/content, professionalism, and production of original news stories. Students with a minimum average of 70% have the opportunity to participate in an industry placement as part of this course.

Prerequisites: Successful completion of all other Level 1 and Level 2 courses in the Journalism program.

BCST 3333 - Video Storytelling

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst3333/

Credits: 8.0

Description: In this hands-on course students research and produce their own video content using ethical storytelling principles. They also have opportunities to be on-air hosts and show producers. Students with a minimum average of 70% have the opportunity to participate in an industry placement as part of this course.

Prerequisites: Successful completion of all other Level 1 and Level 2 courses in the Journalism program.

BCST 3340 - Advanced Journalism

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst3340/

Credits: 2.0

Description: Building on the skills learned in Levels 1 and 2, students will work individually and in small groups to identify, research, pitch and produce original investigative multimedia news stories for online distribution. Students will apply equity, diversity, and inclusion principles when working with marginalized communities. They will also learn to use culturally sensitive and trauma-informed reporting practices.

Prerequisites: Successful completion of all other Level 1 and Level 2 courses in the Journalism program.

BCST 3700 - Digital Newsroom

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst3700/

Credits: 8.0

Description: In this course, students will expand upon the digital newsroom skills learned in Digital Journalism (BCST 2240) and Journalism Practicum 1 (BCST 2235) and apply those skills in an active daily Digital Newsroom. Participants will work in the field and also in the newsroom managing the BCIT News webpage and social media accounts. As a team, students will work to produce original content created specifically for a digital audience

Prerequisites: Successful completion of all level 1 and level 2 courses.

### Level 4 (16 weeks plus 4 week practicum)

BCST 2215 - Web Design for Journalists

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst2215/

Credits: 2.0

Description: In this hands-on, creative course, student journalists will explore aspects of web design. Throughout BCST 2215, students will learn all the skills necessary to construct a personal portfolio website from the ground up. Using a variety of industry- standard digital tools and software, students will plan, design, and build an engaging personal website to showcase their work.

Prerequisites: • Successful completion of all other Level 1, Level 2, and Level 3 courses in the Journalism program or Program Head's approval.

BCST 4431 - Multiplatform Newsroom

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst4431/

Credits: 17.0

Description: This advanced course in newsroom operations builds on the skills learned in the Term 3 lab courses (BCST 3332/ 3333/3700) and BCST 3340 (Advanced Journalism). In the course, students will gather, produce, and repurpose content for a variety of platforms within the BCIT student newsroom. Students will work independently and in teams to produce original audio, video, and digital content to deadline. Students function in various newsroom roles which may include Multiplatform Reporters, Hosts, and Producers.

Prerequisites: Successful completion of all other Level 1, Level 2, and Level 3 courses or Program Head's approval.

BCST 4435 - Journalism Practicum 2

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst4435/

Credits: 7.0

Description: In this course students apply learned skills in a relevant external fieldwork environment (industry). They spend four weeks working under the direction of a workplace mentor at a media related organization, where they may actively participate in the various aspects of day-to-day operations or specialize in specific areas of interest, related to the program.

Prerequisites: Successful completion of all other Level 1, Level 2, Level 3 and Level 4 courses or Program Head's approval.

BCST 4436 - Documentary Capstone

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bcst4436/

Credits: 7.0

Description: This course provides student journalists in their final term with the opportunity to use the skills they have acquired during the previous three terms to produce a video documentary and accompanying digital article. The course builds on skills in journalism video production and critical thinking storytelling to enable the students to develop a concept, a pitch and a final documentary and multimedia digital story. The documentary is produced by a team and will be posted on the Broadcast and Online Journalism website.

Prerequisites: Successful completion of all Level 1, Level 2, and Level 3 courses in the Journalism program or Program Head's approval.

## Program Details

introduction

Your journalism training at BCIT is unlike any you’ll get anywhere else. From the beginning of your first week, you’ll be involved in learning skills and knowledge journalists and media professionals use every day. We have a very active industry advisory committee which helps us keep our program up to date and which mentors our second year students in industry rotations throughout the year. We are live and on the air – our students: Through this program, you’ll develop interviewing skills and announcing techniques, hone your writing, and learn how to deliver compelling news stories. All your courses and experience, including industry internships, are designed to give you the ability and confidence to start and succeed in your journalism career.

Program length

Two years, full-time. The program runs from September to May each year.

Additional program options

Radio Arts and Entertainment Television & Video Production

Program delivery

In person: This program is delivered on campus.

Program location

Burnaby Campus 3700 Willingdon Avenue Burnaby, BC

The reports are organized into three-page summaries containing information on graduates’ labour market experiences and opinions regarding their education.
