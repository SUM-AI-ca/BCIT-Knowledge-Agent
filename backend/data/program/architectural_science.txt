Title: Architectural Science

================================================================================

## Overview

Delivery

in person

Overview text

Architectural Science is a bridge between design theory and building technology. The purpose of this degree program is to develop graduates with highly relevant knowledge and skills in a way that emphasizes holistic and critical thinking at the intersection between living systems, culture and technology, so that our students emerge as industry-ready participants in the real world of sustainable thinking and building. The Bachelor in Architectural Science is a full-time two-year program laddered onto a two-year architectural technology diploma ( BCIT ABT or equivalent) delivering a broadly based, technically current curriculum on the theoretical and applied aspects of architectural science. The program methodology is to teach a ladder of critical thought, following a deliberate and evolving framework through progression, analysis and repetition to ingrain sustainable thinking, reinforced in every core course delivered. Students will work on studio projects of increasing scale and complexity culminating in the Graduating Project: a comprehensive building design merging architectural design and building performance, supported by expert industry consultants. Cultural history, material explorations, project management, building science and communication skills will be taught within the program and incorporated into holistic design solutions, integrating social responsibility with technical expertise. Students will take general education courses in Liberal Studies and Business and choose between several professional electives to complete their Bachelor Degree. The program integrates experiential learning activities, community outreach initiatives and international opportunities, including the option to study abroad for a term. These options may have a limited number of openings subject to an application process.

Program structure

Students must complete a Diploma in architectural technology ( ABT or equivalent) before the Bachelor of Architectural Science. ## Courses

Class hours

### Level 5 (15 weeks)

ARSC 5000 - Architectural Graphics

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/arsc5000/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This course develops a range of architectural graphics skills and strategies for various design communications. The course is structured around a series of manual drawing exercises, exploring two- and three-dimensional representation techniques and digital post-processing to produce effective architectural graphics for different purposes. The course emphasizes graphic communication as a means of exploring architectural potential, articulating design intent, and documenting meaningful project information.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

ARSC 5005 - Design Studio 1

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/arsc5005/

Credits: 7.5

Description: The purpose of this studio is to introduce architectural design as an iterative process of inquiry and experimentation. A series of exercises will provide a structured approach to architectural process and methods (including physical models, concept diagrams, precedent studies and presentation drawings). Students will build on skills acquired in previous architectural and building technology diploma courses to implement first principles of architectural design on small site-specific projects. Detailed context analysis will be conducted to inform meaningful and sustainable architectural solutions.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

ARSC 5110 - History of Architecture

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/arsc5110/

Credits: 3.0

Description: The purpose of this course is to explore the global history of architecture with a post-colonial lens. The course follows a rough chronological arc from the emergence of First Civilizations to the twentieth century. Evolutions of building typologies, materials, systems and methods are examined within the context of the prevailing social, political and economic forces of particular times in history, in cultures around the world. In addition to course lectures and discussions, students will research areas of interest and participate in knowledge sharing with the class.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

ARSC 5350 - Environmental Separators

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/arsc5350/

Credits: 3.0

Description: Through a series of lectures and work assignments, this course will introduce students to broad principles in building science and performance and will provide learning opportunities for basic application of these principles. Students will learn these principles through the lens of the four elements in building science studies: i) exterior climate and region, ii) wall and separator, iii) user and occupant, iv) energy and technology.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

ARSC 6310 - Sustainable Design

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/arsc6310/

Credits: 3.0

Description: The purpose of this course is to present learning resources and metrics to identify, measure and value sustainability in architecture and building construction. Students will develop a thorough understanding of environmentally responsible design, considering both the technical performance of individual buildings and the social context within which sustainability is now being framed. Through a series of lectures and case studies, students will learn to read and apply green building rating systems and assess the impacts of sustainable design strategies. Using a data-driven approach, students will be able to advocate effectively for sustainable design and environmental responsibilities.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

LIBS 7001 - Critical Reading and Writing

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/libs7001/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This is a course in advanced composition and rhetoric, in which students will develop skills in complex critical analysis and interpretation by analyzing and evaluating materials from a variety of discourses or genres, including visual, online, and print; developing and writing essays, including critiques and research papers; applying and discussing principles of rhetoric and critical theory; examining and using methods of interpretation and analysis from the humanities and social sciences; evaluating the credibility of primary and secondary sources, including as it applies to media literacy, and for the purposes of academic research; situating discourses within their historical context and relevant to rhetorical theories of different periods (for example, Aristotle in the ancient world and Bakhtin in the twentieth century). The course format will include lecture, discussion, and both individual and group activities.

Prerequisites: 50% in BCIT ENGL 1177 or equivalent, or 6 credits BCIT Communication at 1100-level or above, or 3 credits of a university/college first-year social science or humanities course.

### Level 6 (15 weeks)

ARSC 6005 - Architectural Design Studio 2

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/arsc6005/

Credits: 7.5

Description: The purpose of this studio is to develop an architectural approach to more complex design problems. Students will broaden their skills and knowledge from previous coursework to develop a site and user-specific design project, at a mid-size building scale. A series of exercises will approach issues of context, program, scale, organization, space, form, structure and environmental systems. Design intentions will be investigated and tested at the scale of the detail with drawings and models.

Prerequisites: ARSC 5005 and ARSC 5000

ARSC 6110 - Language of Architecture

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/arsc6110/

Credits: 3.0

Description: Contemporary Architecture: Theory and Practice in a Changing World. The purpose of this course is to investigate key concepts driving architecture and built form around the world, through an examination of the design and implementation process from concept to detail. Students will work individually and in teams to identify design drivers, program organization, conventions and innovation in architectural problem-solving. The course also considers the social roles and responsibilities of architects as public figures shaping the physical environments of communities, designing ‘places’ for people.

Prerequisites: ARSC 5110

ARSC 6350 - Building Envelope Performance

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/arsc6350/

Credits: 3.0

Description: The purpose of this course is to apply building science knowledge and skills to propose a high-performance sustainable project for Design Studio 4: Comprehensive. Through a series of lectures and assignments, students will execute holistic climate and site analysis, develop design strategies for building resilience, and set performance goals and drivers for the comprehensive project. A laboratory approach is used, developing iterations to improve process and results while promoting whole-building systems thinking.

Prerequisites: ARSC 5350

LIBS 7002 - Applied Ethics

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/libs7002/

Credits: 3.0

Description: Fosters abilities and values required for ethical decision making at work. Develops skills in logical analysis, a working knowledge of moral principles and theories, and the ability to diagnose and resolve moral disagreements commonly found at work. Examines and applies moral principles to historically famous cases in manufacturing, human resources, management, engineering, health care, and computing.

Prerequisites: BCIT ENGL 1177, or 6 credits BCIT Communication at 1100-level or above, or 3 credits of a university/college first-year social science or humanities course.

### Level 7 (15 weeks)

ARSC 6070 - Architectural Tectonics

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/arsc6070/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This course explores tectonics in architecture, relating form to materials, construction and structure. It is the study of architecture as a spatial concept expressed through the relative order of structure, detail and environmental context. The course investigates and tests the design with a built form, further refining the evolution of detail based on aesthetics, function, durability and current construction practice.

Prerequisites: 60% in ARSC 6005

ARSC 7005 - Architectural Design Studio 3

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/arsc7005/

Credits: 7.5

Description: The purpose of this studio is to introduce integrated design to the architectural process, applied to a large architectural project with complex systems requirements. A series of exercises will approach the synthesis of architectural principles with engineering concepts to propose architecturally integrated structures and environmental systems with advanced performance benchmarks. Students will be working independently, in teams and with industry consultants to achieve climate-resilient and regenerative designs.

Prerequisites: 60% in ARSC 6005

ARSC 7006 - Predesign Research

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/arsc7006/

Credits: 3.0

Description: The purpose of this course is to provide a structured approach to predesign research, analysis and response, in order to propose an informed Comprehensive project. A series of lectures, workshops and processes simulate professional practice for complex building pre-design. Working independently, in teams, with industry consultants and stakeholders, students will navigate regulatory frameworks to articulate site, program, budget and development parameters for the future Comprehensive project. The outcome of the course is an architectural pre-design brief ready for implementation in the project context (including authorities having jurisdiction, sociocultural issues, climate resilience, local design guidelines and urban design initiatives).

Prerequisites: 60% in ARSC 6005 and 50% in LIBS 7001

ARSC 7350 - Building Envelope Lab

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/arsc7350/

Credits: 3.0

Description: The purpose of this course is to apply building science knowledge and skills to the architectural design process. This course will integrate with ARSC 7006 (Predesign Studio). Through a series of lectures and work assignments, students will create a holistic site analysis. Design strategies will be integrated for resilience to set performance goals and drivers for the graduating project.

Prerequisites: ARSC 6350

BUSA 7250 - Management Skills and Applications

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/busa7250/

Credits: 3.0

Description: The course provides an overview of the basic skills of a manager and applies these skills through a series of projects and case studies. It examines the evolution of management and the organizational culture and environment. It also teaches the decision-making skills and the skills involved in planning, organizing, leading and controlling, including planning and facilitating change, teamwork, applying motivational techniques and effective communication.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

### Level 8 (15 weeks)

ARSC 8005 - Design Studio 4: Comprehensive

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/arsc8005/

Credits: 9.0

Description: The purpose of this studio is to execute comprehensive design for a large mixed-use high-performance building. Based on their pre-design brief, students will work through a series of exercises to develop industry-standard deliverables. The course models professional integrated design practices, including engagement with industry consultants and stakeholders. Students will demonstrate the ability to synthesize and apply knowledge and skills developed throughout their studies, applying leading-edge tools and techniques for high-performance building to a holistically sustainable architectural design, resolved from concept through to construction details.

Prerequisites: 60% in ARSC 7005 and 60% in ARSC 7006 and 60% in ARSC 7350ARSC 8355‡(‡must be taken concurrently)

ARSC 8075 - Architectural Practice

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/arsc8075/

Credits: 3.0

Description: The purpose of this course is to introduce the working processes and considerations of architectural practice. Students will gain familiarity with the different types of architectural practice organization and basic principles of project management, covering various roles and responsibilities. Case studies are used to illustrate stakeholder dynamics, project sociologistic, political and economics through real-world examples. Students will engage with building industry professionals and professional resources throughout the course.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

ARSC 8355 - Building Science Design Project

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/arsc8355/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This course holistically integrates previously learned tools and processes to inform and refine the graduating project. Through a series of lectures and studio assignments, students will pursue transformative architectural solutions based on course-specific performance goals. Students will self-evaluate building performance on an ongoing basis throughout the course.

Prerequisites: 60% in ARSC 7350 and 60% in ARSC 7006ARSC 8005‡(‡must be taken concurrently)

### Professional Electives (6.0 credits):

ARSC 7040 - Directed Studies in Architectural Science

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/arsc7040/

Credits: 3.0

Description: Directed Studies enables the students to propose an area of study that would lend itself to increased learning through the exploration of study in Selected Topics areas. This Technical Elective course of study may vary from term to term and may include subjects such as: technology and design; environmental ethics; Studies abroad, architectural workshops; Evaluation, Communication and Monitoring of Project deliveries. The student must submit a Statement of Intent which is to be reviewed and approved by the Program Head prior to the student's ability to register for this elective.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

ARSC 7050 - Wood Design

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/arsc7050/

Credits: 3.0

Description: The purpose of this course is to introduce traditional wood design and contemporary applications in large-scale, non-traditional buildings. Students will examine new engineered wood products, manufacturing technologies, and changes to building codes / other legislation to assess new possibilities for wood design and construction. This course will: i) provide an introduction to the attributes of wood as a building material; ii) give an overview of emerging technologies in frame, mass timber and composite construction in British Columbia; iii) explain how building industry use of wood fits within the broader context of environmental, economic and social sustainability; and iv) discuss the physical and psychological benefits of wood in architectural applications.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

## Program Details

Program length

The Bachelor of Architectural Science is a four-year program. Students complete two years in a diploma program prior to entry and two years in the degree program.

Grading

A student who fails or withdraws from one to three courses in a specific term may be required to move from "set" registration to course-by-course registration. In these cases, the student will be notified by mail at the end of term in which the failures or withdrawals occurred. Students will be required to meet with their program head during the first week of the following term to create an approved registration plan. Students on "set" registration will be given first priority for course placement; students who are registering on a course-by-course basis and who are registering for at least 60% of a standard term course load will be given second priority; and students registering course-by-course with less than 60% of a standard term course load will be given third priority for course placement. A student who fails or withdraws from four or more courses in any one term will be required to withdraw from the program. Prior to applying for re-admission the student must complete the failed course(s) successfully through BCIT Part-time Studies or an alternate route approved in writing by the program head. Completion of the missing course(s) does not guarantee re-admission into the program.

Program delivery

In person: This program is delivered on campus.

## Graduating & Jobs

introduction

The Bachelor’s degree in Architectural Science will prepare graduates with the skill set needed for advanced placement within the architecture, engineering, and construction industry.

Graduate employment outcomes

The BCIT student outcomes report presents summary findings from the annual survey of former students administered by BC Stats one to two years after graduation. These reports combine the last three years of available results for the 2022-2024 BCIT Outcomes Surveys of 2021-2023 graduates and for Degree 2020-2022 graduates. The reports are organized into three-page summaries containing information on graduates’ labour market experiences and opinions regarding their education. More detailed information can be accessed at the BC Student Outcomes website. To view these results, you may need to have the Adobe Acrobat Reader installed in your Web browser.
