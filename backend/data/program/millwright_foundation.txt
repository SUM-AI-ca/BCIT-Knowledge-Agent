Title: Millwright Foundation

## Overview

Delivery

in person

Overview text

The Millwright Training prepares students for entry-level employment as an apprentice in the millwright trade. Basic theory and related information along with hands-on shop practice will enable students to become competent in basic millwright duties. Graduates receive credit for first-year apprenticeship technical training. The Millwright Foundation program is for those who want to gain hands-on experience to confidently gain employment and continue their studies as apprentices while working in the field. Millwrights work from plans and blueprints, install equipment and align parts or components. They can also maintain and repair machinery as required. They learn how to use grinders and lathes so they can make their own parts if necessary. Maintenance includes cleaning and lubricating, or adjusting valves and seals. They must inspect and examine the equipment to find and investigate problems and breakdowns, although some millwrights specialize in installation of machinery only. BCIT also offers the Apprenticeship Technical Training in millwright. Students requiring accommodations for in-class exams and Skilled Trades BC Exams must register with BCIT Accessibility Services prior to their program start date. During busier times of the year, it can take several weeks before an appointment is available prompting the need to have this completed before attending class. ### Required Courses:

MILL 1200 - Planning and Workplace Safety

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mill1200/

Credits: 6.0

Description: The Planning and Workplace Safety course trains students to a skill level equivalent to Level One Apprenticeship in Safe Work Practices. Students become familiar with: WorkSafeBC – Occupational Health and Safety Regulations (OHS), Environmental Regulations, Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), Safe Work Areas, Fire Prevention and Control, and Ergonomic considerations. The content of the course is aligned with SkilledTradesBC’s 2014 Competency Profile Chart and includes the following competencies: A1, A2, A3, A4, A6 and A7.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

MILL 1201 - Applied Physics and Math Calculations

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mill1201/

Credits: 6.0

Description: This course reviews the math concepts students will require to perform trade math calculations. The course also provides foundation knowledge in applied physics as it relates to the principles of simple machines and metallurgy. The course content prepares students to Level One Apprentice proficiency. The content of the course is aligned with SkilledTradesBC’s 2014 Competency Profile Chart and includes the following competencies: B1 and B3.

Prerequisites: MILL 1200

MILL 1202 - Measuring and Layout Tools

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mill1202/

Credits: 4.0

Description: This course prepares students to use metric and imperial measuring instruments and layout tools to a level equivalent to Level One of the Apprenticeship program. Students will learn the use, proper handling and storage of measuring tools along with gaining skills in laying out a workpiece. The content of the course is aligned with the SkilledTradesBC’s 2014 Competency Profile Chart and includes the following competencies: C1 and C2.

Prerequisites: MILL 1200 and MILL 1201

MILL 1203 - Industrial Trades Drawing and Technical Information Processes

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mill1203/

Credits: 4.0

Description: This course provides students with an introduction to the interpretation of technical drawings and related technical references. Students will learn to use communication and team skills, as well as skills in accessing technical resources such as assembly and detail drawings, reference manuals and vendor parts catalogues. The content of the course is aligned with SkilledTradesBC’s 2014 Competency Profile Chart and includes the following competencies: A5, A6 and A7.

Prerequisites: MILL 1200

MILL 1204 - Tools, Fasteners and Fittings

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mill1204/

Credits: 4.0

Description: The course introduces beginning students to the proper use of hand tools, portable power tools and various types of fasteners commonly found in Industry. In addition to emphasizing the safe use of tools and fasteners, students will acquire basic skills with a range of tools to produce a workpiece, as well as practice their skills to assemble and disassemble mechanical components. Students will become proficient to Level One Apprenticeship. The content of the course is aligned with the SkilledTradesBC's 2014 Competency Profile Chart and includes the following competencies: B6, C1 and C3.

Prerequisites: MILL 1200

MILL 1205 - Rigging, Cranes and Mobile Equipment

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mill1205/

Credits: 2.0

Description: This course prepares student to use ladders to Level One Apprenticeship proficiency. In addition the course provides students with an introduction to safe rigging practices and a basic introduction to the use and operating maintenance of mobile equipment as well as scaffolds. The content of the course is aligned with SkilledTradesBC’s 2014 Competency Profile Chart and includes the following competencies: C5.

Prerequisites: MILL 1200

MILL 1206 - Shop Fixed Equipment, Machining, Welding and Steel Fabrication

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mill1206/

Credits: 16.0

Description: In this course, students will learn the basics of a range of shop equipment found in typical millwright shops. Students will become competent in machining a workpiece, oxy-fuel cutting and welding, shielded metal arc welding and steel fabrication techniques. Students will have sufficient hands-on opportunities to obtain basic skill levels equivalent to those of a Level One Apprentice. In addition, they will create workpieces of varying complexity. The content of the course is aligned with the SkilledTradesBC’s 2014 Competency Profile Chart and includes the following competencies: C4, F1, F2, F3 and F4.

Prerequisites: MILL 1200 and MILL 1202 and MILL 1203

MILL 1207 - Installation and Maintenance of Rotating Components and Mechanical Systems

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mill1207/

Credits: 4.0

Description: The purpose of this course is to provide students with foundation skills and knowledge to enhance job readiness with employers at an entry level job. Lubrication and the maintenance of seals and bearings is an important and critical part of maintenance. Students will obtain sufficient skill and knowledge in this area to work competently in an entry level job placement. The skills acquired in this course are beyond those of Level One Apprentice but are necessary for the foundation student to meet basic job ready requirements.

Prerequisites: MILL 1200 and MILL 1203

MILL 1208 - Prepare for Employment and Final Project

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mill1208/

Credits: 2.0

Description: In this course students will learn the basic planning skills they need to make a successful entry into the workforce. Students will complete a Final Project designed to take the student through various steps using knowledge they have acquired throughout the program. In their Final Project, students will have the opportunity to apply knowledge regarding safety requirements, rigging requirements, drafting, repairing of threads, packing material, layout procedures, removal and installation of components, repairing of these components and replacing them. In addition, students will learn and work through the process of creating cover letters and resumes directed towards obtaining employment within the millwright trade. A job search component is included in this process.

Prerequisites: MILL 1200 and MILL 1203

## Graduating & Jobs

introduction

The potential for entry into a millwright apprenticeship is good at this time. The expertise gained in this entry-level course is suitable for application to other trades and almost all industries. A graduate from this course should expect entry-level employment until an apprenticeship becomes available. Anywhere that machinery exists there is usually work for a millwright. Employment for millwrights is mainly centred on the major BC industries: mining, pulp mills, wood processing and petrochemical plants, with smaller numbers being employed in the manufacturing field and the food processing industry. The construction industry also employs millwrights on short- and long-term contracts where their main function is the installation of equipment.

Graduate employment outcomes

The BCIT student outcomes report presents summary findings from the annual survey of former students administered by BC Stats one to two years after graduation. These reports combine the last three years of available results for the 2022-2024 BCIT Outcomes Surveys of 2021-2023 graduates and for Degree 2020-2022 graduates. The reports are organized into three-page summaries containing information on graduates’ labour market experiences and opinions regarding their education.
