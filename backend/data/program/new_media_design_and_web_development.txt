## Overview

Overview text

Learn to design and produce interactive, user-friendly media projects, responsive websites, integrated social media campaigns, and visually appealing stories and dynamic, rich multi-media content with the BCIT Diploma in New Media Design and Web Development. Demand for the creative and technical skills of new media professionals is increasing rapidly, as companies look for new and innovative ways to engage with clients and customers. The Diploma in New Media Design and Web Development provides you with applied skills in the following key areas, instrumental for success in this fast-growing field: Employers value BCIT’s applied, job-ready approach to education and training. It’s the BCIT advantage that sets our graduates apart from the pack. Learn more about program goals and how this program prepares you for a career in new media on the Program Details page. This program is right for you if: If this sounds like you, apply today on the Entrance Requirements page, where you will also learn more about entrance requirements for this program. Demand for seats in this program is high, so apply early. New media professionals work in a broad range of positions, throughout all types of public and non-profit organizations, and private industry – from graphic design studios to advertising agencies, digital entertainment companies, and in-house marketing and communications departments. Many new media professionals work independently for clients or go on to launch their own business. Learn more about the career opportunities awaiting you on the Graduating and Jobs page.



## Courses

Introduction

As a student in this program, you’ll have access to the most up-to-date, relevant instruction by working industry professionals, hands-on lab time, tutorial sessions, and field consultations. All this is designed to give you the most current, job-related skills to help you begin your career quickly.

Class hours

Class hours Courses will be scheduled either during the day or evenings on weekdays for a maximum of 27 instructional hours a week.

### Term 1

MDIA 1165 - Digital Photography

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mdia1165/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This course is an introduction to hands-on digital photography and the image optimization processes. The course is designed to provide an emphasis on effective composition, core principles of photography and meaningful photographic contents for web, print and mobile applications. Students will learn how to plan and shoot photos; use DSLR or their own cameras; and explore techniques on lighting, optics, and image management/storage. Other topics include photo retouching, black and white photography, HDR images, panoramic photos and processing camera RAW files. Students will be assessed by a combination of group and individual projects and a final exam.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

MDIA 1189 - Web Services and File Management

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mdia1189/

Credits: 1.5

Description: This course is an introduction to the Macintosh operating system and the BCIT systems that students will be expected to comfortably use during the program. The course will introduce the students to the BCIT lab environment and policies, including network printing, file storage/retrieval and submitting requests for helpdesk support via eHelpdesk. The students will register a domain name, secure their own commercial web space and learn to operate basic web services including File transfer (FTP), email management and C-panel features. Students will be assessed by three individual assignments and a final exam.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

MDIA 1190 - Web Technologies

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mdia1190/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This course will introduce students to the foundation of front-end development, code management, and familiarize them with the basic tools of web design. Focusing on web standards as outlined by the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C), students will learn to hand code HTML 5, CSS3 (Cascading Styles Sheets) to control overall hierarchy structure, functionality, layout and appearance of websites. In addition, students will learn about uploading files to a web server or host. Students will be assessed by a combination of quizzes, and individual projects.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

MDIA 1510 - New Media Communications

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mdia1510/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This course focuses on effective targeted writing for new media applications. Through research, discussion and practice students will create, and prepare content in writing for cross-platform deployment: print, web and mobile. Students will explore how people read screen text in contrast to printed text, view examples of high and low-impact content, and practice adapting traditional print media to new media publications. Students will critically evaluate context, content and audiences in order to define the accuracy, relevancy and effectiveness of written communications. During the course, students will be evaluated through different means: written exam, assignments and projects.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

MDIA 1905 - Graphic Design for New Media

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mdia1905/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This course covers the fundamentals of graphic design workflows and techniques in visual communications. Students will learn the theory and practice of design and layout to visualize and develop problem-solving ideas and render effective content using the principles of design, typography, and colour in layouts for print and web products. Paper based documentation, as well as very basic use of Illustrator or InDesign, will be utilized to complete projects from brainstorming to final presentations. Students will be assessed by a combination of quizzes, group and individual projects.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

MDIA 1915 - Storytelling for New Media

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mdia1915/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This course explores new media storytelling for personal, social and corporate branding. Students will learn about structuring effective narrative of digital media through the creation of written, video, web, and interactive projects. Storytelling techniques in video game, web design, and film will be evaluated. Students develop a critical perspective on how to engage and inspire various audiences across several platforms. The course will also prepare students with the means to choose digital media that best expresses their ideas and to create projects that are in accordance with their interests and career goals. Students will be assessed by a combination of group and individual projects, final exam and a term project.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

MDIA 2185 - Photoshop for New Media

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mdia2185/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This course introduces students to the techniques used in digital image creation, manipulation and management. Students will learn how colour is defined in digital images, the differences between Raster and Vector based graphics and techniques, how to identify the correct resolutions and pixel dimensions for different purposes, destructive versus nondestructive editing techniques, how to isolate and edit a part of an image, how to colour correct images, and the proper workflow for creating a photo based project in Photoshop and exporting the result for variant platforms.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

MDIA 2200 - The Law and New Media

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mdia2200/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This course focuses on the legal and economic constraints associated with structuring a business in the field of new media. Topics examined include the most appropriate business entity given specific legal and tax considerations, improving negotiation skills, establishing a binding media/publishing contract, protecting intellectual property, licensing and distributing creative work, and how to apply a "hands-on" approach to problem solving, acquiring media, and the negotiating and the drafting of contracts, with special emphasis on ethics and critical thinking. Students will be assessed by a combination of quizzes, team assignments, individual projects and written exam

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

MDIA 2533 - Information Architecture

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mdia2533/

Credits: 3.0

Description: Information architecture is an extensive discipline involving the organization, labeling, navigation, and searching systems for web and interactive products that enable users to find and manage information more successfully. This foundation course addresses issues such as how web usability, user experience, and search engine optimization integrate into an effective information hierarchy. Students will construct mobile and web sitemaps, task diagrams and wireframes. A combination of lectures, in-class discussions, hands-on exercises, group work, student presentations, and tool demonstrations will be used throughout this course. Students will be assessed by a combination of quizzes, term project, group and individual projects.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mdia2092/

Students are also introduced to skills that help them to analyze when to get legal and accounting assistance. MDIA 2294 - Web Scripting 1

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mdia2294/

Students will be assessed by a combination of quizzes, group and individual projects.

MDIA 2540 - UI / UX Strategy 1

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/mdia2540/

Credits: 3.0

Description: The course teaches User Interface Design (UI) and User Experience (UX) as a strategic area of development for new media. This involves assessing the technical needs of an interface and the appropriate delivery platform. By understanding the power and limitations of Interface Design and User Experience tools and how to work with them, students will create prototyping systems that effectively employ colour, layout, and typography to receive user feedback. The students will learn about design methods, design process, testing and inspections of user interfaces as well as prototyping in three levels of fidelity both on paper and digitally. Students will be assessed by a combination of quizzes, group and individual projects.

Prerequisites: MDIA 2533

MDIA 2565 - Video Storytelling

Student will work individually on smaller assignments and in teams to produce a final project.

MDIA 3305 - Digital Layouts and Interactive Media

## Program Details

introduction
