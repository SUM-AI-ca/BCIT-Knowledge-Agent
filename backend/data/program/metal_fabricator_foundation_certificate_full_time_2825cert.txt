## Metal Fabricator Foundation
Certificate Full-time School of Construction and the Environment
## Overview
The Metal Fabricator Foundation program prepares students for a career dealing with the selection, layout, shearing, cutting, punching, drilling, forming, fitting and welding of steel plates and structural steel shapes into products for the forest, mining, construction, transportation and agricultural industries.
Delivery: in person. See details .
Typical projects that involve metal fabricators include bridges, buildings, hoppers, conveyors, towers, cranes, heavy-mining equipment, logging equipment, and ship parts and equipment.
The Metal Fabricator Foundation program has General Admission intakes in September and March, as well as special intakes for High School cohorts in February through the High School Partnership Program . Please contact the Admissions Department for specific dates and registration.
If you are interested in working in the marine and shipbuilding industry, you may be eligible to enter the Marine Fitter Apprenticeship program.
Alternatively, if you are already indentured with Skilled Trades BC , or seeking more information on the apprenticeship itself, check out our Metal Fabricator Apprenticeship web page.
### The Program
Basic theory and related information along with hands-on shop practice enable students to become competent in basic steel fabrication methods for products such as steel buildings, tanks and equipment.
Good hand/eye coordination and good physical condition are desirable. Potential students with medical or physical difficulties should contact Accessibility Services to arrange an interview (telephone interview if out of town) with the institute’s rehabilitation specialist at 604-451-6963.

### Program matrix
Required Courses: | Credits
MTLF 1200 | Apply Safe Work Practices Safe work practices follow a set of guidelines on how to perform specific tasks in the shop and should be observed whenever working in the shop to prevent hazards before they happen. In this course, students will learn the theoretical and practical components of what constitutes safe work practices, as outlined in the WorkSafeBC guidelines. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 2.0
MTLF 1205 | Math for Metal Fabricators In this course, you will learn how to use basic mathematics concepts and their use in metal fabrication such as, whole numbers, fractions, decimals, percentages, ratios, proportions, metric imperial conversions, Pythagoras Theorem, use of calculator, perimeter, area, volume basics. In addition, you will learn how to apply Geometric Line theory to develop parallel and intersecting lines for layout. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 2.0
MTLF 1210 | Interpretation of Drawings for Fabricators Metal fabricators use blueprints and sketches and drawings during the fabrication process. In this course you will learn to identify basic lines, views and symbols from drawings. You will also learn to draw a detailed sketch and create a bill of materials based on information in a blueprint. As you work through the course you will use drawings to fabricate structural and plate projects. Prerequisite(s): MTLF 1205 course outline | 3.0
MTLF 1215 | Hand and Power Tools In this course, you will learn to identify and use metal fabrication hand and power tools such as grinders, drills, sanders, threaders, etc. Apply WorkSafeBC standards and regulations, shop specific standard operating procedures, and maintenance for all tools and equipment. Prerequisite(s): MTLF 1200 course outline | 2.0
MTLF 1220 | Metal Fabrication Shop Equipment In this course you will learn to identify and use Metal Fabrication shop equipment during the fabrication of projects. Shop equipment includes plate shear, ironworker, plate rolls, band saws, pipe threader, brake press, and drill presses. You will also apply WorkSafeBC standards and regulations as well as shop specific standard operating procedures for use and maintenance of the equipment. Prerequisite(s): MTLF 1200 course outline | 5.0
MTLF 1225 | Develop & Layout Templates In this course you will learn to use drafting principles and applications in the Metal Fabrication industry. As you work through this course, you will engage in extensive lab work to apply triangulation and parallel line development techniques as well as the alphabet of lines (various kinds of lines) to create templates from blueprints. Parallel line development is a geometrical method of developing flat, two dimensional patterns for cylindrical and rectangular shaped objects. You will also learn the principles of the various types of pattern development. Prerequisite(s): MTLF 1205 and MTLF 1210 course outline | 2.0
MTLF 1230 | Thermal Cutting Equipment & Processes In this course you will learn how to cut ferrous metals using oxy-fuel processes and non-ferrous metals with the plasma arc cutting processes. You will apply the theoretical concepts explained in the class in the practical projects to fabricate structural and plate components. This will include setting up oxy-fuel and plasma cutting equipment in order to cut, pierce and bevel various thickness of metal plate, cope structural shapes for fit-up, and use flame straightening. Prerequisite(s): MTLF 1200 course outline | 2.0
MTLF 1235 | Arc Welding Processes This course introduces the fundamentals of arc welding, which includes the various welding processes, equipment, and accessories. The course provides hands-on shop training in SMAW (shielded metal arc welding) and GMAW (gas metal arc welding). As you work through the course, you will have many opportunities to use the various welding processes and equipment in the shop, under the supervision of the instructor. Prerequisite(s): MTLF 1200 course outline | 2.0
MTLF 1240 | Blueprint Reading This course introduces the basic principles of job specifications and blueprint reading. As you work through the course, you will study line types, isometric views, orthographic projections, dimensioning methods and general notes. Topics include using multi-page blueprints to locate information and solve problems in the shop, using shop drawings by correlating specifications and use math associated with blueprints. Prerequisite(s): MTLF 1205 course outline | 5.0
MTLF 1245 | Pattern Development Plate development is a geometrical method of developing flat, two dimensional patterns for cylindrical and rectangular shaped objects. These objects can be in the form of piping, vessels, tanks, ducting, etc. In this course, you will learn to develop patterns using parallel and intersecting lines in the creation of templates for headers, branches, miters, tees, elbows, and copes and apply those templates to shop projects. Prerequisite(s): MTLF 1205 and MTLF 1210 and MTLF 1240 course outline | 3.0
MTLF 1250 | Handle Materials This course covers the safe practices for lifting and moving objects, the use of personal protective equipment (PPE) and the use of crane or hoist hand signals to direct movements of a load. In this course you will learn to apply math to calculate mass of structural shapes and plates in both imperial and metric units using the structural handbook and mass tables, calculate loads and tensions, center of balance, and identify rigging components to safely hoist and handle a load. Apply WorkSafeBC standards and regulations for loads being hoisted as well as the use of forklift, dollies, jacks, and rollers. Prerequisite(s): MTLF 1200 and MTLF 1205 course outline | 3.0
MTLF 1255 | Project Finishing Preparing metal surfaces for finishing requires attention to specification and adherence to quality standards. Metal products can be finished in a variety of methods, including galvanized, anodized and specialty epoxy finishes. In this course, you will learn how to apply codes and standards for finishing shop projects, including surface preparation and application of primers and paints. Prerequisite(s): MTLF 1200 and MTLF 1205 and MTLF 1210 and MTLF 1215 and MTLF 1240 course outline | 1.0
MTLF 1260 | Shop Practical Projects In this course, you will apply safe work practices to the fabrication of shop projects. The course will require integration of knowledge of shop tools and equipment, blueprint reading and interpretation as well as template development to fabricate a variety of shop projects, such as ring to ring, J hook, machine guard, mounting bracket, Jack stand, load divider, three-piece elbow complete with branch. The course also incorporates the production of a capstone project. Prerequisite(s): MTLF 1200 and MTLF 1205 and MTLF 1210 and MTLF 1215 and MTLF 1220 and MTLF 1225 and MTLF 1230 and MTLF 1235 and MTLF 1240 and MTLF 1245 course outline | 12.0
MTLF 1265 | Perform Quality Control In this course, you will learn to identify and apply codes and standards encountered in the metal fabrication trade, interpret architectural information found on drawings, use measuring instruments to inspect finished products, describe different non-destructive testing methods, and use weld measuring equipment. Special focus will be on identification of weld discontinuities and the use of Liquid Penetrant Inspection, Magnetic Particle inspection, etc. Prerequisite(s): MTLF 1200 and MTLF 1205 and MTLF 1210 and MTLF 1215 and MTLF 1240 and MTLF 1245 course outline | 2.0
Total Credits: | 46.0

## Graduating & Jobs
### Job opportunities
Training prepares students for entry-level employment as metal fabricators. Upon successful completion of the program, students may seek employment as apprentices. Industry contacts are available.
This is a prerequisite program for the Marine Fitter endorsement. Visit the Marine Fitter Apprenticeship page for more information.
### Graduate employment outcomes
The BCIT student outcomes report presents summary findings from the annual survey of former students administered by BC Stats one to two years after graduation. These reports combine the last three years of available results for the 2023-2025 BCIT Outcomes Surveys of 2022-2024 graduates and for Degree 2020-2022 graduates. The reports are organized into three-page summaries containing information on graduates’ labour market experiences and opinions regarding their education. More detailed information can be accessed at the BC Student Outcomes website.
