### Term 1 (15 weeks)

DCOM 1500 - Technical Skills

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom1500/

Credits: 6.0

Description: This foundational course aims to prepare students to develop the skills to perform the tasks they will encounter as they progress through the Digital Communications and Wireless Technologies program. The course will include theoretical concepts, as well as practical application of knowledge. With a focus on electrical safety, the course also includes Workplace Hazardous Material Information System (WHMIS) training. Students will learn the basic skills needed to prepare and solder wires to connectors and electronic components, as well as how to build circuits on a breadboard from a schematic diagram. Students will develop the math skills typically used by a telecom technician, including power of ten and engineering notation, metric prefixes, logarithms, and trigonometric functions, among others. Students will also learn basic technical writing and graphing for technical reports.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

DCOM 1510 - DC Circuits

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom1510/

Credits: 6.0

Description: This course provides the foundation required for understanding resistive electronic circuits, including basic electrical principles and components. The concepts of the quantities of charge, voltage, current, resistance, energy and power are developed. Ohm's Law, Kirchhoff's Voltage Law and Kirchhoff's Current Law are used to analyze series, parallel and series-parallel direct current (DC) circuits. DC test equipment will be used for measurements. General troubleshooting strategies and techniques are introduced, with emphasis on methods used to isolate faults in an efficient and logical manner. Analysis of capacitors and inductors in DC circuits is covered. Electromagnetism and the operation of relays is introduced. Theory is reinforced with hands-on practice.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

DCOM 1520 - AC Circuits

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom1520/

Credits: 6.0

Description: This course provides the foundation required for the understanding of electronic circuits with alternating current (AC) sources. The characteristics of various AC waveforms are discussed and measured. Students will learn how to operate AC test equipment (the oscilloscope and function generator). The concepts and calculations of reactive values are emphasized, including phase shift. Students will study the response to AC of various circuit configurations and apply this knowledge to the analysis of resistor capacitor (RC), resistor inductor (RL), and resonant (RLC) circuits. Various practical applications of circuit configurations are explored, including integrators, differentiators and frequency filters. The operation of transformers is covered, including voltage/current ratios and impedance matching. Theory is reinforced with hands-on practice and exposure to troubleshooting techniques.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

DCOM 1530 - Analog Electronics

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom1530/

Credits: 6.0

Description: This course provides the theoretical and practical knowledge necessary to install, maintain, and troubleshoot circuits that employ discrete and integrated semiconductor devices. These include rectifier diodes, Zener diodes, 3-terminal regulator ICs, bipolar junction transistors, field effect transistors, operational amplifiers and switching regulator ICs. Applications of semiconductors as switches, amplifiers, oscillators and regulators are explored. Circuits are built from schematic diagrams and are tested and measured for their operating parameters. Students further develop soldering techniques, including insertion and soldering of components on a printed circuit board, resulting in a complete audio amplifier circuit as a small project.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

DCOM 1540 - Digital Electronics

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom1540/

Credits: 6.0

Description: This course provides the foundation required for understanding digital electronic circuits. The binary, hexadecimal, and decimal number systems are explained, and techniques for converting from one system to another are introduced. The digital logic devices covered include logic gates (NOT, AND, NAND, OR, NOR, XOR and XNOR), timers and pulse generators, combinational and sequential logic, binary adders, comparators, encoders, decoders, 7-segment displays, multiplexers, demultiplexers, latches, flip-flops, binary counters and shift registers. Students will learn analog-to-digital and digital-to-analog conversion techniques, as well as how to interface a digital output to a load (sinking and sourcing). In addition, students will use standard symbols, schematic diagrams, truth tables, waveform representations, timing diagrams and Boolean expressions to represent digital logic functions and circuits. Theory is reinforced with hands-on practice and exposure to troubleshooting techniques.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

### Term 2 (20 weeks)

DCOM 1550 - Network Essentials

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom1550/

Credits: 6.0

Description: The course will introduce basic networking concepts by exploring how networks function in today's world. Basic switch device and end device configuration will be introduced, including the protocols and models used to describe how data is packaged and transported on modern networks. Numbering systems used by communications protocols, including binary and hex, are introduced. Ethernet switching is examined in both theory and lab exercises. IPv4 and IPv6 are introduced and examined in detail using both network hardware and network simulation software. Security fundamentals are introduced in order to identify network vulnerabilities. The course will culminate in the building and configuration of a small network.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

DCOM 1560 - Structured Cabling

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom1560/

Credits: 8.0

Description: This course introduces the installation and test practices associated with telecommunications cabling. The curriculum is aligned with the Telecommunications Industry Association/Electronics Industry Association (TIA/EIA) standards for commercial buildings. The course provides hands-on training for common cable termination systems. Students will use cable scanners to certify and analyze cable performance. This course also introduces Structured Cable System (SCS) infrastructure design and advanced cabling techniques.

Prerequisites: 60% in DCOM 1500

DCOM 2500 - Telecommunications Essentials

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom2500/

Credits: 6.0

Description: In this course, the major elements of the Public Switched Telephone Network (PSTN) are introduced. The components of the PSTN are examined, including outside plant, switching and trunks. The process of setting up a voice circuit across the network is detailed. The conversion of an analog voice signal to a digital equivalent using the Pulse Code Modulation (PCM) process is discussed. The North American Digital Hierarchy for digital communications networks is examined with a focus on the T-1 standard. The creation of a T-1 digital bit stream from multiple independent analog sources utilizing Time Division Multiplexing (TDM) is explored. The components of a digital information transport system are introduced. Local and remote testing and troubleshooting techniques are implemented and evaluated. Safe use of telecom industrial tools is introduced through lab exercises that expose the student to a variety of workplace scenarios.

Prerequisites: 60% in DCOM 1540

DCOM 2510 - RF Principles 1

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom2510/

Credits: 6.0

Description: This course will introduce students to the concepts of electronics that are related to the transmission and reception of radio frequency (RF) signals. The course commences with a review of relevant basic electronic circuits, including filters, amplifiers and oscillators, reinforced with selected laboratory experiments. Wireless communication fundamentals such as the frequency spectrum, noise, RF transmission spectral characteristics, channel bandwidth, and modulation/demodulation technologies will be covered. Information transmission in analog forms is discussed. Frequency synthesizers and phase locked loop (PLL) circuits are also introduced.

Prerequisites: 60% in DCOM 1520 and 60% in DCOM 1530

DCOM 2520 - Routing and Wireless Networks

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom2520/

Credits: 6.0

Description: This course provides insight into the architecture, components, and operations of routers, switches and wireless devices in a small network. Students learn how to configure a router and a switch for basic functionality. By the end of this course, students will be able to configure and troubleshoot networks with technologies such as static routing, virtual LANs (VLANs), inter-VLAN routing, Dot1q trunking, Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol (DHCP), Stateless Address Auto Configuration (SLAAC), Spanning-Tree Protocol (STP), port security, standard access control lists, and device discovery protocols. Students work with both IPv4 and IPv6 addressing and subnetting. First hop redundancy and basic Wireless LAN (WLAN) configuration are also covered.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

DCOM 2530 - Linux Fundamentals

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom2530/

Credits: 4.0

Description: This course prepares students to use the Command Line Interface (CLI) and programming tools to examine and alter files systems, and to download, manage, and update software without extensive use of a Graphical User Interface (GUI). Topics include navigation of file systems and searching for files or text using regular expressions (regex), the use of system resource monitoring commands, and starting and stopping software processes and system services from CLIs. Students will use both Windows and Linux Operating Systems and will gain experience in automating tasks by creating and/or running custom scripts in Ubuntu Linux terminals and multiple Windows interfaces such as PowerShell and Windows System for Linux (WSL).

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

DCOM 2540 - Voice Networks

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom2540/

Credits: 4.0

Description: This course introduces the applications, concepts and implementation of VoIP (Voice over IP) networks. The operation of a VoIP will be compared and contrasted with that of traditional telephony networks. Students will configure a VoIP telephony system on a PC connected to a VoIP switch. Troubleshooting with a network protocol analyzer will be introduced. Session Initiation Protocol (SIP) phones will be introduced and used in the configured PBX. WAN networks and bandwidth considerations will be discussed with reference to the CODECs necessary for implementation

Prerequisites: 60% in DCOM 1540

### Term 3 (15 weeks)

DCOM 2550 - Professional Skills

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom2550/

Credits: 4.0

Description: This course aims to prepare students for the job market by enabling them to learn and practice the skills needed to obtain and maintain employment in industry. Students will examine the importance of workplace appearance, communications, conduct, ethics, and safety. They will study the effective management of customer relations and conflicts with both customers and coworkers, and practice the fundamentals of teamwork while reflecting on the supervisory skills needed to be effective in the areas of motivation, leadership, and training. Students will also explore the basics of negotiating the terms of an offer of employment and learn how to start their own personal Career Plan.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

DCOM 3500 - Enterprise Networking and Security

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom3500/

Credits: 6.0

Description: Building upon static routing, this course expands on dynamic routing and router configuration, explores networking protocols, and applies routing concepts within a lab environment. Topics include Cisco IOS management, routing protocols, network security concepts, Network Address Translation (NAT) and Access Control Lists (ACLs). Routing protocols covered include Open Shortest Path First (OSPF). IPv6 operations, routing, and address assignment are also explored. Students will learn how to configure routers, interpret routing tables, and troubleshoot connectivity issues. Wireless networks and WLAN controllers are explored through theory and network simulation. Students are provided hands-on experience with routers and switches in a networked lab environment. The course covers wide area network (WAN) technologies and Quality of Service (QoS) mechanisms used for secure remote access. The course also introduces software-defined networking, virtualization, and automation concepts that support the digitalization of networks.

Prerequisites: 60% in DCOM 2520

DCOM 3510 - RF Principles 2

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom3510/

Credits: 8.0

Description: This course introduces the student to the theoretical and operational analysis of Angle Modulation (FM & PM) schemes as applied to radio transmission and reception. Common FM transmitter and receiver configurations, technical specifications, and schematics are investigated. Students will receive hands-on experience with basic analog and digital FM modulation technology. Frequency Division Multiplexing (FDM) in FM radio transmissions are examined and students are introduced to advanced digital modulation techniques. The basic theory of spread spectrum radio systems and DSP analog is introduced. Laboratory exercises include programming and performance testing of commercial Land Mobile Radios (LMR). Basic concepts of LMR dispatching are introduced.

Prerequisites: 60% in DCOM 2510

DCOM 3520 - Transmission Lines and Antennas

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom3520/

Credits: 6.0

Description: This course investigates Radio Frequency (RF) signal propagation in cables and through free space. Concepts related to transmission of the RF signal within cables and causes of transmission impairment are explored. Free space propagation of an RF signal as an Electromagnetic (E/M) field is examined. The properties of RF signals radiated by an antenna system will be explored. RF filtering systems are introduced, including cavity filters. The practical component of this course will include forward and reflected power measurements, Voltage Standing Wave Ratio (VSWR) minimization and Time Domain Reflectometry (TDR) fault location techniques for transmission line systems. Antenna operational parameters will be measured and cavity filter alignment will be performed.

Prerequisites: 60% in DCOM 2510

DCOM 3530 - Navigation and Location

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom3530/

Credits: 6.0

Description: In this course, students will explore microwave theory concepts and device applications. The applications of microwave communications within the navigation industry are examined. As the course focuses on the integration of basic land and marine navigation systems in use today, navigation principles , navigational terms, Global Positioning System (GPS) and associated technologies will be covered. Marine navigational information systems will be examined, including Automatic Identification System (AIS), Global Maritime Distress Safety System (GMDSS), and search and rescue (SARS) systems. Tracking and location systems such as Automatic Vehicle Location (AVL) will be investigated.

Prerequisites: 60% in DCOM 2510

### Term 4 (20 weeks)

DCOM 2560 - Fiber Optics and Telecommunications Design

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom2560/

Credits: 8.0

Description: In this course, students will learn to safely install, splice, test and maintain fiber optic (FO) links. They will discuss light transmission, optical glass characteristics, and Wavelength Division Multiplexing (WDM) in optical systems, and use industry-standard practices to handle FO cables and connectors. In addition, students will have the opportunity to splice and terminate fiber spans using both mechanical and fusion splicing techniques. As part of their hands-on learning, students will interpret and produce fiber optic design documents for premises portions of the network using AutoCAD and/or other software tools. Furthermore, they will practice industry-standard assembly and testing techniques for fiber optic connectors and splice enclosures and perform fiber optic span power budget analysis. In a lab setting, students will operate Single-Mode (SM) fiber cleavers and fusion splicers, Optical Time Domain Reflectometers (OTDR), and other inspection and test equipment.

Prerequisites: 60% in DCOM 1560

DCOM 3540 - VoIP Systems

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom3540/

Credits: 6.0

Description: This course focuses on the advanced features of a VoIP PBX. Initial setup and licensing will be discussed and performed. An enterprise-level VoIP PBX will be discussed and implemented. Topics include DHCP, VLANs, WAN networks, as well as user services and features. SIP and T1 trunking, and Automatic Route selection are discussed in-depth and implemented in the lab. Voice Mail and auto-attendant features are designed and configured. Emergency Services Management will be thoroughly examined both in theory and in the lab. The concepts of system clustering and resiliency will be discussed, demonstrated and configured in the lab.

Prerequisites: 60% in DCOM 2540

DCOM 4500 - Wide Area Network Design and Maintenance

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom4500/

Credits: 4.0

Description: In this course, students will learn how to install, document, operate, and maintain an advanced wide area network (WAN) with fiber optic links. Students will discuss design considerations and the practical operation of fiber optic and other communications links. In a lab setting, students will program and test fiber optic spans, implement and test Coarse Wavelength Division Multiplexing (CWDM) links, and plan and execute network troubleshooting. Furthermore, they will design, program and observe the operation of automated failover routes to restore services following simulated network disasters. The course will focus on the proper procedures for handling network equipment cards and foster teamwork and communication skills for students to create and manage the orderly build and turn up of networks. Students will also identify selected Outside Plant (OSP), cable television (CATV), and electrical distribution system components in the aerial and underground and create a service-entrance design in CAD.

Prerequisites: 60% in DCOM 2560

DCOM 4510 - Managing Connected Devices

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom4510/

Credits: 6.0

Description: Sensing devices, sometimes with their own processing ability, communicate with other devices and systems over the Internet or other communications networks and are described as the Internet of Things (IoT). This course examines the role of a telecommunication systems technician in managing IoT devices. The practical installation, operation and troubleshooting of IoT digital devices, their wireless and wired/fiber-optic connections to networks and their role in telecommunications/digital infrastructure are discussed. Current and prospective IoT technologies will be discussed, and service providers will be surveyed. IoT networks will be designed and created in a lab environment where installation, programming and maintenance procedures will be practiced. Programming and testing of IoT devices will be covered via theoretical and practical exercises. Team building and communication skills between the teams and other stakeholders will be emphasized to ensure the orderly installation, turn-up, and maintenance of IoT devices and networks.

Prerequisites: 60% in DCOM 1540 and 60% in DCOM 3500

DCOM 4520 - RF Applications

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom4520/

Credits: 10.0

Description: This course focuses on the configuration and testing of real-world radio systems. Students will configure, modify, test and document digital radio systems using both conventional and trunked mode operation. Cellular system concepts are introduced, over several generations. Radio over IP (RoIP) technology, using TCP/IP networks, is investigated. Students will build and test advanced antenna systems and study radio site DC Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS) power systems. Students will learn how to correctly use technical manuals and radio system documentation. Advanced troubleshooting methodologies are explored in the lab using logical fault-finding. Additional installation training will be provided in industry-specific hand skills related to rack mounting radio and network system equipment. General safety practices specifically related to the RF industry will also be reviewed.

Prerequisites: 60% in DCOM 3530 and 60% in DCOM 3520

DCOM 4530 - Practicum

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/dcom4530/

Credits: 4.0

Description: This course is a work-sharing experience within the digital communications and wireless industries. Students will be placed with a host company to job shadow professional technicians. Students will work in a field environment and have opportunities to apply their knowledge of test equipment and troubleshooting techniques. Activities will be tracked with a daily log book. Students will select a topic based upon the host company's technologies to research and produce a report for evaluation by faculty. This report will also form the basis of a class presentation on their work experience.

Prerequisites: 60% in DCOM 3500 and 60% in DCOM 3520 and 60% in DCOM 3530Student must be on track for graduation.
