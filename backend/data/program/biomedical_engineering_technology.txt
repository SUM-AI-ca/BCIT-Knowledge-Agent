Overview text

The BCIT Biomedical Engineering Technology program is the only one of its kind offered in BC and the only one in Canada that is specifically targeted to both hospital and medical device industries. Our students have access to BCIT’s extensive array of health technology equipment, which prepares them for the workforce as soon as they graduate. ### Level 1 (15 weeks)

BHSC 1101 - Anatomy and Physiology 1 (BMET)

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bhsc1101/

Credits: 4.0

Description: Together with BHSC 2201, this course follows a systems approach to study the relationship between structure and function in the human body. BHSC 1101 begins with a discussion of cells and tissues as basic structural/functional units then introduces regulation and integration of body systems and the role of control systems in homeostasis. Nervous, endocrine, and other major major systems will also be covered. Where appropriate, each course gives examples of the uses of biomedical instruments in diagnosis and treatment of human disease.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

BMET 1100 - Electronics Principles and Practice 1

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bmet1100/

Credits: 8.0

Description: The course covers theoretical and practical basic knowledge of electrical passive components, electrical quantities, their units and relationships. Major topics covered include: DC, simple transient, and AC circuit analysis of RLC circuits, magnetism and magnetic circuits. Each topic is reinforced by practical applications, laboratory exercises, and hands-on instruction on electrical devices and techniques.

Prerequisites: MATH 1781‡and PHYS 1179‡(‡must be taken concurrently)MATH 1781** and PHYS 1178** (** must be taken concurrently unless prior credit is granted)

COMM 1178 - Technical Communication 1 for Biomedical Engineering Technology

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/comm1178/

Credits: 3.0

Description: Introduces you to the writing, reading, listening, and speaking skills you will need to successfully carry out your professional communication tasks. You will learn how to organize and present technical information, write memos/emails and letters, and prepare and deliver an oral presentation. The first term establishes the principles and basic patterns on which you will build more advanced applications in the second term.

Prerequisites: English 12 or equivalent

COMP 1120 - Introduction to Computer Architecture and Configuration

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/comp1120/

Credits: 4.0

Description: This course introduces students to typical PC architecture and organization. It gives an overview of the hardware organization and how operating system and application software interact with the hardware. The course covers PC system configuration, hardware and software installation as well as basic PC preventative maintenance and troubleshooting.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

MATH 1781 - Technical Math for Biomedical Engineering

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/math1781/

Credits: 7.0

Description: Covers systems of linear equations and determinants with application to electrical, networks, logarithmic and exponential functions including the study of electrical transients, dB gain, logarithmic and semilog graphing, trigonometric functions and the graphs of the sinusoidal functions - right triangle geometry with application to impedance and admittance diagrams, complex numbers, rectangular/polar conversion and AC circuit applications, number base conversion and binary number operations with a brief introduction to Boolean logic and Karnaugh mapping.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

PHYS 1178 - Physics for Biomedical Engineering Technology

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/phys1178/

Credits: 4.0

Description: A general level course with emphasis on the application of basic physics principles to biomedical engineering technology. Topics covered include measurement techniques and reporting, equilibrium mechanics, stress and strain relationships, fluid statics and dynamics, wave motion with application to sound and ultrasound, and applied optics.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

### Level 2A (15 weeks)

BHSC 2201 - Anatomy and Physiology 2 (BMET)

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bhsc2201/

Credits: 3.0

Description: BHSC 2201 is a continuation of the systems approach to the study of Human Anatomy and Physiology begun in BHSC 1101. The major systems covered in this course are: skeletal, skeletal muscle, circulatory, respiratory, digestive, and urinary. Examples of the use of biomedical instruments in diagnosis and treatment of diseases involving these systems are given.

Prerequisites: 50% in BHSC 1101

BMET 2200 - Electronics Principles and Practice 2

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bmet2200/

Credits: 7.0

Description: Provides introduction to AC excited RLC circuits and the behavior of the basic active devices and integrated circuits. Topics include frequency response, RC, RL, RLC circuits, and resonant circuits, 3 phase systems, transformers, power suppliers, semiconductor fundamentals, diodes, bipolar transistor and field-effect transistor fundamentals, BJT and FET amplifier circuits, and the operational amplifier circuits. In Laboratory session, students will build a variety of circuits to confirm the theory and use the common test equipment to analyze and troubleshoot electronic circuits. Corequisites: MATH 2781.

Prerequisites: 50% in BMET 1100 and 50% in MATH 1781 and 50% in PHYS 1178MATH 2781‡(‡must be taken concurrently)BMET 1100, MATH 1781, PHYS 1178

BMET 2205 - Biomedical Computer Networks and Data Communication

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bmet2205/

Credits: 5.0

Description: This course provides an introduction to the fundamentals of data communication and networking. The Open System Interconnect (OSI) model is used to discuss network architectures, protocols, topologies, networking hardware and software. Topics include network media, access control, Transmission Control Protocol/Internet Protocol (TCP/IP), switching and routing, Ethernet Standards, IP addressing, wireless LAN Standards, and network security. Analog and digital data communications, transmission methods, coding schemes are also discussed. During the laboratory sessions, local area networks will be designed, built and managed to enhance understanding of the theoretical concepts, and to gain hands-on and troubleshooting experience with computer networks.

Prerequisites: BMET 2200** and BMET 2215** and COMP 1120 and MATH 1781 and PHYS 1178 (**must be taken concurrently unless prior credit is granted)

BMET 2215 - Digital Electronics

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bmet2215/

Credits: 5.0

Description: Introduces the principles and techniques of modern digital systems. Major topics covered include: number systems, logic elements and equations, combinational logic circuits, flip-flop circuits and sequential logic devices counters, digital arithmetic circuits, memory devices, integrated-circuit logic families and interfacing techniques. Each topic is reinforced by practical applications examined in the laboratory section of the course.

Prerequisites: MATH 1781 and BMET 2200** (** must be taken concurrently unless prior credit is granted)

COMM 2278 - Technical Communication 2 for Biomedical Engineering Technology

You will also write short reports and participate in productive project meetings.

COMP 3152 - Software Engineering

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/comp3152/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This course will give students an understanding of software engineering on a variety of levels. Topics include: basic microprocessor/computer architecture, software development methodology and tools, assembly language programming, and C programming. If time permits, elements of scientific algorithms and real-time programming wil also be included. The goal of this course is to give the student an in-depth view of software engineering starting from the hardware level building up to the use of high level programming languages.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

MATH 2782 - Calculus for Biomedical Engineering

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/math2782/

Credits: 5.0

Description: Covers rules of differentiation, rates of change, related rates, differentials, implicit differentiation with application to electrical and mechanical problems, definite and indefinite integral, calculation of mean, RMS values for various electrical waveforms, integration techniques, and the Fourier series applied to electrical circuits.

Prerequisites: 50% in MATH 1781

BMET 2235 - Investigations in Biomedical Engineering Technology

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bmet2235/

Credits: 3.0

Description: In this course, students will carry out guided independent investigations to explore various selected topics or to solve a given problem. Students will collaborate in small teams to discuss the issue, design experiments that will provide more information, construct and validate the experimental setup, collect and analyze the data, and report the results. In some cases, the results of an investigation may lead to new questions to be explored further as time permits. The course will combine academic content from allied disciplines, which may provide the context for the investigation, or where the investigation provides the context in which content is applied. For example, topics from Physics, Biology or Chemistry may provide the context for an investigation, while Math and Communications are applied to analyze and communicate the results of the investigation.

Prerequisites: BMET 1100

### Level 3 (15 weeks)

BMET 3300 - Electronics Principles and Practice 3

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bmet3300/

Credits: 6.0

Description: This is the third course in the study of analog electronic devices and circuits for the BMET program. The course provides further knowledge of linear and non-linear electronic circuits. Major topics covered include: operational amplifier circuits, oscillators and timers, phase locked-loop circuits, voltage regulation, thyristor devices and phase control circuits, data acquisition systems, analog to digital and digital to analog converters and interfacing devices and techniques. Each topic is reinforced by practical applications examined in the laboratory section of the course.

Prerequisites: 50% in BMET 2200 and BMET 2210 and 50% in BMET 2215 and 50% in MATH 2782

BMET 3301 - Biomedical Devices Technology 1

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bmet3301/

Credits: 6.0

Description: Introduce systems approach to study biomedical instrumentations and systems. Describe the characteristics of biological signals and introduces methods to collect, process, display and record such signals. Discuss principles of operation, functional block diagrams, technical specifications, schematic diagrams, performance testing procedures and common problems of selected biomedical devices. Lab exercises are coordinated with lecture content.

Prerequisites: BHSC 2201 and BMET 2200 and BMET 2205 and BMET 2210 and BMET 2215 and MATH 2782 and CHEM 1205** (**must be taken concurrently unless prior credit is granted)

BMET 3302 - Medical Device Standards

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bmet3302/

Credits: 6.0

Description: Consists of four modules: • Biomedical Engineering Practice: discussion of o The hospital and medical device industry environment o The functions of the biomedical engineering technologists in R & D and technical support settings o Principles and practice of healthcare technology management • Medical Device Standards and Regulations, which includes o The regulatory context in Canada o Relevant safety standards and inspection procedures o Risk management o Design control • Troubleshooting Techniques: introduces formal and practical approaches to problemsolving and troubleshooting • Preparation for Biomedical Engineering Project: preparation of a project proposal to be carried out in BMET 4402. Laboratory sessions, projects and assignments reinforce the theory and allow you to gain experience in electrical safety measurements and troubleshooting techniques.

BMET 3305 - Computer Network Applications in Healthcare

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bmet3305/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This is the second Biomedical Computer Networks and Data Communications course. Students will examine the computer networks with applications in healthcare and in greater depth. The course introduce students to the Wide Area Networks. Topics include IP address sub-netting and super-netting, virtual LAN, IPv6, virtual private network and network security. Theory and laboratory sessions include medical application of infusion pump (wired and wireless), telemetry, physiological monitoring systems, Picture Archiving Communication Systems (PACS) and medical device integration. Healthcare information system standards will be discussed as well as network security and data integrity in the medical network environment.

Prerequisites: BMET 2200 and BMET 2205 and BMET 2215

BMET 3315 - Microcontrollers and Embedded Systems

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bmet3315/

Credits: 5.0

Description: This lab-lecture course introduces the student to the principles and procedures necessary to design and develop hardware and software for microcontroller-based embedded systems. Course topics include: internal microcontroller architecture, structure and operation of various hardware components used aboard the microcontroller (timer, pulse-width modulator, analog to digital converter), the interrupt mechanism, interfacing methods, and software and hardware tools used for debugging and testing. Each topic is reinforced by practical applications examined in the laboratory section of the course.

Prerequisites: BMET 2200 and BMET 2215 and COMP 3152

CHEM 1205 - General and Organic Chemistry for Biomedical Engineering

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/chem1205/

Credits: 5.0

Description: Starts with a review of the atom, moles, chemical equations and stoichiometry. An overview of bonding is presented. Oxidation and reduction will then be discussed with reference to balancing redox equations. Solution stoichiometry is then studied. Acid-base chemistry is studied with emphasis on the difference between strong and weak electrolytes and different types of buffer solutions. Electrochemistry is introduced with emphasis on different types of voltaic cells. Then the major groups of organic compounds are considered and the basic physical and chemical properties of each group are described. The laboratory work will acquaint the student with the basic techniques used in chemistry as well as several techniques used in a clinical laboratory.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

BMET 4402 - Biomedical Engineering Technology Project

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/bmet4402/

Credits: 4.0

Description: During the course, students will undertake and complete a biomedical engineering project. From the concept developed during BMET 3302, students will develop detailed specifications, a project plan and schedule of the project. The project will be carried out with design methodologies that are consistent with ISO 13485 and QSR (21CFR820.30). Following detailed planning, students will participate in the design and development of the project, and will complete the project independently. These tasks may include circuit design, parts acquisition, printed circuit board layout, circuit building, package design and fabrication, wiring, troubleshooting and final testing. Students will maintain a detailed design history file, a logbook of their work throughout the course, and will prepare a technical report at the completion of their project. The process of design, prototyping, verification, validation and documentation will be introduced at different stages of the course. Guidance documents will specify the criteria for expectations with respect to various aspects of design control, risk management, project management, and documentation.

Prerequisites: COMM 3478‡(‡must be taken concurrently)BMET 3300 and BMET 3301 and BMET 3302 and BMET 3305 and BMET 3315 and COMM 3478** (** must be taken concurrently unless prior credit is granted)

## Program Details

introduction

This program aims to provide individuals who are interested in technology and health sciences with entry-level skills to enter a challenging career in biomedical engineering. Biomedical engineering technologists support, develop, and manage healthcare technology. Whereas other allied health practitioners focus on patient care, biomedical engineering technologists focus on the technology used by these health practitioners. The Biomedical Engineering Technology program combines characteristics of both engineering technology and allied health programs. The program graduates biomedical engineering technologists with the ability to participate in the application and development of technology in healthcare, with emphasis on performance assurance, troubleshooting, and problem solving. The curriculum provides practical hands-on understanding in the fundamental principles, operations, and design of medical diagnostic and therapeutic devices and systems (examples: patient monitoring network, cardiac defibrillators, clinical laboratory analyzers, and medical imaging systems). The program provides hands-on laboratory experience throughout, and trains students in engineering problem-solving methodology to allow them to upgrade and maintain their knowledge. BCIT recommends membership in the Canadian Medical and Biological Engineering Society (CMBES) and the Applied Science Technologists and Technicians of British Columbia (ASTTBC). The program encourages students to use their capstone projects to participate in engineering student design competitions organized by the industry. Below is an example: The Simon Cox Student Design Competition is an annual event brings together students from BC universities and colleges to create innovative assistive technology and home automation solutions for people with disabilities. Program length

Two years, full-time beginning in September each year.

Accreditation

Graduates are eligible for registration as Applied Science Technologists (AScT) through ASTTBC after two years of relevant work experience following graduation.

Program delivery

In person: This program is delivered on campus.

Program location

Burnaby Campus 3700 Willingdon Avenue Burnaby, BC



## Graduating & Jobs

introduction

Every hospital and clinic needs biomedical engineering technologists to keep life-saving machines functioning at their best. Biomedical engineering professionals develop new medical devices and products to diagnose and treat patients. Work opportunities are dynamic and diverse, with room for advancement and flexibility. Creative. Innovative. Skilled. Biomedical engineering technology grads have numerous options. From hospitals, technical support, sales, operations, design, and innovation, grads can choose the field that best suits their skills and passion. Entry-level positions, such as a hospital biomedical engineering technologist, start at salaries of approximately $74,000 in the public sector. Graduate employment outcomes

The BCIT student outcomes report presents summary findings from the annual survey of former students administered by BC Stats one to two years after graduation. These reports combine the last three years of available results for the 2022-2024 BCIT Outcomes Surveys of 2021-2023 graduates and for Degree 2020-2022 graduates. The reports are organized into three-page summaries containing information on graduates’ labour market experiences and opinions regarding their education. More detailed information can be accessed at the BC Student Outcomes website. To view these results, you may need to have the Adobe Acrobat Reader installed in your Web browser.
