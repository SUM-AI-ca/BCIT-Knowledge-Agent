## Script Supervision and Continuity for Film and TV
Microcredential Part-time School of Business + Media
## Overview
The Script Supervision and Continuity for Film and TV Microcredential is tailored for individuals seeking to advance their careers in script supervision. This program comprises 60 in-class hours and is conducted in-person. Students receive comprehensive training and preparation to enter the script supervising field.
Delivery: in person. See details .
If you’ve ever been curious about what’s involved to bring the words on the page up into the film you see or wondered why a script supervisor is also in charge of continuity, this program offers some answers. Whether you are already a film technician or contemplating work in Vancouver’s busy TV/film production industry, learning the skills necessary to become a professional working script supervisor might open a new door for you.
## Entrance Requirements
This Microcredential will attract students with little film experience all the way to crew technicians who already work in the industry. Crew technicians in other departments or students new to film can discover an alternative door into the film industry.

XBST 1009 | Script Supervision: The Overview ​In this two-day workshop, you'll learn the basic ins and outs of how a Script Supervisor in the Continuity Department operates. Covering the fundamentals of Script Supervising and Continuity, you'll gain insight into the history and development of this position. With this in-depth overview of what the job entails, you'll have a clearer understanding if this is a career path for you. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 1.0
XBST 1010 | Script Supervision: The Details The role of the Script Supervisor can sometimes appear overwhelming, so maintaining your Zen is imperative to learning and implementing the specifics. In this two-day workshop, you'll break down the Script Supervisor's many responsibilities and see how it all fits logically together. There is a way to address issues and a hierarchy of where to go as things unfold on set. Understand the Script Supervisor's workflow as you learn more intricate details of what the job entails while on the floor. Prerequisite(s): S in XBST 1009 course outline | 1.0
XBST 1011 | Script Supervision: The Paperwork Along with all the hands-on skills, Script Supervisors support their work with documents that are relied upon by Production and Post Production to allow for a smooth, efficient edit. Learn how information is formatted and where this information gets distributed at the end of each shooting day. In this session, you will learn the paper and pencil version of Script Supervising so as to secure your foundation of knowledge in any setting. Prerequisite(s): S in XBST 1009 and S in XBST 1010 course outline | 1.0
XBST 1012 | Script Supervision: The Full Picture ​From pencil and paper to digital format, you will learn a basic understanding of how to navigate through the ScriptE program. Transferring Script Supervising skills into a finished digital version is key for efficient distribution as you advance in your Script Supervising career. You will gain basic knowledge of ​the software while learning to oversee the living Script Supervising elements onset. Prerequisite(s): S in XBST 1009 and S in XBST 1010 and 50% in XBST 1011 course outline | 1.0
XBST 1016 | Script Supervision: The Wrap and Go ​In this final segment of Script Supervision, along with detailed review, there'll be a practical hands-on mock shoot to really immerse yourself in the position. Then it'll be insight into the industry with union discussion and overview. You'll be able to see possible paths forward of where to go from here.​​ Prerequisite(s): 50% in XBST 1009 and 50% in XBST 1010 and 50% in XBST 1011 and 50% in XBST 1012 course outline | 1.0
