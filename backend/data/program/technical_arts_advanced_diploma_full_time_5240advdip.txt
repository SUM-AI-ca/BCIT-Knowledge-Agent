## Technical Arts
Advanced Diploma Full-time School of Business + Media
## Overview
The Technical Arts Advanced Diploma is a 12-month full-time program led by industry professionals. Students gain hands-on skills to conceptualize and execute visual effects, game development, and animation projects. As technical artists, they integrate artistic vision with technical expertise to enhance and streamline the production process.
Note: BCIT programs respond to the evolving needs of industry. Please note this program is currently undergoing a review that may result in changes to the curriculum. For more information or updates, please contact Mike Monks .
Delivery: blended. See details .
With BCIT’s Advanced Diploma in Technical Arts, you will develop the skills to become a creative problem solver, who can visualize and deliver solutions that bridge the technical and content side of visual effects, game, and animation projects. Technical Artists are in high demand in the digital entertainment and interactive media industry for their ability to integrate and synthesize the artistic vision and technical aspects of projects. They collaborate with artists and programmers to set up and maintain the art production workflow, and identify and develop tools to improve, troubleshoot and streamline the production process.
### About the program
Employers value BCIT’s applied, job-ready approach to education and years of experience training students for success in the digital arts sector.
- 1 year to complete – start your technical arts career in 12 months

Learn more about program goals and how this program prepares you for a career in technical arts on the Program Details page.
### Who should apply for the Advanced Diploma in Technical Arts?
This program is right for you if:
- You want a career that combines creativity and imagination with technical expertise.
- You are a programmer with a creative bent, or an artist with an affinity for logic and detail, ready to launch a new career in an exciting field.
- You are already working in the digital entertainment industry and want to expand your skillset and capitalize on growing job opportunities in this emerging area.
- You hold a diploma, degree or equivalent credential, or have a minimum of 5 years work experience in a related field, such as gaming, animation, film, TV, visual effects or programming, and have completed some post-secondary coursework.
- You are well organized and enjoy collaborating with others and working in teams.
If this sounds like you, apply today on the Entry Requirements page, where you will also learn more about entrance requirements for this program.

Technical Artists work in a broad range of positions related to video game production, animation and visual effects for TV and film, and interactive experiences across all digital platforms, in small boutique studios through to large multinational companies.

### Entrance requirements
This program has a two-step admission process. Applicants must meet all entrance requirements and will be accepted on a first-qualified basis after department review as long as space remains.
Step 1: Meet the following entrance requirements
- English language proficiency: Category 2 – English Studies 12 (67%) or equivalent
- Post-secondary education: diploma or degree (or equivalent post-secondary program of a minimum of 60 credits) in any discipline or alternate entry*
- Complete the following form for submission with your online application: Mandatory Applicant Questionnaire [PDF] This form will be used with other entrance requirements in the selection for this program.
- Submit a portfolio of your creative artwork with your online application: Submit your digital portfolio highlighting expertise in artistic or technical fields, featuring the applicant’s best work in 3D art, animation, visual effects, game design, or programming for web, mobile, game, and desktop applications. A portfolio or link to your portfolio must be submitted at the time you apply for the program. Your application will not be processed until all required documents are submitted, so ensure your portfolio is ready before you start your application.

- Complete the following form for submission with your online application: Self-Assessment [PDF]

## Courses
As a student in this program, you’ll have access to the most up-to-date, relevant instruction by working industry professionals, and expert consultation. The program incorporates a blend of hands-on lab time, industry-standard projects, collaborative learning opportunities, and individual assignments. All this is designed to give you the most current, job-related skills to help you successfully launch your career as a technical artist.
### Class hours
### Program matrix
Term 1 | Credits
TECA 5000 | Technical Art Foundations: Scripting This course will cover the fundamentals of computer programming and the logic behind it. Students will learn the essentials of programming and basic scripting for 3D applications, including variables, arrays, different types of loops, conditions and functions. The cousre also introduces students to Python, a standard scripting language used in a variety of technical art fields. Python is used in the production process for scripting complex computer graphic intensive films, animation and 3D tools. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 3.0
TECA 5100 | Technical Art Foundations: 3D Applications Understanding the 3D environment is fundamental for technical artists. This course will cover the basics of 3D applications, animation, materials, and scripting, as well as polygon modeling techniques for simple 3D objects. The course will introduce students to Maya ® , one of the most popular 3D applications used by the visual effects, animation, and game industries. Students will navigate different environments within the Maya ® application, becoming familiar with modeling menus, Hypershade, and render settings. Students will complete a series of 3D projects, creating basic objects, materials, character rigs, and scripts in a 3D environment.​ Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 3.0
TECA 5200 | Technical Arts 1 This course is the first of a three-course series for students to develop the core knowledge and skills of a technical artist in the visual effects, animation and game industries. In this first course in the series, students will be introduced to the role and activities of the technical artist in bridging the programming and artistic side of projects, such as streamlining assets and code or improving visual appeal. Students will learn the complete production pipeline, from the perspective of both the art and engineering side. Throughout this course, students will work under an instructor’s supervision on industry standard technical art projects and develop skills in rigging, technical applications, shader creation, physics, effects and particles. Prerequisite(s): TECA 5100 course outline | 3.0
TECA 5600 | Project Management for Technical Arts This course introduces students to project management tools and techniques in the context of technical arts, and prepares students for a series of team project courses in Terms 2 and 3. In this introductory project management course, students will learn the roles and responsibilities of technical artists and the unique characteristics of the production pipeline for visual effects, animation, and game projects. Students will learn fundamental project planning techniques such as agile or waterfall methdologies. We will discuss the common sources of difficulty in project management, such as communication issues, people management, and project control using check points and milestones. Students will also use project management software to practice their project management skills and become familiar with common software aids. ​ Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 3.0
TECA 6300 | Visual Scripting This course focuses on creating and developing procedures and functions inside industry standard 3D applications and game engines using visual scripting methodology. Students will learn the fundamentals of visual scripting in several 3D applications and game engines through the creation of materials, blueprints, effects, dynamics and event controls. Topics also include creating events and triggers (including collision-based events), developing loops and conditions to execute functions, and performing the required mathematical calculations. Prerequisite(s): TECA 5000 course outline | 3.0
Term 2 | Credits
TECA 5300 | Game Art Assets This course looks at different art assets used in the production and development of a video game. Students will be introduced to standard game engines used in game studios. While exploring the game engine and its different panels and environments, students will be taught the dos and don’ts of creating 3D game models, lighting, textures, and shaders. Students will create a small game level using 3D assets, audio, and textures; visual scripting; and lights, camera, environment effects, and dynamics. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 3.0
TECA 5500 | Scripting in 3D Applications In this course, students will build on their programming and scripting skills within industry standard 3D applications. Programming with Python, students will apply standard coding practices used by technical artists in developing expressions, user interfaces, tools, and functions. In addition, students will learn the essential elements of user-centric design and user experience. ​ Prerequisite(s): TECA 5100 course outline | 3.0
TECA 6100 | Technical Arts 2 Building on knowledge and skills developed in Technical Arts 1, this course will focus on creating 3D-friendly shaders and materials for different projects used in the visual effects, animation, and game industries. In this course, students will use industry standard applications to develop materials and visual effects. In addition, students will create and optimize textures to be imported into 3D applications and game engines ​ Prerequisite(s): TECA 5200 course outline | 3.0
TECA 6400 | Data Structures for Technical Arts In this course students develop object-oriented scripts with Python. The focus is on problem-solving in scripts, proper object-oriented programming (OOP) techniques, and clear coding style. The fundamental principles of OOP (data abstraction, encapsulation, inheritance and polymorphism) are covered with emphasis on inheritance and polymorphism. Students will examine data structures such as linked lists, queues, stacks, trees and common search and sort algorithms. Students will also explore scripting in more depth, creating interfaces, tools, exception handling and user interfaces in Python. Prerequisite(s): TECA 6300 course outline | 3.0
TECA 6700 | Technical Arts Team Project This course is the first of two courses in the team project sequence. The goal of the team project sequence is to prepare students for the deadline-oriented, results-driven, team-based environment that technical artists work in. Students will have the opportunity to use project management skills developed in Term 1. Students will be placed in teams to pitch, prototype, and deliver a technical art project, such as developing and optimizing tools, dynamic assets, procedural shaders and/or textures. Students will experiment with scripting, user interfaces and modeling techniques, and will apply technical and artistic skills specific to the project they are completing. Each week teams will discuss their progress with their instructor and receive feedback through team meetings and online sessions. The instructor will sign off on each milestone as the teams present their work. Prerequisite(s): TECA 5600 course outline | 3.0
Term 3 | Credits
TECA 5400 | Effects and Dynamics This course introduces the concept of dynamics in 3D applications. Students will learn the fundamentals of physics and the essentials of several dynamic elements, such as rigid bodies, cloth, hair, particles, and pyro. Students will create different dynamic objects and experiment with various environmental elements and their effect on objects. An important component of this course is completion of various projects related to dynamic elements and environment, which contribute to students’ individual portfolios. ​ Prerequisite(s): TECA 5300 course outline | 3.0
TECA 6200 | Technical Arts 3 This course builds on Technical Arts 2, where students developed their skills in designing shaders, procedural materials, and textures. In Technical Arts 3, students focus on advanced technical procedures and camera setup to light and render scenes in 3D applications and game engines. They will create expressions and develop scripts for dynamic lighting, camera movement, and effects. Finally, students will learn advanced rendering techniques: managing and prioritizing data, troubleshooting and optimizing materials, and ensuring the smooth operation of rendering processes. ​ Prerequisite(s): TECA 6100 course outline | 3.0
TECA 6500 | Advanced Scripting This course focuses on advanced object-oriented programming techniques in Python. It builds on the skills developed with scripting and data structures. Students will create and develop scripts and expressions that control node attributes within 3D applications and that connect nodes to influence one another. In addition, students will create and implement pre- and post-render scripting options to automate time-consuming processes. Students will also develop tools that provide flexibility and ease of modeling, texturing, shading, rigging, animation, and rendering processes throughout different stages of the production pipeline. ​ Prerequisite(s): TECA 5400 course outline | 3.0
TECA 6600 | Team Skills for Technical Arts Effective teamwork skills are essential to success as a technical artist, as a core member of the production team, or as the team lead. Developing a strong team and improving team performance leads to better project outcomes. Students will participate in the various stages of team development and learn decision-making and conflict resolution techniques, as well as how to solve problems and collaborate effectively with others. ​ Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 3.0
TECA 6800 | Technical Arts Capstone Project In this second of two courses in the team project sequence, students will have further opportunity to experience the types of projects that reflect a technical artist’s day-to-day work environment. They will propose ideas for projects related to visual effects, animation, or games, which should be tools related to dynamics, user interfaces, animation, and/or modeling. Students will form interdisciplinary teams based on their skill sets and interests, and execute collaborative projects that turn their ideas into reality. Teams will plan, track, and troubleshoot each stage of the collaborative project. In this final team project course, students are expected to show advanced technical art and scripting skills. Each week teams will discuss their progress with their instructor and receive feedback through team meetings and online sessions. The instructor will sign off on each milestone as the teams present their work. At the end of the term, students’ must present their finished project to the instructor(s) and an industry panel. Prerequisite(s): TECA 6700 course outline | 3.0
Total Credits: | 45.0

## Program Details
The Advanced Diploma in Technical Arts program was developed with the support and collaboration of Electronic Arts, Sony Pictures Imageworks, and The Coalition. This program is an Unreal Engine Academic Partner .
### Program goals
Upon successful completion of the Advanced Diploma in Technical Arts, you will be able to:
Design and creation
- Design and develop tools for 3D modeling, texturing, and animation.
- Create procedural shaders for 3D applications and game engines.
- Create animator-friendly rigs for 3D models.
- Perform visual scripting in game engines to automate and enhance the asset pipeline.
- Produce an industry standard project (animation, tools, rigs, and game).
- Apply a broad range of software techniques and methodologies used by studios.
Project management
- Plan, design, troubleshoot and carry out a technical art project, from start to finish, with well-defined objectives.
- Track project deliverables and ensure that milestones are met.
- Streamline processes at different stages of art production.
- Communicate effectively with all stakeholders and departments to clarify and advise on goals and actions pertaining to visual effects, animation or game projects.
Communication and teamwork
- Demonstrate effective teamwork and problem-solving skills.
- Demonstrate a professional work ethic.
- Use effective communication strategies for presenting technical arts project proposals related to, for example, development of tools, user interfaces, and assets.
