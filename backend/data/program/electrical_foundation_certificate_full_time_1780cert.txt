## Electrical Foundation
Certificate Full-time School of Construction and the Environment
## Overview
The Electrical Foundation program provides graduates with the theory and practical skills necessary to enter the electrical trade. The electrical worker plans, assembles, installs, tests, repairs and maintains electrical equipment and systems in commercial, industrial and marine facilities.
Delivery: in person. See details .
### The Program
The program emphasizes a hands-on approach to training, where experience gained in the workshop is focused on industry practice. The necessary theoretical component is integrated into the program to complement and enhance the practical work. Industry tours will expose the student to a variety of work environments.

### Program matrix
Term 1 | Credits
TELX 1151 | Applied Mechanics In this course, you will solve problems involving simple machines, mechanical advantage, and efficiency through applying the concept of Work, Power, Torque, and Energy. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 4.0
TELX 1152 | Fundamentals of Electricity ​In this course, you will apply principles that govern the behavior of electrical circuits and prove theories through properly using test equipment. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 8.0
TELX 1154 | Principles of Magnetism ​In this course, you will learn about the concepts of magnetism and electromagnetism, and how they relate to generating electricity. You will solve problems using the principles of magnetism. You will also build an electromagnet. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 4.0
TELX 1156 | Wiring Methods ​In this hands-on course, you will learn how to safely use both the hand and power tools that electricians need. You will gain these skills through performing basic electrical installations in a shop/jobsite environment. As well, you will learn about and follow the safety regulations at all times. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 12.0
TELX 1158 | Electrical Code, Plans and Specifications ​In this course, you will be apply appropriate rules of the Canadian Electrical Code (CEC) toward residential wiring installations. Also, you will interpret and create electrical diagrams. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 8.0
Term 2 | Credits
TELX 2131 | AC Circuit Analysis ​In this course, you will apply trigonometric functions towards vector addition and sine wave calculations. You will also use an oscilloscope to measure voltage, frequency, and relationships involved in alternating current circuits. Prerequisite(s): TELX 1154 course outline | 2.0
TELX 2135 | Electronic Devices ​In this course, you will learn about the "magic" of semiconductor materials, diodes, LEDs, and transistors, find out how Silicon Valley utilized these semiconductor materials to bring us into the computer age, and solve problems involving diodes and transistors. To help you do this, you will analyze electronic components and circuits. Prerequisite(s): TELX 2131 course outline | 2.0
TELX 2137 | Basic Motor Control ​In this course, you will explore the function and operation of common alternating current motor starters. We will concentrate on manual and magnetic starters. Prerequisite(s): TELX 1156 course outline | 4.0
TELX 2140 | Computer Skills and Job Preparation ​In this course, you will learn how and where to research electrical contractors as potential employers. You will also write a resume and apply for some jobs. You may also practice interview skills. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 4.0

## Graduating & Jobs
### Job Opportunities
Graduates may qualify for advanced standing as indentured candidates within the apprenticeship system.
Job opportunities exist in the construction, mining, forestry, food production, manufacturing, grain handling, transportation, utility and service industries. The nature of the electrical trade has changed with the impact of solid-state control and programmable logic controllers. Challenging career opportunities are available to individuals who are prepared to acquire a broad range of technical skills.
### Graduate employment outcomes
The BCIT student outcomes report presents summary findings from the annual survey of former students administered by BC Stats one to two years after graduation. These reports combine the last three years of available results for the 2023-2025 BCIT Outcomes Surveys of 2022-2024 graduates and for Degree 2020-2022 graduates. The reports are organized into three-page summaries containing information on graduates’ labour market experiences and opinions regarding their education. More detailed information can be accessed at the BC Student Outcomes website.
To view these results, you may need to have the Adobe Acrobat Reader installed in your Web browser.
