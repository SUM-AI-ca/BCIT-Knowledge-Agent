Title: Bachelor of Creative Industries

================================================================================

## Overview

Overview text

The Bachelor of Creative Industries (BCI) program’s aim is to prepare students with knowledge and applied skills in three core areas: creativity, technology, and business. The program will take the deep technical and discipline-specific skills students developed during their diploma programs and layer on critical thinking, strategic management, creativity, innovation, sustainability, inclusivity, business incubation, law and ethics in creative industries and digital communication, and emerging technical trends in multimedia. Students from different diploma programs and diverse backgrounds will learn and work together to engage in the challenges and realities of an interdisciplinary role within the creative industries sector. BCI is for applicants who have completed diplomas from any discipline who can demonstrate that their career goals are aligned with the focus of the program and who believe that completing their studies in the program will complement their area of discipline by providing breadth and depth of knowledge in the business of creativity. Graduates emerge from this program with a passion for continuous learning and improvement, driven to make an impact. Career outcomes include key positions in creative organizations such as Director, Producer, Studio Head, Creative/Artistic Director, and opportunities that require high-level strategic thinking that affects and transforms the business as a whole. Equipped to enhance their local creative communities and beyond, students are encouraged to embark on careers that champion originality and innovation.

### Term 1 (15 weeks)

CREA 7000 - The Business of Creativity: Creative Industries Overview

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/crea7000/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This foundation course defines the subject of creative industries as an area of entrepreneurial study and introduces students to the commercial and cultural significance of the creative industries and how they work in practice. The course also examines the various creative disciplines, operational frameworks, creative clusters, technologies relevant to those clusters, and the outlook for creative industries in the context of the emerging circular economy and innovation sustainability practices. Students will develop an understanding of the business of creativity by exploring the dynamic environments within which creative industries operate and the impact they have on society. Students will explore the advantages and challenges creative organizations face in this industry by reviewing a specific creative operation or a creative cluster of their choice. Students will meet periodically to compare readings and notes that contextualize the creative industries and their own projects. The research synergies discovered will give students the opportunity to develop their knowledge and understanding of integration, practices, and real-world applications of the creative industries to begin to map out their own path into a creative industry sector and develop strategies to commence their portfolio and capstone projects in this program.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

CREA 7010 - Project Management in Creative Industries

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/crea7010/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This course introduces students to project management in the creative industries. It enables students to gain a comprehensive understanding of the various aspects of project management in a creative industries context, such as initiation, planning, budgeting, financing, implementation/execution, cost monitoring/controlling, and closing, as well as scheduling, purchasing, contracting, and reporting. Students will learn how to manage a creative project effectively through all phases, develop a project strategy, and report results to inform investors and stakeholders.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

CREA 7020 - Entrepreneurship and Innovation in Creative Industries

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/crea7020/

Credits: 3.0

Description: The digitization of our world has fundamentally changed the ways that businesses are creating value and connecting with consumers—particularly within the creative industries. This course will introduce students to the principles and practices of entrepreneurship within creative industries including the preparation of a business plan. Students in this course will study concepts and techniques, including individual creativity, innovation in the workplace, design thinking, entrepreneurship, leadership, problem identification, implementation of ideas and other practical methods that they can apply to their studies, work, and personal lives. Taking on the role of an entrepreneur with a new creative business concept, students will learn how to build the idea into a viable business venture. As part of the course, students are introduced to creative ways to solve problems in addition to traditional decision-making methods and formal critical-thinking models.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

CREA 7030 - Intercultural and Interdisciplinary Communication in Creative Industries

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/crea7030/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This course is an introduction to the dynamic forces that enhance effective communication between people from various cultural backgrounds. Students will learn how to facilitate intercultural and interdisciplinary communication effectively and mindfully in the creative industries workplace. Through the application of specific interpersonal techniques and theoretical perspectives, communication in the creative industries will be examined through the lenses of different cultural value systems. Power imbalances and their impact on intercultural communications will also be examined. Students will develop a critical perspective and analyze how to adjust and apply strategies and techniques for communicating more effectively regardless of cultural differences and perceived barriers. By helping students develop a truly global perspective, this course encourages intercultural communication competency (ICC). Due to rapid globalization, ICC is not an option, but a necessity.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

CREA 4035 - Speculative Design

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/crea4035/

Credits: 3.0

Description: Design Thinking is a powerful framework that drives creative industries to solve complex problems and push the boundaries of conventional approaches. It empowers individuals to tackle seemingly unsolvable challenges with creativity, curiosity, and strategic vision. In this course, students will dive deep into "blue sky" thinking, where unbounded ideas and radical experimentation are encouraged. Students will approach wicked hard global issues with an abstract, unconventional mindset. Through hands-on projects and collaborative exercises, students will gain the skills to bring ambitious ideas down to earth, creating real-world solutions that are actionable and impactful.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

### Term 2 (15 weeks)

CREA 7100 - Ethics and Professional Practice in Creative Industries

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/crea7100/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This course will explore ethical, legal, and professional issues in relation to practice in creative industries. Students will discuss ethical dilemmas in the creative industries, the impact of government and society on these dilemmas and the legal implications of ethical decisions. Through case studies relevant to creative industries and professional practice, students will be introduced to key ethical and legal frameworks and subsequently apply this knowledge to a variety of scenarios relevant to their own practice. Students will analyze a specific business model for ethical implications, establishing a code of ethics and strategies to achieve them.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

CREA 7110 - Leadership and Team Management in Creative Industries

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/crea7110/

Credits: 3.0

Description: The course focuses on the role of team managers and leaders in creative organizations. Comprehensive development of personal leadership potential is provided through guided reflection, networking, executive coaching, interaction with peers, learning from seminars, guest speakers, academic literature, and case studies, all based on current leadership theories. You will examine the role of leadership and organizational culture in developing, nurturing, and managing effective teams in a creative industry setting. Among the topics addressed are theories of leadership, values, ethics, vision, conviction, and courage; leadership principles and styles; building relationships with stakeholders and shareholders; team building, group dynamics, and creative collaboration; delegation; conflict resolution; stress and change management; communication; character and power; and leadership techniques, tools, and strategies.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

CREA 7120 - Funding and Financial Mgt

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/crea7120/

Credits: 3.0

Description: Strong financial management is critical to the success of all enterprises, including those in creative industries. Regardless of whether you are preparing a business plan to launch a new venture or preparing a budget in a well-established firm, you will need a solid foundation in practices and principles of financial management. This course will provide an overview of core financial concepts such as forecasting, budgeting, financing, cash flow management and understanding financial statements which contribute to the sustainability and the growth of the creative enterprise.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

CREA 8000 - Creative Industries Portfolio Development

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/crea8000/

Credits: 3.0

Description: In this course, students will explore emerging trends in the creative industries and receive individual guidance and support on how to plan, strategize, execute, and promote a digital portfolio to start a business or to find employment. Students will choose one or more specializations in a creative cluster and assess their interests, attitudes and values in relation to these corporate cultures, identifying the best approaches to target their portfolio to sponsors, clients, and prospective employers. Targeted portfolios can be used for a variety of purposes and be classified into different categories, giving the students the option to choose between corporate training, teaching curricula, process/product development, and industry showcase. Students will have the opportunity to create, choose, curate, and integrate individual and collaborative projects in the form of digital artifacts. At the end of the course, students will participate in a creative industries networking event, where they’ll get feedback from faculty and industry. The experience will enable students to gain valuable connections within the creative industries and refine their portfolios as they prepare for their major capstone project and potential internship opportunities.

Prerequisites: All Term 1 courses are required to be successfully completed prior.

CREA 4045 - Frontiers of Technology in Innovation

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/crea4045/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This course explores the cutting-edge technologies and emerging trends that redefine the creative industries. With a focus on the future of work and society, students will gain insights into transformational innovations across rapidly evolving frontiers, equipping them with the foresight to lead in a tech-driven world. Rather than delving into specific tools or current trends, this course encourages design thinking, critical thinking, adaptability, and a deep curiosity about what’s next

Prerequisites: Student must successfully complete all courses in Term before starting this course.

CREA 8100 - Growing the Creative Economy and Global Marketplace

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/crea8100/

Credits: 3.0

Description: The course prepares students for interactions in the rapidly growing fields of interactive and traditional media industries by identifying, analyzing, and implementing the trends, concepts, and technical knowledge needed to thrive. By studying how media technologies develop, operate, converge/diverge, and impact society in our community and in the world, this course equips students with the tools to think critically about media, sustainability innovation and its effects on culture and the future of the creative marketplace. Aside from tools and best practices, students examine the challenges and opportunities of designing projects that take advantage of new and emerging technologies to increase the creative economy's potential.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

CREA 8200 - Creative Industries Capstone

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/crea8200/

Credits: 6.0

Description: This capstone course provides students with the opportunity to integrate and synthesize their learning from previous terms. Working in groups or individually, students will create a business plan for their own creative business, which can be initiated during the course or executed directly after graduation. Students will analyze their own business model, management system, strategic direction, and strengths and weaknesses in relation to the creative industry cluster in which they plan to operate. A student's project may include industry initiatives in collaboration with a local or international organization. As part of this capstone project, the students may specialize in a single discipline or bridge between technical specializations to develop original products and/or services.

Prerequisites: All courses in Terms 2 & 3 must be successfully completed prior to starting this course.

CREA 4055 - Creativity in a World of Emerging Intelligence

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/crea4055/

Credits: 3.0

Description: In an era where artificial intelligence is increasingly capable, can machines truly take over human roles, or is there a new frontier of partnership awaiting us? This course invites students to explore the evolving relationship between human creativity and machine intelligence, experimenting with real-world applications for industry. Students will design digital products without traditional coding skills, using a variety of tools to direct AI toward practical, innovative results. Through hands-on projects, students will gain insights into how machine learning operates, critically evaluating AI's potential impact on industry: Does AI serve to complement human ingenuity, or might it eventually replace it?

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

CREA 4060 - Creative Economics & Market Dynamics

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/crea4060/

Credits: 3.0

Description: How do creative products and services create, capture, and scale value in rapidly evolving markets? This course examines the unique economic principles governing creative industries, from traditional media to emerging digital platforms. Students will explore how creative works are valued, monetized, and distributed in global markets, while analyzing innovative business models that are reshaping creative commerce. Through market analysis and practical explorations, students will learn to identify opportunities, assess market viability, and develop strategies for launching and scaling creative ventures in an increasingly complex digital economy.

Prerequisites: Successful completion of Terms 1 & 2

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/indg1100/

Credits: 3.0

Description: This course will examine the identity of Indigenous people and their strong connection to the land and natural resources. Over their history after first contact, Indigenous societies have endured significant changes due to the legacy of colonization. Students will explore a variety of issues concerning the relation of Indigenous peoples with government, the judicial system, the private sector, and media which represent their identity to mainstream Canada. One of the guiding aims of the course is to provide a historical basis for better understanding new opportunities and challenges among Indigenous communities and individuals arising from involvement in areas of Canadian society in which they have previously played a minor role.

## Program Details

introduction

The Bachelor of Creative Industries is an intensive one-year multidisciplinary program crafted to cultivate adaptive, forward-thinking leaders in the creative economy. Designed for those ready to tackle complex challenges, this program equips students with the skills and vision to drive meaningful change. Industry experts guide students through advanced research and production tactics, demystifying current industry processes and helping them forecast future trends across creative sectors. With a balanced approach to “blue-sky thinking” and practical problem-solving, students build on their existing skills, gaining hands-on experience with research methodologies, strategic thinking, contemporary workflows, and production techniques. A core component of the program is the capstone project, developed in collaboration with an industry partner. Students will identify a real-world problem, propose actionable solutions, and bring their ideas to life, gaining invaluable experience in leadership within the modern economy.

Program length

24 months, full-time*. The program requires a total of 60.0 credits. You will complete 45.0 credits of core courses along with scheduled electives across three terms. Additionally, you will be responsible for taking 9.0 credits from courses outside the core discipline and 6.0 credits from written and oral communication courses. These additional credits can be taken during the three terms or after, depending on course availability. The maximum time to complete this program is five years. *May be completed in an accelerated format. The program offers a competitive entry, work-integrated learning (WIL) opportunity that can be undertaken once all required 60.0 credits for the credential have been successfully completed. This WIL component involves 300 hours of internship work with a creative industry organization locally or abroad. Interested students will apply for acceptance into the internship component, and this experience will be competitive entry and coordinated by the BCIT Digital Arts, Media and Design department in collaboration with the Centre for Workplace Education.

Apply for graduation

Upon successful completion of all program requirements, complete an Application for BCIT Credential [PDF] and submit it to Student Information and Enrolment Services. Allow approximately six to eight weeks for processing. All financial obligations to the Institute must be met prior to issuance of any credential.
