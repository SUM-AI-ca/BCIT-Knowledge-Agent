## Auto Body Repair and Refinishing Technician Foundation
Certificate Full-time School of Transportation
## Overview
Delivery: blended. See details .
The Auto Body Repair and Refinishing program is a 40-week offering, which includes a 6-week work practicum, that will open the doors to a career in the auto body industry. The program allows students to gain the fundamental knowledge required to launch a career as a Red Seal Auto Body Collision Repair or Automotive Refinishing Technician, also known as an Automotive Painter. You will then be ready to start your journey as a registered Apprentice, which will prepare you to return to school for level 2 of the traditional apprenticeship pathway.
Graduates will receive an Auto Body Repair and Refinishing Certificate of Qualification and the following credits:
- Level 1 Technical Training credit plus 625 hours towards the Harmonized Auto Body and Collision Technician apprenticeship program.
- Level 1 Technical Training credit plus 450 hours towards Harmonized Automotive Refinishing Technician apprenticeship program.
Technical Training credit will be applied once you become a registered apprentice with SkilledTradesBC.
You’ll spend approximately 50% of your on-campus schedule gaining hands-on practice in a spacious and well laid out state-of-the-art auto body shop outfitted with the latest equipment. Classroom and shop instruction will be facilitated by faculty with years of industry experience, who are committed to helping you succeed and maximize your skills. Faculty will also assist students with practicum placements at various collision repair facilities throughout the lower mainland.
Topics covered will include:
- Occupational Skills and Safety
- Tools and Equipment
- Sheet Metal Repair
- Welding
- Plastics and Composites
- Surface Preparation
- Auto Body Construction and Components

### Program matrix
Required Courses: | Credits
ABRR 1500 | Perform Safety Related Functions ​This course introduces students to WorkSafeBC's Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) regulations and the Workplace Hazardous Materials Information System (WHMIS). Students will learn how the regulations apply to their workplace environment. This course also covers the proper use of personal protection equipment, general shop safety procedures, and fire safety procedures. Prerequisite(s): Entry to the program. course outline | 4.0
ABRR 1505 | Use Tools and Equipment ​This course introduces students to the safe use, handling, and care of various tools and equipment used in the collision repair and refinishing industry. The safe and acceptable work habits learned in theory will be applied in the shop environment. Prerequisite(s): ABRR 1500 course outline | 5.5
ABRR 1510 | Organize work and use documentation ​This course introduces students to understand the documentation and procedures used to ensure the repair of vehicles is carried out correctly and efficiently. The student will be introduced to the trade terminology and vehicle information required to conduct repairs as per customer or insurance company requirements. Students will also learn how to perform visual inspections on coatings and repairs performed to the vehicle. Prerequisite(s): ABRR 1500 course outline | 4.0
ABRR 1515 | Use Communication and Mentoring Techniques ​This course introduces students how to effectively communicate in the work environment with the employer and fellow employees. The student will also understand the roles and responsibilities of workers in the Auto Body trade and be able describe business and workplace relations. The student will also prepare a resume for employability readiness. Prerequisite(s): ABRR 1500 course outline | 2.0
ABRR 1520 | Use Welding Equipment ​Upon completion of this course, students will be able to safely use oxyacetylene welding and cutting equipment for cutting and heating. Students will also be able to demonstrate the use of GMAW welding equipment and Plasma arc cutting equipment. Prerequisite(s): ABRR 1500 course outline | 8.0
ABRR 1525 | Remove and Install Vehicle Components ​This course introduces students to procedures required to effectively identify and remove or replace various components to the vehicles interior and exterior during the repair and refinishing process. Furthermore, the student will learn techniques used to remove and re-apply decals and stripes to the exterior of the vehicle body. Prerequisite(s): ABRR 1500 course outline | 8.0
ABRR 1530 | Practicum 1 ​This work practicum gives students the opportunity to apply their knowledge at a collision repair facility. The related work experience reinforces material presented in the program and enhances their skills and abilities through interaction with experienced tradespeople. Prerequisite(s): ABRR 1500 course outline | 2.5
ABRR 1535 | Use Repair Materials and Equipment ​This course introduces the student to follow the correct steps to perform a repair to a damaged exterior body panel. Students will learn how to mix and apply body fillers and primer undercoats to selected panels and prepare panels for the application of undercoats using spraying equipment in a spray booth. Prerequisite(s): ABRR 1500 course outline | 3.5
ABRR 1540 | Remove, Repair and Install Metal Panels and Components ​This course introduces students to various categories of vehicle construction and the different methods required to repair each type of vehicle. The course emphasizes an understanding of the materials used in construction, and how to repair, replace and re- align such components. Prerequisite(s): ABRR 1500 course outline | 10.0
ABRR 1545 | Practicum 2 ​This second two-week work practicum gives students the opportunity to apply their knowledge at a collision repair facility. The related work experience reinforces material presented in the program and enhances their skills and abilities through interaction with experienced tradespeople. Prerequisite(s): ABRR 1500 course outline | 2.5
ABRR 1550 | Remove, Repair and Install Plastic and Composite Panels and Components ​This course introduces students to various categories of plastics and composite materials found on vehicles with the emphasis to enable the student to understand and perform repairs, replacement and re-alignment of each type of material. Students will perform a variety of repairs to plastics panels using current repair standards and equipment. Prerequisite(s): ABRR 1500 course outline | 5.0
ABRR 1555 | Prepare Surface ​This course introduces students to methods used to prepare vehicle surfaces prior to the application of paint coatings. The learner will have the opportunity to become familiar with the steps involved to clean, prepare and sand the various substrates found on vehicles such as steel, aluminum and composites and plastics. Additionally, the learner will also demonstrate masking procedures prior to the application of paint coatings. Prerequisite(s): ABRR 1500 course outline | 12.0
ABRR 1560 | Apply Refinishing Materials ​This course introduces students to the cleaning and preparation of panels and vehicle parts prior to applying various types of primers and topcoats on vehicles. Students will learn about different topcoat systems and how to apply them to a prepared substrate. Prerequisite(s): ABRR 1500 course outline | 3.5
ABRR 1565 | Practicum 3 ​This third, two-week work practicum gives students the opportunity to apply their knowledge at a collision repair facility. The related work experience reinforces material presented in the program and enhances their skills and abilities through interaction with experienced tradespeople. Prerequisite(s): ABRR 1500 course outline | 2.5
ABRR 1570 | Detail Exterior Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 3.5
Total Credits: | 76.5
