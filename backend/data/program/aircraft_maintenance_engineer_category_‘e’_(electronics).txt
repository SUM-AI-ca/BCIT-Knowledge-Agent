Title: Aircraft Maintenance Engineer Category ‘E’ (Electronics)

================================================================================

## Overview

Delivery

in person

Overview text

Category E Aircraft Maintenance Engineers (AME-E) are responsible for the certification of an aircraft after maintenance, inspection, repair or modification is completed on any of its electrical or electronic systems. This is an occupation requiring a high degree of responsibility and skill. From modern aircraft with sophisticated fly by wire flight controls, autopilot, navigation, communication, in-flight entertainment, and monitoring systems, to legacy aircraft undergoing the installation of modern avionics. the Category E Aircraft Maintenance Engineer ensures that all systems are serviceable and fit for flight. An Aircraft Maintenance Engineer Category E’s tasks include: The Aircraft Maintenance Engineer Category E (Electronics) program consists of 68 weeks of full-time studies. Approximately 50 per cent of the day is spent on theory in a classroom setting, followed by hands-on practical training in the shops and hangar located at BCIT’s state of the art Aerospace Technology Campus. The program is designed to follow national standards and is approved by Transport Canada (TC). Successful completion of the program will result in a work experience credit of 18 months of the 48 months required by Transport Canada toward obtaining an Aircraft Maintenance Engineer Category E license (AME-E). ## Courses

Class hours

0800–1530, Monday through Friday

### Term 1 (17 weeks)

AVAV 1105 - Standard Aviation Practices

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/avav1105/

Credits: 8.0

Description: This course is designed to introduce students to the basic knowledge and hand skills required to accomplish avionics maintenance procedures in an aerospace environment. There is an emphasis on continuing safety during work practice that includes a lesson on Workplace Hazardous Material Information Systems (WHMIS). Students will learn practices associated with the use of hand tools, power tools, and precision measuring instruments. This course also introduces the student to the Canadian Aviation Regulations and the restrictions imposed when using hardware, metal, and parts in an aviation environment.

Prerequisites: No prerequisites are required for this course.

AVAV 1106 - Structures & Theory of Flight

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/avav1106/

Credits: 8.0

Description: This course is designed to introduce students to the theory of flight, including characteristics of the atmosphere and the physics associated with flight. Students will learn about flight controls for both rotary and fixed wing aircraft and will be able to apply that knowledge during practical workshop sessions.

Prerequisites: AVAV 1105

AVAV 1107 - Direct Current Theory

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/avav1107/

Credits: 10.0

Description: This course is designed to introduce students to the basic knowledge for direct current (DC) theory. The course provides a platform for subsequent lessons in avionics and includes the theories associated with basic DC electricity. Students will learn common terminology associated with DC power and its measurement as well as some of the laws governing DC power. There are lessons on magnetism, electromagnetism, DC circuit components and the different types of DC circuits used in aircraft. This course also will review mathematics and physics topics such as: logarithms, complex numbers, trigonometry, and graphing.

Prerequisites: AVAV 1106

AVAV 1108 - Alternating Current Theory

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/avav1108/

Credits: 8.0

Description: This course is designed to introduce students to the basic theory of alternating current (AC). The course includes lessons on the fundamentals of AC current and describes the types of AC circuits that are encountered in aircraft maintenance. Following lessons on the types of AC circuits, students learn about electrical filters and the principles associated with transformers.

Prerequisites: AVAV 1107

### Term 2 (18 weeks)

AVAV 2105 - Analog Theory

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/avav2105/

Credits: 6.0

Description: This course is designed to introduce students to the basic theory and use of semiconductor components. It is also designed to allow for build-up and analysis of circuits combining resistors, transformers, diodes, capacitors and transistors.

Prerequisites: AVAV 1108

AVAV 2106 - Digital Theory

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/avav2106/

Credits: 6.0

Description: This course provides the theoretical and practical knowledge necessary for the student to install, maintain, and troubleshoot circuits that contain digital logic devices. The digital logic devices covered include basic logic gates, logic functions, flip-flops, counters, shift registers, memories, and interfacing ICs. Practical circuits that employ these devices are also studied. There will also be discussions of general microcomputer architecture and operation.

Prerequisites: AVAV 2105

AVAV 2107 - Propulsion Systems

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/avav2107/

Credits: 6.0

Description: This course is designed to introduce students to the types of propulsion systems used in aircraft. Piston engines, turbine engines, and propellers are included as major learning outcomes in this course. Indication systems specific to each type of engine are also described and practical exercises ensure that students are able to apply theory into practice.

Prerequisites: AVAV 2106

AVAV 2108 - Aircraft Systems

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/avav2108/

Credits: 8.0

Description: This course is designed to introduce students to the various systems used in aircraft. Environmental control, hydraulic, fuel, landing gear, ice and rain protection, pneumatic and lighting systems are included, with an emphasis on their relationship to the work performed by an Avionics Technician. The course includes an application section where knowledge lessons are used to perform practical assignments and projects.

Prerequisites: AVAV 2107

AVAV 2109 - Electrical Power & Distribution

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/avav2109/

Credits: 10.0

Description: This course is designed to introduce students to the knowledge and skills associated with aircraft electrical standards and the design and maintenance of DC and AC power generation systems. The course includes practical exercises and troubleshooting of electrical power systems. Students will learn and practice skills related to power generation and distribution of AC, DC, and emergency power systems on aircraft.

Prerequisites: AVAV 2108

### Term 3 (17 weeks)

AVAV 3105 - Flight Instruments

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/avav3105/

Credits: 6.0

Description: This course is designed to introduce students to aircraft navigation and avionics system monitoring instruments. It is also designed to introduce students to the use of basic flight instruments including the ones used in air data systems, compass systems, and attitude information and control systems.

Prerequisites: AVAV 2109

AVAV 3106 - Radio Theory

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/avav3106/

Credits: 8.0

Description: This course is designed to introduce students to the basic knowledge and skills required for a basic understanding of radio theory. The course includes lessons on amplitude modulation (AM), frequency modulation (FM) and high frequency radio communications (HF). Students will learn about the principles of radio transmission and receiving, including a lesson on transmission lines and antennas. Practical exercises include the installation of an RF connector.

Prerequisites: AVAV 3105

AVAV 3107 - Aircraft Communications

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/avav3107/

Credits: 10.0

Description: This course is designed to introduce students to the basic knowledge and skills required to perform the testing and troubleshooting of aircraft communication systems. Students will learn about the types and standards used in aircraft communications, including VHF-AM, VHF-FM, and HF. This course also describes ancillary communication systems such as ACARS, cockpit voice/data recorders, and cabin entertainment systems. Students will perform functional testing of communication systems and will qualify for issuance of a Radio Operator’s Certificate.

Prerequisites: AVAV 3106

AVAV 3108 - Navigation System 1

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/avav3108/

Credits: 10.0

Description: This course is designed to introduce students to the basic knowledge associated with aircraft navigation systems. It includes lessons on the concepts of navigation for both short and long range navigation. There are also lessons to introduce the student to the principles of instrument landing systems and flight management.

Prerequisites: AVAV 3107

### Term 4 (16 weeks)

AVAV 4105 - Navigation Systems 2

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/avav4105/

Credits: 8.0

Description: This course is designed to introduce students to the basic knowledge associated with enhanced aircraft navigation systems. It includes lessons on the concepts of navigation for air traffic control systems, distance measuring equipment, weather radar, TCAS and TAWS. Where appropriate, lessons will include the maintenance and testing of these avionics systems.

Prerequisites: AVAV 3108

AVAV 4106 - Flight Line Maintenance

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/avav4106/

Credits: 6.0

Description: This course is designed to describe and provide practice for general avionics maintenance procedures. The course includes maintenance, repair, and troubleshooting of communication and navigation systems. It also includes lessons for performing tasks using Built-in-Test Equipment (BITE) and in the interpretation of avionics schematics.

Prerequisites: AVAV 4105

AVAV 4107 - Automatic Flight Control Systems

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/avav4107/

Credits: 4.0

Description: This course is designed to introduce students to the automatic flight control systems (AFCS) used in aviation. The course starts with a basic description of mechanical flight control systems and then describes how automatic flight control is integrated. The basic principles of auto-flight control are identified, including control of the pitch, roll, and yaw axis of aircraft. On completion of the course, students will be able to perform an operational check of a typical AFCS system.

Prerequisites: AVAV 4106

AVAV 4108 - Installation and CARs

Outline URL: https://www.bcit.ca/outlines/avav4108/

Credits: 14.0

Description: This course is designed to build on knowledge and practice from previous courses and will focus on the installation and testing of avionics components and systems. This course also includes a lesson on the Canadian Aviation Regulations (CARS) and provides a good overview of the regulations and standards that govern the avionics technician’s work.

Prerequisites: AVAV 4107

## Program Details

Program length

68 weeks, full-time

Grading

The minimum course passing grade is 70% for Transport Canada. Each course has a theory component and a practical component, and both components must be passed with 70%. The official transcript will show an average of both marks, with a U or F (unsatisfactory or failed) if either component is not passed. If you fail a course during a term, you may re-register to repeat the course. However, if your second attempt is unsuccessful, you will be prohibited from continuing the term and must seek approval from the Associate Dean in order to be readmitted into the program.

Aviation attendance policy

Attendance policy for all Aviation programs differs from other BCIT programs and is regulated by Transport Canada. Canadian Aviation Regulation Standard 566 states: "Students missing more than five per cent of the course curriculum through absence will not qualify for any credit under Transport Canada course approval, unless the lost time is made up through documented supplementary studies, which shall include theory, workshop and laboratory time, equivalent to that missed from the original program. The five per cent absence policy is intended for illness, bereavement, or other circumstances beyond the individual's control."



## Graduating & Jobs

introduction

Graduates of this program may continue their studies to complete the BCIT Bachelor of Technology (BTech) in Technology Management . The BTech program provides you with the flexibility to start your new career and complete your degree part-time within 3 to 5 years. Opportunities are available across Canada in aircraft manufacturing, maintenance, repair and overhaul establishments and by airlines and other aircraft operators. The demand for skilled workers will continue as an aging workforce retires and as the industry grows. Graduates from the Aircraft Maintenance Engineer Category E (Electronics) program have found employment in both the national and international aviation industry. Some graduates have gone on to become managers and owners of domestic and international aircraft maintenance establishments. Overall, Aircraft Maintenance Engineers Category E (Electronics) with work experience, are expected to have excellent job opportunities.

Graduate employment outcomes

The BCIT student outcomes report presents summary findings from the annual survey of former students administered by BC Stats one to two years after graduation. These reports combine the last three years of available results for the 2022-2024 BCIT Outcomes Surveys of 2021-2023 graduates and for Degree 2020-2022 graduates. The reports are organized into three-page summaries containing information on graduates’ labour market experiences and opinions regarding their education.
