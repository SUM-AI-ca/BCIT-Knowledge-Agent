## Architectural and Structural CADD and Graphics Technician (Structural Option)
Certificate Full-time School of Construction and the Environment
## Overview
Delivery: in person. See details .
The Architectural and Structural CADD and Graphics Technician program offers two distinct areas of specialization:
- Architectural CADD and Graphics Technician
- Structural CADD and Graphics Technician
Applicants must indicate their area of specialization when applying.
Architectural and Structural CADD and Graphics Technician programs at BCIT offer training for a variety of applications in architecture, building construction, and industrial applications. CADD (Computer-Aided Drafting and Design) is an integral part of all our drafting programs.
Classes and program activities are typically scheduled between 7:00 AM and 2:30 PM, Monday to Friday.
All students begin in September and must complete a drafting and CADD core and specialty. Total program length: 40 weeks.
### The Program
The Structural CADD and Graphics Technician specialty focuses on drafting and computer applications related to structural building systems and construction. Students develop skills in preparing structural working drawings, detailing building components, and producing construction documentation using industry-standard drafting and CADD practices.
Training includes structural drafting principles, building construction applications, Computer-Aided Drafting and Design (CADD), and Building Information Modeling (BIM) using industry-standard software applications commonly used in the engineering, architecture, and construction industries.
The program incorporates applied and experiential learning components throughout the curriculum. Students receive training to help them secure practicum placements and prepare for professional workplace expectations. Practicum opportunities are typically between two weeks and one month in duration. Practicum does not require an additional fee and is included within the curriculum. The program also includes experiential learning activities such as field and site visits, as well as industry guest speakers.
Courses are offered by registered professionals and practicing technicians and technologists in the field, providing students with industry-relevant knowledge and practical experience. Graduates may pursue opportunities in areas such as structural drafting, architectural drafting, building design support, construction documentation, BIM modeling, and related technician roles within engineering, architecture, construction, and consulting firms.

### Program matrix
Required Courses: | Credits
ASCT 1010 | Introductory Graphics By approaching drawing as both a graphic technique and a mental thought process, this subject area endeavors to enhance the Student's: (1) Skills of observation, visualization, and delineation. (2) Ability to draw effectively and efficiently using the graphic language of the industry. (3) Time management skills by encouraging a pragmatic, disciplined approach to projects & assignments. (4) Graphic communication skills which are required for other subject areas in the course. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 3.0
ASCT 1015 | Blueprint Reading This course provides an introduction to the relationships between construction drawings and building construction. The course goal is to enable students to read and interpret construction drawings, including Floor Plans, Roof Plans, Elevations, Building Sections, Framing Plans and Construction Details. Students will be able to understand the relationship and interdependencies of all drawings represented in contract documents. They will also become familiar with abbreviations, symbols and materials used in construction blueprints. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 2.0
ASCT 1020 | Introduction to CADD Drafting Introduces the use of Computer Aided Design (CAD) application software for Drafting Applications. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 3.0
ASCT 1051 | Mathematics for Technical Applications ASCT 1051 covers those topics in technical mathematics which are important for student success in drafting. Topics include algebraic operations, simple equations, ratios, basic geometry, quadratic equations, graphs, linear equations, vectors, trigonometry, logarithms and exponential functions. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 4.0
ASCT 1110 | Applied Statics ASCT 1110 covers the relationship between applied loads and the resultant support reactions and internal forces developed in statically determinate members and stuructures. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 5.0
ASCT 1120 | Structural Steel Design and Detailing ASCT 1120 introduces students to the design and detailing of steel structures. Topics include design of steel beams, columns, OWSJs, steel tension members, bolted and welded connections for beams and beams-columns, as well as concepts and techniques required to produce engineering and shop drawings for fabrication of structural steel members according to building code requirements. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 8.0
ASCT 1135 | Construction Drawings for Commercial Buildings This course provides students with the knowledge and skills necessary to develop a set of construction drawings from concept sketches, design information, codes and specifications. Students will learn how to graphically convey design and dimensional information and apply drawing conventions and techniques, construction systems and details in regard to reinforced concrete structural systems. The course is delivered through project work on foundation plans, sections, roof plans, schedules, and connection details. Prerequisite(s): 50% in ASCT 1010 and 50% in ASCT 1015 and 50% in ASCT 1020 course outline | 4.0
ASCT 1136 | Reinforced Concrete Structures ASCT 1136 presents theory and code requirements necessary for detailing of reinforced concrete structural elements. The course provides students with the knowledge and skills necessary to develop a set of construction drawings from concept sketches, design information, codes and specifications. Students will gain knowledge about more complex foundation and floor systems, variety of types of concrete slabs, beams and columns, and about producing both engineering and placing drawings. The course is delivered through project work on floor plans, foundation and roof plans and details, sections, beam/column/footing schedules, bar lists and connection details. Prerequisite(s): ASCT 1010 and ASCT 1015 and ASCT 1135 course outline | 9.0
ASCT 1145 | Construction Drawings for Industrial Buildings The primary purpose of this course is to provide students with a fundamental and practical knowledge of how structural steel is used to construct support frames for modern industrial buildings. Special emphasis is placed on how structural drafters in structural design offices prepare the working drawings required to help transform the architect's vision and the engineer's design into reality. The topics introduced will provide students with the knowledge and skills in working drawing conventions, techniques, construction systems and details, in regard to structural steel members. In order to emphasize the process of preparing structural steel design drawings for industrial building applications, students will be introduced to a wide variety of practical drafting examples and assignments that a structural steel design drafter might expect to encounter in an on-the-job situation. Prerequisite(s): 50% in ASCT 1010 and 50% in ASCT 1015 and 50% in ASCT 1020 course outline | 4.0
ASCT 1146 | Steel Construction The primary objective of this course is to broadly cover the subject of structural steel drafting, emphasizing the process of preparing structural steel design drawings and details for commercial and industrial building applications. The topics are chosen so as to provide the student with the knowledge and skills in working drawing conventions and techniques, construction systems and details, and building code requirements in regard to structural steel members. The course will also present an overview of basic structural steel design calculations through the examples of typical design calculations which will demonstrate the type of routine technical mathematic the entry-level structural drafter and designer can expect to encounter on the job. Students will be introduced to tables and reference information found in the Steel Construction Manual and Detailing for Steel Construction as well as Standard Specification Load Tables for Steel Joists and Joist Girders published by the Steel Joist Institute. Prerequisite(s): ASCT 1010 and ASCT 1015 and ASCT 1145 course outline | 4.0
ASCT 1155 | Construction Drawings for Residential Buildings This course presents information required to develop a set of construction drawings for a single family wood-frame structure. It introduces students to materials, methods and details specific to residential construction. The course is delivered through in-depth review of drawing sets for a single family wood-frame house and project work on floor plans, elevations, roof plans, foundations, schedules, and details. Prerequisite(s): 50% in ASCT 1010 and 50% in ASCT 1015 and 50% in ASCT 1020 course outline | 4.0
ASCT 1156 | Wood Frame Construction This course presents theoretical foundation and code requirements necessary to design and detail wood-framed structures. Through the study of building construction assemblies & techniques, this subject area endeavors to develop students’ knowledge of various construction assemblies, issues, methods, analytical & technical construction knowledge, and capacity to implement fundamental principles employed in single and multi-family residential construction. Prerequisite(s): ASCT 1010 and ASCT 1015 and ASCT 1155 course outline | 4.0
ASCT 1160 | Presentations and Communications By approaching communications as a necessary skill to transmit & transfer information, this subject area endeavors to build the Student's: (1) Skill in organizing mental thought processes, ideas, and concepts, in order to effectively communicate professionally in the work place. (2) Exposure to variety of: graphic, written, and oral presentation techniques, appropriate to particular "business-like" situations. (3) Ability to develop clear, precisely communicated, rationale in technical communications. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 2.0
ASCT 1175 | Computer Aided Design This course presents topics in Autodesk applications that are important to for the success of drafting professionals. The course is divided into two components: AutoCAD 3D and Civil3D. Topics covered in AutoCAD 3D component include intermediate CAD operations, editing drawings, constructing multi-view drawings, applying text, font, style commands, dimensioning, hatching, blocks, constructing 3D objects and modifying solid objects. In Civil3D component students are introduced to the fundamentals of Survey data and Civil Road drawings. Students create and manage drawings; edit & create styles and settings; create, import, analyze, manipulate points; subdivide land; create and terrain models; create horizontal alignments; create existing and design profiles; create and use assemblies for corridor design ; create road cross sections. Through lab work, students practice Civil3D skills and apply the skills mastered to a Final Subdivision Project. Students complete a Land Development Project that includes an existing Surface (DTM), Road alignments, Cross Sections and Profiles and a Designed Corridor Model. Prerequisite(s): ASCT 1020 course outline | 6.0
ASCT 1180 | Structural Graphics ASCT 1180 covers those topics in AutoCAD and Xsteel which are important for the success in drafting. Topics include Intermediate and Advanced AutoCAD operations, Viewports and Layouts, Attributes, External Reference Drawings, Inserting Clipboard and OLE Objects, 3D modeling, as well as Xsteel directory structure, creating and editing drawings, advanced modeling, simple and interactive connections. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 10.0
ASCT 1190 | Mechanics of Materials ASCT 1190 covers the relationship between the external loads applied to a deformable body and the intensity of internal forces acting within the body. It also involves studying the deformations of the body and the body's stability when it is subjected to external forces. Prerequisite(s): ASCT 1110 course outline | 6.0
ASCT 1195 | Building Code Through the study of Building Code, this subject area endeavours to develop the Student's: (1) Awareness of the impact of codes & regulations upon design & construction. (2) Ability to interpret the various intents of the building code. (3) Ability to integrate the structural considerations in design & construction documents. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 2.0
Total Credits: | 80.0
