## Marine Mechanical Technician Foundation
Certificate Full-time School of Transportation
## Overview
Delivery: in person. See details .
A Marine Mechanical Technician is a person who installs, diagnoses, repairs and maintains engines, drive trains and other mechanical systems such as electrical, hydraulic and domestic fluid systems used in the recreational marine industry. This career involves all aspects of repairs to recreational and light commercial boats in regards to their diesel, gasoline, outboard engines, conventional inboard drive trains, stern drives, hydraulic and domestic fluid systems, and electronic management and controls.

## Entrance Requirements
### Application processing
Applications are accepted throughout the year.
### Entrance requirements
Applicants must meet all entrance requirements and will be accepted on a first qualified basis as long as space remains. When available intakes are full, qualified applicants are wait-listed.
Applicants must meet all entrance requirements prior to applying to this program. If you are uncertain or don't meet the requirements we strongly recommend you complete any applicable Trades Entry Assessments before applying.
- English language proficiency: one of the following: Category 3 – English Studies 12 (50%) or equivalent or BCIT English Trades Entry Assessment BCIT will accept your Grade 11 English final grade with proof of registration in English 12 for entry into the program.
- Math: one of the following: Foundations of Math and Pre-Calculus 10 (50%) or Workplace Math 10 (50%) or Other acceptable BC and Yukon courses or BCIT Math Trades Entry Assessment

### Program matrix
Required Courses: | Credits
MMTF 1000 | Safety in the Workplace In this course you will be introduced to the elements of safe working practices and will identify the common hazards and risks of accident/injury in a marine industry workplace. Here you will also learn about Workplace Hazardous Materials Information System (WHMIS), fire development and suppression, and the procedure and reasons to Lock Out equipment and the boat systems. This course will demonstrate to you to safe boat yard and marina practices. In portions of the course you will learn about proper lifting, blocking, and securing vessels and their equipment. Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 5.0
MMTF 1005 | Tools and Equipment ​In this course you will be introduced to the various tools and equipment and their use in the marine industry. This course also introduces you to the principles of steel and aluminum construction techniques and the principles of FRP construction techniques along with the selection of lubricants and additives. In portions of the course you will demonstrate the proper use of measuring, specialty tools, fasteners and fittings, shop equipment and the use of composites.​ Prerequisite(s): 70% in MMTF 1000 course outline | 2.5
MMTF 1010 | Regulations and Vessel Operation ​In this course you will learn about safe operation of vessels and obtain a Pleasure Craft Operators Card (PCOC) approved by Transport Canada. This course also introduces you to the typical procedures for commissioning new boats and equipment, procedures for conducting sea trials, the common sources of technical data and the reasons for industrial standards. In addition, you will learn to read and comprehend the terminology, technical data, drawings, charts and graphs and write repair order descriptions related to the marine industry.​ Prerequisite(s): 70% in MMTF 1000 course outline | 2.5
MMTF 1100 | Heating, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration ​In this course you will learn about cabin heating systems and the installation considerations. This course also introduces you to air conditioning theory, its components, and the refrigeration systems. In this course you will demonstrate the servicing of an Air Conditioning (AC), heating and refrigeration system.​ Prerequisite(s): 70% in MMTF 1000 course outline | 3.0
MMTF 1105 | Fresh Water and Bilge Plumbing Systems ​In this course you will learn about thru-hull fittings, their materials and installation procedures. This course also introduces you to components and installation of fresh and waste water plumbing systems, water makers and bilge pump systems. In this course you will demonstrate the installation and servicing of vessel plumbing systems.​ Prerequisite(s): 70% in MMTF 1000 course outline | 3.0
MMTF 1110 | Vessel Support Systems ​In this course you will learn the characteristics of propane and safety considerations, regulatory codes and standards, and proper installation. This course also introduces you to davits, hoists and windlasses, fire suppression systems and electric and mechanical steering systems. In this course you will describe and demonstrate the safety considerations of propane fuel, precautions of installing and operating davits, hoists and windlasses, and the vessel fire suppression systems. ​ Prerequisite(s): 70% in MMTF 1000 course outline | 2.5
MMTF 1115 | Hydraulic Systems ​In this course you will learn the theory of hydraulic principles. You will also learn hydraulic system components as well as hydraulic safety. In addition, this course introduces you to hydraulic steering systems and components. In this course you will describe and demonstrate hydraulic system operation, install system components and diagnose hydraulic issues.​ Prerequisite(s): 70% in MMTF 1000 course outline | 5.0
MMTF 1200 | Metal Fabrication and Trailers ​In this course you will learn about metals and their properties commonly used in the marine industry. You will demonstrate metal working operations, cutting, threading, bending and lay-out techniques. In this course you will learn to describe and service boat trailer components, and adjust weight distribution. In this course you will describe and demonstrate basic metalworking techniques with common marine metals.​ Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 3.0
MMTF 1205 | Welding ​In this course you will learn about welding safety, use of equipment and welding techniques, as they apply to the marine industry workplace. You will use welding equipment safely to perform basic torch operations.​ Prerequisite(s): 70% in MMTF 1000 course outline | 2.0
MMTF 1300 | Electrical Theory and Equipment ​In this course you will learn about the principles of electrical theory, both alternating and direct current electricity and do electrical circuit calculations. In this course you will learn to read electrical schematics and use diagnostic and measuring equipment. In this course you will complete circuit calculations and use diagnostic meters and equipment. Prerequisite(s): 70% in MMTF 1000 course outline | 4.5
MMTF 1305 | Batteries, Charging and Inverters ​In this course you will learn and describe types of storage batteries and the different applications, how batteries are re-charged and serviced. In this course you will learn battery construction, applications and ratings. You will also learn how batteries are re-charged as per the battery's construction and the different ways batteries are re-charged. Prerequisite(s): 70% in MMTF 1000 course outline | 4.5
MMTF 1310 | DC Circuits and Diagnostics ​In this course you will learn DC electrical circuit components, installation techniques and standards. In this course you will learn about the engine starter, gauges and alarm circuits, their components and circuit requirements. This course you will also calculate, assemble and diagnose DC electrical circuits.​ Prerequisite(s): 70% in MMTF 1000 course outline | 2.0
MMTF 1315 | Generator Sets ​In this course you will learn the function of self-contained Alternating Current (AC) generators and the criteria to be considered when installing a new system. You will learn to install a generator and how to diagnose generator faults.​ Prerequisite(s): 70% in MMTF 1000 course outline | 2.0
MMTF 1400 | Engine Theory and Operation ​In this course you will learn the history, theory and operation of the basic 2 stroke and 4 stroke internal combustion engines. In this course you will learn spark and compression ignition engines and their operating principles. Also in this course you will perform routine inspections on various 2 and 4 stroke gasoline and diesel engines.​ Prerequisite(s): 70% in MMTF 1000 course outline | 2.0
MMTF 1405 | Engine Support and Drive Systems ​In this course you will learn about the support systems required for the engine to operate. These support systems include lubrication, cooling, fuel, intake air and exhaust systems. In this course you will learn the basics of drive system components, propellers, gear boxes and marine gears. In this course you will inspect, service and repair engine support systems and drive components.​ Prerequisite(s): No prerequisites are required for this course. course outline | 6.5
MMTF 1500 | Industry Exploration ​In this course you will learn about the recreational marine industry which could include service and repair, parts supplies, sales and customer service. This course introduces you to the recreational marine service industry to gain knowledge about the working environment. In this course you will explore the marine industry by either job shadow, doing a research project or an assigned workshop project.​ Prerequisite(s): 70% in MMTF 1000 course outline | 2.0
Total Credits: | 52.0
