## Highway Design Part 2: Route Selection TSYH 4503
Transystems Course
## Course Overview
An introduction to the development of a highway design from its conception to horizontal and vertical alignment. Survey and mapping requirements and aerial photography are employed in the selection of preliminary alignment, including the detailed horizontal and vertical design of a highway. The course is project- based and requires a detailed design of a section of highway including the selection of radii; superelavation and visible standards for a particular design speed or road classification; calculation of data for horizontal curves (circular arcs and transitional spirals) and vertical curves; calculation of the attainment and removal of superelevation.

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
## Module 1 - Routine Selection - Corridor Location and Selection
- Explain the need for reviewing a number of possible routes for a new highway.
- Explain the term 'corridor' and describe the major factors or controls in the preliminary selection of a corridor.
- Describe the factors which determine whether or not a corridor is viable.
- Describe the mapping and aerial photography requirements for a corridor analysis.
## Module 2 - Route Location - Preliminary Design
- Understand the need to reduce the number of viable corridors to one preferred corridor.
- Explain the process used in eliminating routes owing to cost of construction; geometrics and other factors.
- Describe the survey data required at this stage of highway design.
- Identify the mapping and aerial photography requirements for the preliminary route analysis.
- Use the above process and data to select the preferred route from the available corridors.
## Module 3 - Horizontal Alignment - Minimum Radii and Superelevation
- Describe the factors which should be considered in horizontal alignment design.
- State the factors which govern the selection of a minimum horizontal radius.
## Module 4 - Circular Curves and Visibility
- State and define the five basic elements of simple circular curves and the formulae for determining element values based on two knowns.
- Compute the stationing for tangents, Beginning of Curves (B.C.), End of Curves (E.C.) for simple circular curves.
- Define stopping sight distance horizontal clearance, determine the required value and check for clearance based on curve data.
- Define passing sight distance relative to horizontal alignment, determine the required value and check for clearance based on curve data.
## Module 5 - Transitional Spiral Curves
- State and define the purpose of adding transitional spirals to a circular curve.
- Calculate the minimum spiral lengths by formula based on centrifugal force acceleration and on superelevation application.
- Select minimum spiral lengths from design tables and compare values derived from various tables.
- State and define the terms and symbols used in the geometry of spiralled circular curves.
- Calculate the spiral curve data based on tables, formulae, and basic design data.
## Module 6 - Vertical Alignment
- Understand the importance and use of sight distance in the vertical design of a highway.
- Calculate the centreline vertical alignment for a road using the above data.
## Module 7 - Application of Superelevation
- Explain what is meant by the term 'development of superelevation.'
- Explain and apply design methods for the removal of adverse crown and for the reinstatement of normal crown on a highway.
- Explain and apply design methods for the application of, and the removal of, superelevation for horizontal curves comprising simple circular arc and transition curves.
