## Sensor Interfacing ROBT 4451
Mechatronics and Robotics Course
## Course Overview
This course covers interfacing microcontrollers to the digital and analog sub-systems found in mechatronic systems, robots, and industrial manufacturing systems. Topics include digital serial communications (SPI, I2C, UART), digital-to-analog (DAC) and analog-to-digital (ADC) converters, representing signals in the frequency domain (signal spectra, sampling and aliasing), sensor devices (optical encoders, potentiometers, resolvers, accelerometers), isolation, interconnect and power distribution techniques used in sensor/actuator systems, and MOSFET and SCR based drivers.

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Describe the common digital serial sub-systems used in microcontroller systems.
- Use digital serial sub-systems of a microcontroller to communicate with digital peripherals and sensors.
- Describe various ADC and DAC techniques, applications and their specifications.
- Apply various types of ADC and DAC converters to data acquisition and control applications.
- Build ADC, DAC and signal-conditioning circuits.
- Describe a variety of robotic sensory devices such as potentiometers, resolvers and optical encoders.
- Build the interfacing hardware/software needed for direction, position and velocity control using optical encoders.
- Use the ADC sub-system of a microcontroller to read analog signals.
- Use frequency domain techniques to understand signal spectra, sampling and aliasing in analog and sampled data systems.
- Build transistor and SCR based drivers.
- Use interconnect, isolation and power distribution techniques to minimize noise in analog data acquisition systems.
- Apply design and troubleshooting skills to create functional mechatronic systems.
