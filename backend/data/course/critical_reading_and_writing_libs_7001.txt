## Critical Reading and Writing LIBS 7001
Liberal Studies Course
## Course Overview
This is a course in advanced composition and rhetoric, in which students will develop skills in complex critical analysis and interpretation by analyzing and evaluating materials from a variety of discourses or genres, including visual, online, and print; developing and writing essays, including critiques and research papers; applying and discussing principles of rhetoric and critical theory; examining and using methods of interpretation and analysis from the humanities and social sciences; evaluating the credibility of primary and secondary sources, including as it applies to media literacy, and for the purposes of academic research; situating discourses within their historical context and relevant to rhetorical theories of different periods (for example, Aristotle in the ancient world and Bakhtin in the twentieth century). The course format will include lecture, discussion, and both individual and group activities.
### Prerequisite(s)
- 50% in BCIT ENGL 1177 or equivalent, or 6 credits BCIT Communication at 1100-level or above, or 3 credits of a university/college first-year social science or humanities course.

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Analyze and use complex levels of style and tone suited to a given purpose (GA 7)
- Identify and assess logical and organizational patterns within complex texts. (GA 7)
- Recognize written, oral, and visual discourse conventions within and outside of specific disciplines. (GA 7)
- Compose documents for audiences with different backgrounds and specializations. (GA 7)
- Evaluate rhetorical strategies in a variety of written and digital media (GA 7, 12)
- Develop and apply interpretative and analytical skills. (GA 7, 12)
- Demonstrate the social and ethical implications of rhetoric in historical development and contemporary practice. (GA 7)
- Propose, research, organize, and write a research paper that uses the rhetorical concepts and strategies discussed in the course. (GA 7)​
Effective as of Fall 2023
