## Piping Foundation (Harmonized) PIPE 1140
Piping Course
## Course Overview
Combining theory and practice, this course will provide students with the ability to apply safe work practices on the job-site. Students will control workplace hazards, and apply Occupational Health and Safety Regulations and Workers Compensation Standards. They will learn and use the Workplace Hazardous Materials Information System. Students will learn to select and use personal protective equipment. They will learn to identify fire hazards, learn how to prevent fires and how to select appropriate fire extinguishers for the various types of fires. Students will also learn how to use tools and the many types of pipe used in the piping trades. Students will learn to select and use appropriate tools as well as inspect and maintain them. Students will first be introduced to hand tools and then to portable and stationary power tools. The major focus will be practical projects designed to challenge students in laying out and joining a variety of different types of pipe for different applications. Students will also learn how to test integrity of their piping installations. Students will learn how to select and use soldering materials and equipment appropriate to the piping process. Students will learn how to inspect and maintain soldering equipment. Students will be taught about the many fittings and connections used in the piping trades. They will learn how to select the appropriate fitting for the application and how to properly install and test these fittings. Students will learn how to erect and use ladders and elevated platforms. Students will use hoisting, lifting and rigging equipment. They will learn how to select and use knots, bends and hitches appropriate to the application. Math and science will be applied to piping applications. Students will apply math and science to understand the design and operation of drain, waste and venting systems, steam heating systems, hot water heating systems, potable water systems, and some residential gas systems. Students will be taught the principles of electricity as they apply to the piping trades. Pressure measuring tools will be described and applied to their piping projects. Drafting projects will be used to teach students how to use drafting tools. They will learn how to use drafting symbols, lettering, and line construction. Students will learn to interpret information found on a set of drawings and apply this information to their practical projects in the shop. They will learn the purpose of using different types of drawings to best convey information and to convert between isometric and orthographic drawings. Students will learn how to select and install pipes, valves, and fittings for a variety of piping systems. They will be taught how to properly penetrate structures without compromising the integrity of other building systems. Students will learn about different types of pumps used in the piping trades, how they work, and for which applications each type is best suited. Through practical projects they will learn how to install and maintain pumps. Students will be introduced to electrical theory and applications in the piping trades. Students will develop a resume and apply job search techniques. Students who receive a Certificate in Piping Foundation are eligible for credit with the SkilledTradesBC for the first level of technical training in their chosen piping apprenticeship. (Plumber, Steamfitter/Gasfitter, or Sprinkler Fitter)

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Control Workplace Hazards
- Describe Workplace hazards.
- Manage workplace hazards.
- Demonstrate emergency procedures.
- Describe non-emergency injury reporting procedures.
- Describe how worksite safety policies are established.
- Use information in the OHS Regulation and WCB Standards
- Locate the Parts of the Occupational Health and Safety regulation as it applies to the Plumbing, Steam-fitting, Sprinkler-fitting, Gas-fitting workplace.
- Use WHIMIS
- Describe the purpose of the Workplace Hazardous Materials Information System.
- Explain the content of material safety data sheets (MSDS).
- Explain the contents of a WHMIS label.
- Apply WHMIS regulations.
- Use Hand Tools and Portable and Stationary Power Tools
- Select hand tools, portable power tools, and stationary power tools appropriate to piping processes.
- Use hand tools, portable power tools, and stationary power tools.
- Inspect and maintain hand tools, power tools, and shop equipment.
- Use Cutting, Brazing and Soldering equipment
- Select and use cutting brazing and soldering equipment appropriate to piping processes.
- Inspect and maintain cutting, brazing and soldering equipment.
- Describe and Use Ladders and Elevated Platforms.
- Use Rigging and Hoisting Equipment
- Describe and use hoisting, lifting and rigging equipment.
- Tie knots, bends and hitches.
- Use Mathematics and Science (including Electricity)
- Use mathematics and science, including electricity, to solve problems in the piping trades.
- Describe and use pressure measuring tools.
- Read drawing and Specifications
- Use drafting tools, symbols, lettering and line conventions.
- Convert between isometric and orthographic projections.
- Interpret information found on a set of drawings.
- Use Codes, Regulations and Standards
- Identify codes and standards encountered in the piping trades.
- Identify various environmental agencies that affect the piping trades.
- Apply codes and standards to practical piping projects.
- Describe, Select, and Install Pipes, Valves and Fittings used in the piping trades.
- Penetrate Structures
- Use acceptable methods of structure penetration.
- Select and install fittings used in structure penetration.
- Describe, Install, and Maintain Pumps associated with the piping trades.
- Describe Electricity
- Describe the principles of electricity and circuit concepts.
- Describe the use of electrical test equipment.
- Use Employability Skills
- Describe the skills and theory learned in the Piping Foundation Program.
- Produce a resume.
- Conduct a job search.
- Use Manufacturer and Supplier Documentation
- Describe documentation encountered in the piping trades.
- Use information contained in manufacturer and supplier documentation.
- Use the internet to source manufacturer’s documentation.
