## Health Care Law: A Management Perspective HMGT 7370
Health Management Course
## Course Overview
This online course provides an introduction to basic legal principles, major legislative provisions, and significant legal issues in health care law. The course is developed specifically for managers and leaders in the health care field. Wherever possible, the focus is on BC legislation and case law but for students in other jurisdictions other provinces'/countries's laws can be applied.
### Prerequisite(s)
- No prerequisites are required for this course.

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Appropriately use basic legal terminology in the health care context.
- Decipher legal citations, and locate basic sources of legal information.
- Explain the rule of law, and how the division of powers relates to the provision of health care in Canada or elsewhere.
- State and defend positions on a range of complex issues in health care law.
- Explain the current legal status of a variety of contemporary and issues in health care law.
- Identify the common-law principles and provisions in the Charter and other legislation that define and limit self-determination in health care decision-making.
- Articulate the basic principles of consent to health care, as well as the special considerations that apply to minors and others under a "legal disability".
- Analyze legal issues arising from a variety of scenarios; prepare recommended actions and justification.
- Discuss the torts of battery and negligence in the context of consent to health care.
- Describe the general authority of self-governing health care professions and give specific examples from BC legislation and from what is happening elsewhere.
- Explain the basic legal provisions regarding ownership of and access to health care records.
- Define the essential elements of a contract.
- Concisely summarize major health care legislation.
- Discuss the state of the law in Canada, or elsewhere, regarding sexual sterilization, abortion, and assisted human reproduction.
- Explain how death is defined in law, and compare and contrast the acts of refusing treatment, assisting suicide, and performing "euthanasia".
- Concisely summarize the material facts of a case, the legal issues raised, the respective positions set out in the cases, the court's decision and the implications of that decision.
- Adopt, evaluate, and provide support for one of the positions set out in a case.
- Initiate, contribute, and support online discussion with classmates.
