## Context of Nursing and Healthcare 3 BSNC 3020
Nursing Course
## Course Overview
Drawing on key findings from the In Plain Sight report (Government of BC, 2020), and informed by national nursing publications, BSNC 3020 juxtaposes Indigenous strengths, resiliency and worldviews against colonial forces of Eurocentrism and western superiority to understand the impact of sociopolitical and historical forces on health inequities faced by Indigenous Peoples in Canada today. Exploration of the Healthcare System in Canada demonstrates how social structures and institutions have been informed by colonial actions, which directly and indirectly impact health inequities in Canada. Concepts of cultural safety, social justice, trauma and health care system will be explored in this course. These concepts form the basis for identification of strategies to disrupt the status quo to bring about transformative change for Indigenous Peoples in Canada, as it relates to health and wellness. This blended course will occur using active learning strategies and provide learners an opportunity to explore nursing concerns and present their work as a community of learners. This course includes integrated and experiential learning that seeks to influence cognitive and affective domains of learning. Note to learners: This course explores content that may be challenging or triggering for individuals. For Indigenous students, BSN recognizes that the content may evoke responses that make engaging with the content particularly difficult. You are encouraged to practice self care to protect your spirit and to seek supports available at BCIT as necessary. For non-Indigenous students, the content may result in discomfort, defensiveness or other unsettling emotions. You are encouraged to sit with the discomfort as a means to further understand privilege and positionality in a settler colonial society. You are also encouraged to understand that discomfort is different than safety, and that while exploring this content may be uncomfortable, it does not threaten your personal safety. As noted by Hartrick Doane and Varcoe, “given that our attention and choices are shaped and limited by our own reference points, we need to consciously look beyond our habituated taken-for-granted views to inquire into what we do not know and may not be seeing" (2021, p. 32).​

## Learning Outcomes
​​​​​​Upon successful completion of this course, the learner will be able to:
Professional
- Demonstrates self-care and personal development to build resilience.
- Demonstrates consideration of the values, beliefs, and practices of clients
- Maintains own physical, psychological and emotional fitness to practice
- Recognizes and discusses personal biases and takes action to minimize the influence of personal values, beliefs and position power on client care
- Demonstrates knowledge of the healthcare system at the micro, meso, and macro levels
Advocate
- Identifies the impact of inequities in Determinants of Health, genetics that limit client choice and affect health outcomes
- Articulates the role of primary health care and the determinants of health in health delivery systems and their significance for population health
- Identifies systemic disadvantage for particular clients that effect health outcomes
- Incorporates knowledge of the origins of health disparities and inequities of Indigenous populations and the contributions of nursing practice into nursing care to achieve positive health outcomes for clients
- Incorporates knowledge of the health disparities and inequities of at-risk populations and the contributions of nursing practice into nursing care to achieve positive health outcomes for clients
- Identifies and respects basic human rights
- Recognizes the professional and ethic responsibility to advocate for client safety, fairness, and equitable distribution of healthcare resources within a defined scope of practice
- Uses relational practice to implement nursing care that recognizes the unique requirements of each client
Collaborator
- Identifies organizational structures within the healthcare system
- Identifies roles and scope of practice of healthcare team members
- Identifies conflict and discusses potential solutions
- Identifies multiple perspectives in any given situation to support safe, high quality, client-centered care
- Identifies and describes facets of a strength-based approach to collaboration
Communicator
- Recognizes the impact of communication in supporting safe, high quality, client- centered healthcare
- Demonstrates effective group process and presentation skills
Decision Maker
- Draws on knowledge from nursing and other sciences, humanities, research, ethics, spirituality, relational practice and critical inquiry in the practice of nursing care
- Has theoretical and practical knowledge of relational practice and understands that relational practice is the foundation for all nursing practice
- Reflects on nursing actions and incorporates knowledge into practice
Global Citizen
- Practices culturally safe and equitable care in defined contexts and situations
Leader
- Demonstrates knowledge of the role of the nurse as a leader/change agent/follower within a multidisciplinary healthcare team
- Participates in sharing of thinking about issues and decisions regarding client care and is open to having ideas challenged by others​
