## Introduction to Pharmaceutical Development BIOT 3250
Biotechnology Course
## Course Overview
This course explores the concepts, language, and history of the regulatory framework for pharmaceutical and health product development projects. Topics include History of the FDA, development of drug molecules and drug assay systems, techniques of scientific pharmacology (including pharmacokinetics and pharmacoeconomics), discovery phase and the clinical trials process, regulations, compliance and record keeping, entrepreneurship, and non-R&D career opportunities in health product companies.​

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Explain the historical reasons for development of Drug regulations.
- Assess case studies of project failures/successes and describe the importance of regulations, cGLP and records.
- Describe and differentiate the stages of discovery phase, proof of concept, and clinical trials process.
- Discuss the importance of the clinical trials process for the evaluation of new products (drugs and devices).
- Compare and contrast the regulatory delays and barriers associated with different product types.
- complete practical exercises in graphical and written presentation of data.
- Describe the factors leading to a successful drug development process. (including: Compound/target screening, Pharmacokinetics and Pharmacoeconomics)
- Discuss applications of biotechnology in compound and target screening.
- Describe the roles of clinical Research Organizations (CRO) and outsourcing of the clinical studies process.
- Describe and differentiate national and international regulatory bodies and jurisdiction.
- Use and understand key vocabulary to describe testing and approval of biomedical products.
- Discuss the current and future range of biomedical products from biotechnology.
- Evaluate the ramifications of intellectual property and IP licensing in biomedical projects.
- Describe various career paths within the biotechnology and health product industries.​
