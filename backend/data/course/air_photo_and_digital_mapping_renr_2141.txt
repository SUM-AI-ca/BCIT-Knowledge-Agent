## Air Photo and Digital Mapping RENR 2141
Renewable Resources Course
## Course Overview
Continues from RENR 1120 , with an emphasis on interpreting surficial materials, vegetation identification, and the use of aerial photos and other imaging. Students will transfer aerial photo information to a digital format using monorestitution software.
### Prerequisite(s)
- RENR 1120 RENR 1110 , MATH 1451 NB: a math pre- registration test may be available for students with a MATH 1451 mark between 40-49% who have a GPA over 60.
### Credits
7.0

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
## Aerial Photograph Interpretation Component
- Interpret slope and aspect from topographic maps.
- Draw cross sections and profiles from topographic maps.
- Describe the role aerial photographs play in resource management activities.
- Use photograph elements such as shape, tone, texture, size, pattern and shadow to identify and interpret features.
- Stratify aerial photographs according to established criteria.
- Identify tree species from large scale aerial photographs.
- Interpret generalized bedrock geology of an area.
- Interpret geomorphological features from aerial photographs, and describe their implications for resource management activities.
- Describe the drainage characteristics associated with different surficial materials.
- Interpret terrain classification maps.
- Recognize important wildlife and fisheries habitat characteristics from aerial photograph.
## Digital Mapping Component
- Obtain map and database file information from BC Government Internet sites.
- Obtain magnetic declination information from an Internet site.
- Obtain information about survey monuments from an Internet site.
- Describe the contents of TRIM, BTM, and FC1 files.
- Convert coordinates from different map projections and datums.
- Obtain point and line GPS data.
- Download and export GPS data to a digital map file.
- Create TINs (Triangular Irregular Networks) from field data, contour information, and DEM file information.
- Use TINs to create contour maps, slope, elevation, and aspect maps.
- Create an orthophotograph from a scanned aerial photograph image.
- Describe the process of using monorestitution software to transfer information from an aerial photograph to a digital map.
- Add new linework to a digital map using basic digitizing rules.
- Create neatlines that include geographic and UTM coordinates.
- Create maps with title blocks and legends.
- Create topologically correct digital map polygons.
- Design a simple database.
- Establish relationships between different database tables.
- Query database information from multiple database tables.
- Link database map attribute data to a digital map file.
- Use BTM map and database files to produce a simple land use map of an area.
