## Hydraulics 2 for EET EENG 7718
Environmental Engineering Course
## Course Overview
A continuation of Hydraulics 1 for EET, including pipe networks, pumps, uniform and non-uniform open channel flow, and flow measurement.
### Prerequisite(s)
- 50% in EENG 7715
### Credits
1.0
Retired: This course has been retired and is no longer offered. Find other Flexible Learning courses that may interest you.
## Learning Outcomes
A student will be able to:
- The student will be able to solve hydraulic problems for varying pipe flow conditions
- Analyze close conduit systems (simple series, parallel pipe and looped networks)
- Analyze single pipe and equivalent pipe systems
- Prepare solutions for friction losses and flow control devices such as venturies
- Apply the Hazen-Williams formula to determine the equivalent pipe method
- Apply the Hardy Cross method to solve hydraulic conditions in complex pipe networks
- Understand the relationship between pump performance and efficiency (estimate system curves)
- Describe how to measure pressure, velocity and discharge using various instruments
- Apply methods to determine flows in open channel and closed conduit systems under uniform and non-uniform flow conditions
- Explain the use of weirs, flumes, when and why hydraulic jumps occur
- Determine energy losses and hydraulic gradient lines for these flow devices
- Describe various manometers and their uses
- Solve for hydraulic head values in closed system flow using manometers
- Explain the concepts of static head and total dynamic head
- Describe how centrifugal, turbine and impeller style pumps work
- Use pump performance curves in order to select an appropriate pump
- Solve uniform open channel problems using the Manning equation
- Explain more complex non-uniform open channel flow problems
- Determine and describe the open channel flow regimes
- Assess changes in water surfaces in an open channel contraction, expansion, depression or hump.
- Apply the principles of specific energy in open channels
