## Human Factors Engineering in Health BMET 7104
Biomedical Engineering Course
## Course Overview
This course covers the principles and applications of human factors engineering with focus in health. Through analyzing and evaluating medical technology and user requirements, this course will lead participants into exploring and applying human factor principles in design, evaluation, procurement, and management of medical technology in the healthcare environment. The course will continuously challenge the participants' ability in critically reviewing information and solving problems. Real-life examples and case studies are presented throughout the course. This is an online course using the internet for learning and communication.
### Prerequisite(s)
- A diploma in technology or equivalent combination of education and professional experience.

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to
- Define human factors engineering and identify the major stakeholders of its application.
- Adopt a `human-tec' approach to designing, evaluating, and interacting with medical technology.
- Justify the needs and benefits of applying human factors engineering in the health environment.
- Identify cognitive and physical human limitations and demonstrate how they can impact the safe use of medical devices.
- Identify physical tasks related to the use of medical technology that put users at risk of injury based on established anthropometric guidelines (i.e., NIOSH lifting guide).
- Design improvements to the physical workspace to reduce identified risks that may cause physical harm, injury or discomfort while interacting with medical technology.
- Interpret and analyze standards and regulations (e.g., FDA's QSR, ISO and AAMI Standards) enforcing and guiding the implementation of human factors in medical devices design and development.
- Outline the concepts and theories of human factors engineering in collecting and analyzing data.
- Compare and contrast various usability evaluation methods.
- Discuss the benefits and drawbacks of automation introduced in medical devices and systems.
- Categorize errors types and understand the systemic view of how errors occur.
- Compare and contrast various accident investigation techniques.
- Conduct a Failure Mode and Effect Analysis.
