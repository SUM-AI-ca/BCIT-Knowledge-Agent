## Foundations for RNFA Practice RNFA 5000
Registered Nurse First Assistant Course
## Course Overview
Preliminary course that provides the theoretical foundation for RNFA practice, an expanded perioperative nursing role. Involves 14-16 weeks of facilitated part-time independent study combining on-line learning activities with print-based modules.
### Prerequisite(s)
- Registered nurse with 5 years current perioperative nursing experience and CPN(C). CNOR is also acceptable.
### Credits
3.0

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Distinguish the role of RNFA from that of the perioperative circulating and scrub nurse role.
- Define the role of the RNFA.
- Identify the differences and similarities between practice as a perioperative circulating and scrub nurse and that as an RNFA.
- Identify factors influencing the RNFA scope of practice.
- Analyze issues related to RNFA practice including supports and barriers to the role.
- Describe relevant legislation governing practice.
- Contrast a variety of perspectives related to the role.
- Relate the historical evolution of the RNFA role to current practice in Canada and the United States.
- Utilize a nursing framework as a foundation for RNFA practice with selected case studies.
- Evaluate how the nursing process is used as a foundation for RNFA practice.
- Describe the RNFA's role in the diagnostic process.
- Perform assessments of simulated surgical patients and analyze and interpret the data obtained.
- Recognize how a system approach can be used in assessment.
- Conduct an interview for the purpose of obtaining a health history.
- Interpret data in relation to anesthetic and surgical intervention.
- Explain issues related to assessment, interpretation and communication of data.
- Analyze and distinguish principles of asepsis, infection control and wound healing as they relate to RNFA practice.
- Describe the chain of infection and barriers to microbial transmission.
- Explore patient factors that influence the incidence of surgical site infection.
- Examine methods that reduce the risk of blood-borne exposures.
- Apply knowledge of wound healing and surgical technique to examine possible complications of wound healing.
- Explore the RNFA's role in the prevention of surgical site infections and wound management.
- Integrate knowledge of surgical anatomy, physiology, pathophysiology, pharmacology and operative techniques to predict RNFA practices.
- Integrate and predict knowledge required of the RNFA for selected surgical procedures.
- Analyze intraoperative RNFA nursing practices that facilitate competent surgical assistance.
- Describe the principals of tissue handling, providing exposure and the correct use of instruments.
- Discuss the primary methods of hemostasis used during surgical intervention.
- Compare the main components of assisting for an open surgical procedure with those used for endoscopic procedures.
- Recognize potential surgical hazards and describe the RNFA's actions to reduce patient risk.
- Describe common signs and symptoms of selected complications.
- Critically analyze appropriate intervention sequencce and actions for the RNFA related to potential surgical hazards and emergencies.
- Identify effective communication skills for use as an interdisciplinary team member.
- Explore the concept of partnership and working in collaboration with the surgeon during the intraoperative phase.
- Describe change theory and practice for facilitating personal/professional role change.
- Evaluate research-based systemic inquiry to prepare for practice.
- Participate in collaborative learning activities.
- Develop an individualized personal learning contract for use during RNFA 6100.
