## Modified Master Limited with Simulator Demo NAUT 0485
Ocean Navigation Course
## Course Overview
This entry level course provides students with basic skills, techniques, and knowledge required to navigate in confined waters. Students will be given basic training in the use of nautical charts, as well as Electronic Navigational Charts (ENCs), which are essential for safe navigation. Students will be introduced to navigation rules to be followed by ships and other vessels at sea to prevent collisions between vessels, as set out by the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (ColRegs). Students will be provided practical hands-on training in the use of marine radar and electronic positioning systems. This course also provides students with basic ship handling techniques and responses to emergency situations using desktop simulators. Successful students must have a minimum of 100% attendance.

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Define common chartwork terminology.
- Identify mandatory Canadian nautical charts, documents, and publications required to be kept on board.
- Use the points on a compass card to navigate a vessel, locate an object, and seek direction at sea.
- Define variation and deviation, and calculate compass error.
- Explain the layout of a nautical paper chart.
- Determine distance, speed and time, given any of the two variables.
- Use chartwork instruments.
- Fix a position on a chart using various methods.
- Draw courses and calculate distances on a chart.
- Calculate set, drift, and rate of the current.
- Determine course to steer and course made good, allowing for current and leeway.
- Identify Canadian Aids to Navigation (buoyage system).
- Apply the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (ColRegs).
- Understand the master’s duties in emergencies.
- Use marine radar and electronic positioning systems.
- Use electronic navigation charts for safe navigation.
- Handle various types of vessels.
- Distinguish between stable, unstable, and neutral conditions of stability.
- Understand the dangers of free surface effect.
