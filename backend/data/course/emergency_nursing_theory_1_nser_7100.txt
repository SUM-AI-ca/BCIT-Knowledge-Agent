## Emergency Nursing Theory 1 NSER 7100
Nursing Specialty Emergency Course
## Course Overview
Introduces emergency nursing and focuses on client perspectives of care in emergency settings. Provides opportunities to explore the concept of partnership with clients, families, and health care professionals in a time limited, changing environment. Introduces emergency skills such as assessment, urgency determination, and diagnostic reasoning, along with basic pathophysiology.
### Prerequisite(s)
- No prerequisites are required for this course.

## Learning Outcomes
Participants will:
- Develop a personal definition of health that incorporates theory about determinants of health and their relationship to health promotion.
- Engage in the processes of critical thinking and reflection as they relate to personal values and beliefs about learning and nursing practice.
- Articulate the importance of understanding the phenomenological or 'lived experience' of patients and their families as it relates to the provision of emergency care.
- Examine pathophysiology related to the cardiac, respiratory, neurological, renal, GI, GU, gynecological and immune systems.
- Describe the characteristics of partnership relationships among nurses, patients, families and other health care professionals within the context of an emergency setting.
- Explore and begin to form an understanding of the concept of level of urgency as it applies to clinical decision making in emergency nursing.
- Outline a framework of patient assessment based on an understanding of individuals' needs, underlying pathophysiology and the nature of the emergency environment.
- Understand the use of diagnostic reasoning in identifying health-related problems for emergency patients.
