## Fish Ecology and Physiology RENR 2330
Renewable Resources Course
## Course Overview
This course will introduce students to the ecology, physiology, anatomy, and behaviour of freshwater and anadromous fishes of British Columbia. Lecture topics covered will include the evolution of fishes in the province, ranges and distributions, migration patterns, and life cycles. Students will also learn the taxonomy and key identifying characteristics of freshwater fish species in BC, with an emphasis on species endemic to the province. The cultural significance of fish to Indigenous communities in BC, in particular anadromous salmon, will also be discussed. Labs will focus on basic functions and physiology of fish, focusing on major life support and reproductive systems.

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Identify freshwater native and invasive fish species in BC using internal and external morphology.
- Characterize the natural history of native fishes in BC.
- Describe life stages, diet and foraging strategies, and movement patterns of common fish species in BC.
- Describe the life cycles of anadromous fish in BC.
- Explain the evolution of fishes in BC and how it affects their range and distribution.
- Discuss the importance of fish to Indigenous communities in BC.
- Explain the potential effects of climate change on range, distribution, and behaviour.
- Identify and describe major organ systems of several fish species using virtual resources and dissections.
