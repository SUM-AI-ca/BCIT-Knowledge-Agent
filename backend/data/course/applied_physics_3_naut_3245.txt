## Applied Physics 3 NAUT 3245
Ocean Navigation Course
## Course Overview
This applied physics course is a continuation of Applied Physics 2. The course covers elasticity of materials, application of Hooke’s law, condition of translational and rotational equilibrium when force applied to a body, centre of gravity, bending of beams, fluid in motion, waves, sound, and light, electro chemistry, electrical instruments, electrical protective devices, A/C and D/C machines.
### Prerequisite(s)
- Successful completion of Term 3
### Credits
3.0

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:
- Define elasticity, stress, and strain of the materials.
- Describe the application of Hooke’s law to the measurement of forces.
- Solve numerical and graphical problems involving forces, moments of forces, and equilibrium conditions.
- Find centre of gravity of a plane.
- Define "shear force" and "bending moment".
- Calculate the reaction of a simply supported beam.
- Plot curves of shear force and bending moment for a simply supported beam and cantilever beam.
- Describe application of Bernoulli’s equation to Venturi meter and eductors.
- Calculate the change in pressure resulting from a change in velocity of liquid flow using Bernoulli’s equation.
- Explain the basic concept of simple harmonic motion.
- Perform calculation involving the speed of sound and echoes in air and in water.
- Describe DC machines and A/C alternator.
- Describe Ship's electrical distribution system.
- Draw light ray diagrams to show formation of images.
- Describe electro chemical process.
