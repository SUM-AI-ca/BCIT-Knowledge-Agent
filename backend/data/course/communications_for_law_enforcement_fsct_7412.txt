## Communications for Law Enforcement FSCT 7412
Forensic Investigation Course
## Course Overview
Writing and briefing are fundamental to the crime and intelligence analysis profession. The ability to communicate clearly, concisely and coherently is basic to all intelligence disciplines, even the most technical. This course provides an introduction to important considerations in professional writing: appropriateness, completeness and coherence. It introduces students to the different reporting formats used by law enforcement agencies, their relevance in the operational and court-related settings, and provides essential information regarding the preparation of written products and intelligence briefings.
### Prerequisite(s)
- Admission to a Forensics credential program or permission of the Program Head.

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Identify the different reporting formats used by law enforcement agencies (i.e., RCMP forms 1624, C237, Briefing Notes, Daily Activity Reports, Weekly/Monthly Crime Bulletins, Threat Assessments, Report to Crown Counsel and Operational Plans).
- Evaluate the different types of audiences and purposes associated with each reporting format and translate analytical work accordingly.
- Learn how to condense report-writing to support investigative sections.
- Differentiate between information, fact and assumption and summarize key points efficiently and effectively.
- Interpret and utilize specific law enforcement terminology for operational and court-related purposes.
- Evaluate and cite all sources of information, and assess reliability and validity of intelligence.
- Apply the rules surrounding the dissemination and security of information, the provisions of Third Party Rule, and disclosure requirements as they relate to the role of analysts.
- Design and make use of effective visual aids, such as charts and graphs, to convey complex information and associations.
