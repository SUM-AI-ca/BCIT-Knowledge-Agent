## Fish Ecology and Management RENR 2410
Renewable Resources Course
## Course Overview
The goal of the course is to prepare students to work as technologists in the field of fisheries management with a focus on British Columbia. This course emphasizes fish identification, measurements, ichthyology, life history, basic biological features, fisheries management issues (mark recapture, stock recruitment), fish culture and fisheries techniques.
### Prerequisite(s)
- No prerequisites are required for this course.

## Learning Outcomes
Upon completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Understand the basic biology of fishes.
- Identify to species the important sport and commercial fish in BC.
- Identify to family all common fish found in BC fresh waters.
- Be familiar with common fisheries sampling techniques (gillnetting, seining, tagging).
- Understand concepts of biodiversity conservation of fishes in BC.
- Describe the life histories of important freshwater fish species in BC.
- Learn basic spawning and fish culture techniques of salmon and trout.
- Apply the methods used for fish measurement and aging.
- Understand issues associated with management of BC fisheries.
- Identify BC commercial, game and non-game freshwater fish species.
- Be familiar with major fish-harvesting techniques, particularly those in British Columbia.
- Be familiar with fish culture techniques including water supplies and treatment, containers, loadings and flow, hatchery maintenance and fish release strategies.
- Be familiar with major BC marine fish species.
- Understand basic fisheries population-estimate procedures.
- Have a basic understanding of fish disease and parasites and how to minimize their occurrence under culture conditions.
- Be familiar with steelhead stock assessment and freshwater restoration techniques.
- Be familiar with lake-limnological assessment (water quality, temperature, etc) techniques.
- Know how to use a dichotomous key for fish identification.
- Be familiar with watershed restoration techniques, including instream, off-channel and lake fertilization.
- Understand juvenile salmonid sampling techniques, identification and enumeration methods including tagging methodologies.
- Be familiar with downstream-migrating juvenile salmonid fish-collection techniques (rotary screen traps, inclined plane traps).
- Know about aspects of aquaculture in BC.
- Be able to apply his/her knowledge to the management of BC fresh and salt water fisheries resources.
