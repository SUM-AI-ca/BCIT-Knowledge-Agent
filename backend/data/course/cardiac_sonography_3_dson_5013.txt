## Cardiac Sonography 3 DSON 5013
Diagnostic Medical Sonography Course
## Course Overview
​ DSON 5013 Cardiac Sonography 3 is a continuation of DSON 5012 Cardiac Sonography 2. The theory section of this course includes modules on valve replacement and repair, diseases of the great vessels, infective endocarditis, cardiac masses, pericardial disease, heart failure, and systemic diseases. The emphasis throughout this course is on the integration of hemodynamics and clinical findings with typical and atypical sonographic features in the setting of cardiac disease. The lab portion of the courses teaches the student how to fully assess the heart and how to modify the exam based on pathology. By the end of this course, the student will be able to complete a full echocardiogram.

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion of the theory portion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Describe the etiology, patient history, clinical signs and symptoms, and expected oximetry and auscultation results in patients with various cardiac conditions. (5.1a, 5.2e)
- Integrate knowledge of anatomy and disease processes with the patient clinical history to predict likely echocardiography findings. (5.1e)
- Quantify severity of cardiac pathology by integrating knowledge of anatomy, disease progression, and applying correct variables within calculations. (5.4c)
- Identify and describe how to respond to urgent sonographic findings following standardized hospital protocols. (2.2h)
- Describe the assessment, documentation protocols, and treatment options in the setting of cardiac disease. (Appendix E)
- Describe the role of an echocardiography technician in interventional procedures, including transesophageal echocardiography. (3.2a, 3.2b)
Upon successful completion of the lab portion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Demonstrate effective oral, written, and nonverbal communication to ensure appropriate professional relationships with patients, students, and instructors. (1.1a,b,d,e,f,k, 1.2e, 1.3a,b)
- Consistently demonstrate initiative, professional conduct and judgment in a respectful and cooperative way. (2.1a,c,e,f, 2.2a,c,d,e,i, 2.3a,b,e,f,g,h,i, 2.4a)
- Effectively maintain patient dignity, comfort, and safety when performing sonographic examinations. (3.1a,b,d,e,f,g, 3.3d,f)
- Set up, monitor, and analyze a 3 lead ECG in the lab setting competently. (3.3c,f)
- Perform dynamic/provocative maneuvers appropriately as required, and describe associated hemodynamic effects. (3.3d)
- Set up and operate ultrasound equipment appropriately using knowledge of sonographic principles, techniques, and instrumentation. (4.1b,c, 4.2a,b,d,e,f,g, 4.3b,c)
- Perform modified scope cardiac sonograms, correlating patient clinical history, cardiac test results while integrating knowledge of anatomy and disease processes. (5.1a,b,c,d,e, 5.2d, 5.3a,b,c,d,e,f,g,h,i)
- Perform modified-scope cardiac sonograms, incorporating investigation, recognition of abnormal findings, and exam limitations. (5.3j,k)
- Provide a technical impression to reporting physician, documenting limitations, quality, and completeness of exam. (5.3l,m,n, 5.4f,g)
- Identify and prioritize differential findings. (5.4e)
- Create and maintain a safe work environment for the well-being and safety of patients, health personnel, and self. (6.1a,b,c,d, 6.2a,b,c)
