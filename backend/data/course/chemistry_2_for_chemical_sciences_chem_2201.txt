## Chemistry 2 for Chemical Sciences CHEM 2201
Chemistry Course
## Course Overview
Continues from CHEM 1101 . Topics include electrochemical cells, electron structure of atoms, properties of solids, liquids, gases, colligative properties of solutions, thermochemistry and organic chemistry. The industrial application of chemical principles is emphasised.
### Prerequisite(s)
- 50% in CHEM 1101
### Credits
8.0

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Solve stoichiometry problems involving oxidation-reduction reactions.
- Use the data collected in redox titrations to solve for unknown concentrations of oxidizing or reducing agents.
- Prepare standard solutions for oxidation-reduction titrations.
- Use tables of reduction potentials to assess the relative strengths of oxidizing and reducing agents.
- Write equations to describe the chemical reactions occurring at the anode and cathode in electrolytic cells.
- Analyze the construction of electrochemical cells to determine the voltage which would be produced under standard and non-standard conditions.
- Write equations to describe the reactions occurring in electroplating processes.
- Sketch the electronic structure of atoms using orbital diagrams.
- Solve problems involving 'ideal' and 'real' gases.
- Use phase diagrams to predict the phase changes a substance undergoes when its temperature is changed at constant pressure.
- Calculate the vapour pressure of solutions.
- Calculate the osmotic pressure of solutions.
- Describe the structure, properties and nomenclature of complex ions and coordination compounds.
- Calculate Heat of Reaction using Hess' Law and Standard Enthalpies of Formation.
- Predict the physical properties of selected industrially important organic compounds.
- Identify organic compounds by commonly used names and by IUPAC (systematic) names.
- Predict the chemical reactivity of organic compounds based on their chemical structures.
- Describe the industrial uses of selected organic compounds.
- Describe the occupational hazards, toxicity and storage of selected organic compounds.
- Demonstrate the proper laboratory skills required for:
- Calibration of pH meters.
- Measurement of melting point and mixed melting points.
- Setting up simple and fractional distillation apparatus.
- Preparation of ion exchange columns.
- Re-crystallization of an organic compound.
- Making absorption measurements using uv-visible spectrophotometers.
- Preparation of primary standard solutions.
- Performing redox and EDTA titrations.
- Performing solvent extractions.
- Use proper safety precautions when working with hazardous chemicals in the laboratory.
