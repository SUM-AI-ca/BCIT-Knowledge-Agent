## Writing for Television BCST 2224
Broadcast Communications Course
## Course Overview
This course introduces the fundamentals of dramatic writing and documentary research. Students generate original story ideas, then develop one idea from its concept through to a first draft film or television script. Students also learn basic documentary research skills and treatment preparation. Writing assignments also include character sketches and a story treatment.
### Prerequisite(s)
- BCST 1124

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Develop a story idea from logline through to a short-form (10–20 pages) three-act screenplay using character, plot, theme, and dialogue.
- Explain elements of mythical narratives and apply accepted story structure to various genres of storytelling.
- Recognize elements of good dialogue and write effective dialogue in their own narrative works.
- Create a screenplay in standard industry format.
- Discuss important ethical issues of documentary filmmaking.
- Conduct research and use accepted practices of non-fiction storytelling to write an effective and poignant documentary treatment within the bounds of accepted ethical principles of documentary filmmaking.
- Incorporate important ethical issues of documentary filmmaking into their treatments.
- Write relevant, thought-provoking, and exploratory interview questions and develop strategies for conducting the interviews.
- Discuss new media trends and developments, including transmedia narratives, interactive cinema, installation-based media, web-based content, and mobile applications and games.
