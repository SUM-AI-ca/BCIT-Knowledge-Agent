## Wood Design ARSC 7050
Architectural Science Course
## Course Overview
The purpose of this course is to introduce traditional wood design and contemporary applications in large-scale, non-traditional buildings. Students will examine new engineered wood products, manufacturing technologies, and changes to building codes / other legislation to assess new possibilities for wood design and construction. This course will: i) provide an introduction to the attributes of wood as a building material; ii) give an overview of emerging technologies in frame, mass timber and composite construction in British Columbia; iii) explain how building industry use of wood fits within the broader context of environmental, economic and social sustainability; and iv) discuss the physical and psychological benefits of wood in architectural applications.

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Explain the basic properties of wood (including structural characteristics, durability, and connectors). Explore the properties that influence the use of wood in construction.
- Evaluate the environmental attributes of wood and the relationship between sustainable forest management, wood building, and climate change mitigation.
- Interpret the changing practices and opportunities for new applications for wood.
- Compare and evaluate current approaches and materials being used to build larger and taller structures.
- Evaluate and compare the aspects of project delivery for large wood buildings that impact building performance (including material selection, manufacturing, and construction management)
- Assess the value of wood according to commonly used green building rating systems.
- Compare and contrast the principles of ‘constructive environmentalism’ applied to buildings of different types and sizes to maximize wood use.
- Assess and explain the social, economic, and environmental value of wood as a building material locally and internationally.
- Access the connection between historical and contemporary methods of building in wood.
