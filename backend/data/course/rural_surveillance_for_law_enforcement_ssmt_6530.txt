## Rural Surveillance for Law Enforcement SSMT 6530
Security Systems Management Course
## Course Overview
This skills-intensive surveillance course is designed for law enforcement officers who may be employed in small units or detachments in the conduct of field investigations in rural environments. Course content is focussed on individual skills development and the techniques and procedures employed by a small unit in the conduct of a covert observation post in a rural area.
### Prerequisite(s)
- Participants must meet the following criteria: -Operational member of federal or provincial law enforcement agency/department -Completed basic training with that agency/department -Currently employed or about to be employed in a rural area/environment -Hold an agency medical certificate -An interest in outdoor pursuits is desirable -Permission of the program head or instructor

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Demonstrate the correct selection, packing, and carriage of his/her personal equipment for field preparedness.
- Describe the characteristics and hazards associated to the rural environment.
- Demonstrate the application of basic self-health and personal administration while living under field conditions.
- Describe the effect of the rural environment on movement, concealment, and observation.
- Demonstrate the use of hand signals for silent communication.
- Demonstrate techniques employed in tactical movement in close country.
- Describe the effects of light and terrain in judging distances.
- Demonstrate techniques employed in judging distances.
- Demonstrate use of camouflage to aid in concealment.
- Interpret topographical maps.
- Demonstrate map to ground orientation by inspection/comparison.
- Identify and plot Unit Transverse Mercator grid references.
- Describe and demonstrate use of scale in estimating distances.
- Describe the effect of contour in siting an observation post.
- Interpret stereoscopic maps to effectively perform field manoeuvres.
- Describe the characteristics, application, and limitations of telescopes and binoculars for observation.
- Describe climbing techniques.
- Describe and demonstrate field-sketching techniques.
- Demonstrate observation post recording/logging procedures.
- Describe and demonstrate patrol lying up procedures.
- Describe and demonstrate patrol rendezvous procedures.
- Demonstrate patrol obstacle crossing procedures.
- Describe the categories of sign.
- Describe where sign can be found.
- Demonstrate pace for pace tracking techniques.
- Demonstrate cache recording and reporting procedures
- Demonstrate patrol daily routine and hard routine.
