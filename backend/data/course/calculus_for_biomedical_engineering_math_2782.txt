## Calculus for Biomedical Engineering MATH 2782
Mathematics Course
## Course Overview
Covers rules of differentiation, rates of change, related rates, differentials, implicit differentiation with application to electrical and mechanical problems, definite and indefinite integral, calculation of mean, RMS values for various electrical waveforms, integration techniques, and the Fourier series applied to electrical circuits.
### Prerequisite(s)
- 50% in MATH 1781
### Credits
5.0
Not offered this term: This course is not offered this term. Notify me to receive email notifications when the course opens for registration next term.
## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Use differential calculus to solve problems relating to the electronics for Biomedical Engineering Technology.
- Use integral calculus to solve problems relating to the electronics for Biomedical Engineering Technology.
- Solve equations using Newton’s method.
- Apply fundamental differentiation rules including the product rule, quotient rule and chain rule.
- Perform calculations involving rates of change in electrical and simple mechanical systems.
- Determine maximum and minimum values of a function.
- Apply implicit differentiation.
- Use differentials to estimate errors in measurements.
- Use methods of substitution, integration by parts, table lookup to evaluate the indefinite and definite integral of a function.
- Analyze damped sinusoidal function and apply solution to circuit response.
- Use numerical integration to approximate definite integrals.
- Calculate the average and root mean square value of a periodic waveform.
- Find the Fourier series expansion of periodic signals.
- Relate Fourier series to signal filtering.
