## Silviculture FNAM 3820
Forest & Natural Areas Mgt Course
## Course Overview
This course explores the principles and practices of silviculture the art and science of influencing forest establishment, composition, growth, and quality. Students will develop a comprehensive understanding of forest ecology, stand dynamics, and silvical characteristics of commercial tree species. Emphasis will be placed on applying the Biogeoclimatic Ecosystem Classification (BEC) system to inform silvicultural decision-making and develop sustainable forest management strategies. Students will learn to assess site conditions, evaluate stand structure, and recommend appropriate silvicultural systems and regeneration methods to achieve specific management objectives. The management objectives for stands and forests include biological diversity, cultural heritage, fish & riparian, forage & associated plant communities, recreation, resource features, soils, timber, visual quality, water quality, and wildlife.

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Classify forest sites using the Biogeoclimatic Ecosystem Classification (BEC) system and interpret BEC information to inform silvicultural prescriptions.
- Analyze stand structure, growth patterns, and successional processes to predict future stand development and guide silvicultural interventions.
- Select appropriate management strategies for a site that are aligned with Basic Silviculture Stocking Standards and Soil Conservation Standards.
- Evaluate silvical characteristics and climactic attributes of commercial tree species pertinent to the selection of silvicultural system and regeneration method to recommend a suitable species composition for regeneration.
- Recommend suitable silvicultural systems and regeneration methods based on site conditions, species characteristics, and management objectives, considering factors such as regeneration requirements, growth rates, and desired stand structure.
