## Electroneurophysiology EEG 2 ENPY 2252
Electroneurophysiology Course
## Course Overview
This course is a continuation of ENPY1153 – Electroneurophysiology EEG 1 and will prepare you for your first clinical. You will apply concepts learned in the prerequisite course to complete EEG recordings according to the Canadian Association of Electroneurophysiology Technologists (CAET) Minimal Technical Standards. Emphasis will be on hands-on learning, classifying recordings, and running records with minimal assistance. Topics covered include normal and abnormal EEG waveforms, and a detailed look into clinical conditions diagnosed by EEG. The course also introduces the skill of taking a relevant patient history and emphasizes professional interaction with all patients.

## Learning Outcomes
At the end of this course the student will be able to:
- Obtain a patient history for any electroneurodiagnostic test.
- Perform a routine digital EEG recording.
- Accurately measure and apply scalp electrodes using the 10-20 system, done within 40 minutes.
- Maintain electrode impedances equal to and less than 5 kohms.
- Conduct calibration procedures.
- Adjust filter and sensitivity settings correctly.
- Demonstrate the appropriate use of montages.
- Identify state changes, normal and abnormal waveforms.
- Perform activation procedures and identify waves associated with them.
- Troubleshoot artefacts and other technical problems.
- Document the above and other pertinent information.
- Demonstrate sensitivity to patient and environmental issues when performing any test in an institutional or clinical setting. This includes:
- Patient safety
- Confidentiality
- Professional conduct
- Sensitivity to different patient needs or clinical situations
- Infection control
- Demonstrate the ability to adapt recording procedures for a variety of patient populations.
- Describe different types of seizures and identify expected electrographic abnormalities.
- Describe epilepsy classification, types of epilepsy and corresponding lobe functions.
- Correlate the following clinical disorders with their EEG:
- Vascular, stroke
- Tumors
- Metabolic encephalopathies
- Infectious encephalopathies
- Dementias
- Degenerative disorders
- Critically ill
- Head trauma and post traumatic epilepsy
- Non- organic, psychiatric
- Migraine, headache, pain
- Congenital and developmental disorders
- Describe how the following medication types affect an EEG: antiepileptics, antipsychotics and anesthetic agents.
- Troubleshoot and enhance EEG abnormalities during and after recording.
- Record a seizure accurately and safely while using response testing techniques.
- Identify both normal and abnormal waveforms on previously recorded EEGs and real-time EEGs.
