## Housing Guide
Housing in the Vancouver area can be a challenge, particularly for newcomers. Rental and real estate prices in the Vancouver area are among the highest in the world, though some there are some budget-friendly options including BCIT Student Housing and shared off-campus housing. Costs can vary significantly, but expect to spend between $800 to $2500 per month on housing.
If you are planning to come study at BCIT and don’t already have your accommodation secured, it’s best to start researching housing options as early as possible. Be aware that housing scams do occur and often target newcomers. Be vigilant and review our tips below on this page to help protect yourself so you don’t become a victim of a fraudulent housing post.
## BCIT Student Housing
With the opening of the Tall Timber Student Housing in Fall 2025, BCIT has significantly increased our on-campus housing options and availability. International students accepted into a full-time, cohort-based BCIT program are eligible to apply to stay in Student Housing.
All of our Student Housing options are located at BCIT’s main campus in Burnaby, which is conveniently located between Brentwood Town Centre and Metrotown – both major regional centres with many restaurant and cafes, plenty of options for shopping, and easy SkyTrain access.

## Off-Campus Housing Options
There are a variety of Housing options in the Vancouver area but be aware that a low vacancy rate means that costs are high and competition can be fierce for available units.
The average monthly rental cost for a 1-bedroom apartment in the city of Vancouver is over $2,500, though shared housing options may be found for as little as $600 per month. As a general rule of thumb, housing costs tend to be higher towards the west and north sides of Metro Vancouver, and lower towards the eastern and southern sides of the metro area.
Learn more about various off-campus housing options below.
GEC Living is an all-inclusive, fully-furnished student dormitory option with several locations around Vancouver and Burnaby that are conveniently located near several of BCIT’s campuses. GEC Living is a semi-shared living environment with opportunities for socializing in the common areas with students from around the world attending local schools. Shared bedrooms start at $1,250 per month, though private bedroom and even private apartment options are also available.
Note that GEC Living is not directly affiliated with BCIT.
A homestay with a local family can be a flexible and affordable option.
There are several good reasons to consider a homestay including:
- Generally, these are the cheapest accommodation options
- The cost usually includes all utilities, wifi, laundry facilities, etc.
- The length of stay can be flexible and extendable
- Staying with a local family can be a rewarding experience
- Meals may be included
The following agencies offer local homestay options:
- Homadorma
- Home Sweet Homestay
- Vancouver Family Homestays
- First Choice International Placement Inc.
Vacation rental platforms such as airbnb and VRBO are available throughout the area and offer the flexibility to choose exact dates. These may be worth considering in some situations such as:
- You will stay in BCIT Student Housing but plan to arrive in the Vancouver area before you are able to move in to Student Housing,
- You will have family members accompanying you and require multiple bedrooms,
- You need a temporary accommodation upon arrival in the Vancouver area to allow you some time to arrange for a more permanent housing solution.
Be aware that vacation rentals tend to be on the more expensive side. However, weekly and monthly discounts may be available for longer stays – especially outside of peak summer and holiday travel times.
Both private and shared accommodations available for rent can be found through online rental listings. While it is common locally to find housing this way, be aware that there are housing scams with fraudulent postings. See below on this page for tips to help protect yourself.
Rental listings include:
- Rentals.ca
- Craigslist
- Kijiji

## Signing a lease in Canada
Lease is the commonly-used local term for a tenancy agreement . It is a legal document in Canada that an individual may sign with a landlord or property manager in order to rent a property. Once a lease is signed by both parties, a tenancy is begun and the leaseholder and any other occupants listed on the lease (i.e. family members, housemates, etc.) become tenants . In BC, tenants have certain rights as well as responsibilities as established by the Residential Tenancy Act .
Before signing any lease, read through our tips below.
Explore different areas to see where you might like to live. We suggest spending at least a few months in the Vancouver area before considering signing a lease in your name.
Complete the free, online Renting it Right courses and review the other resources on the Tenant Resource & Advisory Centre website along with their The Tenant Survival Guide .
Bring a friend or family member with you if possible. Make note of any damage (taking photos if necessary) and bring it to the landlord’s attention. Walk around the neighbourhood and check for amenities that may be important to you such as transit connections, grocery stores, parks, cafes, etc.
Take your time and make sure that you fully understand the terms since it becomes legally binding once you sign it.
## Housing scams and fraud
Be aware that unfortunately there are bad actors out trying to steal money from other people. A common scam involves fraudulent housing listings that often appear “too good to be true” and require payment to be sent in advance. When the victim arrives in Canada, they realize the place that they paid to rent doesn’t exist and the money along with the scammer are nowhere to be found. For your own well-being, we advise against transferring funds for housing before you travel to Canada unless you are absolutely sure that you can trust the other party involved.
Learn more about rental scams from the Royal Canadian Mounted Police.
