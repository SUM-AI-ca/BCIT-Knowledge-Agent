## Overview of study permits
A study permit is a document issued by IRCC that allows a foreign national to study at a Designated Learning Institution (DLI) in Canada such as BCIT for a limited period of time.
Most international students coming to study at BCIT are required to have a BCIT study permit in order to comply with Canadian immigration regulations and also to meet BCIT’s study permit requirements for admission. You must apply for a study permit directly to IRCC and you should be aware that application processing times can vary considerably depending on where you may be applying from.
We recommend that you apply for your study permit as early as possible to make sure you will be able to start your studies as planned. If your study permit will expire before you plan to complete your studies, you may need to apply to extend your study permit .
## BCIT’s study permit requirement
BCIT requires international students to submit proof of having a valid BCIT study permit* at least 7 days before starting a program, unless you meet an alternative requirement . If you do not meet BCIT’s study permit requirement (or an alternative requirement ) you may be dropped from your program.
As soon as you receive a study permit approval letter from IRCC based on your acceptance letter from BCIT, you should provide a copy of that letter to statusdoc@bcit.ca for our records. This lets us know you are on track to begin your studies at BCIT as planned.
International students must maintain a valid immigration status with legal authorization to study at BCIT throughout your program. In addition to meeting BCIT’s requirements, international students planning to study in Canada must comply with all government regulations. For more information see IRCC – Do I need a permit to study in Canada?
*BCIT study permit is defined as:
- a valid study permit naming “British Columbia Institute of Technology” as the Institution, or
- a valid study permit not naming any specific DLI provided that can be verified as linked to BCIT.
## How to submit proof of your study permit to BCIT
Create a digital copy by scanning or taking a photo of your original permit and email it as an attachment to statusdoc@bcit.ca from your myBCIT email account. The digital copy must clearly show the entire document and must not be altered in any way.
Or you may bring your original study permit to the International Student Centre during drop-in hours .
## Alternative requirements
You may be able to satisfy BCIT’s study permit requirement without actually having a BCIT study permit if any of the following scenarios apply to you. Please review carefully as there are specific steps you may need to take in order to satisfy the alternative requirements.
See instruction above for how to submit any required documents.
If your BCIT study permit has expired, or will expire before the refund deadline for your program, you must:
- Submit a copy of your expired BCIT study permit along with the IRCC submission confirmation letter demonstrating that you applied for a study permit extension before your previous permit expired, and
- Submit proof of your new BCIT study permit as soon as you receive it.
If your previous study permit was not linked to BCIT then you may not be authorized to study at BCIT until you receive your new BCIT study permit.
In order to satisfy BCIT’s study permit requirement, you must provide at least 7 days before your program start BOTH your:
- Secondary school study permit, and
- Study permit application submission confirmation letter from IRCC (based on your BCIT acceptance letter and dated while your secondary school study permit was valid) .
According to IRCC: “You need to get a new study permit by applying to extend your current one to attend a post-secondary school. You can continue your studies at the post-secondary level while waiting for a decision on your new study permit application even if your study permit has the condition, “Going to primary or secondary school”. If your study permit application is refused, you must stop studying immediately… If you finish your secondary school studies early, your study permit will no longer be valid 90 days after you complete your studies, regardless of the date that is shown on your study permit.”
For more information, see IRCC – Changing your school or program .
As per Canadian immigration regulations: A foreign national may study in Canada without a study permit if the duration of their course or program of studies is six months or less and will be completed within the period for their stay authorized upon entry into Canada .
Therefore, Exchange and Study Abroad students planning to study at BCIT for only one term are exempt from BCIT’s standard study permit requirement.
Note that the BCIT’s standard study permit requirement applies to any student (including Exchange, Study Abroad, or Dual Degree) planning to come to study at BCIT for two terms or more.

If you have a valid status in Canada such as refugee, worker , or visitor ; then you may be authorized to study short-term without a study permit as described above.
If you are considering short-term studies at BCIT without a study permit, first understand that:
- There may be no option to legally extend your studies beyond six months, and therefore it is generally not suitable for individuals wanting to complete an entire program or credential.
- We strongly advise applying for a study permit before beginning your studies, unless you only want to take an individual course on it’s own.
- Students studying short-term without a study permit are responsible for their own compliance with Canadian immigration regulations.
You may encounter a hold on your account blocking registration if you have not provided a study permit or other status in Canada document. In order to remove the hold, you will need to provide, by email to statusdoc@bcit.ca or during in-person drop-in service at the ISC, one the following documents demonstrating your valid status in Canada :
- work permit,
- positive Notice of Decision (for Refugee status ),
- your visitor record, or
- your passport information page, and date of entry to Canada (for visitor status ).
Fully online courses are considered to be distance learning, and do not require a study permit. You may take distance learning courses from outside of Canada, or while having a valid status in Canada such as refugee, worker , or visitor .
You may encounter a hold on your account blocking registration if you have not provided a study permit. In order to remove the hold and proceed with registration, send a short message to statusdoc@bcit.ca with the following information:
- Your name
- Your BCIT student number
- Your current country of residence (if outside Canada) OR your current status in Canada (if inside Canada)
- Which course(s) you are interested in taking
Our staff will review your message and confirm once you are able to register.
Note that most credential programs at BCIT have some required classes, labs, or exams on campus.
If you are family or a staff member of a foreign representative to Canada that has been accredited by Global Affairs Canada (GAC), then you are considered to have Diplomatic status and may be authorized to study in Canada without needing a study permit.
You must submit proof of Diplomatic status in order to be exempt from BCIT’s standard study permit requirement.
## How to apply for a study permit
Most individuals applying for a study permit will require a provincial attestation letter (PAL). Find out if you need a provincial attestation letter (PAL) or not.
Before applying for a study permit from outside of Canada, you will need to:
- Gain acceptance into a program at BCIT
- Review Guide 5269 – Applying for a Study Permit outside Canada
- Prepare the required documents
- Create an account on the IRCC website
### After you apply
After submitting a study permit application from outside Canada, you will receive a letter from IRCC with instructions for providing biometrics (fingerprints and photo). Generally this required within 30 days of when you submit your application, and your study permit application will not be processed by IRCC until your biometrics have been received.
If your study permit application is approved, you will receive a Port of Entry (POE) Letter of Introduction along with a visitor visa or Electronic Travel Authorization (eTA) , depending on your nationality. You must show this Letter of Introduction to officials when you arrive in Canada in order to get your study permit.
Note that US nationals do not require a visa or eTA to enter Canada, so will receive neither with their Letter of Introduction.
For more information, see Study permit: How to apply on the IRCC website.
Note that US citizens and permanent residents of the US may apply for a study permit at a Canadian port of entry.
## Conditions of study permit holders in Canada
Unless you are exempt , you must satisfy IRCC’s study permit conditions while in Canada as a study permit holder.
For more details, review the IRCC operation manual: Study permits: Assessing study permit conditions .
Understand that as a DLI, BCIT is required to report on the academic status of all students whose study permit is linked to BCIT.
## How to apply to extend your study permit from within Canada
The expiry date on your study permit tells you when you have to stop studying and leave Canada. If you plan to keep studying you need to extend your study permit.
Though you can apply for an extension at any time while you have a valid study permit, we suggest applying for a study permit extension at least 5 months before your current study permit will expire.
It is important to note that you can’t extend your study permit beyond your passport’s expiry date, so make sure to renew your passport well in advance if it will expire soon.
To extend your study permit you will need to:
- Request an International Confirmation of Enrolment Letter
- Prepare a copy of your official transcripts (recommended) or unofficial transcripts*
- Prepare to satisfy provincial attestation letter requirements
- Review Guide 5552 – Applying to Change Conditions or Extend Your Stay in Canada – Student – online application
- Follow the instructions at Extend your study permit or restore your status: how to apply
*To request/access transcripts via your myBCIT account, follow this pathway: Student Self Service > Transcript – unofficial and request official
## Provincial attestation letters (PALs) and study permit extensions
## PAL requirement
All study permit applications including extensions require a document to be uploaded in order to satisfy IRCC’s provincial attestation letter (PAL) requirements. The document to upload would either be a PAL (if required) or an explanation letter stating that you meet one of IRCC’s PAL exceptions such as the following:
“You’ve entered Canada and you’re applying for a study permit extension at the same DLI and level of study as your current study permit.”
Note that you must physically be inside of Canada when submitting the application in order to meet the exception above. Refer to IRCC’s provincial attestation letter page for a full list of PAL exceptions.
If you are a continuing BCIT student and believe you require a PAL to support your study permit extension, you may email isc@bcit.ca to request a PAL (include your BCIT student ID number). PALs are limited and will be issued subject to availability.
Note that you may have to give your biometrics (fingerprints and photo) and pay the biometrics fee when you submit your application. If you don’t, there may be delays.
After you apply, IRCC will issue a submission confirmation letter confirming they have received your study permit extension application. Email this letter to statusdoc@bcit.ca along with your student ID number so that we can update your BCIT student account accordingly. You will also need to submit proof of your new study permit once you receive it.
When renewing your study permit, it is also important to remember to update or renew the following:
- Medical Services Plan (MSP)
- Social Insurance Number (SIN)
- Visitor visa (if you require one)

If you apply for an extension before your study permit expires, you may have maintained status . This means that you can stay in Canada under the same conditions as your current study permit until IRCC make a decision on your application.
The previous study permit would need to be linked to BCIT in order for you to be authorized to study at BCIT while on maintained status.

Understand that you must remain in Canada to continue to have maintained status. If you leave the country you may not resume study in Canada until you receive your new study permit.
If your study permit has expired, you need to:
- Stop your studies immediately
- Email isc@bcit.ca to notify the International Student Centre as soon as possible
- Apply to restore your status within 90 days of losing your status.
See Work Eligibility to understand if your study permit allows you to work during your studies.
You’ll need to apply for a new study permit from within Canada .
When you apply online, your personalized checklist will include the Application to Change Conditions, Extend my Stay or Remain in Canada as a Student [IMM 5709]. On that form, check the box to Apply for a study permit for the first time or extend my study permit and fill out the rest of the required fields.
Include a letter with your application explaining why you need the condition removed from your study permit.
In order to get a copy of a lost, stolen or destroyed study permit you must fill out an application and pay a fee to replace the permit.
Your study permit will become invalid on the earliest of the following:
- the expiry date marked on the permit,
- 90 days after the date of your final exam/course (if you successfully complete a program), or
- the day on which you are no longer enrolled at BCIT if you withdraw or are dropped from a program.
To remain in Canada, you would need to apply for a new status such as another study permit, work permit, or a visitor record.
It’s always best to review your study permit immediately when you receive it at the port of entry. If you discover later that there is an error such as your name being misspelled, you can apply to amend your study permit . If an error was made my the officer who issued the permit, then there is no fee.
If you are an international student under 19 you might need to show proof that you have a custodian. A custodian is a responsible adult, who is a Canadian citizen or a permanent resident, who will take care of and support the minor child. A custodian is optional for minors 17 years of age and older, but an officer can request one on a case-by-case basis.
See Studying in Canada as a minor and Study permits: Guidelines on minor children on the IRCC website for more information.
