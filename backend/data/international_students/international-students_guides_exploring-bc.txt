## Exploring BC Guide
## Acknowledgement of First Nations' Territory
We respectfully acknowledge that Indigenous peoples have lived in the lands that make up present-day British Columbia for countless generations.
We encourage you to learn more about Indigenous cultures and support Indigenous businesses on your travels around BC. Visit Indigenous Tourism BC for more information.
##
This guide is intended to help international students at BCIT safely enjoy some of the beautiful sites and exciting activities that British Columbia has to offer.
Browse through some exciting travel ideas at HelloBC and more practical information at Traveling in BC .
### Whistler
An all-season mountain resort less than 2 hours from Vancouver featuring world class skiing, mountain biking, hiking, and much more!
### Victoria
The capital city of BC known for whale watching, a thriving farm-to-table food scene, and low-key European vibes.
### Squamish
Less than 1 hour from Vancouver, this small city is known as "The Outdoor Recreation Capital of Canada" for good reason!
### Kelowna
The largest city in BC's interior located next to beautiful Okanagan Lake in the heart of vineyard and orchard country.
### Tofino
A bustling small town on the wild west coast of Vancouver island known for it's surf scene and proximity to Pacific Rim National Park.
### Harrison Hot Springs
A popular spot among locals known for it's hot springs and pleasant beachfront on Harrison Lake.
### Sunshine Coast
A region featuring maritime villages and plenty of outdoor adventure opportunities just a short ferry ride away from Metro Vancouver.
### Southern Gulf Islands
A series of smaller islands in the Salish Sea accessible by ferry and known for relaxed vibes, fresh seafood, and a creative sense of community spirit.
### Rocky Mountains
Spanning the border of BC and neighbouring Alberta, the Canadian Rockies is one of the most beautiful places on planet Earth!

## Getting around
A major consideration when planning a trip in BC is whether or not you may have access to your own car. Having your own wheels opens up endless opportunities to venture further out from the urban centres into nature. A car can be brought on BC Ferry routes for island adventures.
Without having vehicle access, it’s best to either use a tour company or to visit cities with local public transportation and a walkable downtown core such as Victoria, Whistler, Squamish, Tofino, or Kelowna.
See below for information about various modes of transportation.
If you want to sit back and enjoy a trip while letting someone else worry about transportation logistics, consider booking a tour from a local company such as:
- West Trek Tours
- Moose Travel Network
- Discover Canada Tours
BC Ferries provides maritime transportation between the mainland, Vancouver Island, and other smaller islands. On a nice day, the views from top deck are incredible! Both the Tsawwassen and Horseshoe Bay ferry terminals serve the Metro Vancouver area and can be accessed by public transportation on Translink . Destinations include Victoria (via Swartz Bay terminal), Nanaimo (for connection to Tofino Express Bus ), the Sunshine Coast, Bowen Island, and the Gulf Islands.
Travelers heading to Victoria should be aware that while the Swartz Bay terminal is well outside of the city the #70 Express bus makes for an easy connection to downtown Victoria. Note that Victoria public transit is separate from Translink so BCIT student U-passes are not accepted.
Several companies offer bus services between various cities in the province, including:
- Squamish Connector
- Epic Rides (Whistler)
- Snowbus (Whistler, ski-focused)
- Vancouver Island Connector (Victoria, Tofino, Nanaimo – only)
- BC Ferries Connector (Victoria – convenient alternative to using public transportation)
- IslandLink Bus ( Nanaimo , Victoria, Tofino)
- Ebus (Kelowna, Kamloops)
- Rider Express (Rocky Mountains – Banff, Lake Louise)
Travel by rail can be a rewarding way to take in the stunning natural beauty of BC. Both companies below offer access to panoramic viewing cars.
- VIA Rail (Rocky Mountains, or onwards to eastern Canada)
- Rocky Mountaineer (luxury rail service to the Rocky Mountains)
For information about public transportation systems in cities outside of Metro Vancouver, visit BC Transit and enter the city name.
Note that BCIT student U-passes are only valid on the Translink system in Metro Vancouver.
Driving can be a great way to explore the province of BC. If your are new here, make sure you have a valid drivers license, auto insurance, and that you know the rules of the road.
Be aware that BC has many winding mountain roads and that hazards such as snow, fog, heavy rain, and landslides can complicate even a well-planned road trip. To check up-to-date driving conditions and road closures, visit DriveBC .
See our Navigating Metro Vancouver Guide for information about driving in BC.
## Outdoor adventure safety tips
Adventurers travel from around the world for a chance to experience world-class skiing, biking, hiking, camping, and other adventure sports in BC. While the rewards can be great, there are risks to be aware of whenever venturing into the wilderness or engaging in any adventure sport.
The tips below can help you to plan for a safer outdoor adventure.
### 1. Do your research
Research in advance so that you are aware of any risks you may encounter and so that you have a plan to minimize any potential danger. Many wilderness areas – including some even within Metro Vancouver – have no cell phone signal. Prepare a paper copy of any maps, hiking trail descriptions, etc. that you may need to refer to. Check the forecast before you go and always be prepared for the weather to change. Vancouver Trails is a great resource for planning local hiking trips.
### 2. Prep your gear
As the age-old proverb goes, “Prepare for the worst, hope for the best”. It’s always a good idea to bring plenty of extra food and water, layers of clothing that can protect you from the elements, and emergency supplies such as a first aid kit. Mountain Equipment Company’s packing lists are an excellent resource.
### 3. Share your plan
Bringing a buddy can make your adventure safer, but it’s important when heading onto a serious hiking trail to share your plan (including when you plan to return) with someone NOT joining you. That way if you run into trouble and are not able to check in with them later, they notify emergency services and help a search and rescue team locate you.
### 4. Mind the wildlife
Co-existing with bears, coyotes, and other wildlife is a reality of life in BC. You can try to avoid animal encounters by minimizing animal attractants (such as food) and making noise such as talking or clapping your hands on a hiking trail. Carrying bear spray and knowing how to use it is a good idea just in case. If you do encounter any kind of animal in the wild, make sure to give it plenty of space. Review BC Parks: Staying Safe in Bear Country if you plan to go camping.
