STARTING A CLUB – OVERVIEW

Starting a club is a simple 4-step process.
The Club Program Coordinator is available (virtually or in-person) to support you.
If you have questions, you can also consult the FAQ section.

4 STEPS TO STARTING A CLUB

Submit Application

Complete the “Sanctioning Application for Clubs” form.

Submit the completed package to the Club Program Coordinator at: clubs@bcitsa.ca

The application will be reviewed with the VP Student Experience.

Present at Clubs Committee

You will be invited to a Clubs Committee meeting.

You give a brief (~5 minutes) presentation about your club and its general operations.

Committee members may ask questions or request further information.

Committee and Council Decision

The Clubs Committee votes on whether to recommend the club for sanctioning under BCITSA.

This recommendation is communicated to Student Council.

Student Council votes to approve or deny the sanctioning of the club.

Notification and Orientation

You will be notified of the decision by the Club Program Coordinator.

If approved, an orientation session will be scheduled with the Club Program Coordinator.

FAQ – STARTING & RUNNING CLUBS

Q: How long does it take to process the application form?
A: After applying, it takes around one month for the sanctioning decision to be made.

Q: What are the benefits of starting a club?
A: Depending on club type, sanctioned clubs may receive:

Free room booking at the Student Association building

Access to event funding

Free equipment rentals

Other resources and supports

Potential financial support to attend competitions in other provinces/countries

Access to BCITSA Career Specialists for sponsorship advice, resume help, and other support

Q: What are the three classifications of clubs on the application form?
A: There are three classifications:

General Club:
• Has full rights and responsibilities of a club
• Full access to all club resources
• Must complete the General Club sections of the sanctioning form

Casual Club:
• Less regimented; fewer responsibilities
• Limited access to club resources
• No access to funding of any kind

Designated Club:
• Bears the name of and/or supports policies of a political party, religious organization, or similar
• Normally has rights and responsibilities similar to a General Club
• No access to funding of any kind

Q: What information is needed for my club sanctioning application?

General and Designated Clubs – the form must collect at least:

Club Name

Club Acronym/Initialism (if applicable)

Club classification (General, Designated, etc.)

Primary contact information

List of club executives

List of at least 10 current student members

Club Constitution

Casual Clubs – the form must collect at least:

Club Name

Statement of Intent

Primary contact information

List of at least 5 current student members

Q: Can I switch my club’s classification after it is sanctioned?
A: Yes.

The club must go through the sanctioning process again for Council approval.

You may reuse the same application if the members listed are still current students.

RESUME GUIDE – USING CLUB EXPERIENCE

Purpose

Shows how including club achievements on a resume can demonstrate skills and abilities, especially when professional experience is limited.

How to Get Started

Identify skills and values relevant to the employer (via job description and networking).

Look for emphasized skills such as communication, interpersonal skills, time management, etc.

Research the company and role to decide which club experiences to highlight.

Which Club Experiences to Include

Reflect on what you have done in clubs and how it prepared you for the role.

As a Club Executive, you may demonstrate:

Leadership

Communication

Initiative

Critical thinking

Teamwork & collaboration

Conflict management

Organization

Decision making

Time management

Event planning

Where to Include Club Experience on Your Resume

Experience Section:
If your club role is substantial and comparable to a part-time job, especially if highly relevant to the role.

Extracurricular Activities Section:
If you have multiple clubs/activities and they reflect your initiative, leadership, and professional development.

Education Section:
If the activity is closely related to your academic program or led to a certificate/training.

Questions to Ask Yourself

How much time did I dedicate and what transferable skills did I gain?

Have I been involved in multiple volunteer roles?

Is this club role related to my academic program or certifications earned?

How to List Club Experiences

Treat club experiences like work experience:

Include role/position, organization name, location, and dates.

Use bullet points with accomplishment statements.

Describe responsibilities, skills developed, and specific achievements.

Examples are provided on the page (e.g., Relevant Club Experience, Extracurricular Experience, Education and Training, Case Competitions).
