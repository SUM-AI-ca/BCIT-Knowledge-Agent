## Transitioning from Secondary

Transition from secondary school to post-secondary is a big change. BCIT’s academic environment has unique characteristics that set it apart from your previous experiences in high school.

Review the following content to understand what to expect and being prepared:

## Academic demands

- A full-time workload at BCIT is very demanding, typically consisting of six to eight comprehensive courses per term.
- Instructions are often lecture-style, with a significant lab component in some courses.
- Many assignments involve working with in groups and forming team skills in preparation for future employment.

## 

- Instructors provide less supervision and individual feedback than secondary school professors.
- You will need to be able to effectively use the library.
- You will need to take greater responsibility for your own success and actively seek the student support that will help you improve your success.

## Classroom Dynamics

- Classes can be larger than those in secondary schools.
- Instructors may not monitor your progress as closely as in secondary school.
- Grades are often based on just two or three test scores, making attendance critical to success.

## Accommodations for Equitable Learning

- Students are responsible for connecting with Accessibility Services to assist with setting up accommodations and supports.
- Accommodations may not be the same as those you have had in secondary school.
- Accommodations are designed to level the playing field, not guarantee success. It’s up to you whether or not to utilize approved accommodations.

Understanding these differences will help you to be well-prepared for this new educational experience.
