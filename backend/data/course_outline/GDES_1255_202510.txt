## Course Description
​Effective communication design projects elicit an emotional response from their target audience. To do this, text, image, and carefully chosen graphic elements bond seamlessly to direct the viewer's eye through the layout in a particular way. Students will analyze how Gestalt psychology establishes the hierarchical relationship between design elements in a composition. Examining historic design genres will provide insight into setting the stage for time and place. Applying knowledge of signs and symbols and manipulating content via design principles, students gain insight into complex visual systems. The outcome is a unique visual language, translating an idea into a visual form that clearly communicates a message to the target audience. ​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Use terminology relating to visual form to engage in design discussion.
- Demonstrate the use of Gestalt theory (similarity, proximity, alignment) in a visual communication.
- Use design essentials (such as balance, unity, tension, hierarchy, etc) to reinforce the message and meaning in a composition.
- Choose imagery, including symbols and signs, that strengthens the key message beyond its elemental context.
- Apply a specific design genre (Art Deco, Futurism, etc) to capture a sense of time, place and ideology.
- Apply colour psychology to build impact and enhance the meaning of a message.
- Use colour to affect a sense of space in a composition.
- Demonstrate how compositional principles can be applied to elicit an emotion.
- Investigate colour relationships as a means of strengthening a message.​

## Course Schedule and Assignments
Week: Lesson topics: Assignment
1: Student & instructor Intros Course outline BCIT Policies and Course Expectations What is Visual Language? Why is it important? Visual Literacy + Terminology: In-class Exercise: Students work in groups and discuss design elements to communicate meaning. Assignment 1 - Create a mood board with assigned words (due on week 2 - 18th Jan)
2: Design Genres: A tour through design history Understanding the origin, development and influence of different design genres throughout history. Presentation for Assignment 1: In-class Exercise: Students work in pairs and select two designers from the era (movement) assigned to their group. Assignment 2 - Create a 3-minute presentation. Use two established designers from the in-class exercise. Present the designers' info and their particular use of visual language. (due week 4 - 1st Feb)
3: Introduction to Gestalt theory and elements of visual structure. - Gestalt Principles - Showcase examples - Importance of using Gestalt Principles: In-class Exercise: Students will work in groups, choose a word, and explore how Gestalt theory might approach the concept of that word. Assignment 3 - Create a design using Gestalt Principles (due on week 6 - 22ns Feb)
4: Visual language and storytelling How to choose the right image to strengthen the key message beyond its elemental context Presentation for Assignment 2: In-class Discussion: visual storytelling
5: Introduction to Semiotics: In-class Exercise: Students to work individually or in pairs to practice the theory of semiotics
6: The power of Concepts and the importance of the message Great design is powered not only by beautiful aesthetics but also driven by a strong message supported by a strong concept Presentation for Assignment 3: In-class Exercise: Concept and message development Assignment 4 - Magazine ad for a product to be designed in the style of a chosen era. Thumbnails and roughs. Students are encouraged to create their ads the way they were done during their era, which means, no computers - at least from the start. (due on week 8 - 8th Mar)
7: Colour + Compositions: how these affect our emotions, the message and the sense of space in any visual materials: In-class Exercise: Concept and message development for assignment 4, refined. Development of comprehensive thumbnails and test images
8: Presentation for Assignment 4: Final Assignment - Develop a proposal with a proposed message, three concepts, and a mood board. Deliver a professionally designed mock-up for the final presentation. (due on week 11 - 29th Mar)
9: Visual language for visual creation and problem-solving - Reducing Realism: In-class Exercise: Students will present their work process to each other for feedback.
10: Connecting with the audience through emotions Lab work time: In-class Exercise: Students will present their work processes to each other for feedback and finalize the direction for the final assignment.
11: Final Presentation: Each student will have 3-5 minutes to deliver their final presentation
