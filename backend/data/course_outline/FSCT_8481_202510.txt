## Course Description
This course will introduce you to the various structured analytical techniques that are commonly used throughout the Crime Analysis Cycle as it is applied in Canadian violent crime investigations. You will learn the best practices in analytical software, techniques, and methods used by analysts in major case management for violent crimes, such as homicide, serious assaults, sex offences, extortion, kidnapping, and missing persons. You will also learn how to collect and evaluate publicly available information when completing analytical tasks for crimes of violence.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Apply critical-thinking skills to assess the relevance, reliability, and validity of data collected during violent crime investigations.
- Use GIS software to prepare crime maps for violent crimes analysis, using temporal and spatial data.
- Analyze a variety of sources to develop offender profiles and timelines.
- Identify crime patterns and similar fact analysis in relation to a violent crime, such as analyzing witness statements and open source information.
- Prepare a threat assessment related to an individual’s propensity for violence.
- Complete an intermediate-level communications analysis, which includes phone records and cell tower information.
- Create an intermediate-level link association, timeline, or event flow, and commodity flow charts for violent crime investigation.
- Design and prepare an intermediate tactical crime analysis bulletin related to violent crime.
- Prepare a report and then give a verbal presentation of your analysis that could be used as court testimony.

## Learning Resources
i2 Analyst's Notebook, ESRI ArcGIS Pro, ArcGIS for Excel. Additional readings provided by instructor.

## Course Goals
Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to: • Discuss the different types of investigative techniques associated with major crime investigations. • Describe the role of an analyst working in a major case management (MCM) setting. • Conduct a range of analytic techniques including suspect identification, tracker analysis, communication analysis, cell dump analysis, association analysis, and timeline. • Recommend investigation avenues with sound analytical findings. • Prepare effective analytical products and present findings in a logical and organized manner. • Apply critical thinking to solve challenges associated with analyzing imperfect data. • Describe the requirements of disclosure provisions and the stages of a court process. • Discuss the role of the analyst in post-case evaluations and de-briefing.

## Other information
Assignment 1:

Student will be asked to create a comprehensive background profile using information of themselves.

Students are tasked with creating a document that summarizes everything known about a suspect (namely the students themselves). Students may use made-up data or modify real data from at least 15 sources of data as discussed in class.

Grading is based on the student’s ability to create an aesthetically pleasing document that provides information pertaining to suspect to the investigating team. Data accuracy will not be evaluated

This assignment is worth 20 points (10% of Assignment portion):

- 6 points – Organization and Presentation
- 6 points – Content and Sources
- 3 points - Sourcing and Mechanics

Assignment 2:

Student will be asked to create a comprehensive tracker analysis report pertaining to the class case study.

Students are given a set of GPS tracker data. Using the techniques discussed in class, students are asked to analyze the tracker data and submit a minimal 700-word report in a format discussed in class: background, methodology, Analysis, Conclusions, and Recommendations.

Grading is based on the student’s ability to analyze the tracker data using the appropriate techniques and synthesize the findings with information known about the case study thus far.

This assignment is worth 20 points (30% of Assignment portion):

- 16 points - Purpose and organization (written portion)
- 10 points - Effective use of visual to illustrate your findings
- 4 points - Syntax and Mechanics including sourcing

Assignment 3:

Student will be asked to create a comprehensive a call detail records analysis report pertaining to the class case study.

Students are given a set of call detail records pertaining to one of the suspects in the class case study. Using the techniques discussed in class, students are asked to analyze the records data and submit a minimal 1500-word report in a format discussed in class: background, methodology, Analysis, Conclusions, and Recommendations.

Grading is based on the student’s ability to analyze the call records data using the appropriate techniques and synthesize the findings with information known about the case study thus far.

This assignment is worth 30 points (40% of Assignment portion):

- 16 points - Purpose and organization (written portion)
- 10 points - Effective use of visual to illustrate your findings
- 4 points - Syntax and Mechanics including sourcing

Assignment 4:

Student will be asked to create a comprehensive timeline of the class case study.

Students are to create a visual product that effectively summarizes all relevant information and evidence for the class case study. This may include materials provided during class and supplemental documents in the weekly readings.

Grading is based on the student’s ability to communicate information in a concise manner using a visual timeline.

This assignment is worth 30 points (20% of Assignment portion):

- 12 points - Purpose and Content
- 12 points – Visual
- 6 points – Sourcing and Mechanics

Group Project:

Students will be assigned into one of four real case studies. Selected information pertaining to each case will be given to each group week by week. The task is to compile and analyze the investigative materials provided as a group and present a 5–10-minute presentation in Day 2, 3, 4, and 5.

Every member of the group must contribute to the final product and participate in the oral presentation. Each presentation will include a review of the case, a compilation of the investigative materials provided, and between 1 and 3 products derived from the analysis of the materials.

Each presentation is worth 10 marks:

- 6 points - Purpose, Content, and Accuracy
- 4 points - Oral presentation

Final Exam:

Students will be given 90 minutes to complete 6 scenario-based questions. The purpose of each question is to assess the student’s ability to logically examine different types of data sets in different situations leading to the determination of the appropriate analytical technique to be applied.

The exam is worth 45 marks.
