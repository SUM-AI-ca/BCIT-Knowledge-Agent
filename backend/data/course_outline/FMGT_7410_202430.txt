## Course Description
This is an advanced course in corporate income tax, focusing on the tax planning needs of private corporations and their shareholders. In this course, the student will acquire the foundation tax knowledge and basic research skills needed to be able to do corporate tax- related work at a professional level. This is achieved through a combination of lectures, assigned readings, practice in the application of the law to practical problems, the use of income tax software for both compliance and research purposes, and training in tax research and tax writing. Topics include the computation of corporate taxes, tax integration, owner compensation, corporate re-organizations, surplus distributions, partnerships, trusts, structuring business sales and acquisitions, international business expansion, as well as issues concerning tax compliance, planning and avoidance.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Identify tax issues faced by closely held corporations and their owners.
- Use the Income Tax Act, and professional tax research tools, to determine the tax implications of the issues identified.
- Communicate the issues, the tax implications, the alternatives and recommendations.
- Use T2 software to file corporate tax returns.
- Review, advise on, and provide support for objections, appeals, and reassessments.
- Analyze and advise on tax-planning issues such as the purchase or sale of shares or assets, benefiting from the capital gains deduction on qualified small business corporation shares, succession planning, and reorganizations.
- Evaluate and advise on tax implications of alternative business decisions such as dividend versus salary, sharing small business deductions, personal service business, and acquisition of control.
- Design the use of partnerships and trusts in business and family tax planning.

## Learning Resources
- Canadian Income Tax Planning and Decision Making 2024-2025 Edition, authored by Buckwold & Kitunen and published by McGraw-Hill Ryerson, is the required textbook for the course. It is important for students to ensure that they purchase the latest edition of the textbook, as earlier versions will not be suitable for the course. Additionally, students should verify that the textbook they purchase includes an access code to the McGraw Hill Connect website for the textbook, as this access will be necessary for completing assigned tasks. The hard-copy textbook, which includes access to Connect, is priced at approximately $177 and can be obtained from the bookstore.
- Students should be aware that they have the option to buy access to the Connect website for the textbook, which includes an electronic version of the text, without having to purchase a physical copy. The standalone access to the Connect website, which includes the e-version of the text, is priced at around $112. The instructor will provide an online link where students can directly purchase access to the Connect website from Connect online. This link will be sent by email to all registered students prior to the first class.
- If a student decides they would like to have a physical copy of the text after purchasing stand-alone access to the Connect website, the publisher offers a $35 loose-leaf version of the textbook that can be purchased online directly from the Connect website.
- The Practitioner’s Income Tax Act, 66th Edition, edited by David M. Sherman, Thomson Carswell (referred to as the PITA), is required for this course. You can find this edition at the bookstore. While you may use any relatively current edition of the Practitioner’s Income Tax Act, it is advisable not to use anything older than the 64th edition. Please note that if you choose to use an older version of the PITA, there will be occasions where you will need to refer to online versions of the ITA to access the most recent versions of the law. Additionally, it is important to be aware that relying solely on an online version of the ITA may not always be sufficient for this course, as we frequently refer to Sherman’s annotations while we are studying the various provisions, and these annotations are only available in the PITA. Finally, be aware that you are not permitted to use an online version of the ITA during quizzes, but you are permitted to refer to hard copy versions of the PITA during such quizzes.
- Students will have complimentary access to the Canadian Tax Foundation's TaxFind Online database. This extensive database allows for easy searching of all Canadian Tax Foundation publications from 1991 to 2024. The Canadian Tax Foundation is widely recognized as the leading authority in Canada for scholarly tax research.
- Students will also have free access to the online database Federal Income Tax Collection Platinum Student Edition (FITAC Platinum SE) provided by Knotia, owned by CPA Canada, on the BCIT Library website. This database offers a variety of online resources that are valuable for conducting professional tax research. These resources include Department of Finance Explanatory Notes, Ernst & Young's commentary on the ITA, CRA Interpretation Rulings, Court rulings, as well as the E&Y Guide to Tax Research and Writing.

## Course topics
Conduct of the Course

In Fall 2024, this course will be conducted primarily in a face-to-face format. However, throughout the course we will rely on various online resources, and there will be weekly Readiness Assessment Tests (RATs) that will be conducted online on the Connect website for the textbook. Study Notes and additional study materials will be distributed through the Learning Hub (D2L). When assignment submissions are required, students will generally be instructed to submit them to D2L. Additionally, instructor-produced videos on specific topics not covered in class may be posted on D2L from time to time.

The course employs a partially flipped classroom approach, where lectures are not the primary method of instruction. Instead, students are required to study assigned readings and complete problems before attending class. Class time will be dedicated to clarifying and enhancing students' comprehension of the material. This will be accomplished through discussion with the instructor and fellow classmates, reviewing the assigned readings and problems, and participating in group quizzes.

The first 10 to 15 minutes of each class will consist of a brief overview of the assigned work for the following week. Please note that this overview will not be a comprehensive lecture on the new material to be learned. Instead, it will provide guidance on what students are expected to study before the next class, along with helpful tips to facilitate their learning process.

Following this introduction, the next 15 minutes will be dedicated to reviewing the previous week's group quiz and the RAT (Readiness Assurance Test) completed before class. Subsequently, the remaining 130 minutes will be a lab/tutorial session focused on discussing and reviewing the readings and problems that students were expected to have worked on prior to attending class.

Afterward, in the final 60 minutes of the class, students will break into their groups and do a group quiz based on the material that was just reviewed.

Two 10-minute breaks will be provided during the class to allow students to refresh themselves.

Students are required to have immediate access to the Income Tax Act during all classes. Since we will frequently refer to the wording of provisions and the annotations in the ITA during class discussions, not having the ability to access it may cause you to miss out on important learning opportunities. Also, not having access to the ITA will be a handicap when doing Group Quizzes that require you to find provisions in the ITA. Therefore, students who fail to bring their ITA to class on 2 or more occasions throughout the term will be penalized 1% of their final course grade per subsequent incident.

Study notes: Weekly study notes will be posted on The Learning Hub prior to each class. These notes will outline the learning objectives, and list the assigned readings and the textbook's Key Concept Questions and Problems to be studied for the upcoming week. Often, to assist you with the assigned readings and problems, solutions to some of the assigned problems will be provided along with the Study Notes on D2L. Solutions to the end-of-chapter Review Questions and Key Concept Questions from the textbook can be found on the Connect website. Generally, solutions to assignments that are specifically skill building exercises will not be posted.

Assignment Completion: Students are expected to complete the assigned readings and related assignment problems and should come to class prepared to discuss their work. To effectively learn the material in the course, students must understand how the ITA rules apply to the various scenarios presented in the Key Concept Questions and Assignment Problems assigned each week. This understanding relies on active engagement from students in studying the material and seeking help on topics that require clarification. If a student struggles to comprehend the solution to a problem during class, they are encouraged to ask questions and seek further assistance during office hours or by scheduling Zoom or face-to-face meetings with the instructor.

Readiness Assessment Tests (RATs): Every week, students are required to complete a Readiness Assessment Test (RAT) on Connect before midnight the day before their scheduled class. These quizzes are designed to assess the student's preparedness for the upcoming lecture/tutorial session and group quiz. Out of the 12 RATs scheduled for the term, only the best 8 will be counted for credit. These 8 RATs will make up 15% of the final course grade.

The quizzes will be accessible to students 48 hours in advance of their due time. Each student is allowed one attempt at the RAT, and it must be completed within 60 minutes of having started it. While the RATs are open book quizzes, students should be mindful of time constraints if they haven't adequately prepared beforehand.

It is important to reiterate that the RAT is an individual quiz, and students are expected to complete it using their own knowledge and without any assistance from others. Obtaining help in doing the RATs will be considered cheating.

Group Quizzes: The weekly quizzes at the end of each class will be conducted in groups, and the marks earned on the 8 highest-scoring quizzes out of the 12 written during the term will make up 30% of the student's final course grade. These quizzes are most accurately viewed as topic reviews aimed at helping group members better understand the material studied.

At the beginning of the course, the instructor will randomly assign students to groups consisting of 4 to 5 individuals. This group will remain the same throughout the term. Every week in class, after reviewing the assigned homework from the previous week, each group will work together to complete a group answer sheet, which will then be submitted to the instructor at the end of the quiz.

There will be an additional incentive for the groups that perform exceptionally well on the quizzes. Each week, the group with the highest mark will receive a bonus of 10%, while the group with the second highest mark will receive a 5% bonus. In the event of a tie for the highest mark, each tied group will receive a 10% bonus, but there will be no award for the second highest mark.

It's important to note that each individual within the group will earn the same mark on the quiz as the group. For the 30% of the final grade based on the group quiz marks, only the average of the best 8 out of the 12 quizzes attended by the student during the term will be considered. Students who are absent, or for any other reason, do not participate in a quiz will be awarded zero on the missed quiz.

The group quizzes will be closed book, except for access to the PITA which is allowed. During the quiz, students may ask the instructor for occasional hints. Because quiz groups are competing for bonus marks awarded to the group with the highest and second highest mark on the quiz, students could be damaging their own eligibility for bonus marks if they share their answers on quizzes with other groups.

The best way to prepare for these quizzes, in order to contribute to your group's success, is to complete the weekly readings and assignments. It is also important to ask questions and clarify any uncertainties before the quiz, while the homework is being reviewed in class. Additionally, it is recommended students review the solutions to the quizzes once they are posted, to identify any topics that need further review. This weekly review is valuable because 20% of the questions on each subsequent week's quiz will be based on material that appeared on previous quizzes. To aid with this review, the solutions to the quizzes will be available on D2L for further study purposes.

Peer Evaluation of Group Quiz Participation: The peer evaluation process at the end of the term aims to recognize students who have made an exceptional contribution to the success of their group, as assessed by their peers. The peer evaluation process works as follows: at the end of term, the instructor will distribute to each student a peer review form and a summary of the attendance and performance of their group during the term. Each group member then completes the Peer Evaluation form, evaluating every member of the group, other than themselves, by assigning points that reflect their contribution to the group's success. In a group with 5 members, each student will have 40 points to distribute among the other members. An average performance would warrant an allocation of 10 points. For above-average performance, an allocation of up to 13 points is allowed; for below-average performance, an allocation as low as 7 points is permitted. The peer review process requires that each group member identify at least one member who performed better than average by awarding them a minimum of 11 points, and at least one member who performed worse than average by awarding them no more than 9 points. This requirement ensures that group members genuinely evaluate each other and avoid allocating the same mark to everyone.

The average peer review rating that each student receives from their group's members will make up 5% of the course grade. The algorithm that will be used to compute the course grade based on the average peer review points awarded in the following:

[5% + (average peer review points awarded) - 10].

For example, if your group members award you, on average, the maximum of 13 points, you will earn 8% (i.e. 5 + (13 - 10) = 8) in respect of the 5% of course grade that is dependent upon the Peer Review. If your group members award you, on average, the minimum of 7 points, you will only earn 2% (i.e. 5 + (7 - 10) = 2) in respect of the 5% of course grade that is dependent upon the Peer Review.

T2 Tax Return Assignment: Students will learn how to use T2 ProFile professional tax preparation software and will do a tax return for a corporation based on a case that will be assigned. Students may assist each other in learning the software and analyzing the case, but each student must do their own data entry work on this assignment, and from a data entry perspective the submission must represent the student’s own work. The T2 Tax Return Assignment will be worth 10% of the final grade.

Tax Research Memo Assignments: Students will complete three Tax Research Memorandum (TRM) assignments throughout the term. These assignments will involve analyzing tax issues in an assigned case and preparing a written submission of findings and recommendations. The TRMs must adhere to a strict formatting convention known as the FIRAC format, which stands for Facts (F), Issues (I), Rules (R), Application/Analysis (A), and Conclusion (C). You will receive instruction on how to use this format.

The first two assignments will be completed in teams of two, with each team member earning the same grade on their joint submission. Each of these assignments will be worth 10% of the course grade. The third tax research memo assignment will be an individual assignment and will account for 20% of the course grade. This final tax research memo will serve as a substitute for a final exam.

Unlike the formation of the groups for the group quizzes, which are randomly determined by the instructor, the two-person teams for the TRMs may be self-selected by students who want to work together. Students may notify the instructor, on or before the third class of the term, identifying who they are going to team up with to do the first two TRMs. Students who do not notify the instructor as to who they are going to work with will be randomly assigned by the instructor to a team that they will be expected to work with. Once the teams for the TRM assignments have been determined, changes in the teams will be permitted only with the prior approval of the instructor.

Optional Bonus Mark Assignments: From time to time during the term, depending upon how the course workload is unfolding, the instructor will assign four or five optional bonus mark assignments on which students can earn up to 2 bonus marks (on course grade) on each. Submissions on these assignments will be strictly optional and will be due on or before the day (at 11:59 pm) before the next class following the assignment’s posting. The marking on these assignments is generally up to 1 mark for effort, and up to 1 mark for content. These bonus mark assignments will generally require a one- or two-page written submission of an answer to a problem that has been assigned for study as part of the study material for the particular week. Since the problem that is the subject of the bonus mark assignment needs to be studied regardless of whether or not a written submission is made, it is usually to the advantage for the student to take a bit more time to make a written submission. However, there is no penalty for not making a submission on an optional bonus mark assignment. These optional bonus mark assignments will be individual assignments (unless instructed otherwise) and submissions are expected to reflect the work of the student without the aid of anyone else.
