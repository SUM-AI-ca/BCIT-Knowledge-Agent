### Course Description
​This course introduces students to the fundamental techniques and tools for creating dynamic animations and visual effects. Students will learn to animate vector and raster artwork, utilize text and typography effectively, and apply principles of pacing and timing. The curriculum covers key techniques such as masking, compositing, color correction, and animating layers in both 2D and 3D spaces. Additionally, the course explores the integration of sound design, the creation of interactive animations, and the use of emerging technologies. Students will develop skills in storyboarding and project management, preparing them to execute and deliver motion graphics projects across various platforms, including web, social media, and broadcast. ​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Create and animate vector artwork and shapes, integrating typography and pacing principles to enhance visual storytelling and communication.
- Utilize masking, compositing, and color correction techniques to create polished animations with special effects and enhanced visual appeal.
- Animate layers in 2D and 3D spaces, incorporating creative visual effects and using the principles of timing and motion to produce dynamic sequences.
- Demonstrate proficiency in industry-standard motion graphics software, incorporating emerging technologies to create innovative experiences.
- Develop strong conceptual and storyboard skills, planning and presenting motion graphics ideas with a focus on user engagement and interactive elements.
- Apply project management principles to plan, execute, and deliver motion graphics projects, ensuring timelines and quality standards are met.
- Utilize sound design and synchronization techniques to complement visual elements and enhance storytelling.
- Optimize animations for various platforms, considering file formats, resolutions, and export workflows for web, social media, and broadcast.

### Course Schedule and Assignments
Note: All assignments are due a week from the day they are assigned.
Week # | Topics | Assessments
Week 1 | Introduction to Motion Graphics. Program’s interface and important shortcuts. Importing footage items. Creating compositions. Steps for creating Animation and preview. Quick overview of the Adobe Media Encoder. | Assignment 1- Basic Animation: Take few images (5 to 8) about any topic, import them to a new project and animate them in different styles, one after the other, in the same composition, then render it.
Week 2 | Editing Animation: - Copying, pasting, reversing and stretching keyframes. - Editing a keyframe’s timing or value. - Saving Animation presets. Understanding Transform values. Layer switches versus properties: - Motion Blur, 3D layers and continuous rasterization switches. | Assignment 2- Public service announcement (draft one): Utilize images, videos, solids, text and shape layers, background music, and voice-over files in producing a Public Service Announcement, 30 to 45 seconds in duration.
Week 3 | Working with the Graph Editor: - Editing Value versus Speed graphs. Using Solid and Type layers. Introduction to Shape layers (part 1): - Fill, stroke and trim paths. | Assignment 2- Public service announcement (Final draft): Utilize the topics taught in the past 3 weeks to improve the quality, style, and pacing of this animation.
Week 4 | Shape layers (part 2): - Merge Paths, Repeaters and their applications. Parenting. Expressions: - Wiggle, LoopOut, ValueAtTime and their applications. | Assignment 3- Utilize shape layers in animating a bar (or line) graph (based on real data available online).
Week 5 | Importing Graphics from Illustrator: - Organizing layers in illustrator. - Import as footage (merge / choose layer) or comp. Shape layers (part 3): - Morphing one path into another. - Wiggle Transform and Wiggle Paths. Visualizing directions or measurements: - Movements, Air or liquid flow, temp changes, weight or volume. | Assignment 4- Utilize shape layers (and optionally imported illustrator graphics) in creating an animation representing weight measurements such as 1 Pound, 500 grams or 2 cups.
Week 6 | Video playback control: - Adjusting playback speed, freezing at frames, time remapping. - Applying basic color corrections to videos. - Adding directions on top of videos. Explaining the scope of Project one: An animation explaining “How to” accomplish a particular task: - Utilizing at least 6 video clips. - Adding directions on top of the videos to clarify all the steps. | Project One- part 1: Prepare a proposal for Project one (“How To”), that includes: - What is the topic of the project. - A general description of the project. - A list of the videos intended to be used in it. - - A list of the graphics intended to be added to on top of these videos (clarifying the steps). - A storyboard of the Project.
Week 7 | Introduction to masking: - The two types of masks (vector versus raster) - creating a path, ellipse or rectangle as a mask. - Mask Properties and Modes. In class working session dedicated to Project One. Q & A about topics learned so far. | Project one – Part 2: Finishing the project: Having the rendered MP4 file ready for presentation next class.
Week 8 | Working with the Motion Path: - How to edit anchor points on it. - Roving keyframes. Raster based Masking: - What is alpha, Luma and how does Layer track Matt work. | Assignment 5- Record a video, import it to a composition, freeze it in the middle then utilize masking and shape layers to highlight and outline an important part or detail in the video such as a car or a building.
Week 9 | Text Animators: - Adding text animators and editing range selectors. - Properties we can add to animators. - Working with the Wiggly Selector. - Path Text. Explaining the scope of Project Two: Create motion graphics promoting an organization, service or a product. Utilize mostly vector graphics in it. | Project two – Part 1: Prepare a proposal for the project, that includes: - The topic of the project. - A general description of the project. - A list of the graphics create for it. - A storyboard of the Project.
Week 10 | 3D space: - Understanding the Z axis. - 3D layer comp views. - Adding and animating Cameras and lights. In class working session dedicated to Project Two. Q & A about topics learned so far. | Project two – Part 2: Finishing the project: Having the rendered MP4 file ready for presentation next class.
Week 11 | Final Project Presentation. Course summary (a list of main points taught in this course).
