### Course Description
This course is a continuation of PHYS 2274. PHYS 3274 provides a detailed study of the physics of nuclear imaging with the scintillation gamma camera. The course begins with a review of the relevant radiation detection physics, planar imaging principles and the measures of image quality. The design and function of the gamma camera detector are presented, contrasted and related to detector performance. Gamma camera event processing, including uniformity correction, is also described. The next unit describes the measurement and testing of detector performance including intrinsic uniformity, spatial resolution and count rate performance. The final unit explains the principles and design features of collimators and their impact on system performance. Issues of camera quality control are explained and described. Many of the principles in this course will be practiced both in the NMED 3040 lab course and clinical work in the hospital.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Relate the physics of radiation interactions to nuclear imaging.
- Describe the principles of planar nuclear imaging.
- Evaluate image quality through an understanding of the concepts and measures of contrast, resolution, noise and artifacts.
- Describe the design and basic principles of the operation of gamma cameras in current clinical use.
- Understand the physical limitations of gamma camera technology.
- Evaluate the quantitative performance of gamma cameras for absolute measurements and routine quality control.
- Understand the necessary calibration procedures and how they relate to performance.
- Utilize critical thinking skills to relate camera technology, design and operating parameters to camera performance and image quality through an understanding of the underlying physics.
- Perform and interpret quality assurance tests of gamma cameras and utilize critical thinking skills to diagnose instrumentation problems through an understanding of the principles of operation.
- Interpret block diagrams of electronic instrumentation and technology in technical literature.
- Intelligently use scientific and technical literature related to nuclear medicine imaging to evaluate and incorporate new protocols and technologies into clinical practice.
