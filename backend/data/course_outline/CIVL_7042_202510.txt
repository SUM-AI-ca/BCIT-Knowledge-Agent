## Course Description
This course is intended to introduce a variety of advanced topics in geotechnical engineering and to reinforce the importance and role of case histories in geotechnical engineering. It will also provide an opportunity for students to hear from local industry members in order to develop an appreciation for current geotechnical practice, and innovative design solutions, and provide examples of life-long learning opportunities. The course will be divided into several main themes, most of which will be highlighted by an industry speaker(s) and/or case history study. Writing skills and the ability to summarize and identify key points from a presentation will also be emphasized. Course topics will vary depending on recent occurrences and local projects but will likely include: a review of site investigation techniques and soil logging and their role in the overall geotechnical design process, geohazards, an introduction to critical state soil mechanics and liquefaction, ground improvement, dam / tailings dam design, geotechnical instrumentation, the observational method, and anchor design.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Explain the geotechnical design process, using the observational method and provide specific examples of its appropriate application. [1, 8, 9]
- Identify appropriate site investigation methods for specific soil conditions and visually classify soils. [2, 3]
- Extract and summarize relevant information from lectures presented by geotechnical experts. [2, 4, 7, 12]
- Describe the role of case histories in geotechnical engineering and the importance of continued professional development. [8, 9, 12]
- Explain the framework of critical state soil mechanics and define key concepts and terms. [1]
- Perform and explain a simple liquefaction screening assessment based on insitu testing. [2, 3, 4]
- List potential geohazards, explain where they are likely to occur, how they can be evaluated and identify some mitigation options. [1, 2, 4, 9]
- Discuss the concept of risk and consequence and how these play a role in geotechnical engineering. [2, 8, 9]
- Explain the purpose of geotechnical instrumentation and how it is used in geotechnical engineering. [1, 3]

## Course Goals
This course will offer exposure to a wide variety of geotechnical problems through instructor delivered lectures and a series of guest lectures presenting examples of real-world problems and design solutions. Writing skills and the ability to summarize and identify key points from a presentation will also be emphasized.

Upon successful completion of the course students will understand the importance and relevance of case histories in geotechnical engineering, preparing them for lifetime learning. They will also be aware of many of the issues facing geotechnical engineers in the Greater Vancouver area and will have the tools to understand the problems and carry out simplified assessments.

Course Learning Outcomes:

- Explain the geotechnical design process, the role of engineering judgement and the observational method. [1,8,9]
- Articulate the role of case histories in geotechnical engineering and the importance of continued professional development. [8,9,12]
- Identify appropriate site investigation methods for specific soil conditions and visually classify soils. [2,3]
- Extract and summarize relevant information from lectures presented by geotechnical experts. [2,4,7,12]
- Perform and understand common empirical liquefaction triggering and effect assessment methods. [2,3,4]
- Understand and apply pseudo-static and empirical Newmark deformation analysis methods. [2,4]
- List potential geohazards, explain where they are likely to occur, how they can be identified and some mitigation options. [1,2,4,9]
- Explain the purpose of geotechnical instrumentation and identify appropriate applications for common equipment. [1,3]
- Others dependent on the specific topics and industry speakers. [7,12]
- Being able to work with slope stability software (Slope/W) for static and seismic slope stability analysis. [2,5,6]

## Attendance Requirements
This course includes many guest lectures. The presenters are all professionals working in geotechnical engineering and related fields. To show respect for the speakers and their time, punctual attendance is expected for every class and is mandatory for successful completion of this course. Professional conduct and class participation are also expected. Sign in at the start of each guest lecture and some tutorials will be required to track attendance. When assigned, it is expected that pre-reading will be completed before the presentations or tutorials.

Makeup Test, Exams or Quizzes: There will be no makeup tests, exams or quizzes. If you miss a test, exam or quiz, you will receive zero marks for the work missed. Exceptions may be made for extenuating circumstances or for medical reasons documented by a BCIT Student Medical Certificate (https://www.bcit.ca/files/healthservices/pdf/studentmedicalcertificate.pdf). In such a case, it is the responsibility of the student to inform one of the course instructors immediately. Note that exceptions will not be made for personal events such as vacations, weddings, or travel arrangements.
