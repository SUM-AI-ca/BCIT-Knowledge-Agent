### Course Description
This course introduces forest sampling techniques as they relate to the current policies and procedures for timber inventory, log scaling and waste assessments in British Columbia. This field based course will require students will collect, compile, analyze and report on cruise, scale and waste data. Additional topics include the principles of double sampling and 3-P sampling techniques, as well as wood products manufacturing and end product valuation.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Describe alternative sampling techniques: Double sampling, 3-P sampling, Big Baf/Little BAF
- Prepare field sampling plans that meet statistical requirements
- Apply provincial cruising and inventory procedures
- Compare and contrast the differences between the Loss Factor, Call Grading/Net Factoring (CGNF) and the Vegetation Resources Inventory (VRI) cruising systems in BC
- Apply provincial log scaling procedures
- Apply provincial logging waste survey procedures
- Accurately scale and grade logs and logging waste
- Compare the differences between "statutory log grades" and "market log sorts"
- Compile sampling data and interpret results using standard statistical methods
- Describe the cut control process in BC
- Describe the current log and forest product market trends in BC

Students cannot miss more than 10% of class meetings without prior arrangements with the instructor. This includes missed time resulting from a late arrival – partially missed class time also counts towards the 10% and will be calculated based on time missed. A doctor’s note (using a BCIT approved medical certificate ) may be required within 10 days of the missed class for any illness causing you to miss any assignments/projects/quiz/exam/lab/field trips. Unless otherwise agreed upon, medical appointments, interviews, etc. scheduled during class times are not considered excused absences. At the discretion of the instructor, you may complete the work missed or have the work prorated for excused absences.
Students who miss more than 10% of class meetings may be given a failing grade for the course/program or the student may be removed from the course/program ( Policy 5101 - Student Regulations ).
### Course Specific Requirements
Assignments (FNAM guidelines): Marks will be reduced by 10% for each 24-hour period (or fraction thereof) that an assignment is submitted beyond the due date. For instance, an assignment that would have earned 82% if submitted on time would earn 72% if submitted up to 24 hours late. If this assignment was submitted 25 hours later, it would earn 62%. The application of this penalty or alternate approaches to late submissions will be at the discretion of the course instructor.
Group Work (FNAM guidelines): Group work is a critical part of natural resource professions, and you will need to be able to work as a team effectively. Your learning in this course and program will be impacted if you are not able to collaborate and communicate effectively and respectfully with peers and instructors. Group assignments make up a significant portion of many courses, and it is expected that everyone contributes their fair share. If a student does not contribute their fair share to group work, instructors reserve the right to penalize their evaluations within reason. Repeated offences (lack of contributions to field or written work, inappropriate communications, distracted or inattentive conduct, etc.) will be treated more strongly.
