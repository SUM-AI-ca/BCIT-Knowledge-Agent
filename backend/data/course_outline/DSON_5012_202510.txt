## Course Description
​DSON 5012 Cardiac Sonography is a continuation of DSON 5011 Cardiac Sonography. The focus of the theory component of this course shifts to the etiology, hemodynamics, signs, and symptoms, and sonographic patterns as relevant to the interpretation and quantification of cardiac disease. The course includes modules on systolic and diastolic dysfunction, hypertension, valve disease, cardiomyopathy, diseases of the great vessels, infective endocarditis, cardiac masses, pericardial disease, heart failure, a​​nd systemic diseases. In the lab portion of this course, students will build on the skills learned in DSON 5011. By the completion of this course, students will be able to perform colour and spectral Doppler and complete routine measurements. Students will be introduced to how to modify the exam based on pathology. By the end of this course, the student will be able to complete a full echocardiogram.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of the theory component of this course, the student will be able to:

- Quantify the severity of cardiac pathology by integrating knowledge of anatomy and disease progression, and applying correct variables within calculations.
- Describe the impact on cardiac hemodynamics during provocative/dynamic maneuvers such as the Valsalva Maneuver. (3.3d)
- Correlate results from auscultation with cardiac pathologies. (5.2e)
- Describe the principles and interpretation of spectral Doppler, tissue Doppler, strain rate imaging and ultrasound contrast imaging in the setting of cardiac pathology. (Appendix E)

Upon successful completion of the lab component of this course, the student will be able to:

- Demonstrate reliability and adaptability (2.3h, 2.3i)
- Understand basic troubleshooting. (4.3b)
- Question the patient for relevant clinical information through a patient history, and accurately record this medical information. (1.1f, 1.2b)
- Communicate with the patient throughout the examination, in a manner appropriate to the patient's ability to understand. (1.1d)
- Adapt communication in response to the patient and situation, and for patients with special needs. (1.1c)
- Respond to patient questions and concerns, and communicate departmental reporting procedures to patients. (1.1h, 1.1g)
- Document limitations to exam quality and completeness, equipment, and technical limitations, and identify and respond to exceptions from established echocardiographic protocols and procedures. (2.2g, 5.4f, 5.3m, 5.3n)
- Use medical terminology and standard abbreviations in oral communication and write medical terminology and standard abbreviations. (1.1k, 1.2e)
- Perform palpation of pulses for examination. (3.3b)
- Assess the following structures with 2D, colour, spectral Doppler, and/or tissue Doppler imaging, and measure 2D, M-mode, colour Doppler, spectral Doppler and tissue Doppler (when applicable): cardiac position, abdominal situs, chest and thorax, coronary vessels, hepatic veins, outflow tracts, pulmonary veins, wall layers and segments, all 4 cardiac chambers and appendages, IAS, IVS, great vessels and their branches, all cardiac valves, aortic root, MV / TV annulus, IVC and SVC. (Appendix E)
- Complete an entire routine echocardiogram in a timely manner. (3.1h)
- By participating in a SOH interprofessional workshop, describe the roles within an inter/intraprofessional team, and collaborate with these team members in decision making. (2.3c, 2.3d)

## Course Goals
The goal of the theory portion of this course is for students to apply knowledge of cardiac anatomy and hemodynamics to explain and predict changes seen in situations of systolic and diastolic dysfunction, hypertensive heart disease, valvular disease and cardiomyopathy. The lab component of this course will build on the skills learned in DSON 5011. Students will learn and incorporate colour Doppler imaging and spectral Doppler techniques into the echo scan routine, as well as routine measurements and assessment techniques for each window and view.

## Attendance Requirements
This course aims to prepare students didactically as well as develop the basic psychomotor required to scan the heart. Attendance and participation is required. As a courtesy to other students and the instructor, students are expected to arrive or log into class on time. Both attendance and late arrivals are monitored.
