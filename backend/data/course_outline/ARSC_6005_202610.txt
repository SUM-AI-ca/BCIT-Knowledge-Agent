### Course Description
The purpose of this studio is to develop an architectural approach to more complex design problems. Students will broaden their skills and knowledge from previous coursework to develop a site and user-specific design project, at a mid-size building scale. A series of exercises will approach issues of context, program, scale, organization, space, form, structure and environmental systems. Design intentions will be investigated and tested at the scale of the detail with drawings and models.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- •Discuss architectural design principles, methods, and precedents applied in studio work.
- •Conduct a detailed case study of a contemporary architectural firm and project and report findings using a variety of resources and media.
- •Document and analyze an urban site (including current and historical context, ecologies, urban systems, patterns, Indigenous territories, and authorities having jurisdiction) to inform project response.
- •Develop a program based on research, applying all relevant requirements.
- •Design a mid-size building response based on a concept, client, flexible program, and urban site.
- •Outline structural, mechanical, material and environmental systems appropriate to the project.
- •Assess building components, materials and assemblies according to performance and sustainable design principles.
- •Demonstrate expertise with a range of 2D and 3D physical and digital design tools and techniques.
- •Use architectural conventions to communicate through graphics, models and presentations.
- •Apply all relevant building codes, regulations, and standards, including universal design.
- •Engage in collaborative critique and iterative design processes.
- •Demonstrate professional best practice individually and collaboratively.

### Attendance Requirements
Attendance is mandatory during all ARSC classes, presentations and learning events. Students are expected to be present for any and all learning activities scheduled during ARSC program hours (8:30-5:30 Monday to Friday). Graded course work is conducted during this time and may be impossible to make up. Partial attendance will be considered cumulative absence. Poor attendance may render the student unable to complete the course.
In case of absence, course instructor/s and Program Head should be notified with a minimum 24 hours notice unless medically unavoidable. Documentation for the date of absence is to be provided via email within one week's time. In case of undocumented absence the following protocol will be followed:
- First undocumented absence will receive a warning from instructor
- Second undocumented absence will be referred to program head
- Third undocumented absence will be referred to AD
