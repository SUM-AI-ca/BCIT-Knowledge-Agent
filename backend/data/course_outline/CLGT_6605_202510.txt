## Course Description
This course is a continuation of CLGT 5505 and will build on the basic skills introduced in the first term. Additional interphase FISH techniques will be covered. The use and application of whole chromosome paint probes and loci-specific DNA probes will be introduced. ISCN nomenclature describing the use of FISH for detection of micro-deletions and translocations will be covered. Documentation and interpretation of results will be emphasized.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
By the end of this course, learners will be able to:

- Build on experience gained from CLGT 5505 preparing reagents and media.
- Thoroughly document all FISH experiments.
- Troubleshoot poor or unsatisfactory results and take appropriate remedial action.
- Assess hybridization efficiency, analyze FISH slides, interpret results and understand their impact on patient care.
- Use α-satellite probes for enumeration of chromosome copy number.
- Understands quality assurance and uses appropriate control data to interpret results.
- Use locus-specific DNA probes to detect micro-deletions.
- Verify suitability of clinical indication to the test and use appropriate scoring and selection criteria for evaluation of FISH results.
- Be proficient at using the epi-fluorescent microscope. Know components of epi-fluorescent microscope and their functions.
- Be proficient at description/nomenclature of FISH results using ISCN 2020 guidelines.
- Discuss the use/application of CGH, whole chromosome paints, multi-color karyotyping.
- Demonstrate professional practice when completing tasks and assignments.
- Communicate and interact with instructor and fellow students in a professional manner.
- Work safely in the laboratory while performing FISH assays.

## Course Schedule and Assignments
Assignment Details

- Use of generative AI tools (eg ChatGPT, etc.)

Student analysis of lab data is an important part of student learning. Instructional staff do not believe that generative AI tools like ChatGPT are an adequate substitute for student judgement in analyzing data. Reports to be submitted for marking are modelled after the format used at clinical sites, so there is little opportunity to use generative AI to write these reports. However, if students do choose to use one of these tools, please follow these guidelines:

Cite and number each use, like this [1, ChatGPT, (date)]

Mark all generative AI output, by using “quotes” for using unedited genAI output, and ‘quotes’ for using paraphrased genAI output

For all generative AI content, student must include primary source verification i.e. original research journal articles or chapters from traditionally published textbooks.
