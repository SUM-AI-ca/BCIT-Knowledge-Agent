### Course Description
This course aims to provide the student with a variety of pathological conditions that are commonly encountered in prosthetic and orthotic practice, and an understanding the physical and mechanical properties of materials currently used. Student will examine the common clinical presentation of each presented pathological condition and review current research related to orthotic and prosthetic management of that condition. He/she will develop, justify and evaluate orthotic/prosthetic treatment plans for each specific patient/case study and present these to the class for general discussion. Treatment planning will include a clinical assessment, evaluation of patient pathomechanics, observational gait analysis, identification of treatment goals, prescription design, material science decisions, and anticipated outcomes of the proposed prosthetic /orthotic intervention.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Explain the typical clinical presentation of patients with common pathologies, syndromes and biomechanical anomalies that are commonly encountered in the practice of prosthetics and orthotics.
- Demonstrate the ability to perform and record a thorough patient evaluation with particular attention to the assessment of musculoskeletal and neuromuscular disorders affecting the spine and extremities.
- Demonstrate the ability to identify functional loss and to select appropriate prosthetic devices to address the deficiency.
- Demonstrate basic procedures for safe handling of a physically and/or cognitively challenged patient.
- Know common pathologies, syndromes and biomechanical anomalies commonly encountered in the practice of prosthetics and orthotics.
- Solve basic clinical problems through evaluation of case studies and communicate evaluation to peers.
- Apply theoretical knowledge to generate appropriate prescription criteria.
- Apply theoretical knowledge to generate prosthetic and orthotic treatment goals.
- Apply outcome measures to evaluate if devices supplied have met treatment goals.
- Identify where device designs have failed to meet prescription criteria and treatment goals.
- Recommend changes to designs to allow it to meet treatment goals where it has failed to do so.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the mechanical, physical and chemical descriptions of materials and their properties.
- Identify some metals, plastics and ceramics that are fundamental in the fabrication of prosthetics and orthotics and know their physical properties and molecular structure.
- Understand and investigate the behaviour of these materials.
- Understand the appropriate handling and use of materials in prosthetic and orthotic practice.

### Course Goals
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Explain the typical clinical presentation of patients with common pathologies and biomechanical anomalies that are commonly encountered in the practice of prosthetics and orthotics.
- Demonstrate the ability to perform and record a thorough patient evaluation with particular attention to the assessment of musculoskeletal and neuromuscular disorders.
- Demonstrate the ability to identify functional loss and to select appropriate prosthetic devices to address the deficiency.
- Solve clinical problems through evaluation of case studies and communicate evaluation to peers.
- Apply theoretical knowledge to generate prosthetic and orthotic prescription criteria to meet treatment goals.
- Discuss outcome measures to evaluate if treatment goals have been met.
- Critically evaluate/predict the effectiveness of your treatment plan and provide recommendations for future considerations.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the mechanical, physical, and chemical descriptions and properties of common prosthetic and orthotic materials.
- Discuss how the behaviour of materials influences use in prosthetic and orthotic interventions.
- An awareness of the appropriate handling and use of materials in prosthetic and orthotic practice.
