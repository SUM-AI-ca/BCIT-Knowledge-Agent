## Course Description
This course, in the context of a clinical preceptorship, provides the learner with the opportunity to synthesize and integrate the nursing knowledge and skills acquired through all previous coursework into their practice. With increasing independence and autonomy, learners demonstrate the ability to implement evidence informed practice and apply clinical reasoning to diverse client situations of increasing acuity and/or complexity. The emphasis is on consolidation of practice capacity while safely managing client assignment. In accordance with the BCCNM Professional Standards for Registered Nurses and Nurse Practitioners, and, Competencies in the Context of Entry Level Registered Nurse Practice in British Columbia, learners are building towards transition to licensed practice. Practicum placements include various settings in a variety of institutions throughout the Lower Mainland, possibly remote locations in British Columbia. Learners are paired with an agency Registered Nurse and work their assigned shifts. Furthermore, this final preceptorship practicum provides learners with an introduction to a focus of practice and/or a more extensive experience in medical/surgical nursing. Focus of practice settings are varied and may include perinatal, emergency, pediatrics, neonatal, perioperative, nephrology, mental health, high acuity, primary care or community. Building on the consolidation and nursing practice capacity established in Practice of Nursing 8, emphasis is placed on integrating the depth of knowledge, skills, attitudes, and judgments required for the care of diverse and complex client assignments as preparation for transition to licensed practice. There is a focus on developing teamwork and leadership capacities. Upon successful completion, learners will meet the BCCNM Competencies in the Context of Entry Level Registered Nurse Practice in British Columbia.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the learner meets the following competencies related to the graduate attributes:

Advocate

- Collaborates with health-care team members and/or professional organizations to raise awareness of health inequities, unethical health and social policies,
- Examines and challenges the influence of power within organizational, social, economic and political contexts that influence health and well-being within the context of the nurses' role in the delivery of care
- Collaborates with others for social justice and to advocate for laws, policies, and procedures designed to promote equity, safety and quality of care
- Uses resources in a fiscally responsible manner to make fair and equitable decisions based on the needs of clients
- Advocates for policies and programs that effectively address the determinants of health
- Promotes acceptance of diversity and supports client empowerment to reduce stigma

Collaborator

- Works to prevent and resolve conflict, and negotiate solutions in interactions with clients and the healthcare team
- Demonstrates the ability to collaborate with diverse clients, adapt relational approaches appropriately, and accommodate varying contextual factors in diverse practice situations

Communicator

- Communicates effectively with clients and healthcare team members to support safe, high quality, client- centred healthcare in diverse situations and contexts

Decision Maker

- Independently plans nursing care with clients, within the context of critical inquiry and relational practice, that integrates knowledge from nursing, health sciences and other related disciplines as well as knowledge from practice experiences, client knowledge, patterns and preferences, and influences within the health care context
- Effectively manages complex clinical scenarios and time sensitive situations.
- Monitors the effectiveness of client outcomes to inform future care planning within all contexts and situations
- Actively participates in the continuous improvement of health care quality and client safety
- Articulates clinical reasoning process in support of nursing practice

Global Citizen

- Practices culturally safe and equitable nursing care in diverse contexts and situations
- Accurately assesses the impact of public policies on the performance of one's domain of responsibility and the ability to influence public policymaking at both local and federal levels
- Recognizes and addresses factors that influence the socio-political, economic and historical factors that lead to inequalities in healthcare
- Integrates knowledge of emerging community and global health issues, population health issues and research into nursing practice

Leader

- Collaborates with and acts as a resource for members of the health care team to meet patient/client needs
- Promotes teamwork and garners commitment and participation of others that will optimize nursing practice and client care
- Facilitates organizational change initiatives

Lifelong Learner

- Participates in testing and disseminating nursing knowledge
- Collaborates with the inter-professional team to implement evidence -based practice
- Promotes sustainability and continuous quality improvement in healthcare
- Contributes to the learning experiences of colleagues through support and teaching
- Integrates inter-professional perspectives and current trends in nursing and healthcare into nursing practice

Professional

- Displays initiative, confidence and self-awareness in nursing practice
- Provides knowledge translation to support the professional development of other learners and healthcare team members
- Acts to enhance the quality of the practice environment
- Understands the role of the regulatory body and the relationship of the regulatory body to one's own practice
- Advocates for policies and practices consistent with the standards of the profession
- Independently assesses own practice and undertakes activities to improve practice and meet identified learning goals
- Participates in professional activities to keep abreast of current trends and issues in nursing

## Attendance Requirements
Attendance: As student success is tied to regular attendance of classes, the BSN program strongly recommends that students attend class regularly. Attendance at all nursing theory classes is an expectation of the BSN program.

In all learning environments, students are socialized into their professional role and are thus required to demonstrate knowledge and application of professional nursing standards and ethics. Multiple absences may jeopardize a student’s development of the knowledge base required for competent professional nursing practice and successful completion of course requirements. As adult learners, students are ultimately responsible for their own learning; however, it is also recognized that repeated absences have a negative impact on collaborative learning experiences.
