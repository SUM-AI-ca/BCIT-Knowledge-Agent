## Course Description
This course is the last of three focusing on the diagnostic quality of images. It continues to build on the theory introduced in Radiographic Procedures 1 and 3 as well as Image Analysis and Applied Image Analysis 1 required to assess images of the axial (vertebrae) and appendicular skeletons (excluding skull), chest and abdomen. Emphasis is placed on applying critical thinking skills for the assessment criteria of higher acuity cases and identifying if an exam meets diagnostic parameters or requires a repeat.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Verify accuracy of patient demographics on an image.
- Identify anatomical structures on an image.
- Given a case, describe patient positioning for diagnostic acceptability of images.
- Evaluate radiographic images for technical factors applied based on best practice, clinical information, equipment, and other contextual factors.
- Given a case, describe how radiographic principles affect image quality.
- Discern accuracy of radiographic markers and annotations on an image.
- Evaluate images for artifacts and respond.
- Given a case, evaluate images for normal results and variants.
- Given a case, determine if further images are required.
- Given a case, apply corrective measures as required.
- Apply knowledge of cross sectional and relational anatomy as it relates to clinical situations. ​
