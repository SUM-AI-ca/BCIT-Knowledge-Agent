## Course Description
Descriptive statistics, including measures of central tendency and dispersion, and graphical presentation of data using Excel. Concepts of probability, probability distributions (including binomial, normal, Poisson, log-normal and chi-square distributions). Inferential statistics, including sampling, estimation, confidence intervals, hypothesis testing, correlation and regression, and an introduction to statistical process control.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Use statistical procedures aided by calculator or computer to summarize, analyze and interpret statistical data. [5]
- Compute measures of central tendency, spread, and position; perform statistical tests to identify outliers. [1]
- Specify and validate appropriate methods of sampling and experimental design. [1]
- Arrange raw data in standard statistics tables; interpret and produce bar, pie, and Pareto charts and histograms. [1, 5]
- Apply basic probability to predict events; perform probability calculations using the Adding and Multiplying Rules. [1]
- Apply discrete and continuous random variable distributions; analyse sample distributions via the central limit theorem. [1]
- Estimate parameters of populations using sample data. [1]
- Organize and write statistical reports, including collecting the raw data. [5, 7]
- Solve problems with binomial, normal and t distributions; set up confidence intervals; compare z, t, F and X2 values. [1]
- Formulate and test hypotheses using both the traditional and p-value methods, and identify type I and type II errors. [1, 3]
- Perform significance tests for means, proportions and differences between two means/proportions; conduct Χ2 tests. [1, 3]
- Determine correlation coefficients; conduct linear, power and exponential regressions. [1]

## Course Goals
The goals of this course are to:

- explain the underlying principles, concepts and terminology of descriptive and inferential statistics,
- demonstrate the application of these concepts to mining technologies, using real data and/or referencing relevant industry publications when possible,
- develop fluency with statistical calculator functions and the statistical analysis capabilities of MS Excel.
- develop critical analysis, team work and presentation skills, applicable to addressing mining-related problems involving statistical attributes.

## Course topics
Unit 1: Descriptive Statistics

- Measures of central tendency
- Measures of variation including standard deviation and CV
- Frequency distributions
- Chebyshev's Theorem and the Empirical Rule
- Histograms,frequency distributions, Pareto analysis
- Statistical functions in Excel and Calculators Applications

Unit 2: Probability Theory

- Basic probability rules
- Applications of addition and multiplication laws to sampling
- Introduction to reliability

Unit 3: Discrete Probability Distributions

- General discrete probability distributions
- Binomial probability distribution
- Sampling applications
- Discrete versus continuous probability distributions

Unit 4: Continuous Probability Distributions

- Continuous random variables
- Normal probability distribution
- t-distribution
- Log-Normal probability distribution

Unit 5: Sampling Distributions

- Central Limit Theorem
- Distribution of sample means

Unit 6: Inferential Statistics

- Point and interval estimates for single means and proportions
- Hypothesis testing for single means and proportions
- Confidence intervals and hypothesis tests for difference of means and difference of proportions
- Chi-Squared goodness of fit test

Unit 7: Curve Fitting

- Correlation
- Regression
- Introduction to multiple linear regression

Unit 8: Introduction to Control Charts and Sampling Principles

- Acceptance Sampling
- General theory of control charts
- Variable charts (x-bar, R or s charts)
- Attribute charts (p or c charts)
- Control limits
