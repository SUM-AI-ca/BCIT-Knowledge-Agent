### Course Description
This course examines the relationship between land surveying and law and how laws affect the land surveying profession. Topics include; the Canadian legal system (statute, criminal, common & civil law), Real Property law (interests in land, land registry & title systems, integration and georeferencing), Boundary Law (creation, retracement & re-establishment of boundaries, bornage, hierarchy of evidence, jurisdictional boundaries including maritime zones, offshore boundaries and the law of the sea), Natural Boundaries (emphasis on water boundaries, accretion, avulsion, erosion, navigability), Professional Practice (function of Canadian Board of Examiners, jurisdictional associations, code of ethics, professional standards, negligence, disciplinary process, etc.).
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:
- Demonstrate an understanding of the Canadian Legal System.
- Research and interpret case and statute law.
- Demonstrate an understanding of Common Law and Quebec’s Civil Law and Federal and Provincial statutes as they affect property rights and cadastral surveys.
- Interpret boundary evidence, including the hierarchy of evidence, original monumentation, coordinate-based cadastre and boundary demarcation.
- Understand Limitations of Action, and estoppel.
- Explain adverse possession and prescription, their similarities and differences and the effect of statutes on them.
- Understand the principles, types, and admissibility of evidence and the role of a witness and of an expert witness.
- Understand crown grants, exceptions to grants, transfers through deeds, indefeasible title.
- Explain concepts of road dedication, expropriation, rights of way, easements, bona fide purchaser for value.
- Explain riparian and littoral rights, including the ad medium filum presumption, the doctrines of accretion and erosion, reliction, avulsion, the effect of fill, title to banks, beds and islands.
- Understand the effect of statute and common law on riparian/littoral rights.
- Be able to advise a landowner on how to resolve a boundary dispute based on knowledge of case (or civil) law and appropriate statutes and understand the land surveyor's role in the creation of boundaries as well as in the resolution of disputes.
- Understand the concept of the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea.
- Understand the maritime zones and how they are defined.
- Recognize the scope of a multipurpose cadastre, including fiscal and planning uses, descriptions, title insurance, NAFTA and inter-provincial trade, negligence and professional responsibilities.
- Understand the role of professional associations and their powers and the privileges and responsibilities of their members.
- Demonstrate an understanding of modern and historical case law and its application to a cadastral practice.
