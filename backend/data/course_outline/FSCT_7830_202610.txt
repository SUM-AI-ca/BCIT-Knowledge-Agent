### Course Description
​This course covers the forensic evaluation and documentation of injuries. It is designed to provide the medical, scientific and forensic principles relevant to the forensic health care of and investigation into both the fatalities of and survivors of injuries and trauma. Key topics include assessment and evaluation of various types of trauma - (blunt, sharp, gunshot, motor vehicle, pediatric, fire/thermal), the pathology of wounds, legal aspects of injury documentation, and trauma-informed care. Students will participate in online discussions, weekly quizzes, and mid-term and final assignments. Upon completion of this course, students will earn a digital badge titled "Forensic Injury Documentation and Evaluation"​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Compare characteristics of blunt and sharp injuries, gunshot wounds, and wounds related to motor vehicles, fires, and pediatric settings.
- Assess the mechanisms of injuries and differentiate patterns of injuries from patterned injuries.
- Apply forensic evaluation techniques to both living patients and fatalities.
- Document wounds and injuries for forensic purposes.
- Describe the medicolegal significance of documenting intentional and unintentional injuries.
- Describe strategies for trauma scene management and trauma-informed care.

### Attendance Requirements
This course is online asynchronous. Although you may work through the course at your own pace and time each week, each module's requirements must be completed each week before proceeding to the next module. Students work through the course as a cohort. Students must participate and interact in each week's discussion postings.
### Course Schedule and Assignments
Module / Assignment | Topic / Description | Assessment
Module 1 (Week 1) | Blunt Force Injuries: Identification and understanding of bruises, abrasions, lacerations, and fractures. | Quiz
Module 2 (Week 2) | Sharp Force Injuries: Injury differentials and knife blade anatomy; unique characteristics of sharp force injuries. | Quiz
Module 3 (Week 3) | Pathology of Wounds: Skin injury characteristics, direction of impact abrasions, tramline bruising, stabbing wounds, tissue bridging. | Quiz
Module 4 (Week 4) | Injury Documentation: Forensic documentation of injuries; crime scene photography. | Quiz
Mid Term Assignment | Option A: Forensic evaluation of injuries in your current setting for medicolegal purposes. Option B: Forensic evaluation of injuries in an investigative setting outside healthcare; challenges and establishing credibility. | Written Assignment (2-3 pages) Due by the end of Mod 5
Module 5 (Week 5) | Gunshot Wounds: Firearm injury interpretation, bullets, shotgun shells, gun barrel size, wound distance, impact variations, bullet deformations. | Quiz
Module 6 (Week 6) | Motor Vehicle Incidents: Forensic medical investigation of collisions and pedestrian injuries. | Quiz
Module 7 (Week 7) | Pediatric Injuries: Identification of pediatric bruises, fractures, and patterned injuries. | Quiz
Module 8 (Week 8) | Fire and Thermal Injuries: Fire investigations, skin burn classifications, fire and thermal injuries. | Quiz
Module 9 (Week 9) | Trauma Informed Care: Challenges in caring for victims of violence; trauma informed care practices. | Interactive Discussion
Final Assignment | Video Presentation (approx. 5 min): - Importance for nurses to recognize forensic situations in the emergency department. - Application of clinical forensic principles for optimal medico-legal outcomes. - Role of a clinical forensic nurse. - Need for protocols in supporting nurses. - Integration of previous assignment insights: importance, benefits, challenges, negative outcomes, and trauma informed care. | Video Presentation (approx 5 min)
