### Course Description
`Students apply the principles and laboratory procedures for the modern instrumental methods of chemical analysis routinely used in biotechnology: spectroscopic methods including visible, ultraviolet and infrared spectroscopy, chromatography (gas and high performance liquid chromatography), gas chromatography-mass spectrometry, liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry, electrochemistry and electrophoresis. Specific emphasis will be given to the correct operation and troubleshooting of all instruments used. A laboratory-based project will require the students to demonstrate teamwork, leadership and problem solving skills.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:
- Outline the principle of operation of instruments being used for analysis in biotechnology.
- Assess the advantages and the limitations of selected instruments used for analysis in biotechnology.
- Apply the principles of analysis to instrumental analytical methodology through proper standard and sample preparation.
- Perform analysis using the following instrumentation: visible, ultraviolet and infrared spectrometers, gas, liquid and ion chromatographs, gas chromatography-mass spectrometry, and liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry.
- Interpret data pertaining to instrumental analytical methods.

### Course Specific Requirements
Assignments: Late assignments, lab reports or projects will not be accepted for marking. All work must be done on an individual basis.
Makeup Labs or Exams : There will be no makeup labs, assignments, tests, exams or quizzes. If you miss a lab, assignment, test, exam or quiz, you will receive zero marks. Reasonable accommodation and allowance will be made when students have good reason to be absent in accordance with Policy 5101.
For in-person examinations : Where calculators are permitted in exams, only simple scientific calculators are to be used. Chemistry Department policy prohibits the use of PDAs (personal data assistants), calculators with infrared capabilities, programmable calculators, electronic dictionaries, translators, laptop computers, and cell phones in exams.
Use of Artificial Intelligence: Student analysis of lab data is an important part of student learning. Instructional staff do not believe that generative AI tools like ChatGPT are an adequate substitute for student judgement.
- Any use of AI must align with BCIT academic integrity policies. If AI tools are used to assist in course work (idea generation) students must clearly acknowledge their use, specifying the tool and it’s contributions. Students are expected to critically evaluate any information or content generated by AI tools to ensure their accuracy. The use of AI to complete assignments, lab reports, prelabs, or exams is prohibited.
