## Course Description
The course explores race, gender, and class as intersecting relations that have applicability to academic and working contexts, as well as and applicability to our everyday lives and relationships. Concepts and arguments from the social sciences and humanities dealing with race, gender, and class in inter-related combinations not just as stand-alone concepts will be studied and applied in the course. Course material will focus on but not be limited to Canada and the US. Course material will also have an emphasis on aboriginal issues in Canada. Students will gain opportunities to critically reflect on their own situatedness within these intersections. Additionally, students will learn strategies for critical self-awareness, response, and alliance building that they can take to communities where they work and live.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Examine frameworks for analyzing dominance, privilege, oppressions, and power.
- Identify and describe the ways in which the categories of race, gender, and class as categories are interlocking.
- Explain how these categories structure assumptions, relationships, and practices in both invisible and overt ways.
- Articulate continuities and changes in how these categories are invoked across particular eras, fields of work and study, in Canada and some international contexts.
- Discuss some of the controversies, critiques, advantages, limitations, and ambiguities that accompany looking at race, class, and gender in intersectional ways.
- Examine how different authors, theorists, and practitioners from various fields explain the relationships between race, gender, and class.
- Critically reflect on the student?s own situatedness in the intersections of race, gender, and class.
- Apply ideas drawn from academic and artistic works to contemporary professional and social contexts where race, class, and gender operate.
- Analyze frameworks and issues that arise in anti-oppression and alliance-building.
- Apply critical thinking, response, and alliance building strategies about race, gender, and class to communities where students live and work.

## Course Specific Requirements
- Preparation and Participation for Class: Students should come to class with evidence of prior preparation and completion of the scheduled course material; have scheduled course material handy in print or digital form for use during class; and be thoughtfully engaged during class time for class discussions and activities.
- Coursework Submission: Coursework must be submitted in the format, location, and due date/time specified by the instructor. See detailed instructions provided in class and in course handouts during the term for the specific course component. There will be a 5% deduction on late coursework per day, up to a total of four days, after which that coursework may receive a fail mark or zero. For absence or non-submission of timed exam or presentation coursework by the due day/time a zero mark will be applied. If a student is absent for an exam or presentation, or does not submit a gradable coursework component on time, and seeks accommodation for such coursework submission outside of the due date/time due to illness, medical, or other extenuating circumstances, they must do the following: communicate and consult with the instructor before the due date of that coursework component, unless evidence is provided that this was not possible, and in that case no later than the day after the due date; submit appropriate supporting documentation (ex. BCIT-approved medical certificate); and if accommodation is granted, follow through with the instructor's alternative submission date/time or assessment. If corrections are required to a coursework component, the student must make them and submit them by the due date for the grade on that component to be recorded.
- Academic Honesty and Integrity for Coursework: Coursework components must follow the format and requirements specified for that component. All coursework must be the work and words of the individual student, and all sources used should be formally documented and trackable through in-text citations and a reference list, using instructor-approved citation and format protocols (APA or MLA style). Students cannot use AI-generated content/computer generated text for coursework. In short, unless the coursework is written by the student or cited by the student from sources that can be tracked and found, the student cannot use and submit such coursework. The student must consult with the instructor before anyone can modify or edit a student’s coursework in any way. Students cannot submit coursework for one class, either at BCIT or elsewhere, for evaluation or credit in another class, without prior authorization by the instructor. Coursework will be checked for academic integrity and plagiarism. The course will use BCIT Policy 5104, Student Code of Academic Integrity https://www.bcit.ca/files/pdf/policies/5104.pdf particularly section 8 on plagiarism, cheating, falsifying or misrepresenting, and academic advantage, so students should make themselves aware of this policy.
- Recording, Copyright, Ownership, and Distribution of Course Materials: All course material is for the student’s individual study and use. Students must not record lectures and classroom activities or discussions unless authorized by BCIT (ex. for IAP academic accommodation purposes), nor must students post or distribute course material outside the course, including to any websites, without the required permissions from the instructor and copyright holders.
