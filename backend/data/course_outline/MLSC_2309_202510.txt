## Course Description
Continued study of major groups of bacteria based on epidemiology, pathogenesis/disease spectrum, laboratory diagnosis and therapy/prevention. Apply laboratory protocols in the identification of selected bacteria studied in Level 1 and Level 2. Interpret test results to report identifications at an introductory level. Study instrumentation/automation (including MALDI) at an introductory level. Describe concepts of safe practice in theory for RL3 organisms. Apply safe practices in laboratory for RL1 and RL2 organisms. Refine critical thinking, problem solving, and team-based interpersonal skills through collaborative laboratory workshops and assignments that integrate theory and practice. Learning will be assessed with theory examinations, online learning activities and practical labs.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Describe culture requirements and morphology (microscopic and macroscopic) of selected bacteria.
- Interpret cultures based on growth requirements and macroscopic (colony) morphology unique to these bacteria.
- Prepare and interpret Gram-stained colony smears safely and accurately.
- Select identification algorithms based on interpretation of macroscopic and microscopic features.
- Perform and interpret biochemical testing for specific isolates, safely and accurately.
- Document procedures and test results in comprehensive and accurate lab identification reports. Perform quality control as required for testing and documentation.
- Apply problem solving skills to resolve identification issues by identifying corrective action processes.
- Discuss the role of selected bacteria in health and disease including epidemiology, pathogenesis and the spectrum of disease.
- Describe successes and challenges in the prevention (vaccines) and treatment of infectious disease by these microorganisms.
- Discuss rapid identification relative to patient care, lab safety and bioterrorism, including yeast and RL 3 microorganisms.
- Describe automation/instrumentation commonly used in the routine clinical laboratory.
- Practice Microbiology lab safety and aseptic technique.
- Complete assigned tasks in an efficient (time and resources) and thorough manner.

## Attendance Requirements
Regular attendance and punctuality are important in the student learning experience. While attendance in lectures is not monitored, attendance in lab sessions is mandatory and is documented by Faculty and Staff. In case of illness or other unavoidable absence, students must communicate as soon as possible with the Instructor. Illness must be substantiated by an official medical certificate. Absence for other unavoidable causes must be supported by proper documentation. Excessive absences may result in failure or withdrawal from this course and/or the Program.

If a student is absent from a Midterm, Skills Assessment, or Lab Exam, timely notice must be provided directly to the Instructor. If the absence is deemed excused, the assessment (or an alternate assessment) will be offered at a mutually agreed upon date/time and no mark penalty will apply. If the absence is deemed unexcused, the Instructor will work on a case-by-case basis with the student, the Program Head, and the Associate Dean to determine next steps. Unexcused absences may result in a mark of ‘0’ for the missed assessment.

Late arrival to a Midterm, Skills Assessment, or Lab Exam: Students will be admitted to the assessment up to the halfway point of the assessment timing. No extra time will be permitted and the assessment will be graded in full. If they arrive after the halfway point, they will not be admitted to the assessment. Instead, an alternate time for the assessment will be offered. The final grade for that assessment will be no higher than 60%.

If a student is absent from a scheduled lab session, and the absence is deemed excused, every effort will be made to have the student make up the lab activities. However, due to scheduling and lab supply conflicts, this is often not possible. Excused absences will have no mark penalty; unexcused absences will result in a mark of ‘0’ for that lab session’s activities.

Absent from the Final Exam: If a student is ill or otherwise unable to write the Final Exam at the scheduled time, the Instructor will work with the student, the Program Head, and the Associate Dean to determine next steps. An alternate exam may be offered or another grading policy (e.g. aegrotat) may be applied – see BCIT policy 5301 PR Grading (article 2.2).

Note: The MLS program is currently reviewing BCIT policies around missed exams, laboratory sessions, and skills assessments. A program-specific policy may be implemented at some point during the Winter 2025 term with adequate notice.

## Other information
Use of Artificial Intelligence:

AI-based content generation tools, or AI tools, are permitted for assignments and project work. However, they are not allowed during formal assessments (e.g. midterms, lab exams, etc.). Note that AI output may include incorrect or misleading information, fabricated authors/dates/publications, faked works, or discriminatory ideas. It is your responsibility to ensure the quality, accuracy, and appropriateness of the work you submit in this course.

If you use ChatGPT (or a similar tool) to get ideas and/or partial answers for an assignment and/or to generate any text for a draft or final version of any part of an assignment, you must declare that you have used it, with a short paragraph describing the extent to which it was used, and you must save any generated text from this tool in case it is requested. You will not be penalized for using this tool, but the instructor may ask you to provide the generated text to help with grading decisions.

Material directly copied from any source (e.g. websites, books, AI tools) must be cited. Undeclared use of AI is a violation of academic integrity as it presents work or ideas created by tools or other people as your own. Failure to fully declare the use of this tool will be considered a violation of BCIT academic policy.
