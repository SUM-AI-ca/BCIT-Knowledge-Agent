### Course Description
This course focuses primarily on antimicrobials and antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST). Learners study drug classes and their generations, modes of action and target sites, usual lethality (bactericidal vs bacteriostatic), spectrum of activity, toxicity and/or hypersensitivity. The history and evolution of AST in modern medicine is introduced. Current AST techniques, conventional and automated methods, as well as the screening and confirmation of mechanisms of antimicrobial resistance, are taught and practiced in the laboratory sessions. Reporting strategies recommended by the Clinical Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) are introduced. In preparation for the next course, Specimen Processing in the Microbiology Laboratory is introduced. Theory and laboratory practice emphasize safety and quality control. Collaborative laboratory workshops and assignments enhance critical thinking, problem solving, and team-based interpersonal skills. Learning is assessed through online learning activities, unit exams, laboratory practical activities and workshops, along with formal laboratory and theory exams.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- perform, interpret and report antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST) set-up by each of the following methods: macro and micro-broth dilution, disc diffusion and gradient diffusion.
- recognize the potential for antimicrobial resistance (AMR) and set up, read, interpret and report common antimicrobial resistance mechanisms detected in select organisms.
- validate the results of the AST of common clinically significant organisms using calibrators, standards and quality control practices specific to the practice of clinical microbiology.
- recognize the role of the clinical laboratory in infection control, antimicrobial stewardship and regional and national AMR surveillance.
- practice safe laboratory techniques, including aseptic technique and biohazard spill cleanup, in the processing and testing of infectious materials including bacterial culture materials.
- work in an organized manner, prioritizing testing into routine, urgent and critical categories.
- maintain legible, accurate records of work performance, being mindful of medico-legal parameters.
- perform all laboratory testing in a safe, efficient and resourceful manner.

### Attendance Requirements
The following statements are in accordance with BCIT Policies 5101, 5102, 5103, and 5104, and their accompanying procedures. To review these policies and procedures, please refer to: https://www.bcit.ca/about/administration/policies.shtml
Regular attendance and punctuality are important to the total student learning experience. While attendance in lectures is not monitored it is strongly recommended that students attend all lectures. The lecture sessions are the first run through of the theory material and provide an opportunity for the student to ask for clarification of complex material and to hear first hand how theory content relates to course lab sessions. The most successful students in this challenging discipline attend lectures regularly. Attendance to scheduled lab sessions is mandatory and absences are documented by instructional staff.
In case of illness or other unavoidable absence from theory assessments, lab sessions and exams, students must communicate as soon as possible with the Instructor, indicating the reason for the absence. Students who are seeking accommodation for a medical absence must have a BCIT-approved medical certificate substantiating the reason for the absence. For other absences the student should be prepared to provide appropriate supporting documentation. Excessive absences may result in failure or withdrawal from this course and/or the Program. Extra time will be provided if possible, but is not guaranteed. The exam will be graded in full. If the student arrives after the halfway point, they will not be admitted to the assessment. ### Other information
Evaluation – Theory:
- Self-Assessment Quizzes have been created to align with each teaching section and are designed to assist students in assessing their study progress. These are built into the Learning Hub and are only available for a limited time period. Three attempts of each assessment is permitted, with the last attempt being captured as the mark. These self-assessment activities are factored into the final course grade.
- Term Exams : two smaller assessments are run rather than a single midterm. These are tentatively scheduled in Weeks 5 & 11 of the term. Subject to change as required.
- The Final Theory Exam will be scheduled during BCIT’s formal Exam Week (Week 15).
- All exams will take place on campus and are paper based, in-person exams. These are not open book exams, and no online or printed references are to be accessed. Any violation of these parameters may result in academic penalties as outlined in BCIT Policy 5104 — Student Code of Academic Integrity.
Evaluation – Laboratory:
- Marked Lab Reports are the reports generated by each student during the teaching lab sessions. These are marked to a defined rubric and are returned to the student in a timely manner for review.
- Learning Activities [formerly called ‘Workshops’] are out-of-class activities that bridge theory and practical lab content. They cover concepts and scenarios that cannot be offered within the laboratory sessions. These are a blend of paper based and virtual activities and may occasionally be incorporated into the weekly lab sessions.
- Lab Practical Exam is a timed station exam and will run following parameters established for this exam format in the two prerequisite microbiology courses, MLSC 1309 and MLSC 2309. Due to time constraints there is no formal skills assessment in this course.
Academic Integrity:
Violations of academic integrity, including dishonesty in assignments, examinations, lecture or laboratory sessions or other academic activities are prohibited and will be handled in accordance with BCIT Policy 5104 — Student Code of Academic Integrity, and accompanying procedures: https://www.bcit.ca/files/pdf/policies/5104.pdf
Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI)
AI-based content generation tools, or AI tools, are permitted for out-of-class assignments and project work. However, they are not to be used during in-class work or during formal assessments including term exams, the skills assessment, the practical lab exam or the final theory exam. Note that AI output can include incorrect or misleading information, fabricated authors/dates/publications, faked works or may incorporate discriminatory ideas. It is the student’s responsibility to ensure the quality, accuracy and appropriateness of any work submitted.
If ChatGPT (or a similar tool) is used to prompt for ideas and/or create partial answers and/or to generate any text for a draft or final version of any part of an assignment, students must declare that it has been used, describing the extent to which it was used. Any generated text must be saved and available for instructional staff review if requested. There is no penalty for using tools as specified here, but an instructor may ask for the generated text to assist with grading.
As with other reference materials AI tools must be cited. Undeclared use of AI is considered violation of academic integrity as it presents work, ideas created by AI or other people as your own. Failure to fully declare the use of this tool will be considered a violation of BCIT academic policy.
