## Course Description
Students will build on the patient care skills learnt in HSIP 1000 and learn specific topics relevant to MRI Technologists such as: patient documentation, medico-legal issues, infection control and materials handling. Students will also become familiar with pharmaceutical agents commonly used in the MRI department and the basics of intravenous (IV) insertion. Students will be able to practice starting IV’s in a simulated setting, which will mimic the skills needed for an MRI Technologist to prepare a patient for contrast administration.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Describe and discuss the legal implications of patient documentation. The following concepts, skills, and issues are used to support this Outcome:
- utilizing and adapting written, verbal and non-verbal communication skills
- contents of requisitions and patient charts including thorough complete documentation
- methods and criteria for validation of consent and medical history
- legal and regulatory requirements
- pre-procedure and post-procedure documentation and transfer of care
- Describe and apply infection control and materials handling practices in a healthcare setting. The following concepts, skills, and issues are used to support this Outcome:
- effects of pandemics on healthcare
- patients with compromised immunity
- immunity and immunization practices
- hepatitis and human immunodeficiency virus (HIV)
- procedure for patients with compromised immunity
- safe handling of sharps and contaminated and biohazardous materials
- post exposure protocol
- Workplace Hazardous Materials Information System (WHMIS) including safety data sheets (SDS), labeling and symbols
- disposal of waste in healthcare facilities
- Explain the scope of the pharmaceutical role of the MRI technologist. The following concepts, skills, and issues are used to support this Outcome:
- drugs that act on the central nervous system
- drugs that act on the endocrine system
- drug standards/regulations, pharmacokinetics and the effects of medications on the human body
- methods, routes, and equipment used for drug administration
- medication order in written and verbal forms, appropriate precautions, measurements, and documentation including dealing with medication orders
- drugs used in the treatment of pain
- Describe the anatomy and physiology of the vascular system as related to IV insertion. The following concepts, skills, and issues are used to support this Outcome:
- vessels most commonly utilized
- indications and contraindications
- common complications
- Prepare equipment for IV insertion. The following concepts, skills, and issues are used to support this Outcome:
- catheter
- IV solution bag
- tape, bandages, gauze and other needed supplies
- pump injectors
- Perform and monitor IV insertions under supervision. The following concepts, skills, and issues are used to support this Outcome:
- site preparation
- needle insertion
- solution bag hook up and evaluation of the IV
- maintenance of the IV site
- removal and disposal of used IV supplies
- administration of contrast media
