## Course Description
This course introduces the histological principles, instrumentation and procedures required in the clinical Anatomical Pathology laboratory. Students will study human microscopic tissue recognition (microanatomy), grossing, fixation, tissue processing, decalcification, paraffin embedding, microtomy, histology safety and histology quality management. Laboratory activities will include microscopic evaluation of digital tissue sections, grossing, embedding, microtomy, and methods for routine morphological assessment, quality control, quality assurance and troubleshooting. Evaluation methods include assessment of knowledge via quizzes and exams and performance of practical skills via skills assessments.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Recognize a variety of surgical or procedural specimens received in a diagnostic Anatomical Pathology laboratory.
- Understand the basic procedures of grossing a variety of tissues based on tissue type and structural differences.
- Correctly gross a variety of tissue types following protocols for different anatomical structures.
- Correctly orient and embed a variety of human tissues in paraffin wax for microtomy sectioning.
- Explain the theories of tissue recognition, grossing, fixation, tissue processing, microtomy, embedding, frozen sections, and decalcification.
- Assess the quality of fixation, tissue processing and microtomy and recommend corrective actions for recognized issues.
- Operate and maintain a rotary microtome to correctly produce 10 diagnostically acceptable sections from 10 different blocks in one hour.
- Recognize and state the causes and corrections for all common microtomy artifacts.
- Apply general laboratory safety rules and considerations specific to Anatomical Pathology with the ability to recognize, describe, and prevent safety hazards.
- Correctly recognize gross and microscopic sections, including specific cellular details, of normal basic tissue types including muscle, epithelium, connective tissue, nervous tissue, bone, cartilage, respiratory tissue, circulatory tissue, and skin.
- Understand and discuss the role of quality control and quality assurance within a quality management system in an Anatomical Pathology laboratory.

Histology 2330 Statement on AI Usage:

*Note: different courses at BCIT may have different AI statements. Please read the following and understand the expectations for AI usage in this course.

All assignments and lab reports should be your own original work, created for this class. This is because it is essential to build your personal skills in interpreting results, brainstorming, critical thinking, analysis and communication. Your exams, assignments, and lab activities reflect your current thinking, showcase your ability to produce work at a professional standard, highlight your strengths, and identify areas for improvement. It is important for your success that your level of understanding, ability and progress is clear. AI (artificial intelligence) tools cannot learn for you. For the reasons given above, AI tools may never be used to complete exams, assignments, or lab reports in this course. The policy on plagiarism applies to work or ideas created by tools or other people as your own.
