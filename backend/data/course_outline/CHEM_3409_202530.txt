## Course Description
This course continues from CHEM 2409. The nomenclature, properties, preparations, and reactions of phenols, ethers, epoxides, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters, amines, and amides will be examined. Organohalogens in the environment, mass spectrometry, lipids, amino acids and peptides, and carbohydrates will also be covered. Laboratory work includes the synthesis of organic compounds, isolation and purification techniques, and qualitative analysis using IR spectroscopy, UV spectroscopy, and GC/MS.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Name and draw structures of phenols, ethers, epoxides, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters, amines, and amides.
- Discuss chemical properties of phenol including its acidity and how it functions as an antioxidant.
- Describe methods of making ethers, epoxides, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters, and amides.
- Draw structures of the products in reactions of epoxides, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters, amines, amides, and monosaccharides.
- Describe the dangers of handling ethers.
- Describe the importance of organochlorine compounds in industry and the environmental dangers of organochlorine compounds.
- Describe the use of formaldehyde in the production of phenol-formaldehyde resins.
- Describe the types of ions produced in the mass spectral analysis of an organic compound.
- Determine the structure and mass of fragment ions produced by common cleavage pathways of the molecular ion in mass spectrometry.
- Draw the structures of lipids, amino acids, peptides, and carbohydrates.
- Describe the chemical and physical properties of lipids.
- Discuss how soaps function in emulsifying oils.
- Describe the major classes of carbohydrates.
- Design multistep syntheses to prepare required products from given starting materials.
- Carry out the synthesis, isolation, and purification of organic compounds in the laboratory using appropriate techniques and equipment.
- Identify organic compounds from their infrared spectra.
