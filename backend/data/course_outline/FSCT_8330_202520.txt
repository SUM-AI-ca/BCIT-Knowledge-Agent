### Course Description
This course covers firearms and ammunition, serial number restoration, physical matching and comparison, range determination, gunshot wounds and exhibit handling. Emphasis is placed on firearm and tool mark identification to increase understanding of the uniqueness of tool marks as an aid in investigating and solving crimes.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Explain the general theory of the operation of firearms.
- Demonstrate the importance of evidence preservation and submission in forensic investigation of firearms and toolmarks.
- Explain the principle of serial number restoration.
- Prepare tests for comparison with unknown exhibit samples and explain the principle of tool mark generation.
- Describe factors that make a tool mark unique.
- Differentiate between class and accidental characteristics and explain the role each plays in the identification of tool marks and fired ammunition components.
- Explain the theory of firearm identification.
### Learning Resources
No text book is required for this course , however having access to the Journal of Forensic Sciences will be very helpful, and recommended readings from the journal will be included in some lectures, yet are not mandatory.
### Course Goals
This course covers the principles behind firearm and toolmark examination. Students will learn proper terminology of all things firearm related, they will learn the basics of how firearms, firearm accessories and ammunition function, and how to classify them according to the Canadian Criminal Code . Related analyses such as serial number restoration, trigger pull and shock discharge testing, and range determination will also be covered. The process of microscopic comparison as it relates to expended ammunition components and toolmarks will be covered in detail.
A variety of assignments will be administered such as in-class tests, weekly definitions and in-class midterm and exam.

### Course topics
History of Firearms
Safety, firearm examination, parts and types of firearms, actions, operating systems and cycle of fire
Safeties, magazines, markings, history of ammunition and current ammunition
Criminal Code of Canada as it relates to firearms and firearm accessories
Serial number restoration, trigger pull/shock discharge, sound level meter testing, chemical spot testing
AFTE Theory of Identification, Gunther & Gunther's 4 Factors, class and individual characteristics, post-manufacturing defects, subclass characteristics and manufacturing techniques
Toolmark examination and physical matching
Range Determination
