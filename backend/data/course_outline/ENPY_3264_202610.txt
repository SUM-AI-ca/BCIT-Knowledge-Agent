### Course Description
Evoked potentials and intraoperative monitoring techniques are additional modalities performed by Electroneurophysiology technologists. Students are initially introduced to the use and recording of visual evoked potentials (VEP), auditory evoked potentials (ABR) and somatosensory evoked potentials (SSEP) as they pertain to clinical evaluation of the visual, auditory and sensory pathways. Emphasis will be on recording and stimulation parameters as well as waveform interpretation but will also touch on clinical correlation. The second half of this course will expand on this topic to include the use of evoked potentials and other modalities (EEG, motor evoked potentials and electromyography) in the operating room. Emphasis will be on recording and stimulation parameters of all modalities as well as interpretation and application during various neuro and vascular surgeries. Physiological and non-physiological factors affecting intraoperative monitoring and when it is important to alert the surgeon of changes during monitoring will be evaluated. This course is offered in an online format during students’ clinical period and it is expected that the learning will enhance their conceptualization of the breadth of ENPY technologists’ practice. Assessment includes on-line participation, assignments and written examinations.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Define all recording and stimulation parameters for performing evoked potentials (VEP, ABR, SSEP) as per ABRET (American Board of Registered Electroneurophysiology Technologists)
- Define generators and anatomy of visual, auditory, and sensory pathways.
- Describe routine normal VEP, ABR and SSEP Waveforms, including obligate peaks, latencies and amplitude as well as described troubleshooting techniques for a variety of patient populations and clinical situations.
- Prepare a concise and complete technical interpretation/report of a normal and abnormal VEP, ABR, and SSEP.
- Describe and recognize abnormal EP activity and various disease processes causing abnormalities.
- Define the technologist’s role in the operating room.
- Define the roles of each member of the surgical team.
- Describe techniques used to maintain sterility in the operating room.
- Describe the uses and techniques of monitoring modalities in terms of pre-operative, intraoperative and post-operative phases including EEG, ABR, SEP, EMG, and MEP.
- Describe how anaesthetic techniques and medications are used in the operating room and how they affect intraoperative monitoring.
- Describe how non-physiologic and physiologic factors that are common in the operating room affect intraoperative monitoring.
- Define alarm criteria for alerting the surgical team of monitoring changes.
