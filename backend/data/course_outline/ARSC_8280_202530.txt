### Course Description
​The Graduating Project is comprised of two parts: ARSC 8280 is the pre-design and technical research that provides the basis for ARSC 8800 design work to follow. This course provides a structured approach to conduct the research, complete the contextual analysis, and initiate the critical thinking that lays the groundwork for individual Graduating Project design. The course will be delivered as a series of lectures, seminars, on-site investigations and assignments to guide the investigation process. Students will work independently, in teams and with consultants to prepare a pre-design brief – an essential part of the design process for complex architectural projects. Students will consider history, sustainability, codes and regulations, quality assurance and project management to develop an effective proposal.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:
- Demonstrate effective research skills into essential topics.
- Retrieve necessary information from a variety of sources (including libraries, the Internet, municipalities, the site, the client and surveys).
- Interpret information for effective use.
- Assess advantages and disadvantages in similar projects (e.g. cost, site use, environment, building form, construction options).
- Analyze a site in regards to; natural systems, site forces, user patterns, eco- and construction issues.
- Manage environmental assessment tools.
- Engage in critique and incorporate feedback.
- Conclude the project through independent and team work.
- Compose a complete pre-design brief (including introduction, problem statement, objectives, topic history, precedent studies, design criteria, program, site analysis, conclusion, bibliography and endnotes).
- Make a professional and informative project proposal through graphic and oral presentation(s).

### Course Goals
This course provides a structured approach to pre-design research, analysis and response. Lectures, workshops and processes simulate professional practice for complex building pre-design. Working independently or in teams with industry consultants and stakeholders, students will navigate regulatory frameworks to articulate site, program and development parameters for the future comprehensive design project. The outcome of the course is an architectural pre-design brief ready for implementation in the project context (including authorities having jurisdiction, sociocultural issues, climate resilience, local design guidelines, and urban design initiatives). Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Demonstrate advanced research skills, documenting general context and project-specific information (including demographics, social equity, and contemporary urban issues).
- Analyze a complex urban site (including current and historical context, Indigenous territories, ecological species, systems, and urban design).
- Conduct research into program precedents and stakeholder requirements.
- Identify appropriate architectural precedents to inform future design work.
- Work in teams to interpret national, provincial and regional regulatory opportunities and constraints applicable to future development on the project site (including accessibility, sustainability requirements, ecosystems management).
- Draft an individual predesign brief documenting research findings and requirements for the comprehensive project.
- Simulate professional best practice individually and collaboratively.
