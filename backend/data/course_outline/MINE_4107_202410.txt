## Course Description
Reviews the mineral exploration industry and discusses common exploration techniques and the interpretation of exploration data. The business of exploration is outlined including the roles of different participants, ethics in exploration, the sources of exploration funding and the regulatory requirements of Canadian stock exchanges and securities commissions. The objectives and strategies employed in mining exploration are described including the design of sampling programs. Common exploration techniques and their applications are discussed including mapping, prospecting, remote sensing, geochemistry, geophysics and diamond drilling. Case studies and an exploration design project form an integral part of the course.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:

- Describe the different participants in the exploration industry and their roles [1].
- Outline the sources of funding for exploration and how the exploration business is structured and regulated [1,3,9,10,11].
- Outline the role of the professional technologist, geologist and engineering in mineral exploration [1,8,9,10].
- Discuss the conditions that must exist for a company to consider a given jurisdiction favourable for exploration [1,2,3,9,11].
- List the geological characteristics of different types of exploration properties (grass roots, advanced, prefeasibility) [1,3].
- Describe the procedures for acquiring and maintaining a mineral claims in Canada [1,3,8,9,10].
- Explain the components of an exploration assessment report [1,3,4,6,7,8,9,10].
- Describe exploration techniques including, remote sensing, mapping, trenching, sampling, geochemistry, geophysics, and drilling [1].
- Outline common QA/QC procedures for exploration programs [8,10].
- Outline the characteristics of typical exploration property option agreements [8,11].
- Interpret the results of common exploration data such as geochemical and geophysical data [1].
- Design an effective exploration program including laying out surveys, preparing schedules and budgets and proposing follow-up work [2,3,4,6,7,8,10,11,12].
