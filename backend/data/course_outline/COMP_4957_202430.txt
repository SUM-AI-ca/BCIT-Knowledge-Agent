## Course Description
​In this course, students will apply software engineering concepts for developing solutions to real-life problems. This course will focus on software requirements (functional and non-functional), technical feasibility, and design patterns that are intended to offer standard solutions to problems, make the code more flexible and efficient, and reduce complexity. The design patterns discussed in this course are: concurrency, creational, structural and behavioural. Students will be introduced to architectural patterns that are larger in scope and describe the overall pattern followed by an entire system. Students will be also introd​uced to AI topics that are emergent in the software engineering field, for example how AI is incorporated in the software development process (e.g., coding, testing and software quality), user modeling, and AI-based critical issues related to ethics, privacy and security. ​ During the term, students will plan, design and implement a large project. The project is intended to give students the chance to experience the work and the responsibilities of a real-life project. Students will learn to work in a large team, manage or report to a manager, keep the deadlines, take responsibilities and have initiative.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Explain the software life cycle and project development methodologies.
- Explain the software requirements (functional and non-functional), feasibility, and software engineering practices.
- Explain the use of design patterns in software design and architecture.
- Compare and contrast creational patterns (e.g., factory, abstract factory, singleton, and prototype), structural patterns (e.g., composite, decorator, adapter, and proxy), behavioural patterns (e.g., null object, template method, iterator, and strategy) and concurrency patterns (e.g., active object, monitor object, and thread-specific storage) in software construction.
- Explain how AI is incorporated in the software development process (e.g., coding, testing, and software quality).
- Explain AI-based user modeling techniques.
- Discuss AI-based critical issues related to ethics, privacy and security.
- Contrast and compare architectural patterns (e.g., layered, client-server, microkernel, microservices, MVC, pipe-filter, event-bus, broker, peer-to-peer, blackboard, and interpreter).
- Apply creational, structural and behavioral patterns in software design.
- Apply concurrency in software design including processes, threads, multi-threaded processes, inter-process communications, low-level file and memory access, and multi-tasking (using both threads and processes).
- Apply AI-based software engineering techniques (user modeling, coding, testing, and software quality).
- Design and implement applications involving the above topics.
- Design and implement a large project.
- Take responsibilities (including management responsibilities) for the project planning, feasibility, design, implementation, integration and testing.​

## Course Specific Requirements
Lectures: Lectures will be interactive involving practical exercises and student participation.

Labs: Students will actively participate in in-class planning, design, coding, testing and integration activities.

Assignments: There will be four or five take-home and/or in-class assignments.

Project: Using an agile methodology, students will design, implement and test a large project involving the entire class (set) of students. Students will perform requirement analysis, software requirements specification, will conduct a technical feasibility analysis, identify the best architecture of their project, and design unit tests. The project is intended to give students the chance to experience the work and the responsibilities of a real-life project and will prepare them for working in large teams to build quality software. The class (set) will be organized into teams of 3 – 6 students with team leaders and a project manager. Students will learn to work in a team, communicate effectively, manage or report to a manager, keep deadlines, take responsibilities and have initiative.

Exams: There are a midterm exam and a final exam in this course.

## Course Schedule and Assignments
Week 1 Introduction to software development methodologies. Agile methodology. Project brainstorming (1).

Week 2 Software requirement documents, formal specifications, technical feasibility. Identifying the users' needs and profile. Ethics and sustainability. AI-based critical issues related to ethics, privacy and security. Project brainstorming (2).

Week 3 Universal usability. Applying principles of accesibility, interactivity, user-centered design and visibility. Designing the project architecture (1). Prototyping. Interface design. Team formation.

Week 4 Architectural patterns: (e.g., event-bus, layered, MVC, client-server, master-slave, pipe-filter, peer-to-peer). Designing the project architecture (2). Usability testing (1).

Week 5 Introduction to AI techniques used in developing intelligent systems. User modeling. Milestone 1: Project proposal, prototype and feasibility.

Week 6 AI-based user modeling techniques (1). Introduction to Bayesian networks.

Week 7 AI-based user modeling techniques (2). Adaptive Interfaces. Concurrency patterns (e.g., active object, monitor object, thread pool pattern, thread-specific storage). Synchronization mechanisms (semaphores and monitors). Midterm Review.

Week 8 Midterm week

Week 9 Synchronization and inter-process communications (IPC) patterns. Usability testing (2). Milestone 2: Design and code.

Week 10 Design patterns: creational, structural and behavioural (part 1).

Week 11 Design patterns: creational, structural and behavioural (part 2). Inversion of control through composition pattern: dependency injection. Evaluation of the project design and functional requirements. Usability testing (3). Milestone 3: Revised design and code

Week 12 Evaluation of project's critical issues related to non-functional requirements (ethics, privacy, security and usability). Project integration and testing (1).

Week 13 Project integration and testing (2). Milestone 4: Integration and Testing.

Week 14 Preparing the presentation. Final Project submission. Project presentation.

Week 15 Final Exam
