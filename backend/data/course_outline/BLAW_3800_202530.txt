### Course Description
The course provides an essential understanding of the Canadian statutory framework in general, emphasizing the law applicable to employment relationships in British Columbia. The intricate interface between various statutes and common law principles is explained against the backdrop of the employment contract as the foundation of a non-unionized working environment. Students are introduced to specialized areas of employment law such as human rights, privacy at work, minimum standards and related rights, and occupational health and safety, focusing on statutory compliance and the associated legal implications of non-compliance. Statutory principles that permeate various stages of individual employment relationships in British Columbia, i.e., the hiring process, reciprocal rights and obligations of employers and employees during the employment relationship, termination of employment, and post-employment obligations, are discussed in detail as to assist students to act within the parameters of governing legislation and appreciate the practical application of salient legal principles via examples from case law.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Explain the interface between statutes and common law principles regulating employment relationships in British Columbia.
- Describe the statutory obligations during different stages of individual employment relationships.
- Describe the importance and advantage of having well-drafted policies in place.
- Analyze common law contractual principles and utilize contractual clauses to protect an employer’s interests.
- Apply the complexities of fixed-term employment contracts and strategies to prevent related legal complications.
- Distinguish between the laws that govern individual employment relationships and the legal framework that comes into play in unionized working environments in British Columbia.
- Explain how to assist employers in implementing dispute resolution procedures that comply with legal requirements.
- Describe inclusive working environments and prevent unfair discriminatory practices.
- Identify accommodation requirements that comply with related legal obligations.
- Describe the advantages and complexities of hybrid working arrangements and strategies to prevent potential legal complications.
- Identify general legal risks and use preventative measures to limit legal exposure.
- Conduct legal research, identify new statutory amendments, and stay abreast of the latest trends in employment law.

### Guidelines for School of Business + Media
Attendance: As set out in BCIT Policy 5101 Student Regulations , regular attendance in lectures and labs is integral to student success. Therefore, attendance in class is monitored. Students are responsible for communicating with instructors proactively about both potentially approved and unexcused absences. It is the student’s responsibility to work with their instructor to find an effective means to complete work missed due to both approved and unexcused absences. Students who are seeking accommodation for a medical absence must have a BCIT-approved medical certificate . For other absences, students must be prepared to provide appropriate supporting documentation. If absences are the result of a medical or other accommodation, notification from Accessibility Services personnel will be accepted in lieu of a doctor’s note, on an interim basis. Additionally, attendance for certain assignments, tests, projects, team or group work, safety or equipment training or other course activities such as work integrated learning, as well as participation in discussion groups or other online activities may be deemed mandatory by instructors and may be evaluated as a component of a course in categories such as participation or professionalism.. A student who has ceased to attend a course without explanation before the withdrawal deadline (two-thirds of the way through the course) will receive a Vanished (V) grade. The V grade is calculated into the GPA as a 0 (zero). These requirements are set out in accordance with both BCIT Policy 5101 Student Regulations and BCIT Policy 5103 Student Evaluation More information regarding withdrawal deadlines is available at Academic Dates & Deadlines . Academic Integrity and Use of Artificial Intelligence : The unauthorized use of Artificial Intelligence tools (such as ChatGPT) for any part of student work in this course is prohibited, and, when identified by your instructor, will result in an academic integrity violation. See BCIT Policy 5104 Student Code of Academic Integrity. A violation of academic integrity may result in one or more of the following consequences:
- A written warning;
- Required resubmission of the work in question for equal or lower grade value;
- A lower grade on the work submitted (i.e. a penalty applied);
- A failing grade on the work submitted; or
- An academic misconduct letter being placed on the student’s record.
