## Course Description
This course builds upon the basics of business communication techniques taught in COMM 1112 Communications for Television and Video Production. It emphasizes the writing and research skills needed by professionals in television and video production. Students will develop writing skills by writing industry-specific production proposals and will develop research skills as they work their way through units on effective reading, time management, critical thinking, and advanced research techniques. Topics covered include proposals, persuasive messages, pitching, and industry-specific research on production funding. Also, students will receive introductory training on basic employment skills.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Use effective communication skills when interacting with clients, peers, and supervisors.
- Write and present a persuasive production proposal.
- Create one-page production promotional sheet.
- Use production pitching techniques to persuade a producer or client to approve a production project.
- Perform research and report on broadcasters, film/TV project funding sources and applications and other markets for production proposals.
- Research story ideas and prepare rudimentary questions for one-on-one on camera interviews.
- Examine and employ the use of logic, emotion, and ethos in persuasive appeals.
- Analyse persuasive strategies in documents, advertising, scripts, and business plans.
- Perform rudimentary research on industry employers.
- Conduct oneself effectively in an informational interview to begin establishing a network.
- Write effective résumés and cover letters.
- Conduct oneself properly in a job interview.
- Critically evaluate information to determine if it is accurate, relevant, and complete, and to use critical thinking to develop strong arguments.

BCIT Student Regulations Policy 5101 will be enforced. Attendance will be taken at the beginning of each session. Students not present at that time will be recorded as absent. Students may not miss more than 10% of class time to successfully complete Comm 2212.

BCST/Comm 2212 Policy:

Instructors will issue mark deductions in order to address student attendance issues.

These deductions will apply to the final grade after all other marks have been calculated.

Students are issued a 2% deduction from their overall final grade per occurrence as an indication they have been late for scheduled class activities without notifying the course instructor(s) prior to the start of the class via email or text message and obtaining instructor approval for the lateness. Students are issued a 4% deduction from their overall final grade per occurrence as an indication they have been absent from scheduled class activities without having been excused in writing by the course instructor(s). Advance permission to be absent or late may be granted at the discretion of the course instructor(s) in conjunction with the Television & Video Production Faculty, based on a written request submitted by email to all course instructor(s) a minimum of 7 calendar days prior to the absence.

Deductions for absences may be rescinded if medical documentation is provided within 7 calendar days of the student’s return. Repeated absences for any reason may result in additional course-related activities being assigned to ensure relevant student competence.

Example: Student has a final grade of 80%, but was late three times and has one unexcused absence. These would result in a 10% deduction resulting in a final grade of 70%
