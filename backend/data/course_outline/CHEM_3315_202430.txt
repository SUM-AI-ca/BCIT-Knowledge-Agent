## Course Description
Surveys the various classes of organic compounds likely to be encountered in the workplace. Naming, structure, chemical and physical properties, industrial uses, toxicity and occupational hazards are emphasised. Practical work provides experience with organic compounds and processes.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Predict whether industrially important organic compounds will be solids, liquids or gases at room temperature and pressure.
- Assess the fire and explosion hazards of selected organic and inorganic chemicals.
- Assess compatibility for the storage of selected organic and inorganic chemicals.
- Predict the solubility characteristics of industrially important organic compounds.
- Identify industrially important organic compounds by commonly used names and by IUPAC (systematic) names.
- Predict the chemical reactivity of organic compounds based on their chemical structures.
- Give examples of industrially important organic compounds which are suspected carcinogens.
- Describe the toxic effects of acute and chronic exposure to selected, industrially important organic chemicals.
- Compare the vapor pressure of organic liquids.
- Describe the industrial uses of selected organic compounds.
- Explain how infrared spectrophotometry can be used to determine the presence of functional groups and to identify the compound which may be present in a gas or liquid sample.
- Explain how thin layer chromatography and gas chromatography can be used to identify the unknown compounds present in a gas or liquid mixture.
