## Course Description
This course introduces students to the principles and structure of specific mathematical concepts as outlined in the STCW Code for Watchkeeping Officers. The course builds on mathematical fundamentals and principles to support nautical technical subjects such as astro navigation, stability, and cargo work.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course the student will be able to:

- Execute basic arithmetical operations of whole numbers, fractions, decimals, and ratios.
- Solve problems of proportion and variation (direct, inverse, and joint).
- Perform linear interpolation and extrapolation.
- Simplify algebraic expressions including expressions involving power and roots.
- Perform algebraic manipulation and transpose equations to solve for the unknown.
- Solve simultaneous linear equations and quadratic equations.
- Differentiate between absolute error and relative error and calculate percentage error in areas and volume due to errors in linear measurements.
- Graph ordered pairs and extract values from graphs of ship’s data, and graphically solve simultaneous equations.
- Using a compass and straight edge, carry out basic geometric construction including triangles and its various centres.
- Explore the properties of congruent and similar triangles.
- Identify parts of a circle and their properties, and calculate areas of sectors and segments of a circle.
- Determine the values of the sine, cosine, tangent, and their inverse trigonometric ratios (sec, cosec, cot) of any angle.
- Apply Pythagoras’ formula to solve problems involving right triangles.
- Solve nautical problems involving bearing, distance, and elevation using sine and cosine laws.
- Calculate the perimeter, area, surface area and volume of cubes, rectangular boxes, spheres, cylinders, cones, and pyramids.
- Draw graphs of trigonometrical functions over the range –360° to +360°.
- Identify parts of ellipse and hyperbola.
- Collect, analyse, interpret, and calculate central tendency and dispersion of the various statistical data.

## Course Specific Requirements
Exam Attempts

Students who were unsuccessful in their final evaluation of a course are eligible for one (1) rewrite of their final examination. It is the responsibility of the student to contact the Cadet Program Assistant to schedule their re-examination. A student must book a minimum of ten (10) days prior to the posted re-examination date by emailing marine_exams@bcit.ca.

Nautical Science diploma students must complete and pass all term courses before enrolling in the next academic term (e.g., students must have passing grades in all Term 1 courses prior to enrolling in Term 3 academic courses).

Students in Term 5 have twelve (12) months from the end of their program to complete and pass a final evaluation rewrite.
