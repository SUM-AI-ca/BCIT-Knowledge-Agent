## Course Description
This course examines in detail the contemporary and complex interrelationships of man, his health, and the environment in relationship to maintaining, securing, and delivering an adequate and safe supply of drinking water for small and large scale water supply and distribution systems. Chemical and bacteriological sample results are interpreted. The steps used in investigating and controlling a waterborne illness are also presented.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Identify the major biological, chemical, and physical hazards and illnesses associated with drinking water in Canada
- Describe the public health risks associated with a waterline break with emphasis on the appropriate response to a waterline break
- Describe events/conditions that would contribute to the issuance of a boil water order or drinking water advisory
- Describe the public health significance of drinking water orders/advisories
- Describe the various precautions and actions required of various facilities (e.g. dentist office, school, private residence, long-term care home) in response to a drinking water order/advisory
- Design a potable water sampling program given a site and situation
- Explain the types of guidelines, standards, regulations and objectives associated with drinking water in Canada
- Describe the equipment used in water treatment facilities/plants
- Describe types of equipment used to treat water for small and private systems
- Explain what cross connections are and their public health significance
- Explain what a cross connection control program is and its importance for drinking water
- Describe the physical, chemical and biological processes used in drinking water treatment plants
- Describe water treatment processes used for small and private systems
- Describe point of use and point of entry water treatment equipment with emphasis on their limitations
- Explain the components of a potable water distribution system, with emphasis on associated hazards
- Explain the multi-barrier approach for drinking water systems, including the types of barriers
- Explain what disinfection by-products are and how they are formed in drinking water
- Explain the public health significance of disinfection by-products in drinking water
- Explain Contact Time (CT) and how it relates to water treatment
- Describe log reduction and how it relates to water treatment
- Identify national, provincial and municipal legislation that oversees surface, ground and coastal water qualities
- Compare large and private water treatment methods and their distribution and storage systems
- Identify funding issues, cost benefit analysis, and how to deal with resistance for water treatment.
- Illustrate the importance of water sustainability and conservation.
- Discuss parameters to look for in a source to tap inspection of a water supplier.
