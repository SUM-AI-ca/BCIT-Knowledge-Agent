## Course Description
Provides students with a background in the science of toxicology. The course covers basic biochemistry and the effects of environmental chemical pollutants on organs and body systems. The chemicals discussed include heavy metals, pesticides, organic solvents and air pollutants.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Describe the structure and function of biologically important molecules, e.g., DNA, RNA, enzymes, ATP, neurotransmitters, lipids and carbohydrates.
- Interpret dose response relationships.
- Identify chemical pollutants which are carcinogenic, mutagenic and teratogenic.
- Describe the toxic effect of chemicals on specific organs and body systems.
- Compare the absorption, distribution, biotransformation and elimination of environmental chemical pollutants.
- Describe the acute and chronic health effects of environmental chemical pollutants.
- Relate the physical and chemical properties of the chemicals to likely routes of exposure, storage, binding and biotransformation reactions which may occur.
- Describe the biochemical mechanism of environmental chemical pollutants.
- Examine factors which affect the rate of absorption of chemicals via the GI tract, the lungs and the skin.

## Course topics
Chapter: Topics
1: Introduction Toxicology definition, terminology and its development as a science Sources of hazardous agents Functional groups and their relative toxicity Lemke´s method Importance of pKa and pKow
2: Biochemistry review Characteristics and functions of carbohydrates, amino acids, proteins, lipids and nucleic acids
3: The cell Functions of important organelles The cell membrane Transport processes Metabolism
4: Pathways to toxicity, absorption, distribution, storage and elimination Pathways to toxicity Absorption and distribution; Vd and its importance Sites of storage Routes of elimination
5: Biotransformation Chemical interactions Phase I and Phase II reactions Factors that affect biotransformation
6: Toxicity studies Significance of dose-response relationship Dose-response graphs Types of toxicity studies Probit units
7: Genetic damage caused by chemical toxicants Types of genetic damage Mutagenesis Testing for mutagens Carcinogenesis Teratogenesis Endocrine disruptors
8: Toxic metals Factors that influence toxicity of metals Mode of action Metals of major toxicological concern Metal poisoning treatment Essential metals and metals used in medicine
9: Pesticides Mode of action Organochlorine Organophosphates Carbamates Pesticides as environmental hazards
10: Food Additives and Contaminants Role of food additives Characteristics of common food additives Indirect food additives Food additives of toxicological concern Food contaminants
11: Indoor and Outdoor Air Pollutants Toxic effects of common air pollutants (organic solvents, volatile solids, asbestos, microorgamisms and radon) Types of smog
12: Industrial Chemicals Common aromatic hydrocarbons and chlorinated compounds (physical characteristics and toxicity)
