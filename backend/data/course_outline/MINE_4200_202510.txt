## Course Description
This course expands on the principles and theories of drilling and blasting in both surface and underground mining situations introduced in previous courses (i.e., MINE 2105, MINE 3105). Topics include: the theory of detonation and chemistry of explosives, performance and factors in drilling, penetration and rotation rates, selection of drilling methods, drilling automation, the properties and selection of commercial explosives, initiation and delay systems, basic blast design principles and practices, and blast fragmentation analysis. Current trends in drilling and blasting practices are considered as well as blasting damage analysis and controlled blasting and blast vibration monitoring methods. The economics of drill/blast practice and their interface with transport and crushing systems are also addressed. Case studies in blast design will be examined during the course. This course has a laboratory component where students will gain exposure to an industry standard mining software. The following topics will be covered: • Contouring and surface modeling • Blasting database • Blasting design The students will: • Create a DTM surface • Calculate the volume of solids and generate volume reports • Work with a blasting database • Create a production blast design for an open pit mine • Create a controlled blast design for an open pit ramp • Generate different blasting reports

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Discuss the physics of rock breakage to through drilling and blasting processes [1,3,5].
- Discuss, understand and select the best drilling system to use for a surface mining operation [1,2,3,4,5].
- Understand and explain the thermochemistry of explosives, oxygen balanced reactions and the detonation process [1,2,3].
- Characterize the rock mass and its blastability for a proper blast design [1,2,3].
- Understand and explain how the site-specific geology controls the blast design parameters [1,2].
- Determine the optimum explosive selection required for given ground conditions and for specific blasting applications [3].
- Understand and apply the principles of design calculation for underground drifting [3,4].
- Understand and apply the principles of design calculation for a cut box on an open pit mine [3,4].
- Design functional and efficient blast patterns [4,5,].
- Perform the blasting design check list for risk analysis and apply the proper safety practices for a mine site and initiate a monitoring program to understand and analyze the flyrock process for safety considerations [9].

## Safety equipment or protective clothing
Drilling and blasting are high-risk activities. Safety is of utmost importance and must be reinforced at all levels of training and operation.

For the drilling and blasting course, only explosive dummies will be used for demonstrations in class. No special PPE will be required in class.

However, students must demonstrate propoer care when handling explosive dummies as if they were handling live explosives.

## Course Schedule and Assignments
The course schedule (including assignments, quizzes and the final exam) is posted online, and will be discuss with the students in class at the first meeting.

Week 1: Course Outline & Introduction to Drilling and Blasting

- Course Outline and Learning Outcomes
- Role of drilling and blasting in mining.
- Overview of rock excavation methods.
- History and evolution of explosives and blasting techniques.

Week 2: Drilling Methods and Equipment

- Rotary, percussive, and rotary-percussive drilling methods.
- Drill rigs: Down-the-hole (DTH), top hammer, and rotary drills.
- Drill bit types and their selection.
- Calculation of penetration rates and drill productivity.

Week 3: Mechanics of Drilling

- Factors influencing drillability of rocks.
- Empirical formulae for predicting penetration rates.
- Flushing systems and drill deviation control.
- Maintenance and troubleshooting of drilling equipment.

Week 4: Fundamentals of Explosives

- Classification and properties of explosives.
- Characteristics of high explosives: Velocity of detonation (VOD), density, and energy.
- Thermochemistry of explosives: Oxygen balance, gas volume, and heat of explosion.
- Storage and handling of explosives.

Week 5: Initiation Systems and Accessories

- Detonators: Electric and non-electric systems.
- Detonating cord, boosters, and shock tubes.
- Delay systems and their application.
- Tie-up systems for complex blast patterns.

Week 6: Blast Design Principles

- Objectives of blast design: Fragmentation, throw, and muck pile shape.
- Key parameters: Burden, spacing, sub-drilling, and stemming.
- Empirical formulae for blast geometry.
- Site-specific considerations in blast design.

Week 7: Blast Pattern Design

- Common blast patterns: Square, staggered, and radial.
- Methods for optimizing blast patterns for specific rock conditions.
- Computer-aided tools for blast design (Deswik or Surpac Software will be used)
- Workshop: Creating and analyzing simple blast patterns. (Deswik or Surpac Software will be used)

Week 8: Timing and Tie-Up

- Importance of timing in blasting.
- Principles of delay sequencing to control vibration and fragmentation.
- Tie-up techniques for bench and production blasts.
- Case studies: Comparison of effective vs. ineffective timing.

Week 9: Rock Fragmentation and Breakage

- Mechanism of rock breakage by explosives.
- Factors affecting fragmentation: Explosive energy, delay timing, and geology.
- Measurement and evaluation of fragmentation using empirical and numerical methods.
- Importance of fragmentation in mining productivity and downstream processes.

Week 10: Environmental and Safety Considerations

- Impact of drilling and blasting on the environment: Airblast, flyrock, and vibrations.
- Ground vibration and air-overpressure: Standards and empirical prediction models.
- Blast safety protocols and risk management.
- Regulatory frameworks for blasting operations (BC's Explosives Regulation).

Week 11: Controlled Blasting Techniques

- Techniques for minimizing damage to surrounding rock.
- Pre-splitting, cushion blasting, and perimeter control.
- Applications of controlled blasting in sensitive areas.
- Case studies: Controlled blasting in underground and surface mines.

Week 12: Specialized Blasting Applications

- Blasting in specific environments: Underground, open-pit, and quarrying.
- Secondary blasting and re-blasting techniques.
- Innovations in blasting technology.

Week 13: Advanced Blast Monitoring and Evaluation

- Blast monitoring techniques: Vibration, fragmentation analysis, and 3D imaging.
- Post-blast assessment: Fragmentation analysis and muck pile evaluation.
- Workshop: Blast performance evaluation using sample data.

Week 14: Drill and Blast Project Productivity and Costing

- Blasting quantities estimation techniques for blasting project.
- Cost estimation.
- Drilling and blasting

Week 15: Quality Control & Quality Assurance

- Quality control and assurance for best results
- Other performance Factors
- Course wrap up and final review.
