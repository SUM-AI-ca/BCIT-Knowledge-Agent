### Course Description
NSNN 7300 combines theory and clinical experience to focus on individuals with chronic kidney disease (CKD) who are not on renal replacement therapy. The observational clinical experience in NSNN 7300 provides opportunities for students to interview renal clients diagnosed with CKD Stages 1-4. Students learn about the chronic kidney disease services, renal laboratory, radiological and diagnostic investigations and monitoring, nursing assessments and considerations, and introduction to renal pharmacology and commonly used agents such as anti-anemics, anti-hypertensives and diuretics. In addition to an introduction to CKD management, students will have an opportunity to examine the important role of various health care team members within the multidisciplinary team setting. Attendance at a CKD clinic is mandatory in order to facilitate optimal learning.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
As a healthcare practitioner, you are expected to follow professional standards of practice throughout all SN programs and courses. In this course, you will have opportunities to use the processes of critical thinking, systematic inquiry, communication, collaboration, leadership, professionalism, and clinical decision-making. Upon successful completion of this course, you should be able to:
- Explain transition theory as a concept relevant to clients experiencing chronic kidney disease (CKD) Stages 1-5 who are not on a renal replacement therapy.
- Apply critical thinking and life-long learning strategies to determine their own learning needs in a clinical setting.
- Describe the role of multidisciplinary team members pertaining to the management and care for clients with CKD.
- Summarize modalities of renal replacement therapy.
- Describe the principles of chronic kidney disease management aimed at maintaining or promoting a client’s current health and preparing for renal replacement therapy.
- Conduct interviews with clients in a clinical setting in order to determine the needs of clients in CKD care.
- Using the principles of adult education, assist individuals in managing their experience of CKD.
- Identify and interpret common laboratory, radiological and diagnostic investigations and monitoring results correlated to CKD.
- Using critical decision making skills, decide on an appropriate intervention for a client based on their clinical reports.
- Describe the basic concepts of renal pharmacology.
- Identify and describe the pharmacology of agents commonly used specifically in CKD care.

### Course topics
Nephrology Nursing Clinical 1: Chronic Kidney Disease
Welcome to NSNN 7300. Building upon the knowledge you have gained in NSNN 7200, this course focuses on the experiences of individuals with CKD who are not on renal replacement therapy. This course is aimed at providing you with an opportunity to develop an understanding of the goals of CKD care and the experiences of clients in order to enhance your nephrology nursing practice. This understanding will be gained from a variety of sources: access to the interprofessional or multidisciplinary team, literature review, course content, clinical observations, and client interviews. The clinical placement will be arranged by the program clinical coordinator or program head over a period of one week full-time or a period of two weeks part-time. Please note that an on-site instructor is not provided in this course; however, you will be assigned an onsite preceptor and clinical supervisor for your observational clinical experience and an online course facilitator that is available at specified times. Your clinical placement will be coordinated with the CKD clinic staff. Although this is a clinical course, there are theory components therefore you will be expected to submit two assignments, participate in online discussion forums and virtual classrooms, develop and submit a learning contract for your clinical placement, and complete a final exam at the end of the course. Modules 1 to 5 must be completed prior to scheduled clinical placement dates. This course is divided into five units:
Introduction: This is an introduction to chronic kidney disease care and the CKD clinic Module 1
Introduces the concept of transition theory and the relationship between this theory and chronic kidney disease as it directly relates to clients’ lived experiences.
Module 2
Goals of CKD care such as treatment modality preparation, education, management, and support. Discusses the concept of maintaining health, physiologic considerations, renal tests, potential complications, capacity building, the resources provided by the CKD clinic team.
Module 3 Prepares students for observational clinical experience, the scope of CKD services, and client interviews. Introduction and integration of Situated Clinical Decision Making Framework into a workbook for your clinical experience.
Module 4 Describes and discusses laboratory, diagnostic, and radiologic investigations, assessments, and monitoring related to CKD and renal function.
Module 5 Describes and discusses commonly used agents and medications in clients with chronic kidney disease Stage 1-5 who are not on renal replacement therapies.
