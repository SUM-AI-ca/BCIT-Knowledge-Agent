## Course Description
​This course will integrate species ecology and life history knowledge to introduce students to theoretical and practical components of wildlife management and stewardship in British Columbia. Topics include reviewing federal and provincial regulations that guide recreational harvest and conservation, Indigenous rights and stewardship, and the roles and objectives of government and non‐government agencies in wildlife management. Students will be introduced to experimental designs and methods used to survey wildlife species and confirm species presence or estimate population size. Students will also discuss socio‐economic factors that influence management and stewardship decisions, wildlife management strategies used in BC, current issues in the province, and strategies for mitigation.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Explain the purpose of wildlife management and how provincial and federal regulations and acts are used to manage access to and conservation of wildlife species.
- Assess and discuss legislation as it relates to Aboriginal harvest rights.
- Describe behaviours and adaptations demonstrated by wildlife and explain how these influence wildlife assessments.
- Describe techniques for wildlife surveys that are appropriate for specific species or taxa and their habitat.
- Explain different study designs used to collect data related to wildlife populations and select the appropriate method for a given species​.
- Describe ethical handling of animals, the importance of animal welfare considerations, and the requirements for safe interactions with animals in research and management.
- Evaluate objectives, ecological and socio‐economic considerations, and additional factors associated with wildlife management strategies.
- Identify the origins, signs, symptoms, and management implications of common infectious diseases, parasites, and environmental contaminants in vertebrate populations.
- Demonstrate respectful and professional behaviour that meets industry standards and expectations.

## Attendance Requirements
If you need to miss class, please (1) notify instructors with as much advanced notice as possible (at least 24 hours), and (2) arrange for a classmate to provide you with all missed handouts/notes/instructions. Students cannot miss more than 10% of class meetings without prior arrangements with the instructor. This includes missed time resulting from a late arrival – partially missed class time also counts towards the 10% and will be calculated based on time missed. A doctor’s note (using a BCIT approved medical certificate) may be required within 10 days of the missed class for any illness causing you to miss any assignments/projects/quiz/exam/lab/field trips. Unless otherwise agreed upon, medical appointments, interviews, etc. scheduled during class times are not considered excused absences. At the discretion of the instructor, you may complete the work missed or have the work prorated for excused absences.

Students who miss more than 10% of class meetings may be given a failing grade for the course/program or the student may be removed from the course/program (Policy 5101 - Student Regulations).

## Course Specific Requirements
Marks will be reduced by 10% for each 24-hour period (or fraction thereof) that an assignment is submitted beyond the due date. For instance, an assignment that would have earned 82% if submitted on time would earn 72% if submitted up to 24 hours late. If this assignment was submitted 25 hours later, it would earn 62%. The application of this penalty or alternate approaches to late submissions will be at the discretion of the course instructor.
