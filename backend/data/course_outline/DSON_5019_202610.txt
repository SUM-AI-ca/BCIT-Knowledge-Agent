### Course Description
​Cardiac Sonography Lab 1 introduces students to sonographic imaging of the heart. The focus of this course is to integrate communication, professional responsibility, patient care, ergonomics and workplace safety with the operation of sonographic equipment. Students will learn all the sonography imaging components of a complete routine echocardiogram study, including the assessment of heart structure and function. ​Topics of this course include 2D assessment, m-mode, colour and spectral Doppler assessment and quantification of the heart structure and function. Students are introduced to Inter-Professional Education (IPE) through shared activities with other BCIT SOHS students.​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Maintain professional appearance and manner. (2.1e)
- Employ universal precautions for infection control. (3.1d)
- Practice ergonomic techniques during all scanning sessions. (6.2b)
- Demonstrate reliability and adaptability. (2.3h. 2.3i)​
- Understand basic troubleshooting. (4.3b)
- Verify written, authorized directions for examination, identify self to patient and explain the examination to the patient. (1.2a,1.1a, 1.1e)
- Ensure informed patient consent, maintain privacy, confidentiality, dignity, comfort and safety when performing sonographicexaminations. (2.1e, 2.1f 3.1f)
- Assess and monitor patient's physical and mental status prior to and during examination and respond accordingly. (3.1e)
- Perform palpation of pulses, set up a 3 lead ECG and recognize components of normal sinus rhythm and common arrythmias. (3.3b, 3.3 f)
- Input patient data, select the optimal acoustic window, select the correct exam preset, and orient and manipulate the transducer to optimize the image. (4.1 b, 4.1c, 4.1d. 5.3a)
- Select optimum system and transducer for examination considering patient’s age and size, structures being examined and specific indications for examination. (4.1a)
- Question patients for relevant clinical information through a patient history and accurately record this medical information. (l.lf, 1.2b)
- Communicate with the patient throughout the examination in a manner appropriate to the patient's ability to understand, and adapt this communication . (l.ld)
- Adapt communication in response to patients and situations and for patients with special needs. (l.lc)
- Respond to patient questions and concerns and communicate departmental reporting procedures to patients. (l.lh, 1.lg)​
- Adjust instrument controls, optimize patient position and breathing, and record patient positions and plane of section on the scan, as required. (4.2b, 4.2f, 5.3b. 5.3c)
- Understand the variables and their relationships within calculations and use software calculation packages to perform cardiac m-mode and 2D sonographic measurements, (4.2g, 5.4c)
- Using knowledge of sonographic principles and instrumentation, perform a complete echocardiographic exam in on an uncomplicated case, in a timely manner, while recognizing sonographic appearance of normal structures (4.2b, 5.3g, 3.1h)
- Assess and/or measure the following structures with 2D, colour, spectral Doppler, and/or tissue Doppler imaging, and measure 2D, m-mode, colour Doppler, spectral Doppler and tissue Doppler (when applicable): cardiac position, abdominal situs, chest and thorax, coronary vessels, hepatic veins, outflow tracts, pulmonary veins, wall layers and segments, all 4 cardiac chambers and appendages, IAS, IVS, great vessels and their branches, all cardiac valves, aortic root, MV / TV annulus, IVC and SVC. (Appendix E)
- Document limitations to exam quality and completeness, equipment, and technical limitations. and identify and respond to exceptions from established echocardiographic protocols and procedures. (2.2g,5.4f, 5.3m, 5.3 n)
- Use medical terminology and standard abbreviations in oral communication and write medical terminology and standard abbreviations. (l.lk, l.2e)​
- By participating in a School of Health lnterprofessional Workshop, describe the roles within an interprofessional team and collaborate with these team members in decision making (2.3c 2.3d)​ ​

### Course Goals
The Cardiac Specialty Lab Course develops the technical, theoretical, and patient-care skills needed to perform complete echocardiograms and prepare for clinical practice. Students learn proper probe orientation, systematic probe manipulation, optimal imaging techniques, and how to acquire diagnostic cine-loops. They will build competency in colour and spectral Doppler, routine measurements.
Students gain essential patient-care skills, health and safety practices, basic procedures, and safe equipment use while completing full normal routine echocardiograms.
Given the scope of material and the psychomotor skills required, students should expect significant independent practice to meet course requirements.

### Attendance Requirements
Building scanning skills takes time and practice. Regular attendance and prompt arrivals to the lab are required as a courtesy to all students.
In cases where a student is unable to attend due to foreseeable circumstances, they must inform the Lab Lead Instructor and find a suitable substitute to attend the lab session on their behalf.
Students are responsible for the content they missed and for making up lost practice times.
### Course Specific Requirements
Students are expected to act professionally, with integrity, as well as dress appropriately while in the sonography lab, both for safety reasons as well as simulate clinical expectations.
### Safety equipment or protective clothing
The required dress code for the lab is:
- Scrub pants, a patient gown.
- Closed-toe and closed-heel shoes.
- Long hair must be tied back.
- No food, gum chewing, or scented products are permitted in the lab.
- Water bottles are permitted but must be kept away from the ultrasound machines.
Professional behaviour and attire must be adhered to at all times.
### Other information
Please note that students will be scanning each other's hearts in the lab. As such, a specific dress code, as well as a scanning protocol, will need to be adhered to maintain modesty and privacy.
Coursework includes a blend of in-person instruction, online instruction and the use of laboratory spaces on-campus aligned with BCIT Occupational Health and Safety requirements. Students will be provided with as much notice as possible should there be any changes to the evaluation criteria, delivery methods, and schedule.
