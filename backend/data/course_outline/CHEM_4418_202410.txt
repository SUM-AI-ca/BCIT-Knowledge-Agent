## Course Description
This course examines the chemical processes used in various B.C. industries, the chemicals used, the chemical reactions, the products manufactured, the waste products and pollutants produced. The chemical hazards and the toxicity of the chemicals workers may be exposed to are emphasised.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Describe the chemical composition of the toxic wastes produced by various chemical processes, e.g., degreasing, bleaching, electroplating, wood preservation.
- Identify toxic organic and inorganic chemicals workers may be exposed to in various chemical processes.
- Relate the physical and chemical properties of industrial chemicals to the hazards they present to workers.
- Describe in simple terms the chemical processes and identify the hazardous chemicals workers may be exposed to. Some examples are: cyanide in cyanidation process and electroplating, acids in pickling process, chlorinated organic solvents in vapour phase degreasing, chlorine in bleaching processes, sulphur containing gases in Kraft Process, arsenate salts in wood preservation, chromates in electroplating, fluorides and PAHs in the electrolysis of aluminum oxide.
- Describe the known acute and chronic toxic effects of organic and inorganic chemicals workers may be exposed to in the chemical processes studied. The chemicals include: cyanide, chromate salts, arsenate salts heavy metals (lead, mercury, cadmium and nickel), fluorides, PAHs, PCPs, pesticides, organic solvents, bleaching agents and sulphur compounds.
- Discuss pollution control devices which may be employed to reduce worker exposures in certain processes.
- Describe the storage and handling of organic and inorganic chemicals used in various industries.
- Describe the biochemical effects of selected workplace chemicals. Some examples include: cyanide, heavy metal ions, carbon monoxide and organophosphate pesticides.
- Identify some of the chemicals used as solvents, pigments, binders, biocides and plasticizers in paints, the function of these components and the hazards of exposure in the mixing and application of these paints.
- Compare the physical and chemical properties and toxicity of selected pesticides which are in common use today.
- Identify some of the toxic pollutants workers may be exposed to in smelting processes which use coke as the reducing agent.
- Outline the processes and identify some of the toxic chemicals workers may be exposed to in the petroleum industry. Processes include: cracking and reforming, straight run and vacuum distillation, solvent extractions and sulphur removal.
