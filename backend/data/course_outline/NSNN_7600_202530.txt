## Course Description
Provides further breadth and depth in Nephrology Nursing. Topics include co- morbid conditions, ethical issues, caring for families and renal transplants.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
As a healthcare practitioner, you are expected to follow professional standards of practice throughout all SN programs and courses. In this course, you will have opportunities to use the processes of critical thinking, systematic inquiry, communication, collaboration, leadership, professionalism, and clinical decision-making. Upon successful completion of this course, you should be able to:

- Describe the pathophysiology, assessment and symptom management of the three most common complex health challenges for a renal client: cardiovascular complications, diabetes and acute renal failure.
- Perform a comprehensive assessment for a renal client with complex health challenges building on your own assessment and clinical decision-making skills.
- Utilize a family-centered care approach to address social support issues for renal clients.
- Analyze a renal client through the lens of the renal transplantation process, including donor and recipient evaluation, transplant surgery, immunosuppression, and associated management of complications such as rejection and infection.
- Utilize an ethical framework in exploring end of life decision-making for clients and their families.

## Course Goals
- Develop a systematic approach to the inquiry and management of renal patients with complex health challenges such as cardiovascular complications, diabetes and acute kidney injury.
- Analyze own assessment and clinical decision-making skills in the context of complex health challenges using situated clinical decision making framework(SCDMF).
- Demonstrate an understanding of the renal transplantation process, including donor and recipient evaluation, transplant surgery, immunosuppression and medications, and associated management of complications such as rejection and infection.
- Assess the ethical dimensions of renal transplantation.

## Course topics
This module explores some of the effects of cardiac complications on Anna and her family. You will explore some of the effects of cardiac complications on Anna and her family. Anna has had renal disease for twelve years and has been treated with hemodialysis in the interior community of British Columbia. As a result of her cardiac disease, Anna is faced to move to the city, of Vancouver, where she can be closer to an acute care facility. In addition to examining Anna's experience of cardiac complications, this module explores ways in which nurses can work in partnership with Anna and her family, to minimize intra-dialytic complications and explore her options regarding medications and symptom management. By responding to Anna's concerns about relocating at this late stage in her life, as well as those of her daughters, the nurse facilitates solutions to Anna's relocation dilemma.

This module describes the case of a young woman, Isabella experiencing acute kidney injury as a result of rhabdomyolysis. This case narration is set in an intensive care unit where renal failure is one of the many health care crises such as hemodynamic instability, coma, and respiratory failure is observed. Although the major concern for nephrology nurses is about renal disease, Isabella's case explores the effects of sympathomimetics such as dopamine and dobutamine, effects of mechanical ventilation on acid-base balance and effects of renal failure on nutritional status and fluid and electrolyte balance. Emphasis is also placed on broad assessment skills, collaboration with other specialty nurses and other multidisciplinary discipline and working with affected families in crisis.

This module provides an overview of diabetic complications as viewed through the experience of Burt and his family. Burt faced multiple vascular access problems and infections and is faced with possibility of foot amputation as a result of peripheral vascular disease. Burt became quite depressed as a result of his health experiences and recent losses. As a result, he is having difficulty eating, sleeping and taking care for himself. He was prescribed antidepressants, intradialytic parenteral nutrition (ITPN) and is being assessed for home care. In addition to exploring Burt's experiences, this module explores social support, liaising with community resources as well as nursing management of diabetic complications.

This module will discuss and explore basic immunology and transplantation on its effect on clients and families. You will meet Sasha who will receive a kidney from her sibling. We will follow Sasha through her pre-transplant workup for her surgery. We will continue to explore the experiences of clients on renal transplantation, donor live or cadaveric, transplant surgery, immediate post-operative nursing care and complications of renal transplant recipient and donor, immunosuppressive therapies and medications required post-transplant, renal transplant rejection, and transitions to those who are receiving and losing a transplanted kidney.

This module explores end-of-life decisions in the context of discontinuation of dialysis treatment. You will have a chance to watch an old movie Whose Life is it Anyway, which serves as a medium through which issues on end-of-life decisions are explored. Ethical dilemmas, effects of depression on decision making capacity and effects on both family and care givers are emphasized.
