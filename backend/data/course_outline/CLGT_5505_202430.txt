## Course Description
This is an introductory course in the use of fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) for diagnosis of chromosomal abnormalities. The theoretical and practical principles will be discussed including: prnciples of the FISH technique, preparation of tissues for FISH, types of DNA probes, epi-fluorescent microscopy, clinical applications of FISH, and analysis and interpretation of results. During the laboratory sessions, emphasis will be placed on the development of observational and troubleshooting skills. Documentation and interpretation of results will be emphasized.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
By the end of this course, learners will be able to:

- Follow safe work practices including WHMIS guidelines for safe handling of FISH reagents and probes. Applies the principles of standard precautions.
- Use FISH to analyze and diagnose chromosomal abnormalities in cultured and uncultured human tissue.
- Document all FISH analyses, verify the suitability of test requested and prioritize specimen preparation through proper use of the FISH experimental record sheet.
- Troubleshoot poor or unsatisfactory results and take appropriate remedial action.
- Assess hybridization efficiency, analyze FISH slides, and interpret results.
- Use alpha satellite probes for enumeration of chromosome copy number.
- Use appropriate control data to interpret results.
- Use appropriate scoring and selection criteria for evaluation of FISH results.
- Be familiar with the components of the epi-fluorescent microscope and follows maintenance protocol.
- Build on experience gained from each preceding lab to improve quality and efficiency of technique.
- Recognize CCMG guidelines for specimen and record retention.
- Demonstrate professional practice when completing tasks and assignments.
- Communicate and interact with instructor and fellow students in a professional manner.

## Course Goals
This course will provide theory and laboratory practice in the fundamentals of FISH technology for chromosome enumeration as employed in clinical cytogenetic laboratories.

Students will also be introduced to “interphase cytogenetics” and will gain an understanding of the strengths and limitations of this diagnostic approach.

Students will become familiar with the elements and proper use of the epi-fluorescent microscope for FISH analysis, and also gain experience using a Computer Assisted Image Reproduction System for creating FISH images.
