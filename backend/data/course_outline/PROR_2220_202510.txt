## Course Description
Biomechanics 2 consists of a biomechanical overview of the lower body including the hip, knee, ankle and foot. Covers normal human locomotion in detail.Applications such as stability and balance, motion analysis and gait will be emphasized.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Describe the normal function of the hip, knee, ankle, and foot during the process of locomotion.
- Compare normal gait to pathological gait.
- Analyze the forces (both active and reactive) present during the gait cycle.
- Describe a person’s gait from analyzing their kinematic and kinetic data.

## Course Goals
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Apply the theory of limitations in measurements to the taking, recording and analysis of data.
- Demonstrate, within a laboratory environment, the ability to take, record and analyze data using a computer and computer software (MS Excel).
- Apply the basic concepts of kinematics, both linear and rotational, to the analysis of normal human gait, (with an emphasis on interpretation of graphs).
- Apply the basic concepts of kinetics to the interpretation of forces experienced by the human body under normal and abnormal conditions.
- Describe energy costs in walking using the principles of work and energy.
- Apply the theory of simple machines to the explanation of the lever systems and mechanical advantage in human body.
- Describe the normal function of the hip, knee, ankle, and foot during the process of locomotion.
- Compare normal gait to pathological gait.
- Analyze forces present during the gait cycle.
- Describe a person's gait from analyzing their kinematic and kinetic data.
- Use gait analysis tools to conduct observational and quantitative gait analysis.
