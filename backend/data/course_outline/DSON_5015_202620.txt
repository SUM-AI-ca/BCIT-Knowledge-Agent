### Course Description
DSON 5015 covers advanced echocardiography techniques, such as TEE's, stress echoes, 3D ultrasound, cath lab procedures and other imaging modalities. Students also complete weekly case studies that test knowledge, critical thinking and report writing skills. Students are introduced to pathologies and challenging exams through these case studies and learn to identify and grade severity of abnormalities and pathologies.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- ​Describe the role of the sonographer in interventional procedures, TEE's, contrast imaging and stress echo. (3.2a, 3.2b, 3.3j, 3.3e)
- Correlate results from laboratory tests, aspirations, and biopsies, including ECG, Holter monitoring, and stress ECG tests. (5.2a, 5.2d)
- Correlate results from diagnostic imaging (radiography, computerized tomography, nuclear medicine, and magnetic resonance studies). (5.2b)
- Using case studies, interpret history, signs and symptoms and correlate other relevant information with expected findings within the case study. (5.1a)
- Using case studies, integrate knowledge of anatomy and disease processes and spatial reasoning to interpret images, formulate impressions, and identify and prioritize differential findings. (5.4d, 5.1e, 5.4e, 5.4b)
- Using case studies, record examination procedures and results, and ensure documentation is timely, accurate, concise, and complete. (1.2c, 1.2d)
- Provide a technical impression to the reporting physician. (5.4g)
- Write medical terminology and standard abbreviations. (1.2e)
- Using case studies, evaluate images for quality, orientation, identification, and labelling. (5.3e, 5.3f)​

### Course Goals
This is an advanced theory course focused on cardiac related special procedures as well as echocariographic case studies. The former is intended to familiarize students with the different procedures that occur in a cardiology department and how they relate to echocardiography. The latter allows students to apply their knowledge of anatomy and disease processes to interpret echocardiographic findings and communicate this by completting preliminary reports as they would in the clinical setting.
