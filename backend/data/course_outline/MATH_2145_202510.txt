### Course Description
An introductory course in statistics, this course covers descriptive statistics, including measures of central tendency and variation, and various graphical displays; probability laws; discrete probability distributions, including the binomial and Poisson distributions; continuous probability distributions with emphasis on the normal and t-distributions; central limit theorem; sampling and confidence intervals for the population mean, both with and without the finite population correction. These methods are applied to examples chosen from the Forest and Natural Areas Management field.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Select and prepare an appropriate statistical chart to summarize raw data; students will be familiar with stem and leaf plots, histograms, Pareto charts, box and whisker plots, and scatterplots.
- Compute and understand the meaning of statistical measures for a dataset including:
- Central tendency: mean, median, and mode;
- Position: quartiles and percentiles;
- Variation: range, inter-quartile range, standard deviation, coefficient of variation; and
- Skewness.
- Solve problems requiring the basic rules of probability including the addition and multiplication rules.
- Distinguish between discrete and continuous probability distributions.
- Solve relevant problems using the binomial and Poisson probability distributions.
- Solve relevant problems using the normal and t-distributions.
- Estimate, with confidence limits, the population mean, with or without the finite population correction as appropriate.
- Use spreadsheet and statistical software to do statistical analysis.

### Course Goals
The goals of this course are to provide the student with a foundation of introductory statistical skills needed to solve problems that will be encountered in the Forest and Natural Areas Management program and in future employment in related industrial settings. More advanced topics (inferential statistics) will be covered in Math 3145.
Note:
The student will use words and diagrams to present organized solutions to problems and to draw relevant conclusions from their solutions. The student will be exposed to methods for effective use of calculators as well as the use of Excel spreadsheet software and RStudio statistical software in hands-on computer labs.
