### Course Description
​Allows students to work on projects within guidelines specified by faculty. The projects are drawn from a variety of sources, especially from industrial situations, and may require extensive contact with the business community. Students work in teams and seek advice from a faculty member acting as their project supervisor.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Determine project requirements.
- Plan and schedule project work tasks.
- Design a system that meets the documented requirements.
- Implement a system that has been designed.
- Test and deliver a working system to an industry client.
### Learning Resources
Teams will need to establish their own development, source control and testing environments.
Teams will need to use productivity software, such as Microsoft Office, to complete documentation requirements.
### Course Goals
Students work in teams of up to 4 students to complete projects for clients in industry. Projects must be fully documented and developed using methods and procedures acceptable in industry.
The project team is responsible for all aspects of the project. This includes everything from contacting the client to delivering and installing the software.
Each team will be assigned a project Supervisor. The project Supervisor will work with the students, providing direction on: technology decisions, design strategies, as well as project management issues. Project Supervisors do not actually perform any of the work on the project, nor are they responsible for the success of the project.
Project teams should be sure to manage the expectations of their Supervisor, as well as those of their client, and the course instructor.
Projects are selected by the team from a project catalogue and are to be completed before the end of the course. Some projects are much larger than others, so students should be careful to select a project which matches the abilities and interests of the team members. Some projects may involve much more work than can be accomplished in the course while other projects may involve too little work. In these cases, you are expected to limit or increase the scope of the project.
### Evaluation Criteria
The grade for the course is assigned as follows:
Checkpoint 1 | 17.5%
Checkpoint 2 | 17.5%
Checkpoint 3 | 17.5%
Checkpoint 4 | 17.5%
Participation | 5%
Overall Project, Final Presentation | 25%
TOTAL | 100%
Grading Notes
- Grades for each Checkpoint are assigned by the Supervisor.
- The Presentation/Demonstration grade is assigned by course instructor. This grade will be compiled from a combination of: Client feedback/sign-off; lab work and progress during the term; also the final presentation and demonstration
- One grade is assigned per team, but may be adjusted for each student to reflect individual contribution to the project.
- Students who do not contribute significantly to all aspects of the project will receive a failing grade.
- Students who do not participate in the Final Presentation will receive a failing grade.
- Teams who do not achieve a minimum viable product could receive a failing grade.
- Teams who have not handed-off their work to their sponsor in an appropriate manner could receive a failing grade.
Grading Criteria
The grades assigned at the checkpoints and at the end of the project are based on team performance with respect to the following aspects of software development and project execution:
- Client Relationship
- Project Management Skills
- Teamwork
- Application of Technology
- Tracking overall Effort and Contribution
- Quality of Work
- Requirements Specification
- Documentation and organization
- Coding and Software Implementation
- Testing of all kinds (unit, system, integration, user acceptance, etc.)
- Attendance, Participation

### Course Specific Requirements
Individual Grade Adjustments
Students will be asked to complete peer reviews at the end of the course. This is a process in which you rate the performance of yourself as well as the other members of your team. The results of this peer evaluation may be used to adjust each student’s grade.
Student Contribution
Each student is expected to contribute individually and equally at all times during the project. Additionally, these contributions must demonstrate reasonable quality and application of technology. Students must be able to identify their contribution to the course instructor and/or Supervisor. If it becomes apparent to the course instructor and/or team Supervisor that one (or more) of the team members are not contributing equally to the project, that student will be evaluated separately from the team.
Students who do not contribute significantly to the project throughout the term will receive a failing grade.
Students who do not attend the final presentation will receive a failing grade. Student Participation:
In additional to Core Hours (See Attendance Requirements ), students must attend:
- All client meetings
- All Supervisor meetings
- All team meetings
- All lectures
Absence due to illness will require documentation.
