## Course Description
​The course develops broad knowledge and skills needed to plan and implement restoration activities. The course begins by reviewing a step-by-step process applicable to a wide range of ecosystems for developing, implementing, monitoring, and refining on-the-ground restoration projects. Significant attention is dedicated to designing monitoring programs needed to assess restoration success, including appropriate use of statistical design and qualitative information. Students identify and critically review a restoration plan in terms of this step-by-step process. A major component of this course involves students incrementally developing a restoration and monitoring plan for a degraded site.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Evaluate the ecological and scientific merit of a restoration plan.
- Develop the components of a successful restoration plan.
- Design a rigorous monitoring program for use in assessing restoration success.
- Design a sampling plan to collect biological and physical environmental data.
- Assess restoration success by interpreting quantitative results of monitoring.
- ​Use effective written and oral communication to present and critically review restoration plans​.

## Course Goals
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

• Evaluate the ecological and scientific merit of a restoration plan.

• Develop the components of a successful restoration plan.

• Critically review and modify a monitoring program for use in assessing restoration success.

• Assess restoration success by interpreting quantitative results of monitoring.

• Use effective written and oral communication to present and critically review restoration plans.

More specifically, students will be able to:

1. Describe a sequential process for planning and implementing ER

2. Understand major elements of how to effectively design and implement a restoration project, including:

• Conducting assessments of current and historical site conditions, and of key stressors/impacts

• Establishing desired future conditions (which may entail identifying baseline conditions and reference sites)

• Developing appropriate restoration goals and objectives

• Using commonly employed restoration techniques

• Working with the natural environment to aid restoration (e.g., using ecological succession and natural disturbance)

• Developing a monitoring plan, including understanding basic monitoring techniques, sampling, and statistical analyses

• Developing a maintenance program

• Planning for public consultation and outreach programs

• Assessing the need for permits and approvals

Restoration Plan Review and Presentation

Each student will research and review a published paper of their interest focusing on an ecological restoration case study, approach, or policy. The student will submit the topic to the Instructor by Week 2 for approval and will present the paper to the class later in the term. The assignment will consist of a presentation of the paper with a thorough critique. The student will critique all the components of the paper, which will be evaluated and discussed through an oral presentation (10%) consisting of a 15 minute power point presentation and any additional audio-visual resources or materials (e.g. a video clip, etc.). The final presentation should be uploaded to the Learning Hub. Additional details will be provided in class.

Assignments

The main focus of the course will be to have students actively work through a theoretical habitat restoration project on the BCIT campus and develop a restoration plan with a monitoring plan. The class will be broken up into small teams with four assignments throughout the term, each asking the students to work through a phase of a typical restoration project. Details for each submission will be provided in class. Each submission will be worth 10% and should be uploaded to the Learning Hub. The assignments’ sections are:

1) Existing conditions - biological, physical, disturbance (10%)

2) Conceptual design - landscape level (10%)

3) Conceptual design - planting and wildlife plan (10%)

4) Monitoring plan - indicators and sampling plan (10%)

The final assignment will combine these submissions and ask the students to build on them based on what they learnt in class to produce a final restoration plan with a monitoring plan. This submission will be worth 20% and should be uploaded to the Learning Hub.

5) Restoration and Monitoring Plan (20%)
