## Course Description
Starts with a review of the atom, moles, chemical equations and stoichiometry. An overview of bonding is presented. Oxidation and reduction will then be discussed with reference to balancing redox equations. Solution stoichiometry is then studied. Acid-base chemistry is studied with emphasis on the difference between strong and weak electrolytes and different types of buffer solutions. Electrochemistry is introduced with emphasis on different types of voltaic cells. Then the major groups of organic compounds are considered and the basic physical and chemical properties of each group are described. The laboratory work will acquaint the student with the basic techniques used in chemistry as well as several techniques used in a clinical laboratory.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Describe the electronic structure of atoms in terms of orbitals and how this relates to light absorption and emission spectra.
- Describe ionic and covalent bonds and the major properties of compounds containing these bonds.
- Explain the relation between the intermolecular forces present within a substance and its chemical properties.
- Prepare solutions at accurately known concentration.
- Give examples of properties of acids and bases and use a pH meter to measure pH.
- Explain what is meant by a buffer solution and describe how blood pH is maintained at a constant level physiologically.
- Use the principles of oxidation and reduction to construct simple voltaic cells.
- Explain the operation of batteries such as the NiCd battery and the lead-acid battery.
- Identify the functional groups of the major organic families and draw structural formulas of simple organic compounds and name them.
- For each organic family, list the physical and chemical properties that are of relevance for the study of biological chemistry and physiology.
- Explain what is meant by WHMIS.
- Identify hazards for inorganic and organic substances by evaluating.
