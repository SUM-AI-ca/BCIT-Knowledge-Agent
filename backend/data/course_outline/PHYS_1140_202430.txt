### Course Description
This course is designed to meet the specific needs of Architectural & Building Engineering Technology. Emphasis is placed on good problem solving techniques and practical applications. Topics include kinematics, statics and dynamics, work and energy, fluid mechanics, simple harmonic motion, resonance, waves & sound. There is also a practical laboratory component that teaches proper measurement and data analysis techniques, the use of computer data acquisition and analysis software, and technical report writing
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
From the lecture portion of the course, the student will demonstrate his/her comprehension of the material by successfully:
- Solving quantitative, practical problems using a logical and systematic approach that involves: free body diagrams, selecting suitable equations based on an understanding of the physical principles that apply to the situation, algebraically solving for the unknown quantity in the equation(s), substitution of values (with units) into the equation, and presentation of the final answer with correct significant figures and units.
- Answering qualitative, written and multiple choice questions that test the conceptual understanding of the physical principles that apply to a situation.
From the laboratory portion of the course, the student will have the ability to:
- Properly use scientific instruments to obtain accurate measurements when operating technical equipment.
- Interpret and analyze data using computer software.
- Obtain an estimate of the uncertainty in a calculated quantity.
- Write a clear, concise, and properly formatted technical report.

### Course Specific Requirements
Course Website: All course related documents such as the lecture notes, lecture examples, lab schedule, formulae sheet, listing of grades, test solutions, etc. will be posted in Portable Document Format (.pdf) at the Learning Hub - https://learn.bcit.ca . After logging in, go to 'My Courses’ and under '2024.Fall' , select the ‘PHYS-1140-0 - Applied Physics for Building 1 - 30487 - Lecture’ option for the lecture portion of the course. For the lab portion, select the ‘PHYS-1140-0 - Applied Physics for Building 1 - 304XX - Lab’ option for your particular set. Also, you may receive important e-mail announcements from your instructor over the course of the term, so please check your messages regularly at https://my.bcit.ca .
Pre-Class Preparation: Every week, based on the Proposed Lecture Schedule, you will be assigned sections of the text to be read in advance before they are to be discussed in class. This allows you to more easily understand the material presented in the class and to retain it for use in assignments and tests.
Note Taking: Because the class “Lecture Notes’ are available on the course website in advance of the lectures, they may be printed out or transferred to your computer. Of course, you may preview them ahead of the lectures, and during lectures, the notes may be referenced and annotated at your convenience. More importantly, this allows you to concentrate on listening to the discussion and understanding the concepts being discussed. The lecture example questions are posted in advance as well, but the solutions to them are not posted in advance. It is very important that you actively participate by thinking about the work being presented by the instructor, verifying the mathematics, and giving some thought to the meaning of the solution presented. Also, you are encouraged to participate by answering questions, and posing your own questions as example problems are being discussed in class. The solutions to the lecture example problems done in class will be posted to the course website shortly afterwards.
Term Tests and Final Examination: These will be related to the assigned problems and concepts covered in classes and assignments. Term tests will be non-comprehensive, but the final exam will be comprehensive, involving all of the material covered over the course of the term. A formula sheet will be provided for use during all examinations.
Laboratory: Attendance and performance of laboratory experiments are mandatory. Before coming to the lab, students are expected to have prepared for the experiment by reading the appropriate section of the lab manual. After doing the reading, answer the assigned Lab Quiz for each experiment on the Learning Hub.
Laboratory Absences: Attendance and performance of laboratory experiments are mandatory and occur every week at the scheduled time. Missing two (2) or more labs with no legitimate reason, and not being made-up will result in a failing grade of Unsatisfactory (U) in the course, regardless of other grades. Legitimate reasons include illness and personal or family emergency situations. See the note below regarding illness. The instructor must be informed of this reason as soon as possible, and no later than 48 hours after the missed lab. The student is responsible for reaching out to the instructor so that a make-up lab can be scheduled in a timely manner.
Laboratory Reports: Written accounts of each experiment in the form of 'Laboratory Templates' will be required to be completed for each lab session. Lab Templates will be completed by the student and submitted for marking by the appointed due date and time. After being returned, Lab Templates should be attached to your Lab Binder for future studying purposes. Late lab reports will be penalized, and substantially incomplete reports will receive a mark of zero.
Laboratory Test: Near the end of the term (see posted Lab Schedule), you will be tested on your understanding of the theory, experimental methods, use of scientific equipment, calculations, and the physical interpretation of the results pertaining to the experiments you have performed. During the Lab Test, you will be allowed to freely refer to your own Lab Binder which may contain the following: your own Lab Templates, and any of your own personal, handwritten notes. Unapproved photocopied material will not be allowed.
Workload: Set aside time for physics (and for your other classes, too) every week. It is very important to study regularly, keep up with the work and seek the assistance of the instructor when problems arise. The course load is quite heavy and to succeed you must be prepared to make the appropriate time commitment. The time that you will need to succeed in this course depends on your own background and abilities. As a rough guide, an ‘average’ time allotment is usually one hour for every classroom or lab hour per week. The pace of this (and your other) courses is very fast, so DO NOT FALL BEHIND! With a full load of other classes, it will be extremely difficult to catch up! Because physics is a “cumulative” subject (successful learning of new material is based on a sound understanding of previous material), you must ensure that a firm foundation of knowledge is built upon, starting from the beginning of the course!
Getting Help: Any difficulties that you may have should be resolved as soon as possible. Please consult with the instructor after class, during office hours, or by appointment. Also, the BCIT Student’s Society often hires 2nd year students, who have previously taken physics, to provide free ‘peer tutoring’ at select times and days of the week. In addition, see the note below concerning the Physics Learning Centre.

The centre is located in room SW3-4785, and is open between the times of 11:30 am to 1:20 pm on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday. An instructor from the Physics Department will be in the PLC to answer your questions about labs and assignments, and also to assist you with make-up labs in the adjoining mini-lab room, SW3-4775. Please refer to the schedule posted on the door of room SW3-4785 for the instructor’s office location. It is important to take advantage of this free and valuable learning resource!

Regularly assigned homework (assignments and lab reports) will cover the topics discussed in class and in the lab, and should be completed as the material is discussed in class. All of the assignments will be submitted on-line using the Mastering Physics system at the website: Mastering Physics | Pearson (https://mlm.pearson.com/northamerica/masteringphysics). You must register on the website with a valid access code to be able to view the assignments and submit your answers. An access code is included with your textbook if it was purchased new from the BCIT Bookstore. Used textbooks will most likely have an expired access code; in which case you may purchase a new access code separately on the Mastering Physics website, or else at the BCIT Bookstore. Details of the registration process and a basic introduction to Mastering Physics will be discussed during the first week of lectures.
Please keep in mind that the Assignments represent the minimum number of problems that you should be attempting. In order to obtain sufficient confidence and mastery over the topics discussed in class, solving more problems is generally needed. The textbook contains many additional problems at the end of each chapter, of which the odd-numberd problems have supplied answers at the back of the text. Solve each question or problem with a complete written solution following the methods outlined in class. For future studying purposes, place all completed assignments in a separate notebook and refer to them as the need arises. Students are encouraged to put in the effort to do a good job on the assignments as they closely relate to questions on quizzes, tests, and exams.
Assignments and Evaluation: Assignments must be done on an individual basis, unless otherwise specified by the instructor. All work shall be evaluated as submitted, and submitted work is considered final. Students are expected to complete the required work in the specified order and format, and will be evaluated accordingly.
Late Assignments: It is the student’s responsibility to ensure that assignments are submitted on time. Any portion of an assignment question submitted after the due date and time will be subject to a 20% penalty per calendar day of the value of the entire question. Any portion of an assignment question submitted 5 days or more late will result in a zero grade for the entire question. Students should notify the instructor immediately of any extenuating circumstances relating to late, or potentially late, assignments. For late assignments due to illness, please see Medical Documentation and Missed Work .
