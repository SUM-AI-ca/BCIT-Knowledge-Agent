## Course Description
Emphasises the integration of problem solving strategies with mathematical skills in the context of relevant occupational health and safety applications. Topics include unit conversion (metric and imperial, ppm, ppb), ratio/proportion, area/volume calculations, linear, logarithmic and exponential functions with appropriate curve fitting (least squares), vectors and trigonometry. Applications include chemical mixtures, rinsing problems, fluid/air flow, container volumes, noise relationships, force diagrams, radioactive decay, scaling drawings.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Demonstrate unit conversions involving the Imperial System and Systeme International (SI)
- Determine reasonable precision and accuracy in computations involving measured quantities
- Algebraically rearrange relevant formulae
- Formulate and solve applied problems which require the concepts of ratio and proportion
- Apply trigonometry/vectors to solve technical applications involving right and oblique triangles
- Apply basic algebra and geometry to problems involving areas and volumes, flow rates and mixing or dilution of fluids
- Use logarithms in the measurement of sound levels, radiation, exponential growth and decay
- Interpret and produce graphical presentations of data using uniform, semi-log and log-log scales
- Determine lines of best fit for linear data on Cartesian, semi-log or log-log scales using the method of least squares

## Course Schedule and Assignments
Unit Number: Material Covered
Unit 1: Review of Basic Algebra Skills The “reasonable answer.” Precision, accuracy, and significant figures. Self-review. Algebraic operations. Percentages, ppm, ppb. Unit conversion, especially Imperial to metric. Rectification of units in formulae. Exponents and radicals. Literal equations.
Unit 2: Trigonometry and Vectors Right angle trigonometry. Graphical interpretation of vectors Vectors with application to force problems.
Unit 3: Graphing, Least Squares, and Vectors Review of linear and quadratic equations; graphically and algebraically. Determining the line of best fit (Legendre’s Method of Least Squares).
Unit 4: Logarithmic and Exponential Functions Common and natural logarithms and their properties. Indirect variation, constants of proportionality, inverse square law. Applications in growth and decay, acidity, decibels, and rinsing problems.
Unit 5: Graphical Analysis Utilizing Logarithmic Papers Transformations, log cycles. Exponential and power functions. Linearization of exponential functions on semi-log paper. Linearization of power functions on log-log paper.
Unit 6: Geometry Calculation of perimeter, areas, and volumes. Direct proportion/direct variation. Scale drawings and similar figures. Trigonometric applications.
Unit 7: Systems of Equations (as time permits) Applications involving the solution of 2 or 3 linear equations or the quadratic formula (especially mixtures).
