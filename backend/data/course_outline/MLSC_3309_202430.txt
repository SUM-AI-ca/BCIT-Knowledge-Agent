## Course Description
This course focuses primarily on antimicrobials and antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST). Learners study drug classes and their generations, modes of action and target sites, usual lethality (bactericidal vs bacteriostatic), spectrum of activity, toxicity and/or hypersensitivity. The history and evolution of AST in modern medicine is introduced. Current AST techniques, conventional and automated methods, as well as the screening and confirmation of mechanisms of antimicrobial resistance, are taught and practiced in the laboratory sessions. Reporting strategies recommended by the Clinical Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) are introduced. In preparation for the next course, Specimen Processing in the Microbiology Laboratory is introduced. Theory and laboratory practice emphasize safety and quality control. Collaborative laboratory workshops and assignments enhance critical thinking, problem solving, and team-based interpersonal skills. Learning is assessed through online learning activities, unit exams, laboratory practical activities and workshops, along with formal laboratory and theory exams.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- List the classes of antimicrobials and their subgroupings, generations and spectrum of activity.
- Explain the modes of action and target sites for each class of antimicrobials.
- Perform current AST methods, both conventional and automated techniques, including workflow and testing used to screen for and confirm mechanisms of resistance.
- Read and interpret various AST techniques, including routine testing as well as phenotypic and rapid screening and confirmatory testing of antimicrobial resistant isolates.
- Create isolate antibiogram reports that comprehensively and accurately report AST results following cascade reporting strategies recommended by the CLSI.

## Attendance Requirements
The following statements are in accordance with BCIT Policies 5101, 5102, 5103, and 5104 and their accompanying procedures. To review these policies and procedures, please refer to: https://www.bcit.ca/about/administration/policies.shtml

Regular attendance and punctuality are important in the total student learning experience. While attendance in lectures is not monitored, attendance to lab sessions is mandatory and all absences are documented by instructional staff.

In case of illness or other unavoidable absence, students must communicate as soon as possible with the Instructor. Illness must be substantiated by an official medical certificate. Absence for other unavoidable causes must be supported by proper documentation. Excessive absences may result in failure or withdrawal from this course and/or the Program.

If a student is absent from any of the two Term Exams or the Practical Lab Exam, timely notice must be provided to the Instructor. If the absence is deemed excused, the exam (or an alternate exam) will be offered at a mutually arranged date/time and no mark penalty will apply. If the absence is deemed unexcused the Instructor will work on a case-by-case basis with the student, the Program Head and the Associate Dean to determine next steps. Unexcused absences may result in a mark of ‘0’ for the missed exam.

If a student is absent from a scheduled lab session and the absence is deemed excused, every effort will be made to have the student make up the lab activities. However, due to scheduling and lab supply conflicts this may not always be possible. Excused absences will have no mark penalty, unexcused absences will result in a mark of ‘0’ for the session’s activities.

Final Exam: if a student is ill or otherwise unable to write the final exam at the scheduled time, the Instructor will work with the student, the Program Head and the Associate Dean to determine next steps. An alternate exam may be offered or other grading policy applied – see BCIT policy 5301 PR Grading (article 2.2)

Late arrival to any of Term Exams, Practical Lab Exam or Final Exam: if a student is late for any of these formal assessments they will be admitted to the exam up to the halfway point of the assessment timing. No extra time will be permitted and the exam will be graded in full. If the students arrive after the halfway point, they will not be admitted to the assessment. Instead, an alternate time for the exam will be offered and the final grade for that alternate exam will be no higher than 60%.

**Note: the MLS program is currently reviewing all policies around missed exams, laboratory sessions and skills assessments. A program specific policy may be implemented at some point during the Fall 2024 term, with adequate notice.
