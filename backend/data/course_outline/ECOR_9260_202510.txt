## Course Description
​This course provides students with an introduction to project planning, management, and key policies that guide ecological restoration. Students will participate in real-world situations, including bidding on a Request for Proposal (RFP) for an ecological restoration project for the class to actively manage from initiation to close-out. Students will identify current policies, including those related to climate change adaptation, that may impact their work as restoration ecologists and debate their pros and cons​.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Plan, write and submit an effective and winning project proposal
- Manage an ecological restoration project through the five phases of a project cycle: (i) initiation, (ii) planning and design, (iii) execution through implementation and construction, (iv) project control and monitoring, and (v) completion reporting and close-out
- Adapt restoration projects to parallel key federal and provincial laws that drive ecological restoration and permits required for a restoration project
- Integrate current policies within ecological restoration that may affect management of projects
- Assess critically habitat restoration projects and long-term effectiveness as it relates to climate change, assess these risks, and provide adaptive management
- Network with project stakeholders, including local and Indigenous communities, and assess their interests in restoration projects and their influence on project success
- Develop effective engagement and consultation methods to integrate local and Indigenous communities in a specific restoration project.

## Course Goals
Course Description

This course provides students with an introduction to project planning, management, and community engagement that guide ecological restoration. Students will participate in real-world situations including bidding on a Request for Proposal for an ecological restoration project for the class to actively manage from initiation to close-out. Students will research key management policies, including adapting to projects to climate change, that may impact their work as a restoration ecologist.

Learning Outcomes

1) Planning, writing, and submitting an effective and winning project proposal;

2) Managing an ecological restoration project through the five phases of a project cycle: (i) initiation, (ii) planning and design, (iii) execution through implementation and construction, (iv) project control and monitoring, and (v) completion reporting and close-out;

3) Understanding key federal and provincial laws that drive ecological restoration and permits required for a restoration project;

4) Review habitat restoration project design and long-term effectiveness as it relates to climate change, assess these risks, and provide adaptive management;

5) Identify project stakeholders and their interests in restoration projects, review stakeholder influence on project success, and learn engagement and consultation methods.

Student will work through a series of assignments to gain experience in:

- Planning, costing, scheduling and writing a restoration proposal for submission to a proponent;
- Competing against other companies to try and win a project;
- Acting as a project coordinator and leading a team, while also working within a group to achieve a series of goals;
- Safeguarding and identifying and managing uncertainty and risk over the course of a restoration project;
- Reading and preparing project documents including: health and safety plans, planting plans, engineering plans, and tender documents;
- How to plan and lead consultation with stakeholders and mange stakeholder conflict; and

How to manage a monitoring program through to Project acceptance by a regulator.

## Course Schedule and Assignments
Assignments

Project Proposals (30%)

Students will be divided into companies to review, research, and develop a proposal to be submitted to a funding organization for approval. Students will be graded on the quality of the proposal and feed back from the funding organization. There may be one or two proposals assigned during the course subject to available funding.

In-class Exercises and Participation (25%)

Students will be assigned regular group exercises throughout the term to guide them through their major assignments and overall understanding of the courses material. These assignments, along with general class participation (i.e., contributing to class discussion), will be graded and contribute equally to an over mark.

Individual Project (20%)

Each student will research and review a topic applied to project management of their interest focusing on a case study addressing issues of climate change, safeguarding, or innovation in ecological restoration. The student will submit the topic to the Instructor by Week 2 for approval and will present the paper to the class later in the term. The assignment will consist of a 15 minute presentation with five minutes for discussion. The student will be evaluated based on the quality of the oral presentation (10%) and a brief (1,000 word) review of the paper and why it is important to project management of ecological restoration projects.

Final Exam (25%)

Students will be required to complete a final exam testing them on their knowledge of course material and assigned readings presented during the course.
