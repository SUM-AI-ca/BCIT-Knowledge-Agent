### Course Description
This course examines environmental and management issues common in urban and urban/rural interface areas in British Columbia. Emphasis is placed on integrated infrastructure, land, water, and vegetation management and planning. Both regulatory and non-regulatory tools available to local governments to address environmental issues are examined. Examples of Official Community Plans, zoning bylaws, development permit area regulations, community watershed plans, integrated storm water management plans, streamside protection, and waste management plans are reviewed. Students will also be able to describe typical process and requirements for building permit and development permit applications.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Describe environmental planning issues within an urban and interface settings, and the impacts of urbanization on the natural environment – both within, and outside, urban boundaries
- Describe the range and hierarchy of planning tools available to local governments to address urban environmental issues
- Assess techniques in modelling, mapping, reporting and monitoring existing urban environmental conditions
- Analyze the content of planning documents, including policy documents, community plans, bylaws, and site plans
- Describe the typical process for building permit and development permit approvals
- Describe and interpret the basic components of landscape architecture, infrastructure, subdivision, and housing related blueprints and designs
- Create 3D designs that include landscape and infrastructure components
- Access online land tenure and land assessment databases
- Interpret legal survey plan documents
