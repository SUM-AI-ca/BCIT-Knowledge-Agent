### Course Description
​This course provides a thorough treatment of up-to-date urban storm water management practices. The focus is on the reduction of the impact of urban development on the natural hydrological cycle, flood potential, erosion, and water quality. The course presents the key components of stormwater management in urbanized settings, including legal issues, system assessment, hydraulic modeling, implementation of green storm infrastructure/low impact development and Best Management Practices, impacts of climate change stormwater systems, and monitoring of surface waters and water quality.​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Describe the impact of urban development on the hydrologic cycle and water quality of watersheds and subwatersheds. [1, 8, 9]
- Explain the legal issues relating to stormwater management, including jurisdiction, policy, and regulation. [8]
- Describe the principles of storm water management and identify criteria for stormwater system design and monitoring. [1, 9]
- Identify, select, and assess up-to-date green storm infrastructure/low impact development and Best Management Practices for a stormwater management system. [1, 2, 4]
- Conduct the requisite hydrologic and hydraulic analyses for components of a stormwater management system, selecting and using appropriate software where necessary. [1, 2, 5]​

### Course Goals
Stormwater management is a continually evolving and multidisciplinary field. The goal of this course is to expose students to the state-of-the-art in the field by building on the strong foundations they have obtained in applied hydrology and hydraulics. This is done by both a practical stormwater modeling project as well as a number of guest lectures. Students will gain an appreciation for the challenges of managing both stormwater quantity and quality, balancing management of both regular low intensity rainfall and extreme events. The course will also introduce students to the use of green storm infrastructure to address stormwater quantity and quality management.

Engineering Accreditation
The Canadian Engineering Accreditation Board (CEAB) oversees the accreditation of engineering programs across Canada. To measure the effectiveness of an engineering program the CEAB has identified twelve specific attributes that the graduate is expected to possess and use as the foundation to developing and advancing an engineering career. To ensure that the overall curriculum of the BEng in Civil Engineering program covers these attributes sufficiently, the learning outcomes for each course have been mapped to applicable CEAB Graduate Attributes as shown in the square brackets [] following each course learning outcome.

3. Investigation : An ability to conduct investigations of complex problems by methods that include appropriate experiments, analysis and interpretation of data, and synthesis of information in order to reach valid conclusions.
4. 6. Individual and team work : An ability to work effectively as a member and leader in teams, preferably in a multi-disciplinary setting.
7. Communication skills : An ability to communicate complex engineering concepts within the profession and with society at large.
