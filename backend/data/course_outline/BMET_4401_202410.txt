## Course Description
Presents the principles of operation, application, design, construction, potential hazards and common problems of diagnostic and therapeutic medical equipment (e.g. physiologic monitors, electrosurgical units, cardiac defibrillators, etc.) used in the clinical environment. Cover selected equipment in more detail (may go over schematic diagrams, wiring diagrams, performance assurance tests, calibration procedures and problem solving). Lab exercises are coordinated with lecture content.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Describe the principles of operation and clinical applications of selected biomedical devices.
- Interpret common problems associated with medical devices and their applications.
- Explain functional building blocks of selected biomedical devices.
- Read, interpret and trace signal flow in schematic diagrams of selected biomedical devices.
- Discuss potential hazards of biomedical devices and methods of risk mitigation.
- Perform electrical safety and performance assurance inspections on selected biomedical devices.
- Use equipment manuals in operating and servicing biomedical devices.

## Attendance Requirements
Students are expected to complete all course work and attend all classes, including laboratory sessions. Excessive absences may result in failure or withdrawal from this course. Laboratory work or assessments (such as lab exams, mid-term exams, or final exam) must be completed within the scheduled period, otherwise a zero mark will be assigned. Lab marks will be deducted for late lab report submission. No make-up lab or assessment will be provided except under excused absence (such as illness or other unavoidable cause as described below). In case of illness or other unavoidable cause of absence, the student must communicate as soon as possible with the course instructor. Based on the information provided, the instructor will determine if the absence is deemed to be excused.
