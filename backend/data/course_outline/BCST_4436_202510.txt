## Course Description
​This course provides student journalists in their final term with the opportunity to use the skills they have acquired during the previous three terms to produce a video documentary and accompanying digital article. The course builds on skills in journalism video production and critical thinking storytelling to enable the students to develop a concept, a pitch and a final documentary and multimedia digital story. The documentary is produced by a team and will be posted on the Broadcast and Online Journalism website.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Write a persuasive proposal, based on research, to pitch a viable documentary
- Work effectively within a team
- Demonstrate industry-standard sound-gathering techniques.
- Demonstrate industry-standard video gathering technique, including proper lighting
- Conduct interviews to support documentary focus
- Use industry-standard audio editing techniques which may include voiceover, natural sound, interviews, sound effects, and music.
- Use industry-standard video editing techniques
- Produce a video which demonstrates a strong focus and story arc
- Produce an engaging multimedia digital story showcasing the documentary​

## Evaluation Criteria
Criteria: %
Pitch: 20
Review Quiz: 5
Documentary Film - First Cut: 15
Documentary Digital Feature: 20
Documentary Film - Final Cut: 30
Self and Peer Evaluation: 10

Self and Peer Evaluation

At the end of the capstone project, the documentary team members will complete an evaluation of themselves and their peers. These marks will be averaged and submitted as the final Self and Peer Evaluation mark.

Adherence to Deadlines

Late submissions, without instructor approval, will be docked 5% for each school day submitted past the due date, as per program policy. Late assignments *may* be accepted without penalty, on a case-by-case basis with prior communication (and permission) in advance of the due date/time.

Journalistic Integrity

In addition to BCIT’s policy, academic integrity violations within the journalism program may also be defined as:

- Deliberately inaccurate or misleading reporting
- Misrepresentation of facts or individuals
- Conflicts of interest (including but not limited to interviewing family, friends, roommates, or individuals at places you work or accepting a gift or monetary compensation for coverage, excluding exchanges as part of cultural traditions)
- Falsifying audio, images, or video including the use of artificial intelligence
