## Course Description
This course introduces students to the communication needs of professionals working in OCHS. It covers writing clearly and organizing and presenting information for readers in short reports and instructions. Students also deliver a short oral training session for an OCHS audience.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Set communication objectives and analyze audiences.
- Write clearly and concisely and demonstrate correct use of English.
- Create appropriate style and tone for workplace readers.
- Select and organize information to meet reader needs.
- Design documents for readability.
- Design and integrate effective graphics.
- Use appropriate formats for workplace documents such as emails, memos, letters, and reports.
- Write instructions and procedures.
- Research and deliver an effective oral presentation on an OHS topic.
- Critically evaluate information to determine if it is accurate, relevant, and complete, and to use critical thinking to develop strong arguments.

## Other information
This course has learning outcomes related to developing critical thinking skills about AI (artificial intelligence) tools. You can meet these outcomes by engaging directly with AI tools or through alternative means if you choose not to use AI tools.

All learning activities will comply with BCIT privacy policies. The instructor will guide you on privacy protections. Ethical considerations of AI use will also be addressed.

AI tools may be used in a limited way for completing certain assessments in this course. Your instructor will provide guidelines on acceptable use of AI for each assessment. Limitations on AI use are intended to support you to critically assess the impacts of the tools, build fundamental communication skills, and take responsibility for your learning.

Note that AI-generated output can include incorrect or misleading information, fabricated authors/dates/publications, and may incorporate discriminatory ideas. While the instructor will provide guidance on verifying the quality, accuracy and appropriateness of AI-generated output, you, as the student, have the ultimate responsibility for ensuring that any work you submit for assessment meets the course requirements.

BCIT’s policies on academic integrity apply to all work in this course. You are responsible for checking the accuracy of your work and following sound referencing practices. You must use AI tools ethically and acknowledge use in all work submitted for a grade. Your instructor will provide guidelines on how to meet these requirements.
