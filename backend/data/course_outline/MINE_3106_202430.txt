## Course Description
Provides an overview of the processes of formation and principle characteristics of major mineral deposits. Mineral deposit formation is discussed by considering the source, transportation and deposition of metals and minerals and the textures, forms and mineral alteration of common mineral deposits. This is followed by a survey of major mineral deposit types including precious and base metals, diamonds, coal and industrial minerals. Laboratory exercises will focus on the study of rock sample suites from mineral deposits in British Columbia and elsewhere in North America. Some labs will involve analysis of structures in the field.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Recall the CIM guidelines for resource and reserve definitions [1,8].
- Classify mineral deposits according to common classification schemes [1].
- Describe the sources of ore-bearing fluids and the origin of the mineral constituents in the fluids [1].
- Explain the processes of ore-bearing fluid movement in the crust and the deposition of ore minerals from these fluids [1].
- Recall common mineral textures in ore deposits and their mode of formation [1].
- Describe the process of wall-rock alteration [1].
- List common alteration types and mineral assemblages[1].
- Give examples of common metal-zoning patterns in metallic mineral deposits[1].
- Discuss major theories of ore genesis, including magmatic, hydrothermal, and sedimentary[1].
- Describe the formation and characteristics of the major mineral deposit types studied in the course [1].
- Describe rock samples from selected mineral deposits in British Columbia (altered and unaltered wall rock as well as mineralized rock) [1,2].
- Assess the mineral potential and probable distribution of mineral deposits in different geological regions [3].
