### Course Description
​In this course, students will learn to safely install, splice, test and maintain fiber optic (FO) links. They will discuss light transmission, optical glass characteristics, and Wavelength Division Multiplexing (WDM) in optical systems, and use industry-standard practices to handle FO cables and connectors. In addition, students will have the opportunity to splice and terminate fiber spans using both mechanical and fusion splicing techniques. As part of their hands-on learning, students will interpret and produce fiber optic design documents for premises portions of the network using AutoCAD and/or other software tools. Furthermore, they will practice industry-standard assembly and testing techniques for fiber optic connectors and splice enclosures and perform fiber optic span power budget analysis. In a lab setting, students will operate Single-Mode (SM) fiber cleavers and fusion splicers, Optical Time Domain Reflectometers (OTDR), and other inspection and test equipment.​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Discuss the evolution of fiber optic communications, including current and legacy applications.
- Describe the transmission of light within an optical fiber, both Single Mode (SM) and Multi-Mode (MM).
- Discuss Coarse and Dense Wavelength Division Multiplexing (CWDM and DWDM).
- Describe the construction of an optical fiber cable.
- Identify and analyze light propagation impairments found on an optical fiber.
- Interpret premises fiber optic cabling designs and perform the specified installation, splicing, and labeling.
- Create premises fiber optic design documentation using AutoCAD and other software tools.
- Select and install splices, splice enclosures, and connectors on fiber optic cable spans.
- Inspect and evaluate fiber optic connectors using optical microscopes.
- Test and evaluate fiber spans using a light source and power meter or Visual Fault Locator (VFL).
- Use an OTDR to test and troubleshoot fiber optic spans.
- Create an optical fiber power budget.
- Recognize, describe, and mitigate the dangers inherent in working with glass fibers and fiber optic light sources.
- Safely perform common practices associated with fiber optic span installation, splicing and maintenance.​
