## Course Description
Physics 1164 is a general algebra-based physics course which emphasizes topics of special relevance to mechatronics and robotics. The laboratory portion of the course emphasizes the proper techniques of measurement, experimentation, and data analysis. The lecture portion of the course emphasizes basic Newtonian mechanics, including topics such as kinematics, static and dynamic equilibrium, work, energy, power, torque, rotational motion, linear and angular momentum.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of the lecture portion of this course, the student will be able to demonstrate his/her comprehension of the material by solving quantitative and qualitative problems involving:

- The proper techniques of unit conversion and dimensional analysis.
- Kinematics and dynamics of linear motion in one and two dimensions.
- The use of free-body diagrams and applying Newton’s Laws of Motion in one and two dimensions for both equilibrium and non-equilibrium situations.
- Friction, normal forces, gravity, inclines, tensile and compressive forces.
- The kinematics of rotational motion (angular displacement, velocity and acceleration).
- Moments of forces (torques), couples, moment of inertia and parallel axis theorem applied to both equilibrium and non-equilibrium situations.
- The concepts of work, energy, power, efficiency, and the principle of conservation of energy.
- The concepts of impulse, momentum, and the principle of conservation of momentum for both linear and rotational motion.

Upon successful completion of the laboratory portion of this course, the student will have the ability to:

- Properly use scientific instruments to obtain accurate measurements when operating technical equipment.
- Determine the mathematical relationship between two independent variables in an experiment by properly interpreting and analyzing data using computer graphing/curve-fitting software.
- Obtain an estimate of the uncertainty in a calculated quantity that is based upon measurements.
- Write a clear, concise, and properly formatted technical report.

Course Requirements For Passing Grade

The minimum passing grade for this course is 50% with the condition that both the laboratory and the lecture portions be passed separately with a minimum average of 50% in each. If both portions have an average of 50% or better, the weighted average of both portions will be assigned as the final grade. However, if a failing average of less than 50% occurs in any one portion, but not the other, and the weighted average of both portions is 50% or greater, a failing final grade of Unsatisfactory (U) will be assigned. In cases with a failing average in one or both portions, and where the weighted average of both portions is less than 50%, this failing weighted average will be assigned as the final grade.

## Course Specific Requirements
Course Website: All course related documents such as the lecture notes, lecture examples, lab schedule, formulae sheet, listing of grades, test solutions, etc. will be posted in Portable Document Format (.pdf) at the Learning Hub - https://learn.bcit.ca. After logging in, go to 'My Courses’ and under '2025.Fall', select the ‘PHYS-1164-0 - Physics for Robotics 1 - 31487 - Lecture/Lab Split’ option. Also, you may receive important e-mail announcements from your instructor over the course of the term, so please check your messages regularly at https://my.bcit.ca.

Pre-Class Preparation: Every week, based on the Proposed Lecture Schedule, you will be assigned sections of the text to be read in advance before they are to be discussed in class. This allows you to more easily understand the material presented in the class and to retain it for use in assignments and tests.

Note Taking: Because the class Lecture Notes are available on the course website in advance of the lectures, they may be printed out or transferred to your computer. Of course, you may preview them ahead of the lectures, and during lectures, the notes may be referenced and annotated at your convenience. More importantly, this allows you to concentrate on listening to the discussion and understanding the concepts being discussed. The lecture example questions are posted in advance as well, but the solutions to them are not posted in advance. It is very important that you actively participate by thinking about the work being presented by the instructor, verifying the mathematics, and giving some thought to the meaning of the solution presented. Also, you are encouraged to participate by answering questions, and posing your own questions as example problems are being discussed in class. The solutions to the lecture example problems done in class will be posted to the course website shortly afterwards.

Term Tests and Final Examination: These will be related to the assigned problems and concepts covered in classes and assignments. Term tests will be non-comprehensive, but the final exam will be comprehensive, involving all of the material covered over the course of the term. A formula sheet will be provided for use during all examinations.

Laboratory: Attendance and performance of laboratory experiments are mandatory. Before coming to the lab, students are expected to have prepared for the experiment by reading the appropriate section of the lab manual. After doing the reading, answer the assigned Lab Quiz for each experiment on the Learning Hub.

Laboratory Absences: Attendance and performance of laboratory experiments are mandatory and occur every week at the scheduled time. Missing three (3) or more labs with no legitimate reason, and not being made-up will result in a failing grade of Unsatisfactory (U) in the course, regardless of other grades. Legitimate reasons include illness and personal or family emergency situations. See the note below regarding illness. The instructor must be informed of this reason as soon as possible, and no later than 48 hours after the missed lab. The student is responsible for reaching out to the instructor so that a make-up lab can be scheduled in a timely manner.

Laboratory Reports: Written accounts of each experiment in the form of 'Laboratory Templates' will be required to be completed for each lab session. Lab Templates will be completed by the student and submitted for marking by the appointed due date and time. After being returned, marked Lab Templates should be attached to your Lab Binder for review and study purposes. Late lab reports will be penalized, and substantially incomplete reports will receive a mark of zero.

Laboratory Test: Near the middle and the end of the term (see Lab Schedule), you will be tested on your understanding of the theory, experimental methods, use of scientific equipment, calculations, and the physical interpretation of the results pertaining to the experiments you have performed. During the two (2) Lab Tests, you will be allowed to freely refer to your own Lab Binder which may contain the following: your own Lab Templates that you have created, and any of your own personal, handwritten notes. Unapproved photocopied material will not be allowed.

Workload: Set aside time for physics (and for your other classes, too) every week. It is very important to study regularly, keep up with the work and seek the assistance of the instructor when problems arise. The course load is quite heavy and to succeed you must be prepared to make the appropriate time commitment. The time that you will need to succeed in this course depends on your own background and abilities. As a rough guide, an ‘average’ time allotment is usually one hour for every classroom or lab hour per week. The pace of this (and your other) courses is very fast, so DO NOT FALL BEHIND! With a full load of other classes, it will be extremely difficult to catch up! Because physics is a “cumulative” subject (successful learning of new material is based on a sound understanding of previous material), you must ensure that a firm foundation of knowledge is built upon, starting from the beginning of the course!

Getting Help: Any difficulties that you may have should be resolved as soon as possible. Please consult with the instructor after class, during office hours, or by appointment. Also, the BCIT Student’s Society often hires 2nd year students, who have previously taken physics, to provide free ‘peer tutoring’ at select times and days of the week. In addition, see the note below concerning the Physics Learning Centre.

The centre is located in room SW3-4785, and is open between the times of 11:30 am to 1:20 pm on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday.
