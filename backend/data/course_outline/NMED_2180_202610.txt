### Course Description
​This is an introductory clinical experience that provides two weeks in the nuclear medicine department of an affiliated hospital. The student is provided with opportunities to develop the skills necessary to work safely in a nuclear medicine environment. The student will apply their skills in patient care and assist with nuclear medicine procedures. This course may also include introductory visits to specialized nuclear medicine departments, such as PET-CT, pediatrics, or a central radiopharmacy.​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will achieve the following learning outcomes at an introductory* level:​
- Communicate and interact effectively with patients, the department staff, healthcare team, and BCIT Clinical Coordinator(s) in a professional and timely manner. (1.2.4, 2.1.2, 2.1.3, 2.1.4, 2.2.3, 4.1.1)
- Practice punctuality, accountability, and consistent attendance in a professional manner. (1.3.4)
- Recognize safety principles for patient care, infection control, and radiation protection. (NM.1.1, NM.1.2, NM.1.3, NM.1.5, NM.1.7, NM.1.8)
- Seek and implement feedback from technologists and clinical coordinator(s) in a professional and timely manner. (1.3.4, 2.1.9, 6.1.1, 6.2.2)
- Safely perform basic gamma camera operations. (NM.3.7)
- Assist with patient positioning. (NM.7.4)
- Contribute to interprofessional education by interacting with another department. (3.1.3)
- Demonstrate respectful communication when interacting with people from diverse backgrounds. (1.5)​
*An introductory-level student has no previous clinical experience in a nuclear medicine department, only simulation practice at BCIT. An introductory student will observe and assist with routine tasks and procedures but will not work independently.

### Attendance Requirements
Arrangements to make up clinical time missed due to illness or other extenuating circumstances must be made through the BCIT Clinical Coordinator. Clinical shifts may be scheduled as early as 5:00 AM and as late as 10:00 PM, Monday through Sunday, depending on the site and department needs. Students are responsible for arranging their own transportation to and from their assigned clinical shifts.
### Safety equipment or protective clothing
Hospital regulations require students to wear protective lead aprons in certain clinical areas. These aprons are provided by the clinical sites. Additionally, students must wear their OSL dosimeters at all times while in the Nuclear Medicine department or in any other areas where occupational radiation exposure may occur.
