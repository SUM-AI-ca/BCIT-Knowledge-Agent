## Course Description
​This course provides students knowledge on forensic accounting strategies, data mining opportunities, investigative techniques, and process mapping tools to collate information and plan the investigation. You will learn how to use various open and closed sources of information during an investigation, and will review various forensic software tools. You will demonstrate how to move a fraud and financial crime investigation from initial incident to resolution, and then assess how to adjust your investigation plan in light of investigative challenges.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Identify potentially relevant open and closed information sources for an investigation.
- Develop and justify the proper investigative approach incorporating forensic accounting, data mining and process mapping tools.
- Propose strategies to stop the movement of funds by developing a transaction flow chart.
- ​Assess and mitigate the risk exposure stemming from a fraud investigation.​

## Course Goals
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to do the following:

- Identify and discuss the various steps involved in file coordination in a fraud or financial crime investigation.
- Explain and apply the principles supporting effective file coordination.
- Develop effective and comprehensive file coordination approach by creating a visual representation of your file coordination process by creating a process map
- Plan how to manage all the collected relevant open and closed sources of evidence collected during an investigation.

## Course topics
- Recap of basic Fraud and Financial Crime investigation.
- Introduction to concepts and theory of file coordination of a criminal investigation, including MCM, role, principles, workflow, and business rules.
- Integrate both workflow and business rules into a fraud scenario.
- Continued discussion on the concepts and theory of file coordination of a criminal investigation.
- Case management of fraud and financial crime investigations.
- Structuring the file management response.
- Introduction to the use of an investigative ledger.
- Concept and application of an index based file management methodology.
- Theory and application of Naming Conventions.
- Classification of investigative file materials.
- Review, assessment and integration of sample file materials.
- Understanding different sources of information.
- Criminal standards for evidence collection.
- Legal requirements for admissibility.
- Integration of file coordination into ongoing investigation.
