## Course Description
This course introduces common Industrial Control System (ICS) cybersecurity standards that INC graduates will encounter in the workplace. Building on the concepts developed in the Linux Administration, Ethical Hacking and Penetration Testing, and Industrial Network Security courses, this course prepares students to assist with implementing common ICS cyber security standards as they apply to industrial networks. Topics include: the differences between policies, standards, procedures, guidelines and frameworks ; a general overview of the steps involved in an ICS security standard; the difference between enterprise/business IT security standards and ICS security standards; a survey of ICS cybersecurity standards commonly used in both North America and Europe and their application in automation and process control; and a close look at all three phases of the ISA/IEC 62443 standard with an emphasis on risk assessment and mitigation. ICS standards and frameworks studied include ISA/IEC 62443, NIST 800-82 and NERC CIP. ​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Explain the differences between policies, standards, procedures, guidelines and frameworks. (2)
- Describe in general the steps involved in an ICS security standard. (2)
- Explain the main differences between enterprise/business IT security standards and ICS security standards. (2)
- Describe the steps taken in the NIST 800-82 framework and its application in industry. (2)
- Describe the steps taken in the NERC CIP standard and its application in industry. (2)
- Describe the steps taken in the ISA/IEC 62443 standard and its application in industry. (2)
- List ICS cybersecurity standards commonly used in both North America and Europe. (1)
- Describe the typical application of ICS cybersecurity standards commonly used in both North America and Europe. (2)
- Perform a cybersecurity vulnerability and risk assessment in accordance with the ISA/IEC 62443 standard. (4)
- Recommend mitigating actions based on zones and conduits in accordance with the ISA/IEC 62443 standard. (4)

## Attendance Requirements
Attendance in class is mandatory.

In case of illness or other unavoidable cause of absence, the student must communicate as soon as possible with his/her instructor indicating the reason for the absence.

Prolonged illness which causes the student to miss 10% or more of the lectures and labs will require a BCIT-approved medical certificate submitted to the department, substantiating the reason for the absence.

Unapproved absence of 10% or more of the lectures and labs may result in failure or forced withdrawal from this course.

Laboratories

Participation in all laboratory sessions is required and will be checked. If an illness or emergency arises the instructor and assistant instructor must be contacted as soon as reasonably possible. It may be possible to attend a different lab session but make up lab sessions are not available. If absence is recurring, BCIT’s policies regarding medical certificates will apply.

Makeup Test, Exams or Quizzes

There will be no makeup tests, exams or quizzes. If you miss a test, exam or quiz, you will receive zero marks for the work missed. Exceptions may be made for extenuating circumstances or for medical reasons according to departmental policy and documented by a BCIT Student Medical Certificate (https://www.bcit.ca/files/healthservices/pdf/studentmedicalcertificate.pdf). In such a case, it is the responsibility of the student to inform one of the course instructors immediately. Note that exceptions will not be made for personal events such as vacations, weddings, or travel arrangements.
