### Course Description
​This course starts with the study of engineering survey properties, basic survey equipment errors and their mitigation techniques, the application of optical and electromagnetic measuring principles and techniques, and the associated safety issues. It then continues with student skill development in the techniques of vertical control surveys by exploring appropriate standards and specifications; symmetric and non-symmetric transition curves, including terminal curves; mining and tunnelling surveying, including underground control point transfer and azimuth determination with gyro equipment. The course ends with an introduction to offshore engineering survey. Students learn through lectures, assignments, and hands-on labs.​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Explain different classes of surveys and how they are related.
- Explain the principles, concepts, observing techniques and instrumentation involved in engineering surveys.
- Identify safety issues involved in engineering survey projects.
- Discuss general error properties of measurements, including precision and accuracy indicators, survey standards and survey specifications, and how to minimize the effects of these errors.
- Use high-precision surveying equipment (geodetic level, precision total station) according to specifications and guidelines.
- Apply appropriate standards and specifications in carrying out vertical control surveys.
- Define some commonly used terms in mining surveys.
- Compare different techniques for transferring positions and orientation from surface points to underground points in underground mining or tunnelling projects.
- Use gyro equipment (based on gyro orientation principles in space) to determine astronomic azimuth, including the reduction of the astronomic azimuth to its grid azimuth equivalent.
- Analyze positions and positional uncertainties in mining and tunnelling surveys.
- Compute data for layout of symmetric and non-symmetric transition curves and terminal curves.
- Analyze surveying techniques in offshore engineering surveys.
- Discuss the capabilities and limitations of some survey techniques and instrumentation in transferring positions and orientations underground in underground mines or in tunnelling projects.​

### Attendance Requirements
Lab attendance is mandatory; if you missed a lab, but still managed to hand in the lab assignment at the due date, you will still lose 25% of the marks alloted to that lab assignment (due to failure to attend the lab). Other details are as follows:
- Due date for each assignment/lab will be provided with the assignment/lab; if this date is revised, the students will be duly informed in class.
- Due date will be enforced; late submission of assignment without a genuine document reason is unacceptable according to Department policy. Any assignment handed in late will result in a grade of 0% of the mark allotted to that assignment.
- Every lab question must be substantially completed; no question should be left un-attempted or poorly attempted – the instructor will only provide feedback on questions substantially attempted by the student.
- Students are to respect instructor expectations concerning attendance, assignments, and deadlines
- Each student is expected to display a positive work ethic and to show responsibility for his/her education
- Attendance will be taken during every lab - an instructor's signature on the student lab assignment (during the lab time) is an indication that the student attended and participated in that lab; it is a sufficient proof that the student attended or did not attend the lab.
Field Lab Details
There are three field labs in which the students will be expected to independently collect data. Completing these field labs as scheduled and handing them in on time is mandatory. If any student misses any of these field labs, he/she will be expected to make a personal arrangement to complete the labs on their own time and hand in. The student will only be allowed to do the field lab if they had made prior arrangement (before the original lab was done) with the instructor with an explanation on why they would not be doing the lab as scheduled. Failure to complete all of the field labs will earn the student an incomplete (INC) grade for this course.
- Due dates for the field labs will be provided in class/lab times; if the due dates are revised, the students will be duly informed in the appropriate class/lab times.
- Due dates will be enforced; late submission of a field lab will result in a grade of zero for that lab; although the student will earn a grade of zero for the late submission, the student will not receive INC if the lab is assessed (by the instructor) to have been substantially completed.
Other Details
- Assignments, tests, quizzes and exams must be completed as scheduled; no makeup for assignments, tests, quizzes and exams. In case of illness supported with a BCIT medical certificate (according to the BCIT Policy 5101), the instructor will decide on what to do on the late assignment, missed tests, missed quizzes or exams and communicate such decision to the student promptly.
- Students are to carefully check their grades in any assignment, test, quiz and exam for any possible instructor's mistakes and inconsistencies; any mistake or inconsitency and complaint must be brought to the notice of the instructor for any necessary action within 5 working days, otherwise, it will be interpreted that the student agrees with the instructor on the grading.
