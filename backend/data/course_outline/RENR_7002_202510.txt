## Course Description
This course covers the history, purpose and methods of environmental impact assessment (EIA). The purpose and an overview of approaches to EIA are presented and the Environmental Assessment Act of BC will be used as an example of environmental assessment (EA) policy and procedure.. Environmental assessment procedures according to Canadian Standards Association (CSA) will also be discussed. Examples of EA in other jurisdictions (e.g., USA, South America, et al.) will be reviewed. Methods and techniques for assessing environmental impacts and actions, such as avoidance, mitigation, reclamation or restoration, are surveyed and critically examined through class discussions, guest speakers and case studies.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
At the end of this course the student will be able to:

- Describe the general process of environmental assessment.
- Discuss, with respect to environmental assessment, concepts and methods for valuing natural resources, and ecological goods and services.
- Discuss the role of the public and of advocacy groups in environmental assessment.
- Interpret and apply the BC Environmental Assessment policy.
- Critically assess selected EA case studies from B.C. or other jurisdictions.
- Critically assess various strategies to deal with environmental impacts.
- Describe the purpose and mandate of the Canadian Standards Association with respect to EA.

Attendance/Illness (BCIT policy): In case of illness or other unavoidable cause of absence, the student must communicate as soon as possible with her/his instructor or Program Head, indicating the reason for the absence. Students who are seeking accommodation for a medical absence must have a BCIT approved medical certificate submitted to the program, substantiating the reason for absence. Unapproved absence in excess of the prescribed regulations within this document may result in failure or forced withdrawal from the course or program. Please see Policy 5101 - Student Regulations.

Attendance/Illness (RENR guidelines): If you need to miss class, please (1) notify instructors with as much advanced notice as possible and (2) arrange for a classmate to provide you with all missed handouts/notes/instructions. To categorize an absence or late arrival as excused, medical documentation or approval by the instructor is required. Instructors may allocate course marks to attendance, with marks deductions made for unexcused absences and late arrivals. To pass a course, unexcused absences cannot exceed 10% of class meetings (typically 1.5 class meetings). For unexcused absences/late arrivals, course activities cannot be made up. For excused absences, instructors may assign alternative work as make up (especially for course components that are challenging to replicate such as guest lectures or labs).
