### Course Description
​This hands-on course allows the learner to work in a team, to design and execute a live experiential event. Learners will conduct research, consult with stakeholders, develop a proposal, and operate the event. Topics include event design, supplier negotiations, operations, and reporting. The course emphasizes experience design and event management principles to develop an effective program that meets the marketing objectives of the client. Learners will produce a marketing event for the School of Business + Media. ​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Develop effective public speaking skills by delivering oral presentations (individual and team) to audiences of diverse sizes.
- Use current, practical, and technical terminology in reference to experience design.
- Design and carry out an experiential event to meet the marketing objectives of a client.
- Prepare a strategy, budget, and operational plan for a live event.
- Select web-based tools that can be used in experience design.
- Prepare a post event evaluation report
- Describe current trends in experience design.​

### Course Goals
Building on the foundations of experience design, this course immerses students in the strategic and creative process of crafting impactful experiential marketing initiatives. Key topics include attendee engagement psychology, the role of AI in personalized experiences, designing immersive event spaces, measuring event ROI, and planning large-scale event logistics.
Students develop skills through six scaffolded individual assignments, an individual capstone project with recorded presentation, and hands-on participation in the BCIT Silent Auction. The asynchronous delivery model allows students to engage deeply with each topic, applying concepts to their own chosen event concept that develops over the full 10 weeks. Students after successful completion of the course feel comfortable with:
- Present experiential marketing strategy through recorded video presentations and professional written submissions that demonstrate command of industry terminology and frameworks.
- Apply current frameworks, terminology, and best practices in experience design and event marketing to original, individual work throughout the course.
- Design a complete experiential marketing event concept integrating engagement strategy, AI-powered personalization, immersive spatial design, and clearly stated marketing objectives.
- Prepare a comprehensive strategy, budget, and operational plan for a live experiential event, including vendor management, staffing, logistics, and contingency planning.
- Evaluate and apply AI, data-driven, and emerging digital tools in the design of personalized, measurable attendee experiences.
- Develop a ROI measurement framework that translates qualitative experiential outcomes into quantifiable marketing metrics aligned with client objectives.
- Prepare a post-event evaluation report that critically assesses event performance against stated KPIs, marketing objectives, and budget.
- Analyze emerging trends in experience design — including AI personalization, immersive environments, and large-scale logistics — and assess their strategic implications for the industry.
- Contribute effectively and professionally as a team member in the planning and execution of a live experiential marketing event in a professional venue context.

### Course Specific Requirements
Individual Work Policy
All assignments, the Individual Experiential Marketing Project, and the Post-Event Analysis Report are to be completed individually. There is no group project work in this course with the sole exception of the instructor-assigned Silent Auction teams in Week 9. Students must not divide, share, or co-author individual assignment submissions.
Academic Integrity & AI
Students are expected to act ethically and with academic integrity at all times. The unauthorized use of AI tools to generate written assignment content is prohibited and will be handled in accordance with BCIT Policy 5104.
AI Use Policy — Read Carefully Exception: Assignment 3 (AI-Powered Attendee Journey Map) and the AI/emerging technologies module (Week 3) explicitly require students to use and evaluate AI tools as part of the learning task. Clearly documented AI use is required in these assignments. Outside of these designated uses, AI-generated written content is not permitted.
Online Professionalism
- Discussion board posts should be professionally written. Treat all peers and instructors with respect; this is a professional learning environment.
- Recorded presentations should be filmed in a professional context: quiet space, good lighting, clear audio, and appropriate attire.
- Students are encouraged to go beyond supplied resources and bring independent research and industry examples to their posts and assignments.
- Questions about assignments should be posted to the relevant discussion thread on Learning Hub so that answers benefit the full class.
Preparation
Students are responsible for accessing and reviewing module content before engaging with that week’s assignment and discussion post. It is the student’s responsibility to understand what is required each week and to seek out any missed content, preferably from module recordings and Learning Hub resources.
Mandatory In-Person Event — Week 9
In-Person Attendance Required — Week 9 Attendance at the Week 9 Silent Auction Event (Great Hall, BCIT Burnaby Campus) is mandatory. This is the only required in-person component of the course. Students who are unable to attend due to extenuating circumstances must notify the instructor in writing at least 48 hours in advance. Students who miss the event without prior notification and an acceptable reason will receive a grade of zero for the Silent Auction Event Participation component. Students completing their Intnership ouitside of the lower mainland, and unable to attend in person will be given an alternative assignment.
- Business casual attire is required for the Silent Auction event. More formal attire is appropriate and encouraged.
- Students will be placed into Silent Auction working teams by the instructor. These teams are separate from all individual course work.
- Students who have ceased to engage with the course for 10 or more days without explanation may be assigned a Vanished (V) grade, calculated as 0 in GPA.
This course is delivered fully online and asynchronously. There are no required log-in times. Students are expected to engage with module content, assignments, and discussion boards throughout the week — not in a single session. Consistent weekly engagement is essential to course success.
Online Engagement Expectations
- Log in to BCIT Learning Hub at least three times per week to engage with module content, peer posts, and instructor feedback.
- All assignments and discussion posts are due by Sunday 11:59 pm of each module week. Late submissions are subject to a 10% deduction per calendar day.
- Initial discussion board posts are due by Thursday each week; peer responses by Sunday. Quality of engagement — not quantity of words — is evaluated.
- Modules not accessed or completed within the module week are treated as an unexcused absence for participation grading purposes.
