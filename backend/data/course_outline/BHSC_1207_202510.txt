## Course Description
This course presents information on the structure and function of human organ systems under normal conditions and in response to challenges from the environment. Specifically we explore ways in which organ systems respond to challenges in various work environments. Structure and function of lungs, skin, liver, kidneys, endocrine organs, ears and eyes are discussed, and potential occupational hazards impacting these organs and related systems are identified. Finally we discuss abnormal function of these organs arising from their interactions with specific hazards.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Communicate effectively with other health team members in terms of human anatomy and physiology.
- Apply anatomical and physiological principles to the Occupational Health and Safety field.
- Apply appropriate directional and regional anatomical terminology to the description of body parts, body cavities, and organs.
- Relate the functions of body systems to the common goal of homeostasis.
- Describe the four basic tissue types found in organs of the body (epithelia, connective, muscle, nervous tissues) and identify their relative vulnerability to injurious agents.
- Describe the gross and microscopic structure of the skin and explain the skin's roles in protecting underlying structures and in temperature regulation.
- Describe normal structure and functions of special sense organs (eyes and ears) and explain their vulnerability to occupational hazards.
- Explain the normal role of the endocrine system in the maintenance of homeostasis and predict the consequences of endocrine disruption on body function.
- Describe the normal structure and functioning of the lungs and respiratory airways and discuss the consequences of abnormal respiratory function.
- Describe the normal structure of the liver and explain its role in detoxification.
- Describe the structure of the urinary system and explain how the kidneys function in excretion of wastes and regulation of fluids and electrolytes.

## Course Schedule and Assignments
Week: Content
ONE: Introduction to the course Module 1: Organization of the Body Anatomical position Directional terminology Anatomical planes and sections Body cavities Module 2: Body Cells Cell structure Organelle function Structure of the plasma membrane Membrane transport Cell injury related to workplace hazards
TWO & THREE: Module 3: Tissues Classification of tissues Structure and function of epithelial tissue Structure and function of connective tissue Body membranes Module 4: The Integumentary System Structure and function of the skin Skin wound healing Homeostasis and thermoregulation Effects of workplace hazards on the skin and temperature regulation
FOUR & FIVE: Module 5: The Skeletal System Structure of bone and cartilage Appendicular and axial skeletons Functions of the skeletal system Joint structure and movements Work-related skeletal and joint injuries and disorders Bone fractures and healing Module 6: The Muscular System Classification of muscle tissue Major skeletal muscle groups Skeletal muscle structure Skeletal muscle innervation and contraction Muscle fatigue Work-related muscle injuries and disorders TERM EXAM 1 (week 5)
SIX: Module 7: The Eye and the Ear Structure and function of the eye Structure and function of the ear Structures vulnerable to injury Effects of workplace hazards on the eye and ear
SEVEN: Module 8: The Respiratory System Structure and function of airways and the lungs Control of respiration Effect of respiratory hazards on airways and respiratory function Occupational lung diseases
EIGHT: Module 9: The Liver Structure and function of the liver Role of the liver in detoxification Effects of workplace hepatotoxins (absorbed or inhaled) TERM EXAM 2 (week 8)
NINE: Module 10: The Kidneys Structure and function of the kidneys Role of the kidneys in the removal of toxins Effects of workplace nephrotoxins
SPRING BREAK
TEN to THIRTEEN: Module 11: The Endocrine System Role of the endocrine system in homeostasis Structure and function of the hypothalamus, pituitary gland, thyroid gland, parathyroid glands, adrenal glands, endocrine pancreas, and ovaries/testes. Endocrine Disruptors and their effect on homeostasis
FOURTEEN: FINAL EXAM (last class of week 14)
