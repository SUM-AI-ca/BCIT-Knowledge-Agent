## Course Description
(UBC BIOT 306) Divided into two major sections: Immunology and Virology. The immunology section covers the innate immune response, and the development of the cell-mediated immune response and humoral immune response. The virology section covers the replication cycles of several RNA and DNA animal viruses. The lab provides training in CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing, analysis of gene and protein expression in cells and experience in handling and quantitating Baculovirus.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Describe the role of barriers in defense against infection.
- Describe the effector molecules and cells involved in innate immunity, and how pathogen recognition occurs in the innate immune response.
- Describe the inflammatory response and acute phase response.
- Discuss antigen receptor structure and the generation of antigen receptor diversity.
- Describe T and B cell development.
- Describe the cell-mediated and humoral immune response.
- Describe allergies, hypersensitivity reactions and autoimmunity.
- Describe the structure of typical animal cell viruses.
- Discuss the elements of a typical animal cell virus replication cycle.
- Describe in detail the replication cycle of several RNA and DNA animal viruses.
- Discuss the effects of viral infection on host cells.
- Describe antiviral drugs, vaccines and vaccine development.
- Use CRISPR-Cas 9 to knock-out genes in animal cell lines.
- Generate CRISPR-edited cell clones for analysis.
- Use ELISA, Western blot and qRT-PCR to analyze protein expression and gene expression in animal cell lines.
- Use flow cytometry to analyze immune cell populations from mouse spleen.
- Handle and quantitate recombinant Baculovirus.

## Attendance Requirements
Students are expected to attend all lecture and lab sessions unless they have a serious illness or other medical condition. In the Biotechnology Program, laboratory attendance is of the highest priority since lab experience is what sets this program apart from other undergraduate programs and what prepares students for co-op work terms where it is expected that they will have acquired specific competencies. Furthermore, lab work is typically done in pairs or small teams and students are, therefore, expected to work collaboratively and support their lab partners.

For some lab-based courses, participation marks will be given for completion of laboratory activities and these marks will be worth up to 5 % of the student’s final course grade. A student must be on time for start of the lab period to avoid losing participation marks. These lab activities involve generating lab specific materials and/or data, learning, acquiring and/or practicing new skills and/or procedures. In addition, to submit a lab report or assignment, a student must participate in 80% of the lab periods associated with the lab report or assignment. In the case of illness where a medical note is provided, lab participation marks will not be lost for those missed lab period(s) and will not be counted towards the 80% participation marks required to submit a lab report or assignment.

Please notify your instructor and the Biotechnology program head of any absences. If exams, quizzes, or in-class assignments are missed for reasons of illness, medical notes will be required to reschedule.
