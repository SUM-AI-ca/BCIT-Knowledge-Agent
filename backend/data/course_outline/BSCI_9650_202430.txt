### Course Description
The Research Proposal course prepares the students to define a research problem, develop a viable research methodology, and publish or present their results. They are expected to demonstrate their understanding of the subject matter, define a research problem through analysis of relevant literature and defend the appropriateness of their research design during their research proposal examination.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Formulate research problem through analysis of literature surveys.
- Develop suitable research plans.
- Develop methodology.
- Develop professional autonomy, lines of argument, and make sound judgments independently.
- Demonstrate the feasibility of the proposed project taking the time and available resources into consideration.
- Communicate research plan and methodology orally and in written form.
### Learning Resources
There are no mandatory textbooks for this course. Students are expected to have their own laptops and to co-ordinate with their research supervisors on obtaining the necessary materials and resources to complete their research.
### Evaluation Criteria
Criteria | % | Comments
The research proposal is evaluated by the Thesis Supervisory Committee. The proposal evaluation process consists of two parts: the reading and examination of the research proposal; the thesis defence which consists of an oral presentation by the student followed by one or several rounds of questions by the committee members that could last about one hour. The decision of the Thesis Supervisory Committee is based both on the proposal and on the candidate's ability to defend it. All members of the examination committee are to provide a vote (accepted or rejected). The Thesis Supervisory Committee can render one of four decisions, subject to a vote of majority. The thesis can be: 1. Accepted as submitted, which may include corrections that do not require the supervisor’s approval. 2. Accepted with minor modification s defined as corrections which can be made within no more than four weeks and to the satisfaction of the supervisor. 3. Accepted with major modifications. The Examiner’s Report will include a precise description of the modifications along with a date, within three (3) months for their completion. It may be necessary for the student to re-register for an extension term to complete these modifications. It is then the responsibility of the student’s supervisor to confirm in writing to the Thesis Supervisory Committee and the Building Science Department that the required modifications have been made and approved. It is not necessary for the Thesis Supervisory Committee to reconvene. 4. Rejected. Such a proposal may be re-submitted only once, in revised form. Such re-submission can only be made six (6) months or more from the date of the original defence. Formal re-submission of a proposal follows the same procedure as an initial submission. | The final grade entered will be either S-Satisfactory or U-Unsatisfactory.
### Attendance Requirements
To ensure that the Research Proposal progresses in the right direction, the student needs to schedule regular meetings with the supervisor.
### Course Specific Requirements
The writing of the research proposal should start as soon as the student acquires the proper background on the project and knowledge from the courses and discussion with the supervisor. The proposal is the critical planning of the research project that includes overarching goals, objectives, literature review, scope, equipment, materials and methods, timeline, and expected research outcomes.
Proposal preparation and writing will begin in term 1 of year 1 to complete the research project in time. The student needs to keep in mind that the proposal writing is iterative with continuous feedback from the supervisor. As a general guideline, the expected number of single-sided, double-spaced paragraph pages in the research proposal is between 40 and 50, including about 10 to 15 pages of literature review.

Building Science Graduate Program Policy on Repeating Courses
This policy supercedes the School policy below : No more than two courses may be repeated with no course being repeated more than once. Where a failing grade is obtained in a core course, the student will be required to repeat the course and obtain a passing grade. If a student fails to obtain a passing grade in the repeated course, the student may apply, on the recommendation of the Graduate Program Director, to the Dean of the school for permission to take the course a third time. If permission is not granted, the student may be requested to withdraw from the program. Where a failing grade is obtained in an elective course, the student may repeat the course once or take an alternate course.
