### Course Description
Covers rules of differentiation, rates of change, related rates, differentials, implicit differentiation with application to electrical and mechanical problems, definite and indefinite integral, calculation of mean, RMS values for various electrical waveforms, integration techniques, and the Fourier series applied to electrical circuits.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Use differential calculus to solve problems relating to the electronics for Biomedical Engineering Technology.
- Use integral calculus to solve problems relating to the electronics for Biomedical Engineering Technology.
- Solve equations using Newton’s method.
- Apply fundamental differentiation rules including the product rule, quotient rule and chain rule.
- Perform calculations involving rates of change in electrical and simple mechanical systems.
- Determine maximum and minimum values of a function.
- Apply implicit differentiation.
- Use differentials to estimate errors in measurements.
- Use methods of substitution, integration by parts, table lookup to evaluate the indefinite and definite integral of a function.
- Analyze damped sinusoidal function and apply solution to circuit response.
- Use numerical integration to approximate definite integrals.
- Calculate the average and root mean square value of a periodic waveform.
- Find the Fourier series expansion of periodic signals.
- Relate Fourier series to signal filtering.

### Course Goals
The goals of this course are to introduce, to the student, important concepts that can only be appreciated by studying calculus. The stress is on physical appreciation of the concepts rather than a mathematical abstraction. The basic concepts of calculus are developed heuristically. The student will be required to master fundamental algebraic manipulations and will be expected to solve problems in a systematic and organized manner.

### Course Schedule and Assignments
* This schedule is subject to change at the discretion of the instructor.
Weeks 1 & 2
- Average and instantaneous rates of change for continuous functions.
- The concept of a limit.
- Basic rules of differentiation applied to simple electrical applications.
Reference/Reading: Mathematics for Electronics: Part I - Pre calculus Chapter 8
Weeks 3 & 4
- Rules of differentiation: power rule, product rule, quotient rule, chain rule and implicit differentiation.
- Applications to differentiation to curve sketching and maximum-minimum problems.
Reference/Reading: Mathematics for Electronics: Part I - Pre calculus Chapter 9
Week 5
- Differentials and related rates
Reference/Reading: Mathematics for Electronics: Part I - Pre calculus Chapter 10
Weeks 6 & 7
- The indefinite integral with application to integrating circuits.
- Numerical integration and introduction to simple difference equations.
Reference/Reading: Mathematics for Electronics: Part II - Calculus
Week 8
- The definite integral with application to average and RMS problems.
Reference/Reading: Mathematics for Electronics: Part II - Calculus
Weeks 9 & 10
- Introduction to the differentiation and integration of trigonometric and exponential functions with applications.
Reference/Reading: Mathematics for Electronics: Part II - Calculus
Weeks 11 & 12
- Special integrating techniques with applications including integration by parts.
Reference/Reading: Mathematics for Electronics: Part II - Calculus
Weeks 13 & 14
- Fourier series.
Reference/Reading: Mathematics for Electronics: Part II - Calculus
Week 15
- Final Exam
