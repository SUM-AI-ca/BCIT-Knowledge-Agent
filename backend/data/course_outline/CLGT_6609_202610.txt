### Course Description
This course builds on the theory and principles learned in CLGT 5507 and introduces the students to wet-lab processing of microarrays. Students will gain additional experience analyzing CMA data files and become more familiar with a variety of CMA results and their clinical implications. This course will offer an introduction to genetic counselling issues and emerging applications of CMA to the field of clinical genetics.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
By the end of this course, learners will be able to:
- Use microarray analysis software to view CMA data files.
- Apply the principles of microarray technology when processing samples in a hands-on workshop.
- Evaluate the quality of CMA data using vendors QC metrics and offer troubleshooting recommendations for suboptimal results.
- Use interpretation guidelines and reference databases for CNV assessment (DECIPHER, OMIM, DGV, etc), determine whether a CNV is benign, pathogenic or a variant of unknown significance (VOUS).
- Generate a CMA summary report from CMA data files, including, a summary of data quality, list of findings, an interpretation of the results and a summary with ISCN nomenclature and recommendations for future testing.
- Communicate effectively with other students and the instructor.
- Conduct themselves in a professional manner during lectures and lab sessions.

### Course Schedule and Assignments
Assignment Details
- Use of generative AI tools (eg ChatGPT, etc.)
Student analysis of lab data is an important part of student learning. Instructional staff do not believe that generative AI tools like ChatGPT are an adequate substitute for student judgement in analyzing data. Reports to be submitted for marking are modelled after the format used at clinical sites, so there is little opportunity to use generative AI to write these reports. However, if students do choose to use one of these tools, please follow these guidelines:
Cite and number each use, like this [1, ChatGPT, (date)]
Mark all generative AI output, by using “quotes” for using unedited genAI output, and ‘quotes’ for using paraphrased genAI output
For all generative AI content, student must include primary source verification i.e. original research journal articles or chapters from traditionally published textbooks.
