## Course Description
Operations management may be defined as the design, operation and improvement of productive systems that create the organization's products or services. The demand for quality, time-based competition and international production has demonstrated the importance of operations management to the survival of the organization. The students will be introduced to the fields of Problem Solving, Process Improvement, and Design Based Development. A special emphasis will be placed on the development of teamwork skills and team problem solving and approaches to business improvement opportunities. Within the general educational philosophy of Collaborative Learning, the student, working in learning teams, will learn and apply various process improvement techniques within the Plan, Do, Check, Act (PDCA) cycle, such as Pareto's Law, Activity Sampling, Process Mapping, Multi-column Charts, Check Sheets, Histograms, Ishikawa diagrams, Critical Examination, Brainstorming, Nominal Group Technique, Multi-voting and the Multiple Criteria Evaluation Matrix.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
At the end of this course the student will be able to:

- Explain a systematic problem solving approach to improve business processes.
- Develop a Terms of Reference.
- Identify non-value-added activities and opportunities for improvement.
- Use techniques as benchmarking and performance measures to focus on process improvement activities.
- Describe how eliminating non-value-added activity improves quality and operations.
- Use Interviewing for Information, flowcharts, Pareto Analysis, Ishikawa diagrams, Critical Examination and other “tools” to document and analyse data.
- Use creativity and creative problem solving techniques to develop improved processes.
- Perform a qualitative cost-benefit analysis on an investment decision.
- Explain implementation strategies for new processes.
- Write a report to document findings and propose solutions/recommendations.

## Attendance Requirements
Regular attendance in lectures and labs is seen as integral to student success, therefore, attendance in class is monitored. Unexcused absences in excess of 10% of the time prescribed for this course may result in the assignment of a failing grade. Attendance may be taken at any time during class. A student not present for attendance will be marked absent. A student who leaves class for a period of time deemed excessive by the instructor may be considered absent regardless of whether they are present when attendance is taken.

In case of illness or other unavoidable absence, students must communicate with the instructor, or the appropriate Program Head, as soon as possible (preferably in advance) indicating the reason for the absence. Students who are seeking accommodation for a medical absence must have a BCIT-approved medical certificate (https://www.bcit.ca/files/healthservices/pdf/studentmedicalcertificate.pdf). For other absences, students must be prepared to provide appropriate supporting documentation.

A student who has unexcused absences in excess of 10% of the time prescribed for the course by the Withdrawal Deadline is considered to have ‘Vanished’ and will be assigned a grade of ‘V’. In these cases, no further work will be graded.

These requirements are set out in accordance with BCIT Policy 5101 Student Regulations: https://www.bcit.ca/files/pdf/policies/5101.pdf.

## Course Specific Requirements
Late Submissions:

Assignments are due at the specified deadline. For every 24-hour period (or part thereof) that an assignment is late, 10% of the total possible grade will be deducted. This means that even if an assignment is submitted just one minute past the deadline, it will incur the full 10% penalty for the first 24-hour period.

Group Project:

This course features team-based work. While all team members typically receive the same mark, exceptions may be made if issues arise that disrupt the team's functioning or fairness. Depending on the circumstances, any of the following actions may be taken:

- Marks may be redistributed among team members.
- Individual contributions may be assessed and marked separately.
- A team member may be removed from the group.

These actions may be considered for various reasons, including but not limited to:

- Frequently absent from team meetings or classes.
- Submitting plagiarized or low-effort work.
- Missing team deadlines or class deadlines.
- Behavior that is disruptive or detrimental to team performance.

As in the workplace, your team will need to have a team charter and document infractions, escalating the issue to your instructor at minimum 2 weeks before the assignment is due. The instructor will then guide your team through the appropriate process. In this process, the team will have three options:

Option 1: Mark Redistribution If all members of the group agree that grades should be reallocated, up to 40% will be taken off one student’s score and redistributed to the other students.

Option 2: Separate Marking If group members do not reach an agreement on how marks should be reallocated, each team member’s contributions will be marked separately.

Option 3: Removal from the Group If all conditions outlined below are met, the team may choose to remove a member from the group. That member will be required to complete the remaining work of the term project individually.

In order to initiate this process, the following conditions must be met:

- The team must have a team charter, signed by all members, that states the desired action can be taken (or references this course outline where this policy is described).
- There must be documented evidence of the team member's non-compliance. This may take the form of records of assigned and submitted work, evidence of quality, effort or plagarism issues, documentation of missed meetings, or other relevant examples. Documentation must be created before or as the issues occur, not after the fact.
- The student must have received at least one verbal and two written warnings, with the final warning clearly stating the action the team will take if another violation occurs.
- All remaining members of the team must agree on the chosen course of action.

If these conditions are met, the team may iniate the process by emailing the instructor with evidence of hte above and booking office hours to discuss.
