## Course Description
​Women's Health Sonography 2 covers 1st, 2nd, and 3rd-trimester sonography. Normal and abnormal maternal and fetal anatomy, embryology, and pathophysiology are discussed along with scan techniques to thoroughly assess and document findings. Critical thinking skills are developed through the correlation of sonographic findings with clinical history, laboratory results, and alternate imaging modalities. The theory taught in this course will be integrated with laboratory practice in DSON 2011 through both live and simulated practice.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Differentiate normal intrauterine and extrauterine maternal anatomy and fetal anatomy, anomalous, pathologic conditions, and their spatial anatomic relationships as visualized by ultrasound or schematic diagrams. (4.2b)
- Given a clinical scenario, explain how to investigate abnormal findings on obstetrical sonographic exams. (5.3j)
- Correlate embryonic development with sonographic findings as visualized by ultrasound.
- Explain how to appropriately respond to non-verbal behaviours and patient questions and concerns during gynecologic and obstetrical sonography exams in a given scenario. (1.1h, 1.3b)
- Describe the requisite protocols, images and measurements required for complete obstetrical sonograms and how to set up the room and patient to ensure patient dignity.
- Given a clinical scenario, describe appropriate measurement techniques used during 2nd and 3rd-trimester obstetrical sonograms as described in NCP 6.1 Appendix A. (4.2b)
- With colour and/or PW Doppler (as applicable), assess the endometrium, pelvis, ovaries, uterus, cerebral vessels, umbilical cord, placenta, and cord Doppler. (4.2b)
- Given an obstetrical ultrasound image, evaluate for orientation, identification, labelling, image artifact, and quality.
- Identify proper technical adjustments to create diagnostic images based on image evaluation.
- Explain how to modify the examination, including when to perform a transperineal/translabial scan, based on provided patient symptoms, clinical history, laboratory results, and sonographic findings while integrating knowledge of disease processes. (5.1a, 5.1c, 5.1e, 5.2c, 5.3k,)
- Produce diagnostic data documenting sonographic findings and differential diagnosis using sonographic descriptors and medical terminology based on provided sonographic images. (1.1k, 1.2e, 5.4b, 5.4g, 5.4a)
- Record examination procedures and results and ensure that documentation is timely, accurate, concise, and complete. (1.2c, 1.2d)​
