## Course Description
This course is the final design studio of the Capstone Project where students independently find and research space for a multi-purpose project of social merit. The purpose of this course is to test the implementation of knowledge, skills and students' overall ability to execute a design. This course is the self-directed design component in which students will implement the research and thesis statement proposed in INTD 7500, to produce a design solution of approximately 25,000 sq. ft. that includes presentation drawings, material selection and digital media. Students will also develop contract documents, such as construction drawings and specifications, to provide the optimal amount of information necessary for the successful project execution. Students will present and defend their completed project to peers, faculty and mentors and advisors.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Integrate knowledge and skills acquired to execute a conceptual and fully developed design solution that communicates a clear understanding of design theories and principles, contextual issues and human factors.
- Justify the relevance of the building selection as it relates to the individual scope of the project/program.
- Synthesize information to create a unique design solution that meets the individual project goals and objectives that support the thesis statement developed in INTD 7500.
- Communicate through graphic and written documentation an understanding of the theories and principles of universal design, building codes, life safety and sustainability as they pertain to material and product selection, installation methods and interaction with the end user.
- Build upon the relationship(s) developed with industry mentor(s) and advisors in INTD 7500 with a continuation of shared ideas, critical thinking, self-evaluation, expanding the ability to build upon critiques/ findings.
- Analyze the relationship between space design and building components.
- Assess information acquired through research of products and materials and select the relevant information to produce specifications.
- Evaluate construction information in individual and peer drawings and specifications.
- Generate a set of construction drawings including all relevant legends and schedules.
- Plan a schedule of work for a given time period and demonstrate effective time management.
- Conduct a professional-level presentation to an audience of peers, faculty and industry mentor(s) that demonstrates their ability to communicate their ideas and final design solution with a combination of strong visual, oral and written communication skills.
