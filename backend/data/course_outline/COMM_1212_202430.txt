## Course Description
This course will focus on developing writing and oral communication skills that relate to broadcasting industries. Students will learn how to communicate effectively as professionals, or as members of a team, in a broadcasting workplace. Included within this will be skills development in writing (and reading) and speaking (and listening). The course will also provide practice in grammar and mechanics as needed.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Identify a message to communicate and adapt it to meet audience needs and expectations.
- Use appropriate tone when addressing audiences, e.g. positive, neutral.
- Identify and clarify the purpose in a variety of communication situations.
- Appropriately use a range of written forms of communication for industry situations, including emails, text messages, letters, memos, blogs, basic instructions, procedures, and short reports.
- Use direct and indirect patterns of organization to logically develop ideas.
- Design documents for easy reading using graphic highlighting, bullets, headings, summaries, etc.
- Use professional communication skills to interact with supervisors, peers, and team members.
- Respectfully critique examples of others’ work when writing alone and in a team.
- Express ideas clearly, concisely, and accurately.
- Use search techniques to find, evaluate, and summarize relevant reference materials, avoid plagiarism, summarize and cite sources.
- Use correct grammar, punctuation, and spelling in all communications.
- Create a persuasive message and deliver an effective pitch for a production idea.
- Deliver an effective oral presentation.

## Attendance Requirements
BCST Comm 1212 Policy

BCIT Student Regulations Policy 5101 will be enforced. Attendance will be taken at the beginning of each session. Students not present at that time will be recorded as absent. Students may not miss more than 10% of class time to successfully complete Comm 1212.

Instructors will issue marks deductions in order to address student attendance issues.

These deductions will apply to the final grade after all other marks have been calculated.

Students are issued a 2% deduction from their overall final grade per occurrence as an indication they have been late for scheduled class activities without notifying the course instructor(s) prior to the start of the class via email or text message and obtaining instructor approval for the lateness. Students are issued a 4% deduction from their overall final grade per occurrence as an indication they have been absent from scheduled class activities without having been excused in writing by the course instructor(s). Advance permission to be absent or late may be granted at the discretion of the course instructor(s) in conjunction with the Television & Video Production Faculty, based on a written request submitted by email to all course instructor(s) a minimum of 7 calendar days prior to the absence.

Deductions for absences may be rescinded if medical documentation is provided within 7 calendar days of the student’s return. Repeated absences for any reason may result in additional course-related activities being assigned to ensure relevant student competence.

Example: Student has a final grade of 80%, but was late three times and has one unexcused absence. These would result in a 10% deduction resulting in a final grade of 70%
