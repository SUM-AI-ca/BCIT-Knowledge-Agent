## Course Description
PHYS 2164 is an algebra-based physics course that addresses topics of special relevance to mechatronics and robotics engineering technology. There are three major topic areas: Part 1 Thermal Physics; Part 2 Statics and Properties of Materials; and Part 3 Electricity and Magnetism. The accompanying laboratory program emphasizes the proper techniques of measurement, experimentation, graphical data analysis, and error analysis. Practical applications of the physics discussed in the course are identified in relation to current technology.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
From the theory (lecture) portion of the course, the student will continue to develop the necessary problem-solving skills needed by mechatronics and robotics technologists. Specifically, the student will be able to:

- Solve problems involving temperature, thermal expansion, specific and latent heat of materials, and heat transfer (convection, conduction, radiation) by applying the kinetic theory of matter and thermodynamics.
- Solve problems related to the mechanical properties of materials involving the stress-strain relationship, Young’s modulus, shear modulus, and bulk modulus.
- Design structural members that are in tension, compression and shear by applying the concepts of factor of safety, elastic limit, yield strength, and ultimate strength of a material.
- Calculate forces, bending moments, stresses and strains that act on two-dimensional rigid bodies such as booms, simply supported beams, and trusses by applying the principles of static equilibrium.
- Solve problems related to damped/undamped oscillating mass-spring systems, natural period and frequency, quality factor and resonance.
- Calculate electric force, field, work, energy, potential, and draw electric field lines and equipotential lines for point charges.
- Calculate capacitance of capacitors with consideration to plate separation and area, and the use of dielectric materials between the plates.
- Solve problems involving magnetic fields, flux, force, electromagnetism, and magnetic permeability.
- Define and explain the phenomena of diamagnetism, paramagnetism, and ferromagnetism that are observed in various materials.
- Solve problems involving electromagnetic induction (Faraday’s and Lenz’s Laws), inductors, transformers and magnetic energy.
- Explain the principles of operation of modern electric generators and motors, thermistors, resistance strain gauges, thermocouples, piezoelectric devices, and superconducting materials.

From the practical (laboratory) portion of the course, the student will continue to develop his/her ability to:

- Use scientific instruments to make accurate measurements and to estimate the uncertainty in those measurements.
- Analyse data and obtain the mathematical relationship between experimental variables by using graphical analysis software on the computer.
- Estimate the uncertainty in quantities that are calculated from measurements and the results of graphical analysis.
- Write a clear, concise and properly formatted technical report that explains the significance of the results.

Course Requirements For Passing Grade

The minimum passing grade for this course is 50% with the condition that both the laboratory and the lecture portions be passed separately with a minimum average of 50% in each. If both portions have an average of 50% or better, the weighted average of both portions will be assigned as the final grade. However, if a failing average of less than 50% occurs in any one portion, but not the other, and the weighted average of both portions is 50% or greater, a failing final grade of Unsatisfactory (U) will be assigned. In all other cases with a failing average in one or both portions, and where the weighted average of both portions is less than 50%, this failing weighted average will be assigned as the final grade.

## Course Schedule and Assignments
Regularly assigned homework (due dates to be announced in class) will cover the topics discussed in lectures, and ideally, should be completed shortly after the material is discussed in class. Some homework assignments will be submitted in handwritten form on paper, but most will be on-line using the Mastering Physics system at the website: https://mlm.pearson.com/northamerica/masteringphysics. You must register on the website with a valid access code to be able to view the assignments and submit your answers. An access code is included with your textbook if it was purchased new. Used textbooks will most likely not include an unused code, in which case you will need to purchase a new access code separately on the Mastering Physics website, or else at the BCIT Bookstore (https://www.bcit.ca/bookstore). Details of the registration process and a basic introduction to Mastering Physics will be discussed in the first week of lectures.

Solve each question or problem with a complete written solution following the methods outlined in the lectures. For future studying purposes, place all completed assignments in a separate notebook and refer to them as the need arises. Students are encouraged to do a good job on assignments as they closely relate to questions on tests and exams.

Please keep in mind that Assignments represent the minimum number of problems that you should be attempting. In order to obtain sufficient confidence and mastery over the course topics, solving more problems is generally needed. The textbook contains many additional problems with answers to odd numbered questions at the end of each chapter.

Late Assignments: It is the student’s responsibility to ensure that assignments are submitted on time. Any portion of an assignment question submitted after the due date and time will be subject to a 20% penalty per calendar day of the value of the entire question. Any portion of an assignment question submitted 5 days or more late will result in a zero grade for the entire question. Students should notify the instructor immediately of any extenuating circumstances relating to late, or potentially late, assignments. For late assignments due to illness, please see Medical Documentation and Missed Work.
