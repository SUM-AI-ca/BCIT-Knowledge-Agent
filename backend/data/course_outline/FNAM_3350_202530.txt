### Course Description
This course will introduce linkages between higher-level plans, resource values, and forest management activities in the development of sustainable ecosystems, communities and regional economies. Through field and planning activities, students will apply skills and concepts acquired in other Term 3 courses to design a site plan for a woodlot or community forest. Through this project, students will demonstrate the necessary planning steps, data acquisition needs, and on the ground management tasks required as part of a site-level operations plan. Students will consider the ecological and socio-economic environment in which forests are managed.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Discuss important linkages that exist between, regional land use plans, strategic or watershed level plans, local government plans, and site specific or operational plans
- Describe key legislation, planning and management tools available to local government and resource agencies in forest management
- Discuss the challenges of planning for multiple resource and cultural values on the same land-base
- Write goals, objectives and strategies related to different types of planning activities and management scenarios at the site planning level
- Access and use existing information (e.g. reports, maps, aerial photographs) for pre-field preparation & site-history description
- Conduct field reconnaissance activities related to topography, vegetation, stand structure, timber value, forest health and other relevant resource characteristics
- Use GIS technology to generate maps overlaying reconnaissance inventories for planning purposes
- Develop silviculture and forest harvest prescriptions using field inventory information to meet specific management goals and objectives
- Prepare a comprehensive site plan that is financially viable and in line with high-level plans and management goals and objectives
