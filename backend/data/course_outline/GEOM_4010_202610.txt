### Course Description
This final field survey course for the diploma program brings together all field survey components taught during two years of study. It focuses on the use of electronic total stations, data collectors, digital levels, and GNSS survey-grade equipment using onboard data collection. The course has a heavy emphasis on data-processing, data adjustment, and the presentation of geospatial information through dedicated survey and CAD software. Throughout the course, students work on both individual and group projects. In the second half of the course, students work in groups to pursue their chosen field capstone project. Projects will incorporate topographic measurements, control survey, highway design, subdivisions, and volumetric calculations employing the practical methods and procedures taught throughout the diploma. Emphasis will be placed on project planning and executing surveys using best practices and procedures applied in the geomatics industry.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Properly care for and maintain surveying equipment.
- Lay-out stations and offset lines, run levels and prepare cut sheets.
- Use various total stations to measure distance and angle data and to solve typical survey problems and tasks.
- Collect data using both onboard and handheld data-recording systems and apply necessary corrections.
- Research different methodologies to complete the final survey project to the required specifications.
- Determine the precision of geodetic survey instruments and their ancillary equipment using methods outlined by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO.)
- Calculate and lay out horizontal positions of a spiral curve with corresponding vertical alignment.
- Determine astronomic azimuth using solar observation.
- Plan the field data collection to sufficiently cover the designated site to the required accuracy.
- Collect field data and apply any necessary corrections and adjustments.
- Integrate field and design data to depict the required results to industry standards.
- Summarize final results of the overall project, justify conclusions and make recommendations on the methods employed.
- Produce a professional report containing all of the survey data, computations, analysis and results.

### Attendance Requirements
This is a field class and attendance is mandatory. Every student will be working with a partner and will be relying on their partner to show up to work together. If attending a field class is not possible, email the instructor and the partner PRIOR to the start of class. More than two (2) unexcused absenses will result in a failure in the course.
### Course Specific Requirements
Students will be given fewer lab details and be expected to draw upon their field and office training over the previous 3 terms in order to complete the labs in a timely and professional manner.
GEOM 4010 lab submissions will face careful scrutiny with marks based on field survey accuracy, precision, neatness, quality and analysis of the deliverables.
Submissions must be done on an individual basis unless otherwise specified by the instructor.
Field notes are to be scanned, and submitted to the Learning Hub by no later than 5pm each field day.
Each student must submit drawings that meet the requirements of Provincial CAD Standards.
All field lab / project files are to be submitted to the appropriate Learning Hub Assignment dropbox. As part of the GEOM 4010 QA and QC, ensure your lab / project files also meet the following submission standards:
- Group submissions: All or a portion of the lab submission may be deemed as a group submission. It is expected that all survey team members will contribute equally to the groups submission portion.
- Individual submissions: All or a portion of the lab submission may be deemed as an individual submission. It is expected that an individual submission will be the work done by you as an individual.
- ZIP files are not permitted to be submitted unless specifically requested by the Instructors. .ZIP file submissions will NOT be graded.
- Field notes to be scanned and submitted as PDF's. Ensure your field note images are in a readable (portrait mode) format and not rotated.
Assignments, labs, reports or projects are due prior to the start of the class.
Assignments, labs, reports or projects submitted past the given due date will not be accepted for marking, as per departmental policy.
