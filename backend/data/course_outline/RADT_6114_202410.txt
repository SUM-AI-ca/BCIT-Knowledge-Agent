## Course Description
The second in a series of courses examining the fundamentals of radiation physics and the principles and concepts of dosimetry in the application of beam data to obtain the optimal treatment plan for each patient. Examines the physical applications and concepts relevant to teletherapy and brachytherapy equipment, photon and electron interactions, and beam data collection. Lectures are reinforced with numerical examples and students solve numerical problems where appropriate.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Describe the production of x-rays from kilovoltage machines.
- Describe the production of x-rays from linacs.
- Describe brachytherapy sources.
- Define PDD and apply in mathematical problem sets.
- Calculate equivalent squares.
- Calculate dose to a point using given data.

## Course Schedule and Assignments
Week: Outcome/ Material Covered
1: Review of 'Physics for Radiation Therapy 1'
2 & 3: Principles and basic concepts in radiation therapy physics
4 & 5: Radiation units (Low energy x-rays units, cobalt machines, linear accelerators, other medical accelerators)
6 & 7: Measurement of ionizing radiation: ionization chambers and other dosimeters
8 & 9: Dosimetry of radiation fields
10: Penetration of a photon beam into a phantom: phantoms, tissue equivalent material, dose distribution into a phantom
11: Dose distribution into a phantom: percentage depth dose, equivalent squares/circles
12: Electron Beams: electron interaction, depth dose curves, electron beam energy spectrum & range, symmetry, flatness and penumbra.
13: Continued
14: From measurements to calculations (Kilovoltage X-ray units, linear accelerators)
15: Conclusion & Review
16: Exam
