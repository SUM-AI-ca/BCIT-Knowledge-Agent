### Course Description
The second of two consecutive courses that examine normal structure and function based on a systems approach. This course covers the endocrine system, the cardiovascular system, the respiratory system, the digestive system, and the urinary system.

### Course Goals
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
1. identify major anatomical stuctures and relationships as they appear in traditional gross anatomy and sectional images;
2. relate structure to function in the human body;
3. relate the function of each body system to the common goal of homeostasis;
4. apply anatomical and physiological principles to Nuclear Medicine technology; and
5. utilize anatomical and physiological principles to adapt to new developments in the field of Nuclear Medicine.

### Course topics
I. THE ENDOCRINE SYSTEM
- Hormones
- Anatomy and Physiology of the Hypothalamus, Pituitary Gland, Thyroid Gland, Adrenal Glands and Endocrine Pancreas
II. THE CARDIOVASCULAR SYSTEM
- Contents and relational anatomy of the mediastinum
- ​Anatomy of the chambers of the heart, layers of the heart wall, pericardial serous membranes
- Valves of the heart
- Anatomy of the coronary circulation
- Myocardium and cardiac muscle physiology
- Whole heart physiology (conduction pathways, ECG, cardiac cycle)
- Histology of blood vessels
- Identification of major blood vessels (systemic circulation and pulmonary circulation)
- Determinants of blood pressure and arterial volume (cardiac output, peripheral resistance)
- Determinants of blood flow in systemic veins
- Microcirculation
- Anatomy of the blood - components of the plasma, blood cells
III. THE RESPIRATORY SYSTEM
- Anatomy of the structures of the upper and lower respiratory tracts, lungs and pleural serous membranes
- Physiology of ventilation
- Lung volumes and capacities
- Gas exchange and gas transport in the blood
- Neuronal control of respiration
IV. THE DIGESTIVE SYSTEM
- Anatomy of the structures of the gastrointestinal tract
- Anatomy of the accessory organs of digestion
- Intraperitoneal/retroperitoneal organs, mesenteries, parietal and visceral peritoneum
- Physiology of GI motility, digestion (mechanical and chemical) and absorption
V. THE URINARY SYSTEM
- Anatomy of the structures of the urinary system - kidneys, nephron, renal circulation, urinary tract
- Physiology of urine formation (filtration, reabsorption and secretion in the nephron)
- Hormonal control of urine volume
- Control of H +
- Physiology of micturition
