## Course Description
​In a modern world where visual technologies increasingly facilitate communication and social interaction, visual literacy is vital. Much of our experience in looking is grounded in habit rather than analysis. Through cultural theory and concepts, this course explores how meaning is both created and transmitted in the visual world.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Define "culture" and "visual culture".
- Diagnose the strengths and weaknesses of various communication media.
- Explain how shared understandings of visual culture emerge.
- Discuss the many ways various communication media have impacted our societies.
- Describe the various visual trends that are occurring around the world today and that have occurred throughout history.
- Create and deliver well researched, critically analytical and polished oral and/or written reports.
- Critically analyze and understand, from multiple perspectives, a variety of visual media.
- Evaluate a variety of cultural and historical artistic expressions.
- Read images as text.
- Analyze how visual media communicates in various ways throughout time.
- Identify, analyze and contextualize various artistic styles and time periods.
- Conduct formal analysis and college-level research.

## Course Specific Requirements
Discussion Questions – Students are required to respond to questions on Learning Hub to demonstrate an understanding of core concepts. For each question, students must make at least one original post (minimum of 200 words) and one response to a classmate’s post.

Individual Assignments – Students are required to document visual culture and, using their chosen examples, demonstrate an understanding of core concepts when introducing or describing the images.

Group Presentations – Groups will provide presentations and lead discussions based on chapters of the textbook, as assigned by the instructor. Students are encouraged to use a variety of media (PowerPoint, videos, photos, etc.) and will provide a summary, critical responses to the content, and questions for discussion.

Exhibition Review – The assignment is to write a review in response to an exhibition at the Vancouver Art Gallery. The review will provide a critical analysis (e.g. critique an art exhibit and its rationale), utilizing concepts covered in this course and the textbook. Consider the following questions:

• What was attempted?

• How fully was it accomplished?

• Was it worth doing?

Students are encouraged to provide context regarding the subject of their review and background information (e.g. artist, exhibition space, curator, and display). The review should be at least 600 words in length and must adhere to an approved citation style (e.g. MLA or Chicago Manual of Style).

Participation – Participation is defined as relevant and meaningful contribution to class discussions and professional behavior with your teammates and during field trips. In order to fully participate in the course, it is vital that students are prepared and attentive. Attendance for all course activities is a requirement and will be taken into consideration when calculating the participation mark.

Term Paper and Draft – In a minimum 1000-word essay, students are to write a critical analysis of an artwork or exhibition, using formal and contextual methods. This purpose of this assignment is not to simply provide a description; the essay must clearly articulate an argument. Students are recommended to support their thesis with theories and concepts covered in this course. This assignment must adhere to an approved citation style (e.g. MLA or Chicago Manual of Style). An outline and draft must be approved before a final term paper may be submitted.
