## Course Description
​In this foundational course, students will learn about soil formation, physical, chemical, and biological properties of soils; forest humus; and soil sampling and soil classification systems. Students will practice observing, measuring, describing, and recording different soil properties in the field and in the lab. Students will also practice interpreting and classifying soil data to better understand soil properties and how they relate to plant growth and ecosystem development.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Explain why soil is important and how soil relates to ecosystem/wildlife management.
- Describe dominant factors and processes involved in soil formation.
- Examine a soil pit and record a soil description of the properties of that soil, including the forest floor.
- Use soil sampling tools and soil analysis tools to collect data on soil properties, in the field and the lab.
- Predict soil characteristics such as soil drainage, soil fertility, compactibility, and suitability for plant growth based on observed soil properties.
- Classify soil into the appropriate soil order in the Canadian System of Soil Classification (CSSC) based on soil descriptions and diagnostic horizons​.

## Course Goals
“Land, then, is not merely soil; it is a fountain of energy flowing through a circuit of soils, plants, and animals.”–Aldo Leopold

All life on Earth depends on the soils beneath our feet, and yet soil is often underappreciated or simply forgotten. To understand and manage ecosystems (and the wildlife, birds and fish within those ecosystems), one must first be able to observe, describe and interpret soils. In this foundational course, you will learn about soil formation, physical, chemical and biological properties of soils; forest humus; soil sampling and soil classification systems. You will practice observing, measuring, describing, and recording different soil properties in the field and in the lab. You will also practice interpreting and classifying soil data to better understand soil properties and how they relate to plant growth and ecosystem development. By the end of the course you will value soils and appreciate Indigenous perspectives and land-based learning.

“... for only rarely have we stood back and celebrated our soils as something beautiful, and perhaps even mysterious. For what other natural body, worldwide in its distribution, has so many interesting secrets to reveal to the patient observer?” - Les Molloy
