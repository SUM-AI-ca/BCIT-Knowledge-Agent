## Course Description
Project managers add value to their organizations and various stakeholder groups through the application of their knowledge, skills, and talents. The Project Management Capstone course is your opportunity to use the knowledge you have acquired thus far, in a realistic setting. You will put to work your understanding of the five process groups and ten knowledge areas to demonstrate your readiness to take up new project challenges and launch a career in the project management profession. The intent is to use the project management body of knowledge in real world applied settings. You will have a broad range of choices in adopting a project for course work purposes. The instructor shall provide alternative assignments if logistical challenges are encountered.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:

- Critically analyse strategic goals to propose project ideas.
- Prioritize project ideas relative to weighted criteria through applying selection models.
- Craft a scope statement for a project that describes the project boundaries and key deliverables.
- Create a project charter.
- Develop a comprehensive project plan.
- Evaluate the progress of a project, prepare a status report, and propose needed management changes.
- Prepare a Lesson Learned report and propose improvements to the project management process.

## Learning Resources
REQUIRED: Access to D2L through BCIT's learning tools. This course is delivered through ASYNCHRONOUS DELIVERY, plus optional virtual office hours with the instructor, set up on Virtual Classroom.

REQUIRED: You will need to identify a project, and have a project sponsor , and assign work to a project team (at least 2 others) for success in this course. The project must have tasks that complete during the term (that you will write your progress report on).

Projects and all assignments are individual, not group assignments.

The project should be an active project you are managing outside of this class. It could be work, volunteer or personal interest/ research related, but should have some Workpackages (tasks) that you assign to other people (volunteers, inside your organization, or subconsultants), to show how you will coordinate, budget, and plan the tasks across a team. A key learning for this course is trying to get others to complete their tasks on time and on budget.

The vast majority of participants identify their own projects, with the help of the instructor. Projects do not need to be large but should span 8 -25 weeks.

Project sponsors have an vested interest in your project succeeding, and are usually the person the project is for, but could be a peer, mentor or spouse in some circumstances.

Required:Access to the latest Guide to PMBOK edition as issued by the Project Management Institute. As of 2022 this PMBOK Version 7

## Course Goals
The intent is to use the project management body of knowledge applied to an active project in real world (work or personal) applied settings.

To be confronted by challenges in attempting to execute a project, and learn which PM tools work well for your style; tailoring the tools for your project success, and “learn by doing”.

Students will also share project progress through interactive project logbooks with other students for interactive discussions. We will learn from each other, from the things that go wrong on a project, and by applying PM documentation to real projects. You will develop project strategies to get your project approved, strategies for changes that may arise, and strategies that fit your own project management leadership style to get them completed on time, in budget and in scope.

## Statement for prior learning assessment
This course does not introduce new topics, rather, we discuss how to overcome real-life PM challenges experienced.

Students are expected to bring ideas and share knowledge during on-line discussion groups, much as you need to do in real life when working as a project team leader.

The instructor will proivde practical solutions to common problems that arise during your project execution, such as how to assign budgets to projects that are internal or have no purchases, how to track a progress report, narrowing scope, and gaining control of your project risks.

## Course topics
This is a capstone project that will require use of preceding courses to demonstrate real-world application and prowress with the PM tools.

Learning by doing is the purpose of this course, and requires not just the compilation of PM tools in the plan, but EXECUTION of a project with several tasks during the term, some of which are assigned to others.. Students will be a project manager for a project outside of class, based on an active work, personal or volunteer project.

Core topics include project selection, writing a compelling project charter, scheduling (and Gantt Charts), Budget baselines, scope management plan, risk plans, procurement, resources and quality plans. See table below for details on course units.
