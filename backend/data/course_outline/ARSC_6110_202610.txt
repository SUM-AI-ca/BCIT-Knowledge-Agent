### Course Description
Contemporary Architecture: Theory and Practice in a Changing World. The purpose of this course is to investigate key concepts driving architecture and built form around the world, through an examination of the design and implementation process from concept to detail. Students will work individually and in teams to identify design drivers, program organization, conventions and innovation in architectural problem-solving. The course also considers the social roles and responsibilities of architects as public figures shaping the physical environments of communities, designing ‘places’ for people.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Identify a range of sources for informed discourse on issues relating to contemporary architecture.
- Articulate the role of form in the evolving needs of function in architecture.
- Explain the influences and progression of urban design trends in the local urban form over time, compared to other cities.
- Explain the articulation of intention, values and priorities of a contemporary architect in social context.
- Analyze the process of architectural program development and priorities influencing its arrangement (including local culture, geography, climate and physical context).
- Explain the relationship between architecture and the creation of communities, using local examples.
- Examine how contemporary technology and universal building typologies integrate with local influences to create ‘place-based’ architecture.
- Describe how different cross-cultural approaches to problem-solving create innovative design solutions.
- Demonstrate professional best practice individually and collaboratively.
