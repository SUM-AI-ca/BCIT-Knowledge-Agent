### Course Description
​This course is designed for students who want to reach the Independent User level of English proficiency. In this course, learners improve their listening, speaking, grammar, and vocabulary skills to succeed in BCIT programs and the modern workplace. Students improve their ability to understand different types of conversations between other speakers in academic, technical and professional environments. Students also practice listening to various talks, lectures, and media recordings. By using the ideas and language they hear in class, learners will demonstrate better fluency and a wider range of expressions. Learners show these skills as they deliver short monologues, present on technical and academic topics, and answer basic questions related to their presentations, just as they would in a professional meeting. Students will also work together formally during hands-on projects where they must select and share technical information to present to others. This collaboration helps prepare them for the teamwork required in both academic and career settings. Throughout the term, learners increase the range of B2 vocabulary and grammar and practice using most B2 level language in their speaking to ensure they are ready for the demands of the Canadian workforce.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Identify the main reasons for and against an argument, an idea, or a decision in a clear discussion on a straightforward technical academic topic
- Demonstrate understanding of a story, anecdote, or experience, containing significant sociocultural references, in extended informal work interactions
- Demonstrate understanding of complex informational structure or lines of argument within a clearly spoken lecture on a familiar topic
- Demonstrate understanding of informational and argumentative content in audiovisual material on topics of personal and familiar professional interest
- Take notes during clear and well-structured lectures or talks followed by the student's oral explanation or summary
- Deliver a 4-minute speech or presentation to communicate detailed information
- Deliver a 4-minute speech or presentation on a technical academic topic that gives the advantages and disadvantages of various options and recommends one
- Give a clear, prepared presentation of over 4 minutes on a technical academic topic using a variety of organizational types: structuring information effectively for the audience, giving reasons in support of or against a particular point of view, and giving the advantages and disadvantages of various options
- Recognize that, in intercultural encounters, what one normally understands in a particular situation is not necessarily understood by others in the same way
- Work together on a simple practical problem or challenge, speculating about reasons, causes, effects, consequences, and weighing solutions or explanations​

### Course Goals
Scenario 1
- LIsten to others share opionions about cultural difference and learn to connect them to your own cultural experiences.
- Learn language structures useful to describe cultural experiences.
- Listen to real students talk about their experiences living abroad
- Listen to a TED Talks about cultural identity
- Discuss ideas about cultural identity
- Prepare and record a podcast about cultural identity
Scenario 2:
- Extract key ideas and details about processes from different sources (texts, visuals, videos
- Listen to and discuss an interview about technological evolution.
- Self-assess pronunciation errors and do activities to improve on pronunciation weaknesses.
- Discuss ideas related to technological change and evolution.
- Prepare and deliver a speech about the technological evolution of a particular product, service, or concept
Scenario 3
- Listen to others talk about collaboration
- Observe and discuss the learning style of Canadian post-secondary institutuitions
- Listen to instructions for design specifications/competition rule
- Practice understanding of common reductions
- Collaboratively design, build, and test a structure in a design competition
- Participate in collaborative brainstorming & make suggestion·
- Reflect on experience with oral recording
