### Course Description
Explore advanced topics related to the central nervous system anatomy and physiology and its immediate relevance to the practice of electroneurophysiology with an emphasis on electroencephalography (EEG). Students deepen their understanding of the normal central nervous system (CNS) anatomy and neurophysiology and learn to apply the neurological exam to assessment of nervous system function. Discussion of related material encountered in the clinic, lab, or other courses is encouraged and active participation in learning will be expected. Evaluation of learning will include quizzes, exams, and self-evaluation of participation.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Describe the individual components of a general neurological exam and relate each to the normal function of the CNS region that is being assessed.
- Describe normal anatomy, physiology, and development of the central nervous system, including the brain, brainstem, cerebellum, and spinal cord.
- Relate physiology of neurons to the generation of the electroencephalographic signal.
- Describe the anatomy and physiology of cerebral blood supply.
- Identify the relevant features of central nervous system protection, including scalp, bony and meningeal protection, metabolic support, CSF, and key surface landmarks.
- Locate cortical and subcortical structures in CT and MRI images including functional regions of the cerebral cortex, white matter, basal ganglia, thalamus, limbic system, cerebellum, and brainstem.
