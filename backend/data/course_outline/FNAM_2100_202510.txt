### Course Description
Classification of terrestrial ecosystems is needed to aid in forest management, wildlife management, ecological restoration and conservation. Ecosystems are defined and their fundamental structure and processes are described and discussed. British Columbia’s biogeoclimatic ecosystem classification (BEC) is described and used, both for its direct utility and as an example of a formal ecosystem classification. Practical applications of BEC are described and practiced. Implications of climate change on BEC are discussed. Classification of wetland ecosystems and some other terrestrial ecosystem classifications, including some project specific ad hoc classifications, are described and discussed. Methods of describing, sampling and mapping terrestrial ecosystems are described and practiced.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Outline the importance of terrestrial ecosystem classification
- Demonstrate the importance of climate, vegetation and soil, working at various scales of space and time, in determining the nature of terrestrial ecosystems
- Outline the structure and use of the biogeoclimatic ecosystem classification (BEC)
- Outline the structure and use of commonly used wetland ecosystem classifications
- Compare some other examples terrestrial ecosystem classifications that have been used in British Columbia and other locations
- Apply skills related to plant identification, earth science, soils and measurements to sample and analyze terrestrial ecosystems
- Apply appropriate methods for sampling and describing terrestrial ecosystems
- Outline how to create a project-specific ecosystem classification
- Demonstrate how to map terrestrial ecosystems
- Outline how climate change can affect ecosystem classifications, such as BEC.
