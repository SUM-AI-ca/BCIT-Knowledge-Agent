## Course Description
In this course, you will learn how to apply quantitative and qualitative research methods to crime and intelligence data. It covers topics such as measuring variables, assessing the reliability and validity of acquired intelligence, data preparation and coding, and statistical analysis of quantitative data. You will apply these methodologies and perform a number of simple, though powerful, analyses to describe data and reach conclusions based on these data. This course aims to develop your analytical skills to be a more knowledgeable practitioner, consumer, and evaluator of quantitative and qualitative social science data. These analytical methods will be instructed in the context of the COMPSTAT method of crime analysis as used in contemporary Canadian policing.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:

- Manage large and small data sets for crime and intelligence analysis purposes.
- Evaluate the integrity of large and small data sets.
- Determine which statistics best portray the data.
- Apply methodologies, including COMPSTAT, to problem-solving and data-driven decision-making through proper data collection and research design.
- Present findings verbally and in written format.

## Learning Resources
This course introduces students to the quantitative and qualitative research methods used by crime and intelligence analysts. Topics such as measuring variables, assessing the reliability and validity of acquired intelligence, data preparation and coding, and statistical analysis of quantitative data are covered. This course covers the major statistical techniques which analysts and criminal justice researchers use. This course aims to make students more knowledgeable practitioners, consumers, evaluators and presenters of crime data and research. Students should be able to perform a number of simple, though powerful analyses using Excel to describe data and reach conclusions based on these data.

## Course Goals
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

The six primary goals for this class are: (1) summarizing and managing large and small data sets; (2) examining the integrity of large and small data sets; (3) determining which statistics best portrays the data; (4) comparing more than one variable to others; (5) applying statistics to problem solving and data driven decision-making through proper data collection and research design; (6) presenting your findings.
