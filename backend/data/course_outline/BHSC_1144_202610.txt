### Course Description
Human Behaviour 1 (DSON) explores psychological and sociological concepts, research and applications of relevance to sonographers in training and in clinical practice. This course includes skills for interaction and communication; preventing and managing distress in clients and self; working with cultural diversity; group interaction and work teams; dealing with client needs associated with pregnancy, age and abilities, mortality, managing interpersonal conflict, harassment, reporting child abuse and ethics; balancing expectations of patients with requirements of workplace regarding disclosure of information; and recognizing and responding to patient anxiety and depression. Emphasizes improving sensitivity and human relations skills in dealing with both clients and staff. Students in this program have a particular focus on the unique aspects of caring for clients who are pregnant and their families.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Apply concept of empathy to create respectful and supportive relations with clients and co-workers.
- Integrate effective interpersonal communication skills in order to foster rapport and trust.
- Perform various types of assertion according to appropriateness and desired result.
- Employ methods used to resolve conflict to maintain a professional work environment.
- Explain common cultural differences relating to interpersonal communication styles, patient perceptions, experiences and expectations.
- Demonstrate respect for diversity by interacting with patients from various cultures appropriately and respectfully.
- Use recommended strategies to prevent and reduce distress in both self and clients.
- Describe stress related disorders, and appropriate treatments and interventions.
- Analyze approaches to managing negative feelings in self and clients.
- Manage effectively various types of harassment and discrimination found in the workplace.
- Apply knowledge of developmental differences that impact interactions and procedures in a clinical setting.
- Respond ethically and within legal guidelines to situations where abuse is suspected.

### Other information
Review and Reflect (Weekly In-Class Assignment) (30%): Most weeks will include a short in-class Review and Reflect assignment designed to reinforce learning and encourage deeper engagement with course material. Dates for these assignments are indicated in the course schedule. These low-stakes activities support understanding, application of concepts, and connections across topics.
Review & Reflect Assignments may include:
- Review Questions: Multiple-choice questions on key concepts from the previous week.
- Reflection & Application Questions: Short written or multiple-choice prompts intended to connect, apply, or reflect on the current week’s material.
Review & Reflect assignments must be completed during class; late submissions are not accepted. A password provided in class is required for access. The Learning Hub system will automatically drop the lowest Review & Reflect grade (one free miss) to accommodate unexpected absences. One make-up assignment may be provided for additional absences due to extenuating circumstances; no further make-ups are available.
Clinical Speaking Micro-Skills Quiz (15%): During class time, students will respond in writing to clinical scenarios, demonstrating effective use of empathic paraphrases, probes, perception checks, I statements, and/or assertive communication.
Culture Presentation (25%) + Peer Reviews (5%): Students will work in assigned small groups to create and record a digital presentation designed to learn about other cultures and practice cultural humility. Students will review peers presentations and provide constructive feedback, applying course-related feedback skills while communicating professionally and respectfully. The instructor will provide additional details.
Final Exam (25%): The cumulative final exam will be administered via the Learning Hub Quiz feature and include written-response and short-answer questions. Students must arrive on time; late arrivals will not receive extra time. All personal belongings, including bags and electronic devices, must be left in the designated area. Academic honesty is expected—any cheating, plagiarism, or breach of integrity will result in disciplinary action according to institutional policies.
