## Course Description
Apply the principles and instrumentation required in the clinical anatomical pathology laboratory. Topics covered include human microscopic tissue recognition, fixation, tissue processing, decalcification, microtomy, stain theory plus routine and special stains. Engage in microscopic evaluation of digital tissue sections, embedding, microtomy, biological staining procedures such as methods for routine morphological assessment, carbohydrate stains, quality assurance and troubleshooting. Evaluation of learning is conducted through a range of written assignments, exam, as well as laboratory practice and skills demonstration.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Operate and maintain a rotary microtome to correctly produce 12 diagnostically acceptable sections per hour.
- Correctly embed a variety of tissues.
- Assess the quality of fixation, tissue processing and microtomy and suggest corrective actions for any issues recognized.
- Recognize and state the causes and corrections for all the common microtomy artifacts.
- Correctly perform an H&E as well as the common carbohydrate stains.
- Assess the quality of staining and suggest corrective action if necessary.
- Apply general laboratory safety rules as well as those safety considerations that are specific to Anatomical Pathology.
- Correctly recognize microscopic sections of the following normal tissues: muscle, epithelium, circulatory system, skin, respiratory system, immune system and endocrine system.
- Explain the theory of tissue processing, microtomy, frozen sections, decalcification, stain theory and gross room methods.
- Explain the principle of and chemical mechanism for each of the staining methods performed.
- Predict, on the basis of chemical structure and physical properties, the possible tissue sites with which a staining method may react.
- Briefly explain the clinical application for special stains.

## Course Goals
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Recognize a variety of surgical or procedural specimens received in a diagnostic Anatomical Pathology laboratory.
- Understand the basic procedures of grossing a variety of tissues based on tissue type and structural differences.
- Correctly orient and embed a variety of human tissues for microtomy sectioning.
- Assess the quality of fixation, tissue processing and microtomy and recommend corrective actions for recognized issues.
- Operate and maintain a rotary microtome to correctly produce 10 diagnostically acceptable sections per hour.
- Recognize and state the causes and corrections for all the common microtomy artifacts.
- Apply general laboratory safety rules and considerations specific to Anatomical Pathology.
- Correctly recognize gross and microscopic sections of normal basic tissue types including muscle, epithelium, connective tissue, nervous tissue, bone, cartilage, respiratory, circulatory tissue, and skin.
- Explain the theories of tissue recognition, fixation, tissue processing, microtomy, frozen sections, decalcification, and gross room methods.

## Attendance Requirements
Attendance Requirements

Regular attendance to the lecture and laboratory is seen as integral to student success. Consistent tardiness to both lecture and laboratory sessions is disruptive and not acceptable. Absences and tardiness will be monitored by instructional staff.

Med Lab Program Policy regarding missed Evaluations and Labs:

Missing an evaluation or lab due to a medical or unavoidable reason:

- The student must contact the instructor as soon as possible, ideally prior to the evaluation or lab time.
- If it is a medical reason, the student will be expected to submit a BCIT Student Medical Certificate form (attached) to the Instructor for review.
- For last-minute medical issues, a doctor’s note can be initially submitted to the instructor. However, a complete BCIT medical certificate must follow and be presented as soon as possible.
- If a BCIT Medical Certificate is not submitted then the mark on the evaluation may be changed to a 0.
- For other unavoidable reasons, supporting documentation is expected. Students that meet these criteria will have the opportunity to make up the missed evaluation or lab.

Missing or late to an evaluation or lab due to a non-medical, or avoidable reason and no valid documentation provided to support absence from the evaluation or lab:

- The student is not eligible to make up the missed evaluation and will be given a mark of 0.
- The student is not eligible to make up a missed lab.
- The student will not be given extra time to complete the evaluation if they are late.

Definitions:

Evaluation – any academic event that results in a mark being given. This includes but is not limited to: labs, presentations, homework, quizzes, midterms, skills assessments, and final exams.

Avoidable, non-medical reasons – examples include but are not limited to: sleeping in, missing public transit, traffic. There may be specific scenarios where these reasons may be unavoidable; these will be reviewed on a case-by-case basis.
