### Course Description
Today, every company is in the media business – whether they know it or not. That’s why it’s crucial to learn how to use both online and broadcast media to tell your story. The way to build a strong company and brand in today’s online world is by winning the attention of your target audience through the essential skill of storytelling. Whether it takes the form of a podcast, blog, video content or any of the other countless online methods of online communication, storytelling and pure human communication will never become obsolete. The focus of this course is honing your storytelling skills to create compelling and powerful content that will stand out in this cluttered new media landscape.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Identify professional communicators and provide examples of the most successful forms of social media they engage.
- Develop the skills to communicate a focused message clearly.
- Provide examples of how to weave your own personality into any message.
- Use humour, emotion, and opinion in your storytelling.
- Define the elements of corporate storytelling.
- Describe the concept of narrowcasting.
- Transition your work from the written word to a relaxed, live presentation in front of the camera.
- Create succinct and entertaining Twitter and FaceBook content that enhances your personal brand.
- Demonstrate effective online strategies for getting your content seen by the most viewers through the many forms of social media.

### Course Goals
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to utilize the following skills:
Navigating the media landscape – From traditional broadcasting to podcasting and viral video, new methods of reaching a massive audience are changing all the time. Students in this course will better be able to comprehend and anticipate these trends and use them to better communicate with their audience. They’ll also learn what hasn’t changed, and why storytelling and pure, human communication will never become obsolete.
Effective Communication – Whether through the written word, on-camera, on-mic or even in front of a live audience, the student will develop the skills to verbally get their point across with confidence, skill and style.
Writing For Social Media – the student will learn to create compelling and powerful content that will stand out and get noticed in the cluttered new media landscape. The student will also develop the skills to write in their own voice - letting their own personality shine through beyond being just a “talking head.”
Self Branding – By developing a clearer understanding of exactly who they are, what their message is, and who they are talking to, the student will learn the skills to create an effective brand that will instantly connect with their intended audience. They will also learn to further their brand through the enhanced use of social media tools such as Facebook, X etc.

### Course Schedule and Assignments
Schedule
Week of/ Number | Outcome/Material Covered | Reference/ Reading | Assignment | Due Date
April7th/Week 1 | Introduction to Storytelling Including a broad outline of what constitutes 21 st century media, the opening week will introduce the student to the basic concepts of storytelling and story structure, along with the ways in which human beings are programmed to both tell and listen to stories. Students will begin exploring their own personal stories with an eye to presenting their story through media.
April 14th/ Week 2&3 | Writing For Your Voice The basics of writing for broadcast will be introduced, with techniques to make the students’ writing more suitable for “the ear.” The differences between storytelling through speech and through the written word will be explored. The student will begin to focus in on what their message will be and develop the skills to communicate that message clearly and succinctly using their own unique personality to get their point across. Students will further examine the concept of storytelling and how it pertains to their particular content (examples of this content could include a news, sports or entertainment broadcast, a corporate report or presentation, or a You Tube clip). | “A Historical Story” Write a one minute story for broadcast about a unique moment in history
April 28th/Week 4 | Persuasive Storytelling The student will learn the basics of “getting your point across,” whether it’s a message to market a product or an opinion piece meant to change the hearts and minds of an audience The techniques used to make your writing more compelling will be examined, including word choice and the structure of a persuasive message. | “Editorial” Write a short editorial for broadcast (for example, Why we need to control pollution!)
May 5th/Week 5&6 | Corporate Storytelling Students will examine how storytelling is used in not only advertising but all sorts of marketing and branding content. The concept of finding the important narrative elements of an individual or organization will be explored, along with the technique of finding those stories that will represent that person or organization to the outside world. | “Elevator Pitch” Learn to tell the story of you in one minute
May 19th/Week 7 | The Entertaining Story Students will learn how to tap into such qualities as humour, emotion and opinion and inject it into their writing. The student will also learn how the qualities of brevity and pacing can make their writing soar. | “Your Story” Tell a quick story that's true, about you...and told in a few.
May 26th/Week 8 | Establishing Your Brand The student will now begin to develop and define their own brand, effectively discovering what their message is and whom their intended audience consists of. The student will learn to display the skills needed to promote this brand on various social networking websites by creating content for such platforms as Twitter and Facebook which will entertainingly and succinctly enhance their brand.
June 2nd/ Week 9 & 10 | Intro to podcasting This class will introduce the student to the world of audio podcasting, with examples of the most successful podcasts of the day. The concept of the “microstar” will be defined, exploring those personalities who have carved out careers by appealing to a seemingly smaller core audience which can turn out to be substantial thanks to the global reach of the world wide web. Lab work will include the students conducting their own short podcast, interviewing another student in the class. The student will learn basic interviewing skills and the ability to display “storytelling” skills in an interview setting, by bringing out the story of the person they are interviewing. | “Voice Actors Guide To Home Recording” Jeffredy P.Fisher and Harlan Hogan (suggested, not required) | “Your Podcast” Create an audio or video podcast about any topic you like
June 9th/Week 11 | Stories That Sell Sales and marketing using effective storytelling is the topic for these 2 weeks, looking at a number of diverse products, brands and individuals. The basic principals of video production will be introduced, including video sequencing to better tell your story.
June 16th/Week 12 | Getting Your Message Out Students will learn effective online strategies for getting their content seen by the most viewers, including theories on the phenomenon of viral video vs. the slow, steady dissemination of content. Students will develop the ability to use the internet to have their content seen through smart branding and identifying a targeted audience. The concept of “social equity” will be defined as students will learn how they can encourage their audience to distribute their content for them, displaying social media techniques to make sure this content is put in front of the people who want and need it most. | “5 Shot Sequence” Create a simple video using just 5 distinct shots
June 23rd / Week 13 & 14 | Classical Influences In Modern Storytelling Work on the final project is now in full swing. The project will consist of a piece of content ready for broadcast on the internet, whether that is a You Tube video clip or audio podcast. Students will now demonstrate the skill needed to present their work on-camera or on-microphone with brevity and flow. After 14 weeks, students will be able to demonstrate: -skill to better tell their own story, using personality to create compelling content. -ability to speak comfortably on camera, microphone, or in a live presentation setting. -an understanding of the theories of “corporate storytelling,” and how to use the strategies when creating content. - ability to write effectively for broadcast with brevity, wit and style. - a full understanding of the new media landscape, and how internet content is now of course its own platform, but also an integral part of traditional radio and television as well. | “Final Assignment” Create a piece of audio or video content all about anything you choose
