## Course Description
This course introduces students to the exciting world of structural packaging design and its fundamental role in a product’s protection, portability, and interactivity. The course covers everything that goes into successful three-dimensional packaging, from design all the way to final assembly. Using industry-standard packaging design software, students gain a thorough understanding of how to produce common die-lines, create prototype samples, and when to break the mould. Students are introduced to a wide variety of packaging materials and learn about the applications for product protection and sustainability. Finally, students are educated in packaging’s role in marketing, shelf presence and user-experience.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Explain packaging terminology, functions, and operations.
- Proficiently operate industry-standard packaging design software.
- Describe common file preparation mistakes and how to address them.
- Produce die-lines for a range of carton styles and applications.
- Produce prototype samples and apply allowances and rebates in design to ensure proper functionality of finished package.
- Differentiate between types of folds and their uses with a range of substrates and applications.
- Determine the appropriate media necessary to achieve safe product delivery.
- Select an appropriate manufacturing method based on product requirements.
- Calculate a package’s shipping requirements, including weight, size and pallet load.
- Implement effective packaging ergonomic techniques to improve the end user’s experience.
- Explain packaging’s role in sustainability including re-use ability and recycling streams.
- Describe the characteristics of successful packaging design, including branding, shelf presence, and user-experience.​

## Safety equipment or protective clothing
The Graphic Communications program utilizes a unique print lab facility, with equipment donated by the generous business owners in our community. Students must always respect the equipment in the lab, and comply with the following rules:

- Loose hair or long beards must be tied back or contained in a hat or hair net.
- Remove jewelry, scarves, coats, or loose clothing.
- Gaming, videos, or loud music that can cause distraction are not permitted.
- Mobile devices are not permitted when working around machinery.
- No food or drink in the lab (closed water bottles are allowed at desks only).
- PPE is required to be worn when working with chemicals.
- No video recording or photography is permitted in the lab.

These rules are for the safety of all students using the equipment in the lab and are overseen at the discretion of all faculty. Failure to comply with lab rules will not be tolerated and may result punitive action including dismissal from class, formal warning, course failure, or withdrawal from the program.
