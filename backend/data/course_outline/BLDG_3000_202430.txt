## Course Description
This course introduces students to the theory of architecture and principles of architectural design through a series of lectures and practical design problems. The lecture component introduces topics where the historical, cultural, technical, and economic roots of contemporary architecture are explored. The lab periods comprise a series of design studios where architectural problems are explored. Projects increase in complexity from the three-dimensional arrangement of space needed to encompass a specific human activity to the comprehensive design of small buildings, and incorporate functional and behavioural requirements, siting and contextual considerations, structure, construction, environmental controls, and codes and regulations. Students discover their own potential by exploring the issues raised by design problems and by searching for solutions, They learn to develop, explain, and defend their work in situations varying from individual tutorials and group seminars to discussions and critiques involving the entire class. Students develop an awareness, sensitivity, perception and appreciation of architecture and its influence and role in our lives.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
At the end of this course the student will be able to:

- Review building codes as they apply to existing use space.
- Design a creative solution to an existing retrofit space that meets the client's program and requirements as well as current building code requirements.
- Adapt a retrofit design to existing service conditions, including lighting layout, HVAC, structural, plumbing, sprinkler, fire safety, and other systems.
- Develop a Reflected Ceiling Plan incorporating architectural, lighting and HVAC features.
- Prepare colour rendered presentation drawings of student design proposals.
- Research and write a report on a contemporary architect or architectural firm.
- Use the library, internet, and other sources to acquire photographic or digital images for incorporation into the architectural research project.
- Design a one- or two-family residential project, including off-street parking, that meets the client's functional and spatial requirements.
- Develop facade design related to roofs, bay windows, recesses, cantilevers, fenestration, exterior features, cladding, etc. .
- Design dwelling units that respond to site issues, including view, slope, sun orientation, vegetation, site features, etc.
- Construct a scale model of the student design proposal.
