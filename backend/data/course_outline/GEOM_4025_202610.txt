### Course Description
This course discusses cadastral and land use planning issues that affect the land surveying profession. These issues include legislation, long-term planning of official community plans and community visions, land use, site analysis development, environmental issues, public involvement, and the planning process and costing. Students identify how laws and rules, including legislation, court decisions and bylaws, impact land survey practice. The course also includes the history of survey systems and cadastral practice and how historical records influence the modern-day cadastral fabric and land title systems.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Explain the types of law in the Canadian legal system.
- Explain sources of law.
- Describe the process of creating a law in various Canadian jurisdictions.
- Describe various estates in land.
- Describe the differences between covenants, statutory rights of ways and easements.
- Explain the components of the Dominion Land Survey System (DLS).
- Illustrate a township system to the level of a legal subdivision.
- Identify the different types of boundaries.
- Apply the principle of pro-rationing to re-establish boundaries.
- Determine the acceptance or rejection of legal evidence when determining boundaries.
- Describe legal monumentation for boundary demarcation.
- Research and report on land registry systems and various types of multipurpose cadastres.
- Describe various terms used in land descriptions and create both a parcel and legal description using a metes and bounds description.
- Describe aboriginal title of indigenous peoples.
- Apply ethics to solve a variety of cadastral dilemmas.
- Describe planning concepts, including sustainable development, land use, development potential, floor- space ratio, and gross and net density.
- Describe land use regulations and the role of government in land development.
- Describe the role of surveyors in the planning and development process.
