## Course Description
​This course introduces students to the concepts of land use and land ownership under common law and applies research of both statute and case law. It examines the role of the land surveyor, the contribution of the surveying profession to land development, and the legal and institutional frameworks that govern the work of land surveyors. Fundamental concepts of property, ownership, land tenure systems, and the historical underpinnings of each are discussed. Real property law concepts are explored as they relate to survey systems and standards, land registration, and land description. The course concludes with studies in boundary law. Students address professional issues such as ethics, negligence, and duty of care and how they are applied in different scenarios.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Describe the basics of the Canadian legal system, hierarchy of various government organizations and entities, and the process of creating laws, case law, courts, and so on.
- Research and interpret legislation and case law to address land issues.
- Describe artificial, descriptive, and natural boundaries.
- Articulate property rights, land tenure, survey systems and land registration in Canada, including the processes of subdivision, land transfer, and boundary resolution (including bornage) and the land surveyor's role.
- Explain regulations, ethics, disciplinary powers, procedures, and the legal framework for the surveying and engineering professions.
- Explain various interests in land (for example, covenants, easements, joint tenancy and tenancy in common, profits-à-prendre, right-of-way, and so on).
- Explain the types of mineral tenure in British Columbia, including mineral, placer, oil and gas, coal, and so on.
- Explain the various land survey systems and settlement patterns across Canada and the US (seigneurial, townships, district lots, DLS, and survey systems in western Canada).
- Undertake a preliminary environmental assessment and greenspace analysis for a proposed development.

## Learning Resources
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Research and interpret legislation and case law and be able to apply the principles to land surveying.
- Have an understanding of boundaries (artificial, natural, and descriptive) and the land surveyor’s role in the creation and interpretation of them.
- Understand the processes when boundaries may be altered through adverse possession, estoppel, conventional boundaries, accretion, reliction, and erosion.
- Understand property rights, land tenure, survey systems and land registration in Canada including the processes of subdivision, land transfer, and boundary resolution (including bornage) and the land surveyor’s role.
- Have a working knowledge of the institutional framework land professionals work within.
- Exemplify professional ethics and duty of care.
- Understand the evolution of aboriginal rights & title in Canada.
