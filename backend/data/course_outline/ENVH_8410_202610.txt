### Course Description
This directed studies course focuses on the application and integration of knowledge and skills acquired in ENVH 8400 (Research Methods). Provides a faculty supervisor to monitor work with a sponsoring agency to independently complete the research project, prepare a formal research report and present research findings. Utilizes fiscal and time- management strategies, oral and written communication skills and critical thinking/problem solving abilities. Requires an off-campus/practicum component.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Apply research methodology to a chosen and previously approved specialty area.
- Complete the research project within the proposed time frame and budget.
- Write a formal research report following the structure requirements of an article for a scientific journal.
- Present their independent research findings to an audience consisting of interested stakeholders.

Assignment 1: Supervisory Meeting | 10 | Meeting to present final methodology and statistical results. This will be a 20-minute discussion with the instructor. Assignment Q&A Sheet to be submitted during the meeting.
Assignment 2: Methods and Results Paper | 20 | Methods and Results (real data) but having addressed comments from instructor during supervisory meeting.
Assignment 3: ﻿ Discussion Paper | 30 | Discussion, Limitations, Conclusions, Recommendations, Knowledge Translation, and Future Studies.
Assignment 4: Journal Submission | 25 | Summary of your study findings in a structured abstract style with the component sections completed. See Journal Submission marking guideline. Both documents to include revisions from all instructor feedback.
Assignment 6: Oral Presentation and Question Period | 10 | PowerPoint Presentation to colleagues where practicum is located (if doing spring practicum) OR, to classmates and Instructors at BCIT and/or BCCDC (if doing summer practicum). Presentation to occur early/mid May. Please arrange for you presentaiton as soon as possibe. Evaluations to be submitted by Friday, May 22, 2026.

### Course Specific Requirements
See marking schemes for all 6 assignments posted on D2L, and follow them when preparing your assignments.
Each student should review and comply with BCIT Policy and Procedures 5101 — Student Research Papers/Ownership/Control and then complete the two-page consent form (LIB‑73) for the publication of their research paper in the BCIT Environmental Health Journal . In the consent form, the student will be requested to provide written permission to allow BCIT’s Environmental Health Program to publicize the title of the research project and the abstract, the body of work, together with the author’s name, to members of the environmental health profession and others. Once this has been done, your final research paper will be published in the BCIT Environmental Health Journal.
Please submit consent form LIB‑73 before Spring Break. The form is available electronically at the following link: https://www.bcit.ca/files/pdf/policies/lib_73.pdf
In accordance with BCIT policy, plagiarism will not be tolerated. The research submitted must be entirely original work done by the student submitting, with appropriate citations and acknowledgements of other work(s) as incorporated into their submission.
It is not anticipated that there will be “formal” class time necessary except for the first week of the term and individual meetings with your supervisors to clarify statistics and any other project‑related questions. The initial class will be used to set schedules, administrative duties, and responsibilities for the term. A guided learning format will require, from each of you, a considerable amount of self‑motivation and time‑management skills.
## Use of AI
AI‑based content generation tools, or AI tools, are permitted for out‑of‑class assignments and project work. However, they are not to be used during in‑class work or during formal assessments including both midterm exams, the skills assessment, the practical lab exam, or the final theory exam.
Note that AI output can include incorrect or misleading information, fabricated authors/dates/publications, faked works, or may incorporate discriminatory ideas. It is the student’s responsibility to ensure the quality, accuracy, and appropriateness of any work submitted.
If ChatGPT (or a similar tool) is used to prompt ideas and/or partial answers for an assignment and/or to generate any text for a draft or final version of any part of an assignment, students must declare that it has been used, describing the extent to which it was used. Any generated text must be saved and available to Instructional staff if requested. There is no penalty for using AI tools as specified here, but an instructor may ask for the generated text to assist with grading.
As with other reference materials, AI tools must be cited. Undeclared use of AI is a violation of academic integrity as it presents work, ideas created by AI or other people as your own. Failure to fully declare the use of this tool will be considered a violation of BCIT academic policy.
