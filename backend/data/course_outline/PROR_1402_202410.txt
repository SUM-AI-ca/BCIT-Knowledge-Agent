## Course Description
Examines ethical issues surrounding patient management and professional practice. Addresses legal implications of patient assessment and record keeping and covers topics such as labour relations and small business practice. Draws examples from actual prosthetic and orthotic cases and other allied health professions.

## Course Goals
This one-credit theory course addresses issues, concepts, and ethical principles related to professional prosthetic and orthotic practice within the allied health care team. It is designed to explore contexts relating to professional responsibility under Orthotics Prosthetics Canada (OPC) . These include a practice standards (cannons of ethical conduct, scope of practice), interprofessional collaboration (National Interprofessional Framework), professional values and responsibilities, confidentiality, OPC complaints resolution and facility accreditation. Legal responsibilities related to documentation are also discussed. The OPC Code of Ethics is applied to clinical practice scenarios and ethical decision making.

Upon successful completion, the student will have the opportunity to:

- Explain the mandate and the regulatory requirements of the OPC governing P&O practice.
- Apply the OPC Scope of Practice Standards and Canons of Ethical conduct specific practice scenarios.
- Discuss strategies to prevent or mitigate legal risks associated with P&O practice.
- Examine personal and professional ethical values, and implications to ethical decision-making using clinical practice scenarios and in small group discussions.
- Identify impacts of unconscious biases, stereotyping, privilege and racism can have on health outcomes.
- Understand the need and impact to practice client-centred care, and the rights of clients to privacy, confidentiality, and informed consent to health care.
- Examine competencies involved in National Interprofessional Competency framework and how this impacts the delivery of P&O care
- Discuss strategies to effectively manage quality of work-life issues such as excessive workload, work stress/burnout, unsafe practices in the workplace and disruptive behaviour/bullying.
- Envision ways of advancing one’s professional practice as a life-long P&O learner within the P&O profession
