## Course Description
HRMG 4200 Employment Relationships and Conflict Management focuses on understanding employment relationships and the nature, sources and management of workplace conflict – the friction, disagreement, or discord arising from a perception of incompatible interests between workplace participants. The course provides frameworks for thinking about employment, conflict at work and introduces structures, processes and best practices to manage that conflict Through the examination of theory and practice the course builds on conflict management principles introduced in Organizational Behaviour and Introduction to Labour Relations.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Explain the structure and characteristics of employment relationships that have direct employee-employer relations (employee relations) and those where a union represents an employer’s employees (labour relations)
- Identify, analyze and diagnose the sources and causes of workplace conflict and recommend the most appropriate paths to conflict and dispute resolution
- Explore issues in depth, generate multiple options, evaluate options, and develop acceptable solutions with parties; utilize appropriate negotiating/conflict styles and techniques
- Assist with workplace investigations into matters such as misconduct, harassment etc.
- Handle questions, complaints and grievances in a helpful, constructive manner that contributes to a positive and productive labour-management climate
- Assist in preparing for and participating in the collective bargaining processes, Labour Relations Board (LRB) proceedings and arbitration hearings
- Articulate and exhibit appropriate workplace behaviours including working effectively as a team member
- Prepare concise written materials and deliver effective presentations

## Course Goals
The primary goal of HRMG 4200 is to further understanding to enhance practice through the examination and exploration of:

- Context: The interrelated conditions with-in which work, and workplaces exist, and events occur.
- Conflict: The inevitable result of people associating with one another. A clash between individuals arising out of a difference in thought processes, attitudes, understanding, stated positions, interests, and sometimes even perception.
- Negotiation: Communication for the purpose of persuasion. Our primary inter-personal decision-making process to reconcile conflict.
- Theory to Practice: Best practices to manage workplace incidents that invariably arise.
