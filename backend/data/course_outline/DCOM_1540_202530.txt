### Course Description
​This course provides the foundation required for understanding digital electronic circuits. The binary, hexadecimal, and decimal number systems are explained, and techniques for converting from one system to another are introduced. The digital logic devices covered include logic gates (NOT, AND, NAND, OR, NOR, XOR and XNOR), timers and pulse generators, combinational and sequential logic, binary adders, comparators, encoders, decoders, 7-segment displays, multiplexers, demultiplexers, latches, flip-flops, binary counters and shift registers. Students will learn analog-to-digital and digital-to-analog conversion techniques, as well as how to interface a digital output to a load (sinking and sourcing). In addition, students will use standard symbols, schematic diagrams, truth tables, waveform representations, timing diagrams and Boolean expressions to represent digital logic functions and circuits. Theory is reinforced with hands-on practice and exposure to troubleshooting techniques.​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Describe the differences between analog and digital electronic circuits.
- Describe TTL and CMOS digital voltage levels.
- Compare digital output technologies, including open collector and tri-state.
- Interface digital outputs, sinking and sourcing loads.
- Build, test and analyze digital timers (astable and monostable).
- Describe logic gate operations (NOT, AND, NAND, OR, NOR, XOR and XNOR).
- Use the standard symbols to represent logic gates in schematic diagrams.
- Interpret digital datasheets, truth tables to analyze the operation of digital circuits using timing diagrams.
- Perform number system conversion between binary, hexadecimal and decimal.
- Build, test and analyze various combinational logic gate functions and applications—including binary adders, comparators, encoders and decoders, multiplexers and demultiplexers.
- Build, test and analyze circuits with 7-segment LED displays.
- Build, test and analyze various sequential logic gate functions and applications—including latches, flip-flops, binary counters and shift registers.
- Build, test and analyze various digital-to-analog and analog-to-digital converters.​
