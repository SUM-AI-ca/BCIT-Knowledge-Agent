## Course Description
This course introduces engineering materials and material processing at an appropriate level for pre-service Technology Education teachers. Principles of math, science, technology and engineering are learned in an integrated way. Students will be able to identify and describe the properties of materials commonly used in high school technology education classes including ferrous and non-ferrous metals, plastics and wood.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Define the common terms used to describe material properties.
- Identify a range of materials commonly used in high school shops.
- Describe the properties of a range of materials commonly used in high school shops.
- Describe the role of carbon in steels and cast irons.
- Describe metal processing variables, including hot working, cold working, annealing and heat treatment.
- Explain the process of precipitation hardening in aluminum.
- Select the appropriate material for a specific task.
- Conduct mechanical property tests on a wide variety of materials using ASTM standard methods, including tensile, hardness and impact testing, and interpret the results of the tests.
- Document lab results and observations.
- Analyze experimental results to identify experimental errors.

## Course Goals
The goal of the course is to provide the necessary theory and practice to enable the students to introduce students to the properties of materials typically used in BC High School Technology Education classes. Students will learn to identify common woods, metals and polymers, predict their behaviour in typical applications and processes, and select appropriate materials for use in projects.
