### Course Description
Descriptive statistics, including measures of central tendency and dispersion, and graphical presentation of data. Basic probability techniques, binomial and normal probability distributions. Inferential statistics, including large and small sample estimation and hypothesis testing with an introduction to experimental design, linear correlation and regression, and acceptance sampling in an applied setting.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Prepare a complete statistical description of relevant sampled data including the calculation of appropriate measures of central tendency and variation and the construction of a histogram using both calculator functions and computer software.
- Use the problem-solving strategies of Pareto and cause and effect (CE, fishbone) diagrams to focus process improvement efforts.
- Compute and understand the meaning of: mean, median, range, standard deviation, variance, and standard t and z scores.
- Solve problems requiring the basic rules of probability including the addition and multiplicative laws.
- Distinguish between discrete and continuous probability distribution models.
- Solve relevant problems using the binomial and normal probability distributions.
- Estimate with confidence intervals for population parameters (means, difference between means, difference between proportions) using large (using z-scores) and small samples (using Students).
- Specify and validate appropriate methods of sampling and experimental design (e.g., random vs. non-random sampling, paired differences testing, etc.)
- Formulate and test hypotheses for means, proportions, differences between means and proportions, paired differences for large and small samples.
- Construct an operating characteristic curve (OC) for a given sample size and acceptance number.
- Distinguish between Type I and Type II errors and related ? and ?-risks in the context of hypothesis testing and acceptance sampling.
- Apply principles of linear correlations and regression and determine correlation coefficients using experimental data.

### Course Goals
The goals of this course are to:
- explain the underlying principles, concepts and terminology of descriptive and inferential statistics,
- demonstrate the application of these concepts using real data and/or referencing relevant industry publications whenever possible,
- develop fluency with statistical calculator functions and statistical software
- develop critical analysis and written presentation skills

### Course topics
Unit 1: Descriptive Statistics (week 1-3)
- Measures of central tendency
- Measures of variation including standard deviation and CV
- Frequency distributions
- Chebyshev's Theorem and the Empirical Rule
- Histograms,frequency distributions, Pareto analysis
- Statistical functions in Excel and Calculators Applications
Unit 2: Probability Theory (week 4 and 5)
- Basic probability rules
- Applications of addition and multiplication laws to sampling
- Introduction to reliability
Unit 3: Discrete Probability Distributions (week 6)
- General discrete probability distributions
- Binomial probability distribution
- Sampling applications
- Discrete versus continuous probability distributions
Unit 4: Continuous Probability Distributions (week 7 and 8)
- Continuous random variables
- Normal probability distribution
- t-distribution
Unit 5: Sampling Distributions (week 9 and 10)
- Central Limit Theorem
- Distribution of sample means
Unit 6-7: Inferential Statistics (week 11 - 14)
- Point and interval estimates for single means and proportions
- Hypothesis testing for single means and proportions
- Confidence intervals and hypothesis tests for difference of means and difference of proportions
Unit 8: Curve Fitting (week 15)
- Correlation
- Regression
- Introduction to multiple linear regression
Unit 9: Control Charts and Sampling Principles (week 15 or time permitting)
- Acceptance Sampling
- General theory of control charts
- Variable charts (x-bar, R or s charts)
- Attribute charts (p or c charts)
