## Course Description
This course presents the basic principles of soil mechanics and testing procedures through lectures, problem-solving sessions, and laboratory work. Topics include the geotechnical design process with a focus on the role of geology, site investigation and laboratory testing, mass/volume relationships, soil classification, effective stress, permeability, consolidation, methods of subsurface investigations. ​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Explain the geotechnical design process, focusing on the role of geology, site investigation, lab testing and the inherent uncertainty. [1, 3]
- Calculate Volume-Weight relationships for soils. [1, 2]
- Classify soils using the USCS and perform and interpret the associated Atterberg Limits and particle size distribution tests. [1, 3, 5, 6]
- Explain the characteristics and uses of different soil types. [1, 2]
- Explain pore pressure, the principle of effective stress and solve related problems. [1, 2]
- Explain the mechanics of consolidation and overconsolidation and calculate the magnitude of 1D settlement. [1, 2]
- Explain the principles of Terzaghi's 1D theory of consolidation and perform related settlement rate calculations. [1, 2]
- Perform and interpret the results from an oedometer test. [3, 5, 6]
- Prepare industry level summary reports for laboratory tests. [3, 5, 7]
- Explain the basic principles and methods of subsurface investigation. [3]
- Calculate geostatic and induced stresses in soils [1,2]​

## Course Goals
The general goals of this course are for students to:

- Explain the process for geotechnical engineering and design and the importance and use of site investigation and laboratory testing as they relate to real engineering projects.
- Understand the inherent uncertainty in soil conditions.
- Understand the influence of soil conditions on civil engineering projects.
- Be able to carry out and interpret some basic laboratory tests to characterize soil.
- Be able to correctly apply some basic theories of soil mechanics such as effective stress and consolidation and make associated calculations.

Engineering Accreditation

The Canadian Engineering Accreditation Board (CEAB) oversees the accreditation of engineering programs across Canada. To measure the effectiveness of an engineering program the CEAB has identified twelve specific attributes that the graduate is expected to possess and use as the foundation to developing and advancing an engineering career. To ensure that the overall curriculum of the BEng in Civil Engineering program covers these attributes sufficiently, the learning outcomes for each course have been mapped to applicable CEAB Graduate Attributes as shown in the square brackets [] following each course learning outcome.

2. Problem analysis: An ability to use appropriate knowledge and skills to identify, formulate, analyze, and solve complex engineering problems in order to reach substantiated conclusions.

3. Investigation: An ability to conduct investigations of complex problems by methods that include appropriate experiments, analysis and interpretation of data, and synthesis of information in order to reach valid conclusions.

4. 6. Individual and team work: An ability to work effectively as a member and leader in teams, preferably in a multi-disciplinary setting.

7. Communication skills: An ability to communicate complex engineering concepts within the profession and with society at large.
