## Course Description
This directed studies course focuses on the application and integration of knowledge and skills acquired in ENVH 8400 (Research Methods). Provides a faculty supervisor to monitor work with a sponsoring agency to independently complete the research project, prepare a formal research report and present research findings. Utilizes fiscal and time- management strategies, oral and written communication skills and critical thinking/problem solving abilities. Requires an off-campus/practicum component.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Apply research methodology to a chosen and previously approved specialty area.
- Complete the research project within the proposed time frame and budget.
- Write a formal research report following the structure requirements of an article for a scientific journal.
- Present their independent research findings to an audience consisting of interested stakeholders.

## Evaluation Criteria
Criteria: %: Comments
Assignment 1: Supervisory Meeting: 10: Meeting to present final methodology and statistical results. This will be a 20 minute discussion with the instructor. Assignment Q&A Sheet to be submitted during the meeting.
Assignment 2: Methods and Results Paper: 20: Methods and Results (real data) but having addressed comments from instructor during supervisory meeting.
Assignment 3:﻿ Discussion Paper: 30: Discussion, Limitations, Conclusions, Recommendations, Knowledge Translation, and Future Studies.
Assignment 4: Journal Submission: 25: Summary of your study findings in a structured abstract style with the component sections completed. See Journal Submission marking guideline. This document will be archived permanently in the BCIT Student Environmental Health Journal repository at the discretion of the instructors.
Assignment 5: Complete Paper with Raw Data & Appendices AND Journal Resubmission: 10: (i) Compilation of literature review paper, methods and results paper, and discussion paper in one continuous document (Include all appendices e.g. raw data, complete experimental procedures), and (ii) revised journal submission. Both documents to include revisions from all instructor feedback.
Assignment 6: Oral Presentation and Question Period: 5: PowerPoint Presentation to colleagues where practicum is located (if doing spring practicum) OR, to classmates and Instructors at BCIT and/or BCCDC (if doing summer practicum). Presentation to occur early/mid May. Please arrange for you presentaiton as soon as possibe. Evaluations to be submitted by Friday, May 26th, 2023.

* Unless agreed upon by the instructors, late submissions willl be deducted 10% per day for 3 days. Submissions received 3 days after the due date will receive a mark of zero.

## Course Specific Requirements
See marking schemes for all 6 assignments posted on D2L, and follow them when preparing your Assignments.

Each student should review and comply with BCIT Policy and Procedures 5101 — Student Research Papers/Ownership/Control and then complete the two-page consent form (LIB-73) for the publication of their research paper in the BCIT Environmental Health Journal. In the consent form, the student will be requested to provide written permission to allow BCIT’s Environmental Health Program to publicize the title of the research project and the abstract, the body of work, together with the author’s name, to members of the environmental health profession and others. Once this has been done, your final research paper will be published in the BCIT Environmental Health Journal. Please submit consent form LIB-73 before Spring Break. The form is available electronically at the following link: https://www.bcit.ca/files/pdf/policies/lib_73.pdf

In accordance with BCIT Policy, plagiarism will not be tolerated. The research submitted must be entirely original work done by the student submitting, with appropriate citations and acknowledgements of other work(s) as incorporated into their submission.

It is not anticipated that there will be “formal” class time necessary except for the first week of the term and individual meetings with your supervisors to clarify statistics and any other project related questions. The initial class will be used to set schedules, administrative duties, and responsibilities for the term.

A guided learning format will require, from each of you, a considerable amount of self-motivation and time management skills.
