### Course Description
HRMG 3170 Human Resource Dynamics and the Management of Workplace Change focuses on the people side of change management — the alignment of the organization's culture, values, people, and behaviors — to encourage the desired results. The employment aspects of change are examined and the processes and methodologies used for developing successful change management initiatives are introduced. Students will explore capacity-building and strategic alignment tools that drive measurable objectives within the context of change management and its influence and impact on organization culture. In preparation for the HRMG 4900 Human Resource Business Consulting Project, students will review best practices for the development of project terms of reference and project consulting skills.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:
- Articulate the nature and dynamics of work, workplace change and the role of the human resource function in organizational change initiatives.
- Design and use effective change management strategies including how to:
- Initiate, plan, execute and imbed change initiatives.
- Recognize that individuals reaction to change will be different and propose strategies to best deal with those reactions.
- Develop a change management and communications plan.
- Utilize skills required to support or lead a change project from conceptualization, project articulation, planning and execution through to celebrating successes and sharing the benefits and results with employees.
- Propose methods for aligning people with change, appealing to emotions and facts.
- Develop processes and approaches to deal with conflicts inherent in workplace change, building on the concepts introduced in HRMG 4200 Employment Relationships and Conflict Management.
- Describe and exhibit behaviours appropriate to a multidisciplinary team.
- Research, prepare and present effective oral and written materials.
- In preparation for the HRMG 4900 Human Resource Business Consulting Project, students will:
- Apply best practices for the development of project terms of reference.
- Develop a change initiative project plan.
- Apply consulting skills.
- Create a communications strategy for their change management plan.

### Course Goals
HRMG 3170 Human Resource Dynamics Workshop builds on the employment relationships and conflict management concepts and constructs explored in HRMG 4200.
The goal of the HRMG 3170 is to further understanding to enhance practice concerning people, change, and the dynamics inherent in the combination of the two through the examination of: • the changing nature of work, the nature and dynamics of workplace change and the role of the human resource function in change initiatives, • process best practices to facilitate workplace change, and • the how and why individuals approach work and change as they do and how to build constructive working relationships through better understanding individuals and their expectations of one another.

### Course Specific Requirements
• All Assignments: All assignments are due as noted on the course outline or as identified by the instructor. Late assignments will be penalized by 20% per calendar day and will not be accepted after 5 calendar days past the due date. In exceptional circumstances, some flexibility and discretion will be used in implementing this policy, providing that the instructor is advised of the circumstances well before the due date of the assignment. Students are expected to identify and address any important team issues, concerns or problems as they occur. Note: In order to have the team assignments count toward one’s course grade, the student must receive a passing grade in the individual-work components taken as a whole.
• Makeup Tests, Exams or Quizzes: There will be no makeup tests, exams, or quizzes. If you miss a test, exam, or quiz, you will receive zero marks. Exceptions may be made for documented medical reasons or extenuating circumstances. In such a case, it is the responsibility of the student to inform the instructor immediately
• Ethics: BCIT assumes that all students attending the Institute will follow a high standard of ethics. Incidents of cheating or plagiarism may, therefore, result in a grade of zero for the assignment, quiz, test, exam, or project for all parties involved and/or expulsion from the course.
• Attempts: Students must successfully complete a course within a maximum of three attempts at the course. Students with two attempts in a single course will be allowed to repeat the course only upon special written permission from the Associate Dean. Students who have not successfully completed a course within three attempts will not be eligible to graduate from the appropriate program.
• Course Outline Changes: This course outline and the course schedule forming a part of the outline may be changed by the instructor. If changes are required or advisable, the instructor will provide reasonable notice.
• Class Start Time: The instructor will make every effort to commence all sessions at the scheduled start time. Given the nature of the subject area, the emphasis on collegial discussion and exercises, and agenda topics that are communicated at the outset of class, all students are expected to be on time at the start of each lab or lecture session.
• Participation: Active participation in labs is essential to accomplish course learning outcomes. All assigned reading must be completed before the lab. Your grade for participation will be determined by regular attendance, the extent to which you are prepared for class, your contribution to class discussions, and the extent to which you enable others to participate. Your ability to demonstrate appropriate workplace behaviours, both in lectures and labs, is also considered when determining your grade for participation.
o Excellent participation (9 – 10) is defined as a student attending all labs, consistently participating, and moving ahead in class discussions. Volunteering high quality analysis and insights or applications of course content. Assisting other class members in reaching course outcomes and understanding course content. Helping others to participate effectively. Being punctual, attentive, and engaged in assigned class activities. Is not distracted by personal technology during classes and does not distract others
o Satisfactory participation (6 - 7) is defined as a student missing no more than one lab, being prepared with good observations and analysis when called upon. Understanding assigned readings and being able to explain or discuss the concepts put forth in classes and readings. Participating in a manner that is not dominating or overwhelming of others. Not distracting others.
o Unsatisfactory participation (0 – 4) is defined as not being prepared when called upon, missing classes, presenting poorly reasoned observations and analysis, being “off-task” or distracted in class activities, not contributing to large class discussions or small group activities, dominating class or small group discussions, talking with other classmates at inappropriate times, arriving late to classes or leaving early, etc.
