### Course Description
​Learners will explore human relationships with microorganisms, prokaryotic (bacterial) cell structure, metabolism and growth, and the principles of antimicrobial action and resistance. In laboratory and seminar, learners will study techniques employed in the clinical microbiology laboratory to identify bacterial isolates and correlate culture growth. Evaluation includes assessment of theoretical knowledge and demonstration of practical skills.​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Describe host (human)-microorganism interactions in health, infection, and disease.
- Describe the significance of prokaryotic (bacterial) cell structure, metabolism, growth, and genetics as they pertain to diagnostic microbiology.
- Differentiate bacteria on the basis of macroscopic (colony) morphology.
- Differentiate bacteria on the basis of microscopic morphology by applying principles of cell structure, staining techniques (Gram stain), and brightfield microscopy.
- Correlate culture growth in relation to physical and nutritional requirements.
- Perform and interpret differential tests used in identification schemes.
- Discuss foundational principles of antimicrobial activity and resistance.
- Discuss pathogenicity and clinical significance for selected groups of bacteria.
- Identify and report selected bacteria growing in culture by correctly adhering to differential testing and laboratory reporting guidelines.
- Practice safe microbiology laboratory procedures, including aseptic technique.

### Attendance Requirements
Regular attendance and punctuality are important in the student learning experience. While attendance in lectures is not monitored, attendance in lab sessions is mandatory and is documented by Faculty and Staff.
In case of illness or other unavoidable absence, students must communicate as soon as possible with the Instructor. Illness must be substantiated by an official medical certificate. Absence for other unavoidable causes must be supported by proper documentation. Excessive absences may result in failure or withdrawal from this course and/or the Program.
If a student is absent from a Midterm or Lab Exam , timely notice must be provided to the Instructor. If the absence is deemed excused, the exam (or an alternate exam) will be offered at a mutually agreed upon date/time and no mark penalty will apply. If the absence is deemed unexcused , the Instructor will work on a case-by-case basis with the student, the Program Head, and the Associate Dean to determine next steps. Unexcused absences can result in a mark of ‘0’ for the missed exam.
Late arrival to a Midterm or Lab Exam : If a student is late for either a Midterm or Lab Exam, they will be admitted to the exam up to the halfway point of the assessment timing. No extra time will be permitted and the exam will be graded in full. If they arrive after the halfway point, they will not be admitted to the assessment. Instead, an alternate time for the exam will be offered. The final grade for that exam will be no higher than 60%.
If a student is absent from a scheduled lab session , and the absence is deemed excused, every effort will be made to have the student make up the lab activities. However, due to scheduling and lab supply conflicts this may not always be possible. Excused absences will have no mark penalty; unexcused absences will result in a mark of ‘0’ for the session’s activities.
Absent from the Final Exam : Timely notice must be provided to the Instructor. If the absence is deemed excused, the Instructor will work with the student, the Program Head, and the Associate Dean to determine next steps. An alternate exam may be offered or other grading policy (e.g. aegrotat) applied – see BCIT policy 5301 PR Grading (article 2.2). Unexcused absences can result in a mark of ‘0’ for the missed exam.
- Note: the MLS program is currently reviewing BCIT policies around missed exams, laboratory sessions, and skills assessments. A program-specific policy may be implemented at some point during the Fall 2025 term with adequate notice.
### Other information
Use of Artificial Intelligence
AI-based content generation tools, or AI tools, are permitted for assignments and project work. However, they are not allowed during formal assessments (e.g. midterms, lab exams, etc.). Note that AI output may include incorrect or misleading information, fabricated authors/dates/publications, faked works, or discriminatory ideas. It is your responsibility to ensure the quality, accuracy, and appropriateness of the work you submit in this course.
If you use ChatGPT (or a similar tool) to get ideas and/or partial answers for an assignment and/or to generate any text for a draft or final version of any part of an assignment, you must declare that you have used it, with a short paragraph describing the extent to which it was used, and you must save any generated text from this tool in case it is requested. You will not be penalized for using this tool, but the instructor may ask you to provide the generated text to help with grading decisions.
Material directly copied from any source (e.g. websites, books, AI tools) must be cited. Undeclared use of AI is a violation of academic integrity as it presents work or ideas created by tools or other people as your own. Failure to fully declare the use of this tool will be considered a violation of BCIT academic policy.
