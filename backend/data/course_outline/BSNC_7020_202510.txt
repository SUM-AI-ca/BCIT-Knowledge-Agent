## Course Description
​This blended course builds on foundations established from previous context of health care courses and provides learners with the opportunity to actively consider and build their capacity for transition to new graduate nurse (NGN) practice. Learners will critically reflect on and self-assess practice readiness and the roles and responsibilities of NGNs and discuss the challenges and opportunities with transitioning from student nurse to NGN. Completion of critical reflections, self-assessments, and development of a transition to practice plan supports practice readiness and achievement of entry-level competencies. This work will serve as preparation for the final terms of the program. ​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will meet the following competencies:

Collaborator

- Works to prevent and resolve conflict, and negotiate solutions in interactions with clients and the healthcare team

Communicator

- Communicates effectively with clients and healthcare team members to support safe, high quality, client-centred healthcare in diverse situations and contexts

Decision Maker

- Articulates clinical reasoning process in support of nursing practice

Leader

- Promotes teamwork and garners commitment and participation of others that will optimize nursing practice and client care

Professional

- Displays initiative, confidence and self-awareness in nursing practice
- Understands the role of the regulatory body and the relationship of the regulatory body to one’s own practice
- Independently assesses own practice and undertakes activities to improve practice and meet identified learning goals

## Attendance Requirements
Attendance: As student success is tied to regular attendance of classes, the BSN program strongly recommends that students attend class regularly. Attendance at all nursing theory classes is an expectation of the BSN program.

In all learning environments, students are socialized into their professional role and are thus required to demonstrate knowledge and application of professional nursing standards and ethics. Multiple absences may jeopardize a student’s development of the knowledge base required for competent professional nursing practice and successful completion of course requirements. As adult learners, students are ultimately responsible for their own learning; however, it is also recognized that repeated absences have a negative impact on collaborative learning experience.
