## Course Description
There will be extensive discussion on the components of a Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) system and an MRI department. This course also prepares students for patient screening, assessing and assisting patients with MRI exams. The following areas are discussed at a level required of a MRI Technologist: clinical history taking, patient and public screening, contrast agents and sedative administration, physical and psychological preparation for a MRI exam, safety regulations and biological hazards associated with MRI.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Describe and explain the considerations when installing an MRI system.
- The following concepts, skills, and issues are used to support this Outcome:
- Types of MRI scanners
- layout of an MRI facility
- field considerations
- MRI safety zones
- shimming and shielding the magnet
- accident prevention
- data archiving systems
- Describe the system components of MRI scanners and accessory equipment used in clinical practice and how to respond to changes in the environment.
- The following concepts, skills, and issues are used to support this Outcome:
- magnet systems
- cooling systems
- magnet quench and safety procedures
- cryogen hazards
- main magnet, gradient, radio frequency (RF) and shim coils
- ancillary equipment including power injector, monitoring and biopsy equipment
- Quality Control (QC) equipment and procedure
- Emergency procedures in MRI
- Scanner trouble-shooting procedures
- Describe the health effects associated with various MRI fields, recognize safety hazards and take appropriate action.
- The following concepts, skills, and issues are used to support this Outcome:
- Static magnetic field and spatially varying static field
- gradient magnetic field and acoustic noise
- radiofrequency (RF) field and the antenna effect
- bio-effects, hazards and safety protocols associated with magnetic fields
- specific absorption rate (SAR)
- exposure limits for a static, gradient and radiofrequency magnetic fields
- recommended guidelines for pregnant patients and staff
- Safety Code 26
- Emergency procedures in the MRI environment
- Adapting safety practices for equipment
- Describe and employ techniques used to recognize and alleviate claustrophobia and patient discomfort in the MRI environment.
- The following concepts, skills, and issues are used to support this Outcome:
- factors contributing to a patient's claustrophobia, anxiety and discomfort
- techniques used to manage patient comfort
- assess and adapt to individual patient needs
- factors that contribute to positive patient experiences
- building rapport and maintaining a secure environment
- monitor and respond to changing patient conditions
- ongoing informed consent and effective communication
- sedation and anesthesia
- considerations for pediatric patients
- Demonstrate safe screening principles and procedures to determine the suitability of individuals and items entering the MRI environment.
- The following concepts, skills, and issues are used to support this Outcome:
- Clinical history review and assessment for discrepancies, contraindications and completeness of patient information
- Adapting communications with patients, family members, support persons and interprofessional team members
- Preparation and screening of all persons entering the MRI room
- Education of risks and safe practices for all persons entering the MRI environment
- Safe, conditional and unsafe status
- Investigations for safety status on equipment, implants, objects and devices
- Appropriate action when safety concerns are identified
- Screening documentation process
- Pre and post procedural care instructions for patients and their families
- Hearing protection and ongoing communication techniques
- Describe the drugs utilized in the MRI environment. The following concepts, skills, and issues are used to support this Outcome:
- Describe the considerations for the use of contrast and other pharmaceutical agents in the MRI environment.
- The following concepts, skills, and issues are used to support this Outcome:
- Types of contrast and other pharmaceutical agents (anesthetic, antianxiety, antihistamine, antiperistaltic, bronchodilator, sedative, vasodilator, organic nitrates and contrast) and their effects
- Contraindications to contrast administration
- Calculating appropriate contrast dosage
- Preparing contrast agents
- Methods, routes and equipment of drug administration
- Recognizing and responding to adverse reactions
- Nephrogenic Systemic Fibrosis (NSF)
- Informed consent, medication orders and documentation requirements
- Pre and post procedural care instructions
- Medical Radiological Technologists (MRT's) role with drug administration
- Considerations of administering contrast
