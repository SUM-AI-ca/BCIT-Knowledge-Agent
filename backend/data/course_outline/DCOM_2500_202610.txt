### Course Description
​In this course, the major elements of the Public Switched Telephone Network (PSTN) are introduced. The components of the PSTN are examined, including outside plant, switching and trunks. The process of setting up a voice circuit across the network is detailed. The conversion of an analog voice signal to a digital equivalent using the Pulse Code Modulation (PCM) process is discussed. The North American Digital Hierarchy for digital communications networks is examined with a focus on the T-1 standard. The creation of a T-1 digital bit stream from multiple independent analog sources utilizing Time Division Multiplexing (TDM) is explored. The components of a digital information transport system are introduced. Local and remote testing and troubleshooting techniques are implemented and evaluated. Safe use of telecom industrial tools is introduced through lab exercises that expose the student to a variety of workplace scenarios.​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Describe the major elements of the PSTN and their operating parameters.
- Discuss noise impairment and reduction techniques on the PSTN.
- Describe the process of converting an analog signal to digital using PCM.
- Determine the bandwidth requirements for the digital equivalent of an analog voice signal.
- List the efficiencies of a TDM system.
- Explain the multiplexing processes used in a T-1 TDM system.
- Describe the North American digital hierarchy system (T-1).
- Build, test and troubleshoot a T-1 network.
- Perform signal power measurements and conversion between watts and dBm.
- Safely use basic hand tools, both telecommunications specific tools and general use.​
