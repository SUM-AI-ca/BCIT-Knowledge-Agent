## Course Description
This introductory course is unique in Canada. It introduces health care professionals to the complex biopsychosocial role that sexual health has in patient care and best practice standards for this practice. The course uncovers the interplay between physiological, psychological and social contexts of sexuality and sexual function through an exploration of the health care professional's personal values and beliefs around sexuality. The course explores sexual issues stemming from medical, surgical and psychiatric illnesses, with a focus on chronic illness and disability including: An in-depth exploration of issues faced by our patient populations, exposure to modes of therapies and treatments for sexual health concerns, instruction on methods for sexual interviewing and history-taking and discussion of educational and counselling theories and methods. This course was developed in conjunction with industry partners, including the BC Centre for Sexual Medicine and Sexual Health Rehabilitation Service (Vancouver Coastal Health), Men's Health Initiative of British Columbia, the University of British Columbia's departments of psychiatry, gynecology and urologic sciences, and the Rick Hansen Institute (funding partner).

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
As a healthcare practitioner, you are expected to follow professional standards of practice throughout all SN programs and courses. In this course, you will have opportunities to use the processes of critical thinking, systematic inquiry, communication, collaboration, leadership, professionalism, and clinical decision-making. Upon successful completion of this course, you should be able to:

- Provide culturally responsive and value-neutral sexual rehabilitation care.
- Demonstrate the use of a biopsychosocial approach in sexual health care.
- Describe the components of the Sexual Rehabilitation Framework.
- Apply the Sexual Rehabilitation Framework to assess for and provide sexual health care.
- Identify the emotional/social sexual implications for clients experiencing a range of disabilities, chronic illnesses and age-related changes.
- Identify treatment options and resources available for sexual adaptation following disability, chronic illness and aging.
- Discuss with clients how sexual response and reproductive health may change with disability, chronic illness and aging in relation to physical limitations.
- Exemplify ethical standards and professionalism in therapeutic communication with sexual health care clients.
- Educate health care professionals on how to integrate sexual health assessment and care into their practice and refer clients to specialized services when required.

## Course topics
Module 1 introduces foundational principles of sexual health rehabilitation (SHR) to guide your practice and provides important historical and foundational knowledge as a basis for your learning journey in SHR. The module reflects on how SHR was founded in Canada and how it has evolved as a practice. As you work through the module you will increase your self-awareness and clarify your own values, while acknowledging the importance of providing neutral and respectful care and support to your patients.

Module 2 reviews the history of the scientific study of sex, several models related to sexual response, and a framework exploring the dimensions of sexuality. These will provide you with context on how we have come to understand and provide care for those with concerns or changes related to sexual health and intimacy. The Sexual Rehabilitation Framework (SRF) will be introduced as a guide to help you begin to understand how to assess and address concerns specific to your client. You will learn how to approach this topic from a holistic perspective.

Module 3 reviews sexuality and sexual development through the lifespan, with a focus on youth with disabilities and the older adult. Many older adults are maintaining their health as they age, and they are also remaining sexually active; therefore, we need to be aware of how to best support this population. This module explore some of the cultural and societal issues that impact sexual health outcomes with a primary focus on Indigenous populations, LGBTQ2SAI+ populations and people with disabilities as examples of Canadian communities that have historically experienced discrimination. We explore the concept of intersectionality the experiences of discrimination due to multiple overlapping power imbalances., and how practices such as colonialism and the oppression of LGBTQ2SAI+ people have had widespread effects on the sexual health of our communities.

Module 4 introduces a number of practical models and tools to build your own professional communication skills and prepare you to initiate and respond to conversations about sexual health. These include the PLISSIT Model, the 3-Step Method, and specific strategies for talking about sexual health rehab. This module also discusses how culture, religion, and society influence the formation of our own values and beliefs around sexuality and our attitudes about the sexuality of others. In this module, we will explore healthcare situations where ethics and sexual health intersect, and values and beliefs may be challenged.

Module 5 examines the sexual health implications of disability and chronic illness through the lens of the Sexual Rehabilitation Framework (SRF). In this module, we will also examine a number of specific disabilities and chronic illnesses, and the common sexual concerns relating to each.

Module 6 reviews the interventions and support strategies commonly used in sexual health rehabilitation and the roles of various specialists and services you may refer patients to when more complex issues arise, using the Sexual Rehabilitation Framework (SRF) as a guide. Several concepts, such as the principles of sexual health rehabilitation and the three pillars of support, will be reviewed. They will help guide you in understanding the considerations to consider when selecting an intervention.

Module 7 introduces the Canadian Guidelines for Sexual Health Education (2019) to prepare you, as multidisciplinary clinicians, to address sexual health rehab in a variety of applications. The core principles of sexual health education, goals of comprehensive sexual health education, determinants of sexual health and well-being, and the Information-Motivation-Behavioural Skills (IMB) Model will be explored. Communication strategies and counselling techniques will also be reviewed.
