### Course Description
This course covers the duties and legal responsibilities involved in public works inspection; examines inspection techniques, reports and data collection; and examines relevant clauses of MMCD general specifications for Public Works construction. Inspection requirements and quality control for soil placement, asphalt cement, Portland cement concrete and underground utilities are discussed.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- List and describe the duties and responsibilities of a public works inspector.
- Describe and use appropriate inspection techniques.
- Collect required data and prepare an inspection report.
- Discuss relevant clauses of MMCD general specifications for construction.
- Classify soils and identify required material tests.
- Demonstrate compaction and field density tests.
- Recognize simple water-related problems in soil and recommend remedial solutions.
- Identify asphalt cements and monitor the asphalt manufacturing and paving process.
- Identify and assist in quality control tests and inspection on Portland cement concrete.
- Monitor forming, curing and finishing of concrete.
- Ensure proper material handling and installation and testing of water, sewer, and underground wiring facilities.
- Monitor location and construction of roads.
- Execute performance evaluation on existing pavement structures.
### Learning Resources
Course materials will be provided through instructor-led lectures, slide decks, and practical lab exercises.
Key references include the Master Municipal Construction Documents (MMCD) , relevant CSA standards (e.g., concrete and materials testing), and applicable BC municipal specifications and standard details .
Supplementary resources such as sample drawings, inspection reports, testing procedures, and real-world case studies will be used to reinforce learning.
Students are encouraged to review provided materials regularly and seek out additional industry references to support their understanding of public works inspection practices.
### Course Goals
The goal of this course is to equip students with the practical knowledge and field-ready skills required to perform effective public works inspection across municipal infrastructure projects. Students will develop a clear understanding of the inspector’s role, authority, and legal responsibilities, including how to operate within the framework of contract documents and the Master Municipal Construction Documents (MMCD). Through applied learning, students will build competency in observing and documenting construction activities, identifying and communicating deficiencies, and verifying compliance with specifications.
The course emphasizes hands-on inspection of key infrastructure systems, including soils and compaction, roadworks and pavement performance, asphalt and concrete construction, and underground utilities such as piping and geosynthetics. By the end of the course, students will be able to assess field conditions, interpret test results, and produce clear, professional inspection reports that support quality assurance, risk management, and successful project delivery in a municipal environment.

### Course topics
Course Topics:
- Role of the Public Works Inspector
- Municipal Infrastructure Systems Overview
- Roles and Responsibilities (Owner, Engineer, Contractor, Inspector)
- Inspector Authority, Duty of Care, and Liability
- Contract Documents and Introduction to MMCD
- Inspection Methods and Workflow
- Field Documentation and Reporting Practices
- Deficiency Identification and Communication
- Soil Classification and Subgrade Preparation
- Compaction Theory and Field Density Testing (Proctor, Nuclear Gauge)
- Road Classification and TAC Guidelines
- Roadworks and Pavement Structure Components
- Asphalt Distress Identification and Pavement Assessment
- Asphalt Materials, Mix Design, and Construction Practices
- Asphalt Quality Control and Common Defects
- Concrete Materials, Placement, and Curing Practices
- Concrete Inspection and Field Testing Methods
- Municipal Pipe Materials and Applications
- Trenching, Bedding, and Backfill Requirements
- Underground Utility Installation and Coordination
- Utility Conflicts and Field Verification
- Introduction to Geosynthetics and Their Applications
- Geosynthetic Installation and Common Failures
- Integrated Field Inspection and Reporting Practices
