## Course Description
Introduces the concept of 3- dimensional computer-based modeling as a tool in the design and manufacture of artifacts. Presents various means of producing, viewing and employing models including wire frames, surfaces, and solids. Considers the use of CAM software to create 3-dimensional models and generate toolpaths to machine complex surfaces using CNC machines.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Develop products using a formal, well documented design process.
- Plan and complete object drawings which communicate effectively using formal drafting conventions and standards, using the computer as the primarily drawing tool.
- Develop process plans to expedite the safe and efficient manufacture of products using both conventional and CNC tools.
- Employ three dimensional computer drawing and /or modeling techniques to develop designs and communicate these designs to others, including public school students.
- Assign tool path parameters to geometry to safely and efficiently machine parts on a CNC machine.
- Graphically simulate tool motion to verify machining operations.
- Generate programs for a specific CNC machine using CAM software.

## Attendance Requirements
Regular attendance in lectures and labs is seen as integral to student success, and will be monitored. Students are expected to be punctual and attend class for the full duration. Late arrivals or early departures may be considered as absences. It is the student’s responsibility to obtain missed course material in a timely manner. Absence from more than 10% of the scheduled course hours may result in the issuance of a warning. If additional scheduled sessions are missed, withdrawal or course failure may be recommended.

## Course Specific Requirements
Practical assignments for CNC may cross over into the disciplines of metal and/or wood where actual machining will take place. Because of safety concerns, CNC programs will be run only under the direct supervision of a qualified instructor. To facilitate practical assignments, machining time will be made available during regularly scheduled lab hours. During these times, students not involved in machining will remain in the computer lab to complete assigned work and further develop expertise with the software. This scenario offers a high degree of autonomy, as does being a teacher. It is expected that students will make good use of the time available.
