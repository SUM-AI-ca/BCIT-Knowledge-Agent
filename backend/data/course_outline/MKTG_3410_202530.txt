### Course Description
​Marketers use branded experiences to create opportunities to connect with their customers. Learners will explore the world of experience design using methodologies such as design thinking and experience mapping to create engaging live events for the corporate, sports, and non-profits markets. Learners will design a series of branded live experiences to explore the various elements of live event design and production. This course includes visits to various event venues. ​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Develop effective public speaking skills by delivering oral presentations (individual and team) to audiences of diverse sizes.
- Apply design thinking framework to experience creation.
- Apply design thinking framework to experience creation.
- Prepare an event pitch for a client.
- Assess the costs and prepare a budget for a live experience.
- Develop a comprehensive plan incorporating: a strategic and operational plan, sponsorship strategy, marketing plan (including target markets), financial and risk management assessment and evaluation​.

### Course Goals
By the end of this course, students will:
- Understand Core Principles of Experience Design Develop knowledge of the Experience Economy and design thinking frameworks as applied to live events in corporate, sports, and non-profit sectors. Recognize how marketing, branding, and audience engagement intersect with event planning and execution.
- Apply Practical Event Management Skills Create attendee journey maps, budgets, volunteer plans, site/vendor evaluations, and event marketing campaigns through weekly applied labs. Demonstrate the ability to integrate sustainability, accessibility, and inclusivity principles into event design and delivery.
- Use Emerging Tools and Industry Practices Experiment with generative AI and digital tools to support brainstorming, analysis, and planning while critically evaluating outputs for accuracy, feasibility, and ethics. Gain familiarity with professional practices such as RFP writing, sponsorship strategy, risk management, and crisis communication.
- Develop Professional Communication and Presentation Abilities Strengthen skills in pitching ideas, storytelling, and delivering persuasive presentations to diverse stakeholders. Practice teamwork and collaboration in labs and group projects, reflecting the realities of the event industry.
- Adopt a Reflective and Ethical Mindset Build self-awareness around decision-making, creativity, and the role of technology in shaping professional practice. Value ethical considerations, diversity, and sustainability as core to responsible event management.

### Course Specific Requirements
Preparation for Class: This course is composed of a lecture and lab each week where attendance and participation is expected by all students. For each session students are expected to be prepared. It is each student's responsibility to understand what is required and to complete the readings and other preparation before each lecture or lab. Preparation will usually involve some readings and questions. Students are encouraged to go beyond the supplied resources and find additional research on the topic at hand. There is no textbook for this course and students will need to read from electronic sources in order to understand class content and complete assignments. Participation and reflection will be encouraged and evaluated as part of this course.
Professional Behavior: In order to create a productive and professional learning environment this course follows these guidelines:
- Students are expected to attend all classes and labs, except in the event of illness or some other very good reason for being absent. In such cases the students must e-mail the instructor as soon as possible to advise the extent and reason for the absence.
- Students will refrain from general, non-class related surfing of the net, playing computer games or reading material not related to the session; e-mail, chat or instant messaging. This is an expected professional courtesy to colleagues and instructors
- Students are expected to be prepared for each class, which means being able to add value to discussions, general knowledge or opinion-based input will not be sufficient. Students are expected to volunteer to speak during class, the instructor will also call on students at random to ask questions or invite opinions.
- Punctuality is extremely important as students who arrive late disturb all others. Punctuality is key skill for event professionals. Students arriving late for class should enter through the rear door of the classroom. Being late will impact your participation grade.
- All students are expected to contribute during class discussions in order to enrich the learning experience for everyone. However, the individual student should not monopolize or dominate discussions, precluding or intimidating others from contributing. When someone is talking is expected that everyone will listen and not hold parallel conversations
- In general, students are expected to behave as professionals during lecture and lab time.
Generative AI Tools:
Within this class, you are welcome to use generative AI tools (ChatGPT, GPT, DALL-E and others) in a totally unrestricted fashion, unless stated in advance of the learning activity that Generative AI tools can not be used. Generative AI tools can not be used during exams/quizs.
However, you should note that all large language models still have a tendency to make up incorrect facts, fake citations and have inaccurate outputs, and image-generation models can occasionally come up with highly offensive products. You are responsible for any inaccurate, biased, offensive, or otherwise unethical content you submit regardless of whether it originally comes from you or a foundation model.
If you use generative AI tools, you must acknowledge use. Undeclared use of the tool/technology are considered a violation of the academic integrity policy. You must document the use and the prompts used to generate the results. You must also evaluate the output for accuracy, make the necessary corrections and add citations as required.
Having said all this, the use of generative AI tools is encouraged, as it may make it possible for you to submit assignments with higher quality, in less time.
BCIT’s policy on plagiarism still applies to any uncited or improperly cited work by technologies or other human beings, or submission of work by other tools or people as your own.
An APA style citation might look like this:
- Reference page: OpenAI. (2024, January 17). [ChatGPT response to a prompt about three prominent themes in BCorp businesses]. https://chat.openai.com
- Acknowledgements might look like this:
- I acknowledge the use of ChatGPT for the generation of three key ideas that I then researched to ensure accuracy and further developed.
- Prompts used: 1) What are the advantages of... 2) How can we improve cost effectiveness when... 3) What ethical challenges relate to ...
