### Course Description
​This course provides the opportunity to develop a complete communications plan from the situation analysis to creative solutions. It reviews the role of strategic planning as it applies to media, creative, sales promotion and research, and examines those areas of promotion where each fit in the promotional mix and when would each best be applied.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Describe a model of the Integrated Marketing Communications process. Explain the steps in developing a marketing communications program.
- Explain how the various decisions of the marketing mix influence and interact with advertising and promotional strategy.
- Explain the importance of advertising and promotion objectives. Differentiate between marketing objectives and communication objectives. Use a comprehensive framework for setting IMC objectives.
- Define advertising creativity. Describe the creative strategy planning process. Assess the effectiveness of various types of message appeals and sources.
- Identify three key decisions for creative tactics. Assess the effectiveness of various creative execution styles.
- Explain the role and functions of specialized marketing communications organizations.
- Explain the reasons for measuring promotional program effectiveness. Evaluate alternative methods for measuring promotional program effectiveness.
- Develop a complete communications plan.
- Describe the advertising regulation system in Canada. Evaluate the ethical perspectives of advertising. Assess the social effects of advertising. Explain the economic role of advertising and its effects on consumer choice, competition, and product costs and prices.
- Cultivate proactive groupthink conduct such as: responsibility, accountability and professionalism that foster team spirit and success.

### Course Goals
Classroom and asynchronous online course aspects: there are deadlines for content, coursework and team assignments. The course will build on material covered in MKTG 1102. This course provides the opportunity to develop a complete communications plan from situation analysis to creative solutions. It reviews the role of strategic planning as it applies to media, creative, sales promotion, and research, and examines the areas of promotion where each fits into the promotional mix and when each would best be applied. Strategic campaigns will be discussed, along with case examples in the text.
Students will, in stages or deliverables, develop a strategic Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC) plan while working in teams. Students typically enjoy the project's collaborative nature and the opportunity to apply course concepts to a real-world marketing communications challenge. Students will have access to all textbook content, and SmartBook chapter assignments will cover most of the textbook. The chapter assignments in Connect SmartBook are graded based on completion rather than accuracy to encourage familiarity with key marketing concepts in integrated marketing communications.
The Midterm will cover the chapter content presented to date and include a student video presentation. The Final exam will cover the remaining chapter content and include a self-reflection element. Online discussions, peer reviews of team members, and teamwork will be necessary to complete this course.
Students will also explore how artificial intelligence (AI) tools can support marketing communications research, strategic planning, and creative development. AI will be used to assist with tasks such as exploring audience insights, brainstorming campaign ideas, refining messaging, and developing research questions. Emphasis will be placed on developing effective prompts, critically evaluating AI-generated information, and integrating AI responsibly as a professional tool within the strategic marketing communications planning process.
The use of AI applies to these outcomes or objectives:
- AI as a tool, not a shortcut
- Prompt development as a skill
- Critical evaluation of AI outputs
- Application to the IMC plan
- AI tools may be used for research support, brainstorming, and idea development, but all submitted work must reflect the student’s own analysis, interpretation, and writing.
### Evaluation Criteria
Chapter Assignments | 15% | Complete Connect SmartBook Chapter Quizzes as students learn and apply the content of each chapter in the textbook's 8th edition - deadlines apply. Students learn by completing questions that link directly to the textbook for a progressive learning experience.
Mid Term | 10% | Video Presentation
Team Project | 45% | Group Presentation and Typed Document - Business Standards. Team member presentations include a PowerPoint and a written IMC Plan submitted on the due date for the Video Assignment. Deliverables or reports will be required as teams develop their IMC plan - all count for marks. The team project's overall value is 45% and runs for more than half of the course.
Final Exam | 10% | Course reflection and opinion questions, including self-analysis.
Participation and Student Professionalism | 15% | Participation marks are awarded for individual assignments, including simulations and role-playing, as well as team assignments.
Discussion Posts and Engagement | 5% | They are composed of posts and replies. Students demonstrate full engagement in a discussion with one another without using AI. Generally, students write a post and then offer three replies to other students' posts.
### Attendance Requirements
Students must attend class and keep up with and complete all the contents of all modules, chapter assignments, discussions, projects, exams and teamwork.
### Course Specific Requirements
Completion of an assigned IMC plan in stages, in a team with equal participation, is mandatory in this course. Business standards on submitted documents apply. It is expected that all students use Microsoft Office applications, as MS Office is free to BCIT students. BCIT also requires students to have a computer for courses, not just a smartphone.
Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools such as ChatGPT and other generative AI platforms may be used in this course as research and planning support tools . Students may use AI to assist with tasks such as brainstorming ideas, exploring target audiences, developing research questions, organizing campaign concepts, and refining marketing messages.
AI should be used as a support tool rather than a substitute for original work . Students are responsible for critically evaluating AI-generated information, verifying facts using credible sources, and ensuring that all submitted work reflects their own analysis, understanding, and strategic thinking.
Students may be asked to demonstrate how they used AI tools in planning and developing their work. Any use of AI that replaces original student work, analysis, or writing may violate academic integrity policies.
The goal of using AI in this course is to help students learn how marketing professionals can responsibly integrate emerging technologies into research, strategic planning, and integrated marketing communications campaigns. Students will learn through experience how to apply AI in a learning environment.

### Course topics
All course topics will be covered in the 13 modules. Some chapters may only be reviewed. Please note that this course uses the 8th edition only and not the 7th edition. Students are expected to view the content in all modules. Access to McGraw-Hill Connect is required.
An example of an AI assignment in an early class:
Prompt: “Act as a marketing strategist. Identify three potential target audiences for a sustainable clothing brand targeting Gen Z in Canada.”
As part of the objective of critical evaluation, students will be asked to determine what the AI missed or did incorrectly. Analyzing the AI's responses, pinpointing any gaps or inaccuracies, and honing analytical skills in the process not only sharpens students' critical thinking but also enhances their ability to discern valuable insights in real-world applications.
