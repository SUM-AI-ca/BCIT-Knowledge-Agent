### Course Description
A theory course introducing the concepts of recognizing, evaluating and recommending control measures for common chemical and biological hazards in the workplace. Sampling protocols and the application of exposure limits for chemical hazards are examined in detail.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Describe the routes of entry of chemical and biological contaminants into the body.
- Define and differentiate between terms used to describe hazardous materials including: gas, vapour, vapour pressure, liquid, solid, particulate, and aeros
- Describe the major components and considerations involved in an air sampling program.
- Define and explain the appropriate use of the following sample types: full-shift, partial shift, grab, personal, & area sampling.
- Define LOD and LOQ and utilize them in determining minimum sample volumes.
- Apply statistical indices to assess a sample’s statistical significance.
- Access and effectively utilize Standard Methods for sampling of chemical hazards.
- Explain the meaning of accuracy and precision regarding sampling and testing methods
- Apply gas laws in calculations related to occupational hygiene sampling and analysis.
- Use dimensional analysis in solving occupational hygiene problems and calculations.
- Explain the meaning, uses and limitations of LD50, LC50, IDLH, & ALARA.
- Explain the application of TLVs including TWAs, STELs, Ceiling Limits, TLV Notations, mixtures, extended work days, and excursion limits.
- Describe the development process and limitations of TLVs.
- Apply OHS legislation regarding TWA, STELs, Ceiling Limits, mixtures, extended work days.
- Define and differentiate between PELs, RELs, and other commonly used terms to describe exposure standards.
- Explain the application of BEIs.
- List BEIs for substances commonly found in the workplace.
- Describe the importance of particle size selection in evaluating aerosols.
- Describe the basic components of an exposure control plan.
- Design an exposure control plan for a specified workplace chemical or biological hazard.
- Describe and differentiate between Dilution Ventilation and Local Exhaust Ventilation.
- Identify common causes of poor indoor air quality.
- Identify and describe a variety of common chemical and biological hazards, including those related to major Canadian industries.
- Differentiate between the different biosafety levels and related control measures.
- Explain the absence of exposure limits for most airborne biological hazards.
- Differentiate between and sample for respirable, thoracic, or inhalable particulates
- Carry out air and bulk asbestos and silica sampling for regulatory compliance
- Differentiate between hi, medium and low risk activities when working with asbestos
