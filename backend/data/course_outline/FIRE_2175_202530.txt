### Course Description
​This course introduces students to the general structure and layout of the current BC Fire Code, including an overview of the Code adoption process and key content from the Preface. Students will receive a general introduction to Division A (Parts 1, 2, 3), Division C (Parts 1, 2), and Division B (Parts 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, and 7). Topics covered include indoor and outdoor storage, classification of flammable and combustible liquids, and related safety measures such as spill control, containers and portable tanks, cabinets for container storage, and hazardous processes and operations. Students are expected to bring a copy of the current BC Fire Code to the first class.​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Describe the structure and layout of the BC Fire Code, including Divisions A, B, and C.
- Explain the process for adopting and updating the BC Fire Code.
- Identify the purpose and general content of the Preface and key Parts of each Division.
- Navigate and interpret Parts 1, 2, 6, and 7 of Division B.
- Summarize the key components of Parts 3, 4, and 5 of Division B, including regulations related to indoor and outdoor storage.
- Classify flammable and combustible liquids according to the Code.
- Recognize requirements for spill control, containers and portable tanks, cabinets for container storage, and hazardous processes and operations.
- Apply foundational knowledge of the BC Fire Code to real-world fire safety and prevention scenarios.​

### Course Goals
FIRE2175 is intended to provide the technician, code user, with an introduction to the general structure and layout of the BC Fire Code. including an awareness level introduction to the Fire Safety Act, OFC Support, NFC (BCFC) Preface, terminology, numbering and general use.
Content for this course is intended for general reference purposes only, as an introduction to the code and is not a substitute for advice from the local authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) or other subject matter expert.
When necessary, for Administrative and Enforcement questions related to the BCFC, you may contact a Fire Service Advisor (FSA) from the BC Office of the Fire Commissioner (OFC),
Course statements regarding compliance have not been evaluated by the AHJ or OFC and may not be applicable in all situations.
Course content is not intended to diagnose, evaluate, or address any specific code compliance or manufacturing process issue. Each issue or topic requires individual review and action based on the specific situation, facts, and circumstances.
Course topics may include, but are not limited to:
- general overview of the process for adopting the Code;
- a scan of the Preface content;
- brief introduction to Division A, Parts 1, 2, 3; and Division C, Parts 1,2;
- a general overview of Division B, Parts 1 to 7.
Students are required to bring a copy of the current BC Fire Code to the first class
