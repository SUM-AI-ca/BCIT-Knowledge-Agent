### Course Description
Learners will develop voice techniques that will allow them to project emotions and thoughts with clarity. This vocal development will include breathing techniques, pitch development using range and resonance zones as well articulation, gained through extensive practice of vocal exercises. Students will be required to keep a journal with reflection on their practice and personal development throughout the course. In addition, professional announcing requires knowledge of the physical attributes required for performance. Students in Media Performance 1 are introduced to basic exercises associated with strengthening the human body to improve performance in a multi-media environment.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course the student will be able to:
- Distinguish between the various breathing styles.
- Identify parts of the vocal anatomy.
- Distinguish the major vocal elements that comprise individual speech personality.
- Explain the elements of a holistic approach to voice and the interaction between intellect, emotions, and physical elements.
- Perform a ½-hour vocal warm-up using Kristin Linklater’s workouts.
- Demonstrate proper vocal technique for performance in any medium.
- Compare and contrast using voice in conversation and working with a script.
- Perform a credible 5-minute news cast with few errors, mindful of cultural, racial and gender diversity.
- Articulate the differences between writing for the eye vs. the ear (speech).

## Assignment #1: 'My Voice -- How Did I Get Here?'
It's time for some personal reflection. As a guide, we are going to use two articles I wrote and posted for you in week one : ‘My Middle: the Beginning of a Beautiful Relationship’ and ‘My Voice: a Love/Hate Relationship'. Once you review them, write some reflections on what you like and don't like about your voice... and how you think this came to be .
As I shared in the articles, do you think there are some events in your life that contributed to this? Remember, you are going to post these reflections to the group so share what you're comfortable with knowing others might read it. That said, the more we choose to be vulnerable and reveal, the more cathartic this process will be.
Keep in mind, too, this is a pretty safe environment : everyone has to participate. You are all beginning to know each other by now. Recognize that we are a cohort that will be together for the remainder of your education, so there's much incentive to remain supportive and respectful.
Write a couple pages of reflections about yourself and your voice. For format, this can be stream-of-conscious style rather than formal essay. This is a self-marking assignment, so see the simple guide on the next page to help choose your mark.
Once completed and posted, you'll then take one or two thoughts that you'd like to share with the group and post them under the 'Discussion' tab (also under 'Activities', similar to the 'Assignment' tab). Cut and paste one or two reflections from your assignment to the discussion. They can be as short or long as you wish , just make sure you put it into context so it makes sense on its own.
Once you post your own, have a look at what others posted and add a comment to the thread of at least two others.
Again, you choose the grade you want on this assignment using the key on the next page (yes, you read that correctly – you grade yourself). Provided you follow through on what it is you promised, I will give you the mark you request. For page format , please use a 12-pt font and double-space along with standard margins.
My Voice – How Did I Get Here? (1-2 pages)
Begin by listing the qualities of your voice. What do you like and what don’t you like? Once you think you’ve identified the main traits of your voice, it’s time to explore some possible contributions to what may have affected your development. Here are some interesting issues you could explore:
- Can I think of some major events in my life that may have affected how I see myself?
- Do I view the world a particular way that may influence my posture or how I deploy my physical energy?
- Are there some physical traits about me that I think might be affecting my voice?
- Can I think of any personality traits that might be affecting the way I sound? Am I uncomfortable around crowds or in new environments?
- Do I avoid sharing personal things with others or tend to withdraw when I feel vulnerable?

Now that you’ve completed a personal analysis of our voice, let’s turn to you in general: what brings you to BCIT at this particular point in your life? And more specifically, what are you doing in a strange course like this one ?! Is it what you expected? What are you hoping to achieve through your education? Where would you like to end up upon graduation?
We are attempting to clarify where you’re trying to go with all of this and keep you focused . If you’ve never taken a performance course like this before, you may discover it’s easy to get sidetracked in the novelty of it all… and lose track of what you’re trying to learn. The methods deployed can be unusual as well, so some students withdraw from the process when they get outside their comfort zone. Being uncomfortable can be good : it means we’ve succeeded in getting you to view yourself and your voice from a new – if more challenging – perspective.
I don’t want you to leave this course exactly the way you entered it: you already know what you know! I want you to learn what you don’t know ! I’m hoping you leave this course a changed person and a changed voice – I’d like you to develop the vocal skill to perform better than ever!
So… write a couple pages of reflections about your aspirations and goals. For format, this can be stream-of-conscious style rather than formal essay. This is a self-marking assignment, so see the simple guide on the next page to help choose your mark.

## Assignment #3: ‘Reflections on my Vocal Development'
Yup, it's time for more deep reflection! Since your personal vocal exploration across the next few weeks is largely monitored and scrutinized by you, it would only seem natural that it’s you who would best judge your performance and sense of accomplishment. Accordingly, you will be given the tools to grade yourself with this evaluative instrument. Provided you follow through on what it is you promised, I will give you the mark you request. Please include a brief explanation and justification for your grade at the end of your assignment .
You simply need to write down reflections on your learning while it’s in process . Try to target writing 500 words (2 pages) each weekly entry. You are to type reflections for three-weeks consecutively, with thoughts completed at the end of each week for a total of 3 journal entries. Please put the entry number and date at the top of each day’s reflection.
For best results, don’t view this exercise as life-journaling – the more you reflect on your daily life in general, the lower your grade will be (stick to the task at hand: the development of your voice!). Use the key on the following page to decide what your mark will be out of 30 (matching the mark with the corresponding amount of energy you have to dedicate to this assignment). For page format , please use a 12-pt font and double-space along with standard margins.
DUE DATE: This assignment must be posted to the Learning Hub no later than Tuesday, November 18th, 2025, before 11:59 pm.
Reflections on my Vocal Development? (6 pages)
Here are some interesting issues you could explore:
- What have I found this week that’s new?
- What’s had the highest impact on me in this week of vocal exercises?
- Describe something Kevin mentioned in lecture that really got me thinking.
- Is there something I’m really struggling to understand in this process?
- No matter how much I try, there’s something I cannot agree with about how to best develop my voice.
- Describe what shocked you the most in your vocal development.
- Above all, I will take this one element away from this course and apply it for the rest of my life!

## Assignment #4: ‘Demonstrate Your Vocal Warmup Routine'
It’s time to design your own vocal warmup!
We have now completed 2 official Vocal Workouts. Which of the many exercises did you get the most out of? You will combine the exercises you liked best into one warm-up routine. This is a routine you could deploy before any performance – perhaps before a personality shift… or in a studio just before a voice-over performance.
It is intended to be brief, lasting 3 to 5 minutes. Choose a combination that involves the following:
- some form of a breath exercise
- touch sound in some way at your relaxed, optimal pitch
- run through some power of some kind (triads, arpeggios, calling vowels)
- do a range exercise or two
- and complete with some articulation exercises
Once you have written out what you want to do and verified that your routine times out, simply video yourself performing your workout. Complete by posting your short video to the assignment folder.
DUE DATE: This assignment must be posted to the Learning Hub no later than Tuesday, November 25 th , 2025, before 11:59 pm.
My Vocal Warmup (3-5 minute video)
Here are some things to keep in mind for your video:
- Ensure you have audio turned on.
- Try for a well-rounded workout.
- To maximize your grade, make sure that you’ve briefly included the 5 elements listed above.
- Try to achieve decent sound and light for your video.
- Think about what works best for you to achieve a warmed-up voice, ready to perform.
- Have fun with it!
## Assignment #5: ‘Demonstrate Your Final Yoga Sequence'
This is your final BCST 1111 assignment, and it shouldn’t take much time to complete.
It is intended to be brief, lasting 2 to 3 minutes. Choose 5 different poses from the sequence that Kat taught you in the yoga video lectures. Keep your video simple with the following tips:
- Shoot your sequence straight through with your laptop camera
- Try to frame it reasonably well so we can see you clearly
- Turn a light on you from behind the camera so the shot is lit well
- Feel free to explain what you’re doing briefly as you go
- Have fun and keep it short!
Once you have written out what you want to do and verified that your sequence times out, simply video yourself performing your workout. Complete by posting your super short video to the assignment folder.
DUE DATE: This assignment must be posted to the Learning Hub no later than Tuesday, December 2nd, 2025, before 11:59 pm.
