### Course Description
​Students will examine the effects of lights, cameras, and surface interactions. Students will setup cameras and camera effects and create a shading network for a high quality 3D scene that is optimized for efficient rendering. Both offline and real time renderers will be explored and the benefits to both will be examined. Students will be able to render their shots in passes, and composite them together in an editing package. Focusing on industry standard presentation techniques, camera moves, pacing, and framing will be explored so students can showcase their work effectively.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Analyze how cameras work in a 3D environment.
- Assign appropriate shaders to 3D models.
- Create high quality shading network for multiple purposes.
- Apply camera effects such as motion blur and depth of field for better and more realistic appearance.
- Render scenes in offline renderers and real time renderers.
- Render in passes and composite shots back together.
- Showcase renders in an effective, industry standard format.

### Course Goals
The goal of this course is to expand upon the foundation of Lighting 1 and provide a thorough education on the Lighting, Rendering and Compositing process. Students will be introduced to advanced lighting techniques as well as industry used compositing practices.

### Course Schedule and Assignments
May 6 | Week 1 - Intro to Lighting 2 Introduction to myself and the curriculum. Discuss the role of a Production Lighting Artist. We will get to work producing various exterior lighting solutions that match selected reference material. In doing so, we will refresh students on: Analyzing reference material (camera basics) and what makes good reference content Various light types and controlling their attributes The principles of illumination Constraints and Rigging Image types and formats Rendering and render settings Sampling and quality control settings Naming convention and submission guidelines Some Photoshop painting Assignment: Light and render a sphere on a plane, recreating two lighting conditions from selected reference of naturally occurring scenarios. Choose reference examples that represent times of day and/or weather conditions that are unique to each other. Do not choose reference that contains artificial light sources or a digital reproduction.
May 13 | Week 2 - Interior Lighting Natural There will be a lecture on THE ILLUSION OF SHAPE before we get working on lighting an interior space through natural sources of illumination (sun/sky). Through this, students will learn about: Analyzing reference material and what makes good reference content Cameras and Composition (Staging) Global Illumination methods (strengths and weaknesses) Dome vs Environment Light Light placement (three-point lighting for environments) Contribution Scales and Render Stats Shaders and materials Depth of Field (camera) Redshift Post FX Assignment: Produce a lighting solution for the Bedroom Asset that matches the selected reference material. Choose reference material of naturally occurring lighting conditions and of a suitable environment.
May 20 | Week 3 - Interior Lighting Artificial Using the same interior asset as last week, we will gather reference material and produce a lighting solution for a space which is illuminated by artificial light sources. There will be a lecture on SETTING THE MOOD. We will cover: Various options for creating artificial sources Emissive materials Global Illumination limitations and techniques to resolve Render Optimization Assignment: Produce a lighting solution for the Bedroom Asset that matches the selected reference material. Choose reference material of artificial light sources or environments.
May 27 | Week 4 - Intro to Compositing This class, students will be introduced to Nuke. We will review the interface and a variety of nodes before seeing how we can use post-processing to improve the results of our Bedroom renders. We will render some AOV’s and use them to improve our result in comp. We will cover: Basic Nuke interface Introduction to Node Based Compositing Read, Write, Grade, Color Correct Rendering and compositing Ambient Occlusion, Depth, Crytomatte
June 3 | Week 5 - Evaluation/Feedback/Review The main purpose of this class is to provide feedback on the assignment submissions and giving time for students to address the notes and improve their results. Students can request I re-explain or re-demonstrate any subject or topic that has previously been presented. There will be a lecture on GETTING ATTENTION.
June 10 | Week 6 - Linear Workflow and AOV’s I will present a lecture on LINEAR WORKFLOW / COLOR MANAGEMENT before we head into Maya and explore rendering and compositing AOV’s. Students will gain knowledge on: Light Physics History of display devices sRGB colorspace versus ACES workflow Application practice and setup (Nuke, Maya, Substance…etc.) Rendering and Compositing AOV’s (basic/advanced) Assignment: Demonstrate your understanding of Nuke and AOV’s by successfully rendering and composting them (Instructor led exercise).
June 17 | Week 7 - Light Effects This class, students will learn to produce a variety of light effects observed in the natural world, as well as, how to fake various light effects. We will cover: Caustics, Volumetrics, Photometric Gobo’s, Caustics, Volume, Smoke, Fire
June 24 | Week 8 - Lighting Analysis – Matching Reference Today’s lesson is back to practicing lighting. I will lead students through the process of lighting the Bathroom asset, in an attempt to match the reference it was produced from. New topics of discussion will be: Trace depth settings Materials and Shaders Importance of modeling to scale Assignment: Produce a lighting solution for The Bathroom Asset that closely matches the reference image it was created from.
July 1 - No Class | Canada Day
July 8 | Week 9 - Render Layers – Beach Bug I will deliver a lecture on LIGHTING CARL & ELLIE’S STORY demonstrating how to properly utilize Render Layers for compositing purposes. By the end of the lessons, students will be able to: Properly analyze a composition and evaluate how to separate it into layers Sufficiently name and produce a variety of render layers Produce a variety of render layer overrides Composite render layers in Nuke Assignment: Demonstrate your understanding of compositing Render Layers by successfully rendering and composting them (Instructor led exercise).
July 15 | Week 11 - Lighting Integration Students will be given a lecture on LIGHTING INTEGRATION that uses Johannas Vermeer’s technique. We will then put it to practice by attempting our own integration and producing a working lighting solution. Students will learn: How to approach integrating CG elements with live action Image planes and backplates Matteshadowcatcher Assignment: Using Vermeer’s Technique for matching reference, reproduce a lighting solution that matches the reference image provided. No not use comp or post-effects. How to compose your final submission will be demonstrated in class.
July 22 | Week 12 - Lighting and rendering an animated sequence (1 of 4) This is the first lesson in a series of classes where we will learn to light and render an animated sequence. In this lesson, we will familiarize students with: Alembic file animation How to approach shot lighting GI limitations what what’s best left for comp Assignment: Students will light and render two animated shots that combine to make a sequence. Continuity, as well as demonstration of shape, mood, and visual interest, will be evaluated.
July 29 | Week 13 - Lighting and rendering an animated sequence (2 of 4) Building of our scenes from last week, this class will focus on setting up our scenes for rendering and creating render layers before batch rendering. We will cover: Render Setup/Render Layers File formats and compression settings Unified Sampling and render optimization Batch rendering
Aug 5 | Week 14 - Lighting and rendering an animated sequence (3 of 4) For the final class on rendering animation, we will take our renders into Nuke and build a comp tree to assemble our shots. We will review: How to produce a rack focus in Nuke How to produce glow and bloom effect in Nuke
Aug 12 | Week 15 - Lighting and rendering an animated sequence (4 of 4) The main purpose of this class is to provide feedback on the assignment submissions and giving time for students to address the notes and improve their results. Students can request I re-explain or re-demonstrate any subject or topic that has previously been presented.
