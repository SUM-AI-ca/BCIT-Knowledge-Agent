## Course Description
​Cardiac Sonography 1 integrates anatomy, the cardiac cycle, and hemodynamics with sonographic principles in the imaging of the heart. Common electrocardiogram (ECG) rhythms are introduced. The focus of this course is on the assessment and interpretation of patterns in the normal heart as seen with 2-dimensional and M-mode imaging. Color and pulsed wave Doppler are introduced. Theory learned in this course will be integrated with on-campus guided laboratory practice sessions.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of the theory component of this course, the student will be able to:

- Identify cardiac anatomy and correlate valve and chamber movement with the cardiac cycle.
- Correlate results from oximetry and/or auscultation. (5.2e)
- Given an echocardiographic image, differentiate artifact and normal variants from pathology in 2D and m-mode imaging. (4.2e, 5.3h, 5.3i)
- Describe examination protocols for structures of interest in 2D and m-mode, applying sonographic principles and instrumentation techniques as listed in Sonography Canada NCP 6.1 Appendix E. (4.2b)

Upon successful completion of the lab component of this course, the student will be able to;

- Maintain professional appearance and manner. (2.3a)
- Employ universal precautions for infection control. (3.1d)
- Practice ergonomic techniques during all scanning sessions. (6.2b)
- Assess and monitor the patient's physical and mental status prior to and during examination and respond accordingly. (3.1e)
- Verify written, authorized directions for examination, identify self to the patient and explain the examination to the patient. (1.2a, 1.1a, 1.1e)
- Ensure informed patient consent, and maintain privacy, confidentiality, dignity, comfort and safety when performing sonographic examinations. (2.1e, 2.1f 3.1f)
- Set up a 3-lead ECG and recognize components of normal sinus rhythm and common arrhythmias. (3.3 f)
- Select the optimum system and transducer for examination considering the patient's age and size, structures being examined and specific indications for examination. (4.1a)
- Adjust instrument controls, optimize patient position and breathing, and record patient positions and the plane of section on the scan, as required. (4.2d, 4.2f, 5.3b. 5.3c)
- Understand the variables and their relationships within calculations and use software calculation packages to perform cardiac m-mode and 2D sonographic measurements. (4.2g,5.4c)
- Input patient data, select the optimal acoustic window, select the correct exam pre-set, and then orient and manipulate the transducer to optimize the image. (4.1b, 4.1c, 4.2a 5.3a)
- Interrogate anatomy in all planes, with 2D and/or m-mode imaging: cardiac position, chest and thorax, coronary vessels, hepatic veins, outflow tracts, pulmonary veins, wall layers and segments, all 4 cardiac chambers and appendages, IAS, IVS, great vessels and their branches, all cardiac valves. (5.3d, Appendix E)
- Measure the following structures with 2D and/or m-mode imaging: outflow tracts, wall layers, all 4 cardiac chambers, and great vessels. (Appendix E)
- Using sonographic principles and instrumentation knowledge, perform a complete echocardiographic exam on an uncomplicated case, recognizing the sonographic appearance of normal structures. (4.2b, 5.3g)

## Course Goals
This is a first-year Cardiac Specialty theory & lab course. As such, the theory component covers a range of information necessary for assessing the normal heart, including cardiovascular anatomy, physiology, and basic ECG interpretation.

The lab portion focuses on appropriate probe orientation, reading the anatomy seen on the screen in the optimal window, methodically making one probe movement at a time, optimizing required anatomy/ technical settings, and creating diagnostic cine-loops. Appropriate probe manipulation is a psychomotor skill that takes time, patience and a lot of practice to gain proficiency.

Due to the nature and scope of the concepts covered, students should expect to dedicate a significant amount of time outside of scheduled classes, and labs to building their didactic and psychomotor skills to meet Level 1 skill expectations.

## Attendance Requirements
Building scanning skills takes time and practice. Regular attendance and prompt arrivals to lab is required as a courtesy to all students.

In cases where a student is unable to attend due to foreseable circumstances, they must inform the Lab Lead Instructor and find a suitable substitute to attend the lab session on their behalf.

Students are responsible for the content they missed and for making up lost practice times.

## Course Specific Requirements
Students are expected to act professionally, with integrity as well as dress appropriately while in the sonography lab both for safety reasons as well as practice for what is to come in clinical.

## Safety equipment or protective clothing
The required dress code for the lab is:

- Scrub pants, a patient gown.
- Closed-toe and closed-heel shoes.
- Long hair must be tied back.
- No food, gum chewing, or scented products are permitted in the lab.
- Water bottles are permitted but must be kept away from the ultrasound machines.

Professional behaviour and attire must be adhered to at all times.

## Other information
Please note that students will be scanning each other's hearts in the lab. As such, a specific dress code, as well as a scanning protocol, will need to be adhered to maintain modesty and privacy.

Coursework includes a blend of in-person instruction, online instruction and the use of laboratory spaces on-campus aligned with BCIT Occupational Health and Safety requirements. Students will be provided with as much notice as possible should there be any changes to the evaluation criteria, delivery methods, and schedule.
