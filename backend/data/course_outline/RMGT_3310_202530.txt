## Course Description
This is a detailed study of automobile insurance in Canada. It deals with legislation related to automobile insurance and policies and/or regulations, concentrating mostly on personal coverages. It gives students an overall view of the different provincial approaches to automobile insurance and some of the problems related to it. Students will deal in detail with owners' policies or coverages specific to BC.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Describe the evolution of automobile insurance in response to the changing needs of society.
- Analyze various approaches to no-fault systems of vehicle damage and identify some of the problems with earlier plans.
- Compare significance of written applications in provinces where the government is the sole provider of basic automobile insurance and in provinces were private industry provides all automobile coverages.
- Describe the operation of compulsory Autoplan third party liability coverage with respect to who is indemnified, the coverage provided and what exclusions exist.
- Identify the possible sources of indemnity for residents of British Columbia injured by an uninsured or unidentified motorist, and outline the coverage provided under Autoplan with respect to accidents caused by uninsured or unidentified motorists in British Columbia.
- Differentiate between the processes used by ICBC and private industry, to amends automobile insurance coverages/policies.
- Differentiate between an ICBC driver's policy and one issued in a province that does not have a government insurance plan.
- Compare coverages provided in each Canadian province with respect to:
- Who offers coverage,
- The basic compulsory coverages including limits,
- What additional coverages are available and from whom; and
- Compare the issue of fault between jurisdictions.

## Course Goals
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Explain why automobile insurance is a legal requirement in all provinces and territories and how a residual market supports this legislation
- Describe the key points regarding: The Compulsory and Optional coverage available through ICBC and Private Insurers Including the No Fault system just introduced Automobile Endorsements, Special Coverage and additional coverage optional policies
- The Compulsory and Optional coverage available through ICBC and Private Insurers Including the No Fault system just introduced
- Automobile Endorsements, Special Coverage and additional coverage optional policies
- Outline factors that determine automobile rates and how underwriters use these rates to determine policy premiums
- Explain the different way claims are settled in provinces and territories with differing laws – tort, no-fault or combination
- Describe the different features of Government Insurance Plans in BC, Saskatchewan, Manitoba and Quebec. Compare those plans to the private plans in the rest of Canada
