## Course Description
Introduces common chemical and physical factors that potentially constitute environmental and/or occupational health hazards. Includes examples such as gases and vapours, noise, and radiation (including light) and temperature extremes. Through lectures and laboratory exercises, teaches how to recognize these factors, evaluate the degree of the hazard using appropriate environmental sampling equipment, and be aware of possible control measures. Requires an off-campus practicum component.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Recognize that a health concern or issue exists.
- Describe steps to identify, reduce, or eliminate a health hazard, using specific examples.
- Interpret selected environmental and industrial hygiene legislation.
- Recognize, measure, and evaluate chemical and physical factors including gases, vapours, particulates, noise, ionizing and nonionizing radiation and temperature extremes in residential, public, and workplace settings.
- Discuss the effects on human health of physical agents, such as noise, vibration, ionizing and nonionizing radiation, extreme temperatures, and odour.
- Assess physical hazards using appropriate environmental sampling, monitoring or investigation methods.
- Communicate risk and mitigation strategies to people affected by the physical agents.
- Outline procedures for conducting environmental surveys which will be valid in compliance and enforcement actions.
- Identify control strategies for chemical and physical hazards.
- Discuss chemical toxicity terminology and standards including LD50, LC50, IDLH. TLV, BEI, ALARA.
- Recognize the importance of evaluating the impact of noise on the community.
- Describe factors that can influence either reduction or propagation of sound transmission.
- Assess noise levels for comparison with applicable standards and legislation.
- Analyze the relationship between noise and land use planning.
- Describe the nature and types of radiation.
- Explain the radioactive decay process.
- Identify the typical products of radioactive decay.
- Identify the exposure routes for radiation.
- Compare and contrast man-made and natural radiation.
- Outline the risk and mitigation methods associated with radon gas.
- Differentiate between ionizing and nonionizing radiation.
- Categorize different types of ionizing and nonionizing radiation and their common sources.
- Evaluate potential or perceived health hazards associated with nonionizing radiation sources (eg: tanning salons, cell phones and towers, electrical power lines).
- Evaluate environmental conditions and other factors that may contribute to thermal stress.
- Interpret environmental sampling methods for accuracy, precision, sensitivity, and detection limit.
- Evaluate environmental sampling methods for appropriate applications and limitations.
- Solve basic problems and perform calculations related to data analysis (unit conversion, dilution calculations, calibration graphs, etc.).​
