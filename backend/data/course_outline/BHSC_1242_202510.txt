### Course Description
Explores psychological and sociological concepts, research, and applications of relevance in the learning and clinical practice of prosthetics and orthotics. Topics include recognizing and managing stress and anxiety in clients and self, critical skills for communication and interaction, accommodating disability and developmental stage, conflict resolution, challenges and opportunities presented by cultural and other diversity in the clinical environment, pain and pain management, human rights and medicolegal implications practice. Emphasizes improving human relations skills with clients, staff, and self.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Discuss the moral, medical and minority models of disability and explain their implications.
- Explain stresses of managing responses to a visible disability and negative attitudes to disability.
- Explain and apply a model for improving empathic interaction.
- Explain how a client's self-concept and self-esteem might be affected by disability, rehabilitation and by the client's use of prostheses and orthoses.
- Select and apply appropriate stress management techniques for self and clients.
- Describe and apply human relations skills in establishing rapport with clients, their families and co-workers.
- Use appropriate communication skills to facilitate patient and family compliance to treatment regimes.
- Use appropriate assertiveness techniques in classroom role playing scenarios depicting patients, family members, technologists and co-workers.
- Apply conflict resolution techniques including handling anger in role playing scenarios.
- Describe the processing of pain, pain phantoms, and apply selected strategies of pain management.
- Identify important developmental factors to be considered when dealing with clients and suggest appropriate strategies for accommodating specific disabilities and developmental stages.
- Demonstrate understanding and application of ethical and human rights principles, and outine relevant issues relating to medico-legal implications of practice.

### Other information
FIRST HALF: WEEKLY READINGS AND LECTURE NOTES
I will make your readings and lecture notes available via The Learning Hub each week . It is strongly recommended that you prepare for class by reviewing your readings ahead of time. The lecture notes will be available so that you can follow along and make notes during class time. The first half of the course will be focused on lecture and skills practice – and this will serve as the content for the midterm exam.
SECOND HALF: SEMINAR TOPICS
As you will see in the course outline, the second half of the course is focused on presentation and discussion of pertinent topics in the field of Psychology and P&O. This will be primarily student-led learning supported by your Instructor. In order to assess your learning of the seminar topics, you will be asked to take notes and engage in each other’s presentations. I will post your presentations on The Learning Hub for reference and review. As well, I will prepare a study guide to support your learning in terms of content and to help you focus on preparing for the final exam.
ASSIGNMENTS

EXAM GUIDELINES AND PROCEDURES
1. MISSED EXAMS: Documented illness as well as unforeseen events and circumstances shall be taken into consideration for any missed exams. Students are required to notify the instructor before the exam start time. Any notification sent after the exam start time will not be taken into consideration and no re-write opportunity will be given. Make-up examinations may utilize alternative assessment methods (different from the original) and will be at the discretion of the Instructor.
2. ONLINE EXAMS AND LATE EXAM START TIMES: All exams will be written on The Learning Hub. You will be responsible for having access to a computer with reliable internet on the day and time of the exam. It is recommended you login early and ensure that your computer and internet are working. The exam will only be available for the duration of the designated exam time. Any answers entered after the exam time will not be saved by Learning Hub, and you will be logged out of the exam. For example, if the exam is from 9 am – 11 am, any answers entered after 11 am will not be saved. Students are expected to be logged in and begin writing exams on time. Students will not be permitted to write the exam if they log in to begin the exam later than 30 minutes after the start of the exam. The student must contact the BHSC department head (contact info provided by the instructor) to request that an alternative arrangement is made. It is the sole discretion of the BHSC department head to permit an alternative arrangement.
3. If there is reason to believe the integrity of an exam has been compromised, the instructor (after deliberation with the department head) may cancel the exam session and reschedule a different exam date and time.
