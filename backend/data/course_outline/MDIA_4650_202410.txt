## Course Description
In preparation for their internship, new media students will participate in an integrated curriculum utilizing the theory and practice, learned in portfolio development and career preparation courses. This course introduces workplace and client-consultant scenarios with role-playing and reviews relevant to new media operations. The purpose of this course is to increase student readiness for the challenges of working with industry as employees or consultants. Students will solidify and evaluate their understanding of common practices, policies and challenges encountered in the workplace during the internship. Topics covered in this course may include business and social etiquette, professional networking, culture and relationships with co-workers, corporate and organizational culture, understanding supervisory styles under waterfall and agile-scrum, disclosing a disability, working for non-profit organizations, professionalism with technology and social media, working alone, freelancing for new media, and securing a mentor. The course concludes after internship completion with in-class review of internship experience and work completed.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Prepare an organizational introductory document to learn about their internship’s organization and their role within the organization.
- Assess interests and abilities in their field of study.
- Write effective internship weekly journal entries.
- Prepare an internship experience report.
- Improve professional social media presence and digital portfolio.
- Demonstrate understanding of industry practices and terminology.
- Integrate knowledge of critical thinking and ethics with effective business practices in dealings with host agencies, clients, colleagues and suppliers.
- Demonstrate strong communication skills.

## Guidelines for School of Business + Media
Attendance: Regular attendance in lectures and labs is seen as integral to student success, therefore, attendance in class is monitored. Unexcused absences in excess of 10% of the time prescribed for this course may result in the assignment of a failing grade. Attendance may be taken at any time during class. A student not present for attendance will be marked absent. A student who leaves class for a period of time deemed excessive by the instructor may be considered absent regardless of whether they are present when attendance is taken.

In case of illness or other unavoidable absence, students must communicate with the instructor, or the appropriate Program Head, as soon as possible (preferably in advance) indicating the reason for the absence. Students who are seeking accommodation for a medical absence must have a BCIT-approved medical certificate ). For other absences, students must be prepared to provide appropriate supporting documentation.

A student who has unexcused absences in excess of 10% of the time prescribed for the course by the Withdrawal Deadline is considered to have ‘Vanished’ and will be assigned a grade of ‘V’. In these cases, no further work will be graded.

These requirements are set out in accordance with BCIT Policy 5101 Student Regulations. More information regarding withdrawals from Flexible Learning courses is available at Refunds and Withdrawals
