## Course Description
This modular course is designed to further expand students’ visual storytelling skills developed in BCST 3120 Television and Video Production 3. Through the various modules the students will learn to apply, with less instructor supervision, previously developed aesthetic and technical production skills to produce original single-camera narrative productions, live-streamed and live-recorded multi-camera switched productions, and videos for real clients. Students will also learn how to analyse the needs of a real world client, develop and propose a production idea, schedule and budget, and then produce videos that meet the client’s needs, while managing client expectations within limited budgets and timelines. At the end of the course, the students will have developed intermediate or industry entry-level skills in a variety of roles.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Utilize a storyboard to demonstrate creative pre-visualization of an original short single-camera narrative script.
- Create breakdowns of all elements necessary to execute a faithful visual interpretation of an original short single-camera narrative script.
- As a member of a production team, and with limited instructor supervision, contribute to the planning, organization, execution, and management of multi-camera productions, original narrative single-camera productions that display story structure and mood, and original video productions that meet a client’s needs including preparation of all necessary production documentation.
- Use aesthetic elements such as lighting, direction, camera operation, audio, and post-production techniques in the creation of original narrative video projects that present story structure and mood.
- Use aesthetic elements such as lighting, direction, camera operation, and audio in the creation of multi-camera productions, demonstrating an intermediate level of technical and creative skills.
- Perform under limited supervision, and to an industry entry-level proficiency a number of the production crew positions required to produce a multi-camera production.
- Using current broadcast cameras and non-linear editing software, create an original short video that meets broadcast quality audio and video and tells a story visually.
- Conduct an analysis to determine a client’s video production needs.
- Provide input to the creation of a script that meets a client’s video production needs.
- Pitch ideas to a client.

## Attendance Requirements
BCST 4120 Policy:

Instructors will issue marks deductions in order to address student attendance issues.

These deductions will apply to the final grade after all other marks have been calculated.

Students are issued a 2% deduction from their overall final grade per occurrence as an indication they have been late for scheduled class activities without notifying the course instructor(s) prior to the start of the class via email or text message and obtaining instructor approval for the lateness. Students are issued a 4% deduction from their overall final grade per occurrence as an indication they have been absent from scheduled class activities without having been excused in writing by the course instructor(s). Advance permission to be absent or late may be granted at the discretion of the course instructor(s) in conjunction with the Television & Video Production Faculty, based on a written request submitted by email to all course instructor(s) a minimum of 7 calendar days prior to the absence.

Deductions for absences may be rescinded if medical documentation is provided within 7 calendar days of the student’s return. Repeated absences for any reason may result in additional course-related activities being assigned to ensure relevant student competence.

Example: Student has a final grade of 80%, but was late three times and has one unexcused absence. These would result in a 10% deduction resulting in a final grade of 70%
