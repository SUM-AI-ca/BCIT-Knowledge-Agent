## Course Description
This course provides an overview of the basic principles in general chemistry with practical applications. Topics covered include: atomic structure, theories of chemical bonding and molecular structure, stoichiometry, physical properties of molecules and materials, chemical equilibrium, electrochemistry, principles of chemical kinetics, and an introduction to organic chemistry. The material shows the relevance of chemistry in engineering emphasizing true problem solving over algebraic mastery.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Describe, in general terms, the atomic structure of elements. [1]
- Use the periodic table to find the grouping of elements and obtain their properties. [1]
- Classify chemical compounds. [1, 2, 3]
- Solve stoichiometry problems. [1, 2, 9]
- Evaluate the gaseous state of matter using various gas laws. [1]
- Perform solubility calculations. [1, 3, 4, 5]
- Explain the properties of acids and bases. [1, 2, 3]
- Account for the variation in physical properties of substances in terms of intermolecular forces present in the substances. [1]
- Predict the type of solid formed by a substance and describe its general properties. [1]
- Analyze the construction of electrochemical cells to determine the voltage produced under standard and nonstandard conditions. [1, 2, 3, 7]
- Discuss reaction rates and factors influencing reaction rates. [1, 3, 7]
- Classify basic organic compounds. [1, 9]

Engineering Accreditation

The Canadian Engineering Accreditation Board (CEAB) accredits bachelor of engineering programs across Canada. To ensure programs graduate well-rounded engineers who are prepared to enter engineering practice, the CEAB requires graduates to possess 12 specific attributes (see below for descriptions). The learning outcomes for each course have been mapped to relevant CEAB graduate attributes. The numbers in [ ] after each learning outcome indicate the graduate attribute to which the learning outcome is mapped.

CEAB Graduate Attributes

1. A knowledge base for engineering: Demonstrated competence in university level mathematics, natural sciences, engineering fundamentals, and specialized engineering knowledge appropriate to the program.

2. Problem analysis: An ability to use appropriate knowledge and skills to identify, formulate, analyze, and solve complex engineering problems in order to reach substantiated conclusions.

3. Investigation: An ability to conduct investigations of complex problems by methods that include appropriate experiments, analysis and interpretation of data, and synthesis of information in order to reach valid conclusions.

4. Design: An ability to design solutions for complex, open-ended engineering problems and to design systems, components or processes that meet specified needs with appropriate attention to health and safety risks, applicable standards, and economic, environmental, cultural and societal considerations.

5. Use of engineering tools: An ability to create, select, apply, adapt, and extend appropriate techniques, resources, and modern engineering tools to a range of engineering activities, from simple to complex, with an understanding of the associated limitations.

6. Individual and team work: An ability to work effectively as a member and leader in teams, preferably in a multi-disciplinary setting.

7. Communication skills: An ability to communicate complex engineering concepts within the profession and with society at large. Such ability includes reading, writing, speaking and listening, and the ability to comprehend and write effective reports and design documentation, and to give and effectively respond to clear instructions.

8. Professionalism: An understanding of the roles and responsibilities of the professional engineer in society, especially the primary role of protection of the public and the public interest.

9. Impact of engineering on society and the environment: An ability to analyze social and environmental aspects of engineering activities. Such ability includes an understanding of the interactions that engineering has with the economic, social, health, safety, legal, and cultural aspects of society, the uncertainties in the prediction of such interactions; and the concepts of sustainable design and development and environmental stewardship.

10. Ethics and equity: An ability to apply professional ethics, accountability, and equity.

11. Economics and project management: An ability to appropriately incorporate economics and business practices including project, risk, and change management into the practice of engineering and to understand their limitations.

12. Life-long learning: An ability to identify and to address their own educational needs in a changing world in ways sufficient to maintain their competence and to allow them to contribute to the advancement of knowledge.

## Course topics
Topics: 1. Atoms, Molecules and Ions (Self-review / Available on TLH: bcit.ca/learn) - Textbook Chapters 1, 2, 3, 8, 9, 10

- Atomic structure, atomic and mass numbers, valence electrons, electron configuration, Isotopes, Atomic weight, Lewis theory of chemical bonding.

2. Organic Chemistry (5 lectures) - Textbook Chapters 3, 26, 27

- Introduction, chemical formulas, functional groups, hydrocarbons, alkanes, cycloalaknes, alkenes, polymers, alkynes, aromatic compounds, intermolecular forces.

3. Stoichiometry & Gas Laws (5 lectures) - Textbook Chapters 2, 3, 4, 6

- Chemical equations, formula and molecular weights, the mole and Avogadro's number, calculations using balanced equations, limiting reactants and excess reactants, percent yield, stoichiometry of chemical reactions involving gases.

4. Aqueous Solutions (4 lectures) - Textbook Chapters 4, 5, 14, 18

- Solutes and solvents, water as a solvent, concentrations of solutions (molarity, molality, mole fraction, percent, ppm, ppb), solubility, saturated solutions, precipitation reactions, solubility equilibria, criteria for precipitation and dissolution.

5. Electrochemistry (5 lectures) - Textbook Chapters 3, 5, 19

- Oxidation numbers, oxidation and reduction reactions, balancing redox reactions, voltaic cells, cell potentials, Nernst equation, concentration cells, batteries, electrolysis, corrosion and applications.

6. Acids and Bases (5 lectures) - Textbook Chapters 5, 16, 17

- Definitions of acids and bases, pH, autoionisation of water, strong acids and bases, weak acids and weak bases, using pKa to calculate pH of weak acids, buffers, buffer pH, titration curves.

7. Periodic Properties of the Elements (3 lectures) - Textbook Chapter 9

- The periodic table, sizes of atoms and ions, ionization energy, electron affinity, electronegativity, polar molecules.

8. Chemical Bonding - Additional Aspects ( 3 lectures) - Textbook Chapters 10, 11

- Lewis structures, formal charge, resonance structures, exceptions to the octet rule, shapes of molecules, valence-shell electron pair repulsion (VSEPR) theory, hybridization of atomic orbitals, bond length, the bond-dissociation energy.

9. Liquids and Colligative properties (4 lectures) - Textbook Chapters 12, 14

- Vapour pressure, boiling point, surface tension, phase diagrams, colligative properties.

10. Solids (3 lectures) - Textbook Chapter 12

- Crystalline solids, crystal structure (molecular, ionic, covalent-network), metallic crystals, salts.

11. Chemical Kinetics (3 lectures) - Textbook Chapter 20

- Understanding chemical reactions, reaction rates and information from rate laws, factors influencing reaction rates, temperature dependence of reaction rates.
