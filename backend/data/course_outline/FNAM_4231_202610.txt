### Course Description
This course provides the student with the opportunity to become involved with an applied research project of particular interest in the field of Renewable Resources. Experience will be gained in planning a project, working productively with other individuals in a team environment, recording relevant information in a number of different formats (including journals), meeting the stated objectives of the project, and reporting on a renewable resource-related group project. Technical Projects 2 focusses on project implementation and reporting.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Select a topic for a technical project.
- Formulate goal statements for technical projects.
- Compose objective statements required to achieve goals for technical projects.
- Determine appropriate data sources for technical projects.
- Analyze the results of research projects in order to make conclusions and/or recommendations relevant to the project.
- Prepare literature reviews for technical projects and describe the purpose of a literature review.
- Document research sources in a manner consistent with accepted practices.
- Develop appropriate strategies to achieve objectives for technical projects.
- Prepare and present a project proposal and work schedule.
- Implement a technical project.
- Conduct research in a manner consistent with accepted practices.
- Write concise project progress reports.
- Prepare meeting agenda and minutes.
- Prepare a project report of professional quality.
- Present the project to a knowledgeable and critical audience.

### Attendance Requirements
Attendance/Illness (BCIT policy): In case of illness or other unavoidable cause of absence, the student must communicate as soon as possible with their instructor or Program Head, indicating the reason for the absence. Students who are seeking accommodation for a medical absence must have a BCIT approved medical certificate submitted to the program, substantiating the reason for absence. For other absences, the student should be prepared to provide appropriate supporting documentation. Unapproved absence in excess of the prescribed regulations within this document may result in failure or forced withdrawal from the course or program. Please see Policy 5101 - Student Regulations .
Attendance/Illness (RENR guidelines): If you need to miss class, please (1) notify instructors with as much advanced notice as possible (at least 24 hours), and (2) arrange for a classmate to provide you with all missed handouts/notes/instructions. Students cannot miss more than 10% of class meetings without prior arrangements with the instructor. This includes missed time resulting from a late arrival – partially missed class time also counts towards the 10% and will be calculated based on time missed. A doctor’s note (using a BCIT approved medical certificate ) may be required within 10 days of the missed class for any illness causing you to miss any assignments/projects/quiz/exam/lab/field trips. Unless otherwise agreed upon, medical appointments, interviews, etc. scheduled during class times are not considered excused absences. At the discretion of the instructor, you may complete the work missed or have the work prorated for excused absences.
Students who miss more than 10% of class meetings may be given a failing grade for the course/program or the student may be removed from the course/program ( Policy 5101 - Student Regulations ).
### Course Specific Requirements
Group Work (FNAM guidelines): Group work is a critical part of natural resource professions, and you will need to be able to work as a team effectively. Your learning in this course and program will be impacted if you are not able to collaborate and communicate effectively and respectfully with peers and instructors. Group assignments make up a significant portion of many courses, and it is expected that everyone contributes their fair share. If a student does not contribute their fair share to group work, instructors reserve the right to penalize their evaluations within reason. Repeated offences (lack of contributions to field or written work, inappropriate communications, distracted or inattentive conduct, etc.) will be treated more strongly.
