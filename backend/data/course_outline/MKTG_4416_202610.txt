### Course Description
The four day per week 10-week internship provides students with a work experience position in a wide spectrum of industry sectors within the digital marketing field. On-the-job performance is fully evaluated.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Develop specific career/job/role-related skills.
- Gain experience in at least one segment of the practical business world and will have an understanding of the nature of careers within it.
- Gain exposure to a working role within the marketing field.
- Develop a network of business contacts through the work experience.

### Course Goals
How To Make the Most Out of Your Internship : Here are tips to help you land the job or at the very least get an excellent recommendation.
Set goals early on. What do you want to learn? What do you want to accomplish? Have this conversation with your sponsor at the beginning of the internship. They will be honest about how they may be able to build that into your experience. Many appreciate that you are driven and focused.
Be realistic. An internship provides exposure to the work and the field; you are not being called in to run the business. You may be required to perform menial tasks like filing, photocopying, or even making a pot of coffee. Remember there are menial tasks included in all jobs. Do them with a smile on your face and you’ll make a great impression.
Gain trust. If you perform exceptionally well with the initial uninteresting tasks, they will start to trust you with more interesting ones. So even if you don’t like what you have been given initially, take it on with enthusiasm, positivity and a smile. Complete the task with accuracy, professionalism, and a positive attitude and you will soon be given more meaningful work.
Be curious. Show an interest in the business. Ask questions. You are not expected to know everything and the more questions you ask, the more you’ll learn about the job and the industry.
Take initiative. When you finish a task, rather than wait for another to be given to you, ask for more work yourself. If there is no work available, ask others in the organization if they need help. You can even create your own work. For example, read company literature and industry journals; learn about the competitors and industry trends; organize files; ask to job shadow in another department. Whatever you do, do not sit idle!
Exude professionalism. The employees may put their feet up on their desks, gossip over the water cooler, check their Facebook newsfeed, and wear jeans and sneakers, but you are still an intern trying to prove worth. Yes, you’ll want to fit into the company culture but when it comes to office conduct, interactions, and dress, be professional.
Take notes. Carry a pen and notepaper with you everywhere, even when you think you are only going to poke your head into an office with a quick question or hello. Information and instructions can be given at any point throughout the day. If you rely on your memory, you will inevitably forget details. Plus, note taking will make you look organized and prepared, and can be invaluable for referencing when the internship is over.
Do not procrastinate. If you are given a task to complete, do it now! Demonstrate that you are keen and capable. Most businesses live and die by deadlines.
Be a perfectionist. You are entering a detail-oriented field. You’ll want to demonstrate that you care about the work. Check constantly for spelling, grammar, punctuation, formatting and mathematical errors. Double-check everything you do.
Ask for feedback and advice. The people around you are full of knowledge and are generally happy to help. Ask how you are doing, how you can improve and advice about your career. Soak up as much learning as you can to help you grow.
Build your network. Make an effort to meet the people that work within the organization. Send a LinkedIn invitation, and at the end of the internship, ask those you worked closely with to write a reference on your LinkedIn profile. If a position is not available at the end of the internship, at least you will be able to leave with a network of people that may be able to help you in the future.
Ask for the job. If you have not been approached with a job offer, arrange a meeting to demonstrate how you could be a valuable asset to the organization. It may seem silly to you, but they won’t know that you want to work there unless you say so. And if they can see you as a valuable asset, they just may create a position for you.
Thank you. Never underestimate the impact of a thank you note. When the internship ends, send a thank-you note to the people you worked with, thanking them for their time and for everything they taught you.

### Attendance Requirements
Any internship absence for any reason must be communicated to your Sponsor and BCIT Faculty Advisor before the absence. BCIT attendance requirements will be strictly enforced
Regular attendance at the internship is seen as integral to student success, therefore, attendance in class is monitored. Unexcused absences in excess of 10% of the time prescribed for this course may result in the assignment of a failing grade and/or removal from the course. In case of illness or other unavoidable absence, students must communicate with me, or the appropriate Program Head, as soon as possible indicating the reason for the absence. Students who are seeking accommodation for a medical absence must have a BCIT-approved medical certificate. For other absences, students must be prepared to provide appropriate supporting documentation. These requirements are set out in accordance with BCIT Policy 5101 “Student Regulations”.
