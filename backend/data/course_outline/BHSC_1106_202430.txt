### Course Description
The first of two consecutive courses that examine normal human structure and function based on a systems approach. This course includes basic human cytology and histology, that serve as the foundation for the study of body systems; the course also covers the skeletal system and the nervous system. BHSC 2206 covers the remaining systems.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Relate structure to function in the human body.
- Relate the function of each body system to the common goal of homeostasis.
- Apply anatomical and physiological principles to Nuclear Medicine technology.
- Utilize anatomical and physiological principles to adapt to new developments in the field of Nuclear Medicine.

### Course topics
I. INTRODUCTION
- Descriptive Anatomy Terminology
- Review of Biology Essentials (self-directed online module + quiz)
II. RELEVANT MOLECULAR AND CELL BIOLOGY
- Transcription and Translation
- Protein Targeting and Translocation
- Cell Metabolism
III. HISTOLOGY
- Introduction to Tissues Types (Epithelial, Connective, Muscle, Nervous)
- Epithelial Tissue
- Connective Tissue
- Serous Membranes
- The Integument
IV. THE SKELETAL SYSTEM
- Long Bone Anatomy, Compact vs Spongy Bone
- Bone Cells
- Processes of Ossification, Bone Growth, Bone Remodeling
- Calcium Homeostasis
- Synovial and Solid Joints, Synovial Joint Classification
- Cartilage Tissue
V. THE NERVOUS SYSTEM
- Neurons and Neuroglia
- Neuron Physiology
- Anatomy of the Cerebrum, Diencephalon, Brainstem and Cerebellum
- Transmission of Sensory and Motor Information
- Peripheral Nerves, Cranial Nerves and Reflexes
- Autonomic Nervous System
- Protection of the CNS Bony Protection (Skull and Vertebral Column) Meninges and Venous Sinuses
- Blood-Brain Barrier
- Ventricular System
- Cerebral Blood Supply and Drainage
