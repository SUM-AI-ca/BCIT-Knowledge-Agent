## Course Description
Students write job application letters and resumes and practice job interviews. They also write short reports and indirect messages common in the civil engineering field. In addition, students complete a semi-formal letter-proposal, as well as give a persuasive oral report on a group project. Students practice skills needed to collaborate with others in producing technical documents.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Write a job-specific resume and application letter for a position in the student's field. [7]
- Give and receive constructive feedback on the resume and application letter. [6]
- Prepare for job interviews, and do peer and self-assessments of performance. [6, 7]
- Write persuasive and negative news messages using an appropriate pattern of organization, style, and tone. [7]
- Chair and participate in workplace meetings. [6]
- Collaborate as part of a team to accomplish work-related communication tasks; develop techniques for starting and managing group projects and sharing workload. [6]
- Analyze group dynamics and active listening strategies; evaluate approaches to conflict response. [6]
- Research, edit, and write a letter proposal. [7]
- Deliver a professional persuasive presentation of this proposal. [7]
- Write a formal laboratory research report [7]

## Other information
Grading Guidelines:

90-100% An outstanding assignment. Its purpose is clear and immediate. Details are clear and explicit. The assignment shows excellence in choice of words in keeping the document precise, yet concise. Its content, tone and style are well adapted to its audience. Information is well organized and easily accessible. The assignment has a smooth flow. The English language, tone, grammar, and punctuation are nearly perfect.

80-89% An excellent assignment. The purpose of the document is clear and immediate. Details are clear and explicit, yet concise. The document’s content, tone and style are well adapted to its audience. Information is well organized and easily accessible. The assignment has a smooth flow. The English language, tone, grammar, and punctuation are excellent. The assignment may otherwise only contain a minor issue.

70-79% A good assignment. The purpose of the work is clear and immediate. Key details are clear and concise. The content, tone and style are adapted to the audience. Information is well organized. The work has a smooth flow. The English language, tone, grammar, and punctuation are good. The assignment may otherwise only contain a number of minor issues.

60-69% An assignment that does several things well but needs improvement in one these areas: the document does not have a clear main-idea sentence; the content needs to be better adapted to its audience; the assignment does not include all key information in the body of the document; or the assignment is affected by poor flow, organization or access, clarity, wordiness, and/or English.

50-59% A document which meets the basic requirements of the assignment. Readers will be able to understand the assignment’s purpose and some key details, but doing so would require reader effort.

0-49% An assignment that fails to meet the basic demands of the assignment as the document would not achieve its intended purpose.

*Professionalism: Homework and attendance are worth 10 percent of your course grade. Completing all professionalism assignments and attending most classes will maintain your full professionalism mark (i.e. you will receive a ‘10-out-of-10’ for professionalism). Missing more than 10 percent of classes will result in deductions to professionalism. Not completing an assignment marked professionalism will also result in a deduction from your professionalism for that assignment. The amount deducted for each missed assignment is listed in the course schedule above. E.g. if an assignment worth 3 professionalism is not completed, 3 marks will be deducted from the 10 course marks allotted to professionalism.
