## Course Description
This course will explore the interrelationship between technology and society. The course will focus on how societal forces shape and are shaped by the meanings, development, spread, and uses of technology. Concepts, perspectives, and arguments from the social sciences and humanities will be studied and applied to analyze connections between society and technology.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Discuss how the creation, development, meanings, uses, and consequences of technology are shaped by various societal forces including economic, political, cultural, and psychological forces (GA 9)
- Identify, explain, and apply concepts and frameworks on the study of technology and society from various humanities and social sciences disciplines (GA 9)
- Critically engage with some of the controversies, paradoxes, and ambiguities in the relationship between technology and society (GA 9, 10, 12)
- Identify how cultural or societal ways of thinking about gender, race, and social class influence the construction, meanings, and uses of technology in society. (GA 9, 12)
- Examine technological innovations and their consequences from societal perspectives. (GA 9)
- Investigate user-technology relationships and representations. (GA 9)
- Identify issues that arise when surveying technological inventions and discoveries in world history. (GA 9, 12)
- Articulate how technology functions within diverse knowledge bases, values, and systems in different societies. (GA 9, 12)
- Discuss worldviews, power relations, and contested constructions of science and technology within and between societies. (GA 9, 10, 12)
- Examine arguments regarding the transfer and spread of technology within and across societies (GA 9, 12)
- Pursue inter-disciplinary angles on technology and society in relation to students' areas of study, work, and experience. (GA 9, 12)​

- Essay assignment (wk 12): answer an assigned question/topic using further investigation/research on specific technologies and societal issues connected to assigned course material.
- Final Exam (wk 15): essay; tests understanding, analysis, and application of course material topics, concepts, and issues selected for the exam pertaining to cross-cultural perspectives on technological development, practice, and transfer.
- Participation (throughout course): requires (i) regular, punctual attendance in class; (ii) attentiveness in class; and (iii) active, thoughtful contributions to class activities and discussions, informed by evidence of prior preparation and completion of coursework as scheduled for class, and per study questions in the Reading Guide.

## Course Specific Requirements
- Preparation and participation for class: Students should come to class with evidence of prior preparation and completion of the scheduled course material for that class, and their notes for their responses to Reading Guide questions. They should have scheduled course material handy in print or digital form for use during class, and contribute actively and thoughtfully for class discussions and activities. Students should regularly use the BCIT Learning Hub for course files, scheduled course activities, and scheduled coursework submissions.
- Coursework submission: Coursework must be submitted in the format, location, and due date/time specified by the instructor. See detailed instructions provided in class and in course handouts posted in the Learning Hub during the term for the specific course component. For late coursework assignments, there will be a 5% deduction per day, up to a total of four days, after which that coursework assignment may receive a fail mark or zero. For absence or non-submission of a timed exam or presentation coursework by the due day/time a zero mark will be applied. If a student is absent for an exam or presentation, or seeks accommodation for assignment coursework submission outside of the due date/time due to illness, medical, or other extenuating circumstances, they must consult with the instructor before the due date of that coursework component, unless the student provides evidence for circumstances that made it impossible to do so, and in that case no later than the day after the due date. The student must also submit appropriate supporting documentation (ex. BCIT-approved medical certificate). If corrections are required to a coursework component, the student must make them and submit them by the instructor- specified due date for the grade on that component to be recorded.
- Academic Integrity: Coursework components must follow the format and requirements specified for that component. The coursework submitted must be the student's work, content, and wording, unless cited by the student from sources that are formally documented using instructor-approved citation and format protocols (APA or MLA style) through in-text citations within the assignment or presentation, and a list of references at the end of the assignment or presentation. All sources used and cited for summary, paraphrase, or quotes, must be trackable, with live links in the reference list, and through attachments with the assignment if the source is not available through the BCIT library or behind a paywall. Fabricated or falsified sources are not acceptable for use or citations. Students must not submit AI-generated content for coursework. Students must not submit coursework for one class, either at BCIT or elsewhere, for evaluation or credit in another class, without prior authorization by the instructor. Coursework will be checked for academic integrity and plagiarism. The course will use BCIT Policy 5104, Student Code of Academic Integrity https://www.bcit.ca/files/pdf/policies/5104.pdf particularly section 8 on plagiarism, cheating, falsifying or misrepresenting, and academic advantage, so students should make themselves aware of this policy.
- Recording of lectures, discussions, and classroom activities: students must not record lectures and classroom activities or discussions unless authorized by BCIT policy 4501 (ex. for IAP academic accommodation purposes). Also see BCIT policy 5201 https://www.bcit.ca/files/pdf/policies/5201.pdf
- Copyright, ownership, and distribution of course materials: Course materials are for the student’s individual study and use. Students must not post or distribute course materials outside the course, including to AI sites/applications, or any websites, without the required permissions from the instructor and copyright holders.
