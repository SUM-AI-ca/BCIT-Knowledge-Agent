## Course Description
This course covers basic principles of applied science relevant to the Power Engineering field, with an emphasis on applied mechanics and thermal engineering. Course topics include work, power, and energy, forces and vectors, levers, machines, motion, pulleys and gears, stress and strain, heat energy, steam, combustion, and the gas laws.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
At the end of this course the student will be able to:

- Discuss principles of work, power, and energy.
- Describe principles of motion, momentum, and force.
- Describe how energy is converted from one system to another.
- Explain the concepts of heat energy, steam, and combustion.
- Explain the gas laws as applicable to Power Engineering.
- Graph simple algebraic functions.
- Use the fundamental laws of logarithms to solve exponential and logarithmic problems.
- Solve triangles using trigonometry.
- Determine the resultant of two or more vectors.

## Course Goals
Chapter 1 – Work, Power and Energy

- Define and state common units of mass, density, force, weight, energy, work, power, efficiency, and pressure
- Calculate defined properties
- Apply concept of conservation of energy
- Describe two-stroke and four-stroke mechanical engines

Chapter 2 – Forces and Vectors

- Differentiate between scalar and vector
- Deconstruct vectors into x- and y-components
- Add vectors Graphically Numerically
- Graphically
- Numerically
- Define and apply concept of friction

Chapter 3 – Levers and Beams

- Apply concept of fulcrum and moments in beam calculations
- State and apply of laws of equilibrium
- Define and calculate reaction forces
- Define and calculate center-of-gravities of 2-D items
- Calculate Safety Valve lever calculations

Chapter 4 – Simple Machines

- Define and calcluate load, load distance, effort, and effort distance
- Define and calcluate mechanical advantages
- Define and calcluate velocity ratios
- Calculate velocity ratios specific to inclined planes
- Calculate velocity ratios specific to wheel-and-axles
- Calculate velocity ratios specific to pulleys
- Calculate velocity ratios specific to differential pulleys
- Calculate velocity ratios specific to screws
- Calculate velocity ratios specific to worm gears

Chapter 5 – Equations of Motion

- Differentiate between speed and velocity
- Explain Newton’s law of inertia
- Apply Newton’s laws of motion to 1-D and 2-D problems

Chapter 6 – Pulleys and Gears

- Calculate speeds in gear systems
- Calculate speeds in pulley systems
- Define and apply the concept of slippage
- Calculate speeds in step-cone pulley systems
- Sequentially calculate speeds in pulley trains and gear trains

Chapter 7 – Stress and Strain

- Define and calculate load, stress, and strain
- Explain stress-strain relationship
- Define elasticity, plasticity, ductility, brittleness, hardness, and toughness
- Define and apply yield point, ultimate tensile stress, and Young’s modulus of elasticity
- Define and apply factors of safety
- Calculate stresses in cylinders

Chapter 8 – Thermodynamics

- Define heat, conduction, convection, radiation, and temperature
- Define and use specific heat capacities
- Define and use sensible and latent heats
- Draw a schematic of a calorimeter
- Define water equivalent in terms of calorimetry
- Calculate specific heat capacities using data from a calorimeter
- Calculate metal expansion due to heat

Chapter 9 – Steam Tables

- Define saturation point in terms of steam tables
- Differentiate between vapours and gases
- Define and calculate dryness fraction
- Define and apply the concept of enthalpy
- Define entropy
- Define and apply the concept of adiabatic systems
- Define critical point
- Explain the information found in steam tables
- Define saturated steam, superheated steam, and subcooled liquids
- Use steam tables to determine properties of saturated steam, superheated steam, and subcooled liquids
- Use steam tables to determine the phase of water under different temperatures and pressures
- Use steam tables to solve common industrial thermodynamic problems

Chapter 10 – Combustion

- Specify the amount of oxygen found in air
- Define the three elements of the fire triangle
- List the four requirements for complete combustion
- List the chemical reaction formulas that occur in combustion
- Define and calculate theoretical oxygen requirements
- Define and calculate heating values for fuels
- Differentiate between higher and lower heating values
- Convert from mass to volume (and vice versa) for flue gas analysis

Chapter 11 – Gases

- Define and apply the Ideal Gas Law
- Define isothermal, isometric, isobaric, adiabatic, and polytropic systems
- Calculate pressures, temperatures, volumes, and energies for isothermal, isometric, isobaric, adiabatic, and polytropic systems
