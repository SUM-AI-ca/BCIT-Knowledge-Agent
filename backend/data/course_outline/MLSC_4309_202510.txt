## Course Description
Concepts introduced in previous microbiology courses are integrated and applied. Infections are studied by body system, with companion laboratory exercises designed to simulate the workflow of routine specimens received in clinical microbiology laboratories. Common pathogens are presented in simulated cultures, which students work through following specific protocols, emulating those used in the modern clinical laboratory. Students are guided to recognize pathogens among normal or contaminating microbiome. Antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST) techniques, introduced previously are now applied: AST is performed, interpreted and reported following protocols appropriate to both the pathogen and the specimen site. Students learn to prepare clinically accurate reports, reporting both pathogen identifications and their antibiograms. Theory and laboratory practice emphasize safety and quality control. Collaborative laboratory workshops and assignments enhance critical thinking, problem solving, and team-based interpersonal skills. Learning is assessed through a variety of learning activities, unit specific laboratory activities and workshops, midterm exams, skills assessments as well as formal laboratory and final theory exams.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- List clinically significant isolates and possible normal flora and/or contaminants in each of the following body systems:
- skin, soft tissue and wounds
- gastrointestinal tract
- genital tract
- urinary tract
- upper respiratory tract
- lower respiratory tracts
- circulatory system
- central nervous system including cerebral spinal fluid (CSF)
- sterile body fluids.
- Describe the processes that lead to infections in each body site, differentiating endogenous and exogenous sources.
- Demonstrate an understanding of collection and transport recommendations and limitations for specimens from each body site.
- Describe the pre-analytical processes required for specific specimens such as when decontamination and/or concentration is required or when acceptance/rejection criteria should be applied.
- Describe methods commonly used in the direct microscopic examination of specimens sent for culture and susceptibilities (C&S), understanding when these are appropriate to use and their limitations:
- Gram-stain
- Acridine Orange stain
- AFB stains: Auramine-O and Kinyoun
- calcofluor white/KOH preparation
- India Ink preparation
- Direct fluorescent antibody stains
- Select media and incubation conditions appropriate for the isolation of clinically significant organisms in specimens from specific body systems.
- Read and assess routine cultures from simulated specimens representing each body system, differentiating potential pathogens from normal and/or contaminating flora, then following protocols specific for each specimen site to guide workup and reporting.
- Discuss AST strategies for specimens from different body systems.
- Perform, interpret and report antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST) appropriate to the pathogen isolated and the specimen site, following guidelines set by the Clinical Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI).
- Create accurate culture reports, including 'pathogen specific reporting' when appropriate, which provide clinically relevant information to guide clinicians in patient treatment plans.
- List smear and culture results considered critical values and/or reportable.
- Perform all culture work following recommended good laboratory practices, including aseptic technique, following biosafety protocols and the correct handling and disposal of infectious materials.
- Describe the relationship between the clinical microbiology laboratory, Public Health officials, facility Infection Control departments and antimicrobial surveillance networks.

## Course Specific Requirements
Assignment Details

Learning activities, workshops and other out-of-class assignments will be handed out throughout the term and will have a defined due date. Marks will be deducted for late assignments: - 25% for each day past the due date.

All out-of-class assignments must be completed – if any are missing at the end of the term, the course may be marked as ‘Incomplete’ (INC). A final grade will be not assigned until the missing learning activities/assignments/workshops and/or projects are submitted.

Artificial Intelligence:

AI-based content generation tools, or AI tools, are permitted for out-of-class assignments and project work. However, they are not to be used during in-class work or during formal assessments including both midterm exams, the skills assessment, the practical lab exam or the final theory exam. Note that AI output can include incorrect or misleading information, fabricated authors/dates/publications, faked works or may incorporate discriminatory ideas. It is the student’s responsibility to ensure the quality, accuracy and appropriateness of any work submitted.

If ChatGPT (or a similar tool) is used to prompt ideas and/or partial answers for an assignment and/or to generate any text for a draft or final version of any part of an assignment, students must declare that it thas been used, describing the extent to which it was used. Any generated text must be saved and available to Instructional staff if requested. There is no penalty for using AI tools as specified here, but an instructor may ask for the generated text to assit with grading.

As with other reference materials AI tools must be cited. Undeclared use of AI is a violation of academic integrity as it presents work, ideas created by AI or other people as your own. Failure to fully declare the use of this tool will be considered a violation of BCIT academic policy.
