### Course Description
This course will introduce students to the concepts of Industrial/labour relations in the current environment, with emphasis on the legal and practical framework in British Columbia. The fundamental facts and issues of labour-management relations will be explored. The legal framework of industrial relations will be examined in addition to the topics of collective bargaining, collective agreement administration, and grievance and arbitration processes. Additional topics will include labour-management collaboration and the nonunion workplace. Emphasis will be placed on how employers and trade unions can effectively manage their legal duties and obligations and their workplace relationships with one another.

### Course Goals
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:
- Recognize the differences between the unionized and nonunion workplaces and the relationship management methods that are appropriate to each.
- Discuss the origins of unionization and the implications unionization has on workplace culture, processes, and roles for employees, unions, and employers.
- Outline the rights, duties, and obligations employers and unions have under the Labour Relations Code.
- Review the preparatory and execution elements of collective bargaining and of processes to break impasse such as strikes, lockouts, mediation, and interest arbitration.
- Explain the primary functions and principles of collective agreements and contract administration.
- Discuss the grievance procedure and process.
- Recognize the principles of progressive discipline and the determination of appropriate discipline.
- Describe the grievance arbitration process, including the preparatory and execution elements.
- Provide examples of when union-management collaboration is appropriate and discuss how such collaboration can be best achieved.
- Recognize the relationship management features of industrial relations and labour relations and the context in which they are exercised.
- Apply one or more of the following: Albert O. Hirschman’s Exit-Voice-Loyalty Model, (2) the Pluralist Approach and the Unitarist Approach, and (3) the Confrontation Approach and Collaboration Approach as aids in analysis and decision making in industrial relations, labour relations, employment relations, and human resource management. And explain how conflict management and employee voice frameworks can be used to understand issues in industrial relations, labour relations, employment relations, and human resource management.

### Course topics
This course will introduce students to the concepts of industrial relations and labour relations with an emphasis on Canada and British Columbia. The historical context, processes, activities, issues, and other fundamentals of industrial relations will be studied within conflict management and employee voice frameworks. The legal framework of labour-management relations will be examined in addition to the topics of trade unions, management, collective bargaining, collective agreements, collective agreement administration, and grievance and arbitration processes. Additional topics will include labour-management collaboration and the nonunion workplace. Emphasis will be placed on how employers and unions can effectively manage their legal duties and obligations and their workplace relationships with one another. Conceptual frameworks for the course content may include one or more of the following: (1) Albert O. Hirschman’s Exit-Voice-Loyalty Model, (2) the Pluralist Approach and the Unitarist Approach, and (3) the Confrontation Approach and the Collaboration Approach.
