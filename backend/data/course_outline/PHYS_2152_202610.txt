### Course Description
​This course is designed to meet the specific needs of Architectural and Building Technology. Emphasis is placed on fundamental understanding of the relevant physics, good problem-solving techniques, and practical applications. The lecture portion of the course begins with an introduction to units and significant figures, reviews of: kinematics, Newton’s laws of motion, work, energy, and power. Applications include fluid mechanics, thermal physics, electric circuits, light & illumination. The practical laboratory component emphasizes proper measurement and data analysis techniques, the use of data acquisition, graphing, and data analysis software, and technical report writing. ​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
From the lecture portion of the course the student will demonstrate his/her comprehension of the material by successfully:
- Solving quantitative problems using a logical and systematic approach that involves: (1) sketching diagrams for clarification where needed, (2) selecting suitable equation(s) based on an understanding of the relevant physical principles, (3) algebraically solving for a formula with the unknown quantity, (4) substitution of values with units into the solved formula, (5) presentation of the final answer with correct significant figures and units.
- Answering qualitative written or multiple-choice questions that test conceptual understanding of the physics that applies to a situation.
From the laboratory portion of the course, the student will gain the ability to:
- Use scientific instruments to make measurements with an understanding of experimental uncertainties and errors.
- Record measurements and perform calculations with data in Excel-based spreadsheet applications.
- Analyze data and produce graphs using an Excel-based application.
- Obtain an estimate of the uncertainty in a calculated quantity, and round-off the calculated quantity to the correct number of significant figures.
- Write a clear, concise, and properly formatted technical report.

### Course Specific Requirements
Course Website: All course related documents such as the lecture notes, lecture examples, lab schedule, formulae sheet, etc. will be posted in Portable Document Format (.pdf) at the Learning Hub - https://learn.bcit.ca . After logging in, go to 'My Courses’ and under '2026.Winter' , select the ‘PHYS-1152-0 - Applied Physics for Building - 93051’ for the lecture portion of the course. For the lab portion, select the ‘PHYS-2152-0 - Applied Physics for Building - XXXXX’ for your particular set. You will receive important announcements from your instructor through your mybcit e-mail over the course of the term, so please check your messages regularly at https://my.bcit.ca .
Pre-Class Preparation: Every week, based on the Proposed Lecture Schedule, you will be assigned sections of the text to be read in advance before they are to be discussed in class. This allows you to more easily understand the material presented in the class and to retain it for use in assignments and tests.
Note Taking: Because the class “Lecture Notes’ are available on the course website in advance of the lectures, they may be printed out or transferred to your computer. Of course, you may preview them ahead of the lectures, and during lectures, the notes may be referenced and annotated at your convenience. More importantly, this allows you to concentrate on listening to the discussion and understanding the concepts being discussed. The lecture example questions are posted in advance as well, but the solutions to them are not posted in advance. It is very important that you actively participate by thinking about the work being presented by the instructor, verifying the mathematics, and giving some thought to the meaning of the solution presented. Also, you are encouraged to participate by answering questions, and posing your own questions as example problems are being discussed in class. The solutions to the lecture example problems done in class will be posted to the course website shortly afterwards.
Term Tests and Final Examination: These will be related to the assigned problems and concepts covered in classes and assignments. Term tests will be non-comprehensive, but the final exam will be comprehensive, involving all of the material covered over the course of the term. A standard PHYS 2152 Formulae Sheet (posted on the Physics Lecture Page) will be provided for use during all examinations.
Laboratory: Attendance and performance of laboratory experiments are mandatory. Before coming to the lab, students are expected to have prepared for the experiment by reading the appropriate section of the lab manual. After doing the reading, answer the assigned Lab Quiz for each experiment on the Learning Hub by the assigned due date and time.
Laboratory Absences: Attendance and performance of laboratory experiments are mandatory and occur every week at the scheduled time. Missing three (3) or more experimients with no legitimate reason, and not being made-up will result in a failing grade of Unsatisfactory (U) in the course, regardless of other grades. Legitimate reasons include illness and personal or family emergency situations. See the note below regarding illness. The instructor must be informed of this reason as soon as possible, and no later than 48 hours after the missed lab. The student is responsible for reaching out to the instructor so that a make-up lab can be scheduled in a timely manner.
Laboratory Reports: Written accounts of each experiment in the form of 'Laboratory Templates' will be required to be completed for each lab session. Lab Templates will be completed by the student and submitted for marking by the assigned due date and time. After being marked and returned, Lab Templates should be attached to your Lab Binder for future studying purposes. Late lab reports will be penalized, and substantially incomplete reports will receive a mark of zero.
Laboratory Test: On two (2) occastions over the term (see posted Lab Schedule), you will be tested on your understanding of the theory, experimental methods, use of scientific equipment, graphical analysis, calculations, and results of the experiments you have performed. During a Lab Test, you will be allowed to freely refer to your own Lab Binder which may contain the following: your own Lab Templates, and any of your own personal, handwritten notes. Unapproved photocopied material will not be allowed.
Workload: Set aside time for physics (and for your other classes, too) every week. It is very important to study regularly, keep up with the work and seek the assistance of the instructor when problems arise. The course load is quite heavy and to succeed you must be prepared to make the appropriate time commitment. The time that you will need to succeed in this course depends on your own background and abilities. As a rough guide, an ‘average’ time allotment is usually one hour for every classroom or lab hour per week. The pace of this (and your other) courses is very fast, so DO NOT FALL BEHIND! With a full load of other classes, it will be extremely difficult to catch up! Because physics is a “cumulative” subject (successful learning of new material is based on a sound understanding of previous material), you must ensure that a firm foundation of knowledge is built upon, starting from the beginning of the course!
Getting Help: Any difficulties that you may have should be resolved as soon as possible. Please consult with the instructor after class, during office hours, or by appointment. Regularly assigned homework (assignments and lab reports) will cover the topics discussed in class and in the lab, and should be completed as the material is discussed in class. All of the assignments will be submitted on-line using the Mastering Physics system at the website: Mastering Physics | Pearson (https://mlm.pearson.com/northamerica/masteringphysics). You must register on the website with a valid access code to be able to view the assignments and submit your answers. An access code is included with your textbook if it was purchased new from the BCIT Bookstore. Used textbooks will most likely have an expired access code; in which case you may purchase a new access code separately on the Mastering Physics website, or else at the BCIT Bookstore. Details of the registration process and a basic introduction to Mastering Physics will be discussed during the first week of lectures.
Please keep in mind that the Assignments represent the minimum number of problems that you should be attempting. In order to obtain sufficient confidence and mastery over the topics discussed in class, solving more problems is generally needed. The textbook contains many additional problems at the end of each chapter, of which the odd-numberd problems have supplied answers at the back of the text. Solve each question or problem with a complete written solution following the methods outlined in class. For future studying purposes, place all completed assignments in a separate notebook and refer to them as the need arises. Students are encouraged to put in the effort to do a good job on the assignments as they closely relate to questions on quizzes, tests, and exams.
Assignments and Evaluation: Assignments must be done on an individual basis, unless otherwise specified by the instructor. All work shall be evaluated as submitted, and submitted work is considered final. Students are expected to complete the required work in the specified order and format, and will be evaluated accordingly.
Late Assignments: It is the student’s responsibility to ensure that assignments are submitted on time. Any portion of an assignment question submitted after the due date and time will be subject to a 20% penalty per calendar day of the value of the entire question. Any portion of an assignment question submitted 5 days or more late will result in a zero grade for the entire question. Students should notify the instructor immediately of any extenuating circumstances relating to late, or potentially late, assignments.
