## Course Description
This communication course is designed for students needing additional assistance in meeting the language standards of a credit introductory business and technical Communication course. It teaches practical business communication techniques for planning, organizing, selecting, writing, and presenting information in business or industry. It covers the same content as COMM 1103, but has additional communication development and English grammar practice activities to support your learning. NOTE: COMM 1106 is equivalent to COMM 1103. Although COMM 1106 may not be included on the program course list or program matrix, students may choose either COMM 1106 or COMM 1103 for their program. Students do not need to take COMM 1103 if they take COMM 1106. Students do not need to submit transcripts for entrance to this course. BUT, in the case of formal or informal grade appeals or reviews they will be asked to show proof of their English requirements. Students who wish to confirm their English level are strongly advised to take COMM 0015.​ Students scoring below "C+" in English Studies 12 are encouraged to take COMM 1106 instead of COMM 1103.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Follow a systematic process in planning and writing business and technical documents.
- Select and organize information to meet the needs of your readers.
- Write sentences that are clear, concise, easily readable, and correct.
- Use a positive, audience-oriented tone in written and oral communication.
- Make written information more accessible through appropriate use of headings, lists, and white space.
- Use the most effective strategies and patterns in organizing routine memos, emails, letters and short reports.
- Prepare and deliver an effective oral presentation on a work-related topic.
- Critically evaluate information to determine if it is accurate, relevant, and complete, and to use critical thinking to develop strong arguments.

Evaluation deadlines are important in this class and are enforced to the minute. Deductions may range from 10% to 100%, depending on the assessment and lateness. You must have access to reliable electronic devices (a good internet connection if we need to do virtual classes). You will need to be able to use PowerPoint and Word documents, which are free to all BCIT students; please get them if you do not have them. If you have issues (health or technical ) before or during an assessment, please contact me immediately.

Nothing is accepted beyond 30 minutes without effort to communicate with me, and then it is up to my discretion, depending on the problem. I may allow you to retake an assignment or exam, but a minimum of 20% may be charged depending on the situation. Chapter quizzes cannot be made up at home.

If you have a valid emergency with official documentation, alternative assessment solutions can be accommodated (alert me and then contact the Program Head for evaluation). Being direct and discussing any issues before the assessment is always better. Often, a compromise can be found that is not available afterwards. Plagiarism and cheating are not tolerated (including accessing internet sources during an assessment, e.g., ChatGPT). By reading these rules of conduct, you accept that anything you submit is your work, and penalties will be imposed for late submissions when no official documentation is provided.
