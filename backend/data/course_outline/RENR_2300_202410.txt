## Course Description
​In this course, students will learn about key ecosystem principles and how ecosystems are described. Several ecosystem classification systems have been developed for British Columbia, such as the Biogeoclimatic Ecosystem Classification, the Wetland and Riparian Ecosystem Classification, and BC's Ecoregion Classification system. Students will practice observational and data recording skills in the field through the collection of soil and plant information and analyze this information.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Explain key ecosystem principles such as energy flow, nutrient cycling, succession, adaptation, and biodiversity.
- Compare and contrast ecosystem components and processes across a range of ecosystem types.
- Observe, collect, and record plant and soil data to describe and identify different ecosystems in the field.
- Apply ecosystem classification systems in different situations and ecosystem types.
- Discuss effects of natural disturbance, climate change, and anthropogenic activities on ecosystem classification​.

## Course Goals
An ecosystem is made up of the interconnected relationships between living organisms and their surrounding environment. Ecosystems are complex and have emergent properties which require consideration when managing fish and wildlife. In order to better understand and describe ecosystems, various principles and classification systems have been developed.

In this course you will learn about key ecosystem principles and how ecosystems are described. British Columbia is a complex place, so many different ecosystem classification systems have been developed that apply in different types of ecosystems and situations, such as the Biogeoclimatic Ecosystem Classification, the Wetland and Riparian Ecosystem Classification and B.C.’s Ecoregion Classification system. You will practice your observational and data recording skills in the field though the collection of soil and plant information, and analyze this information to make suitable fish & wildlife management interpretations.
