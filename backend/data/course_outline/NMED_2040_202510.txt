### Course Description
Integrates foundational knowledge of nuclear medicine with clinical practice applications to prepare students for entry to Clinical Experience 1. Through a combination of lectures, labs and simulations, students begin developing skills and judgement for reviewing requisitions, interviewing patients, explaining procedures, positioning patients, and performing venipuncture. Basic concepts are introduced regarding inter-professional collaboration, ECG, pediatric nuclear medicine, PET/CT, sonography, and radiation therapy. Guest lecturers from clinical sites and allied health professions provide students with overviews of clinical practice and students observe clinical practice through visits to several clinical nuclear medicine sites. The program clinical coordinator outlines performance expectations for clinical practicum rotations.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Relate foundational concepts of nuclear medicine and technologist responsibilities with practice observed at clinical sites.
- Identify risk factors and apply risk management principles to contribute to patient safety improvement processes.
- Assess requisitions and procedure documentation to verify accuracy, completeness and compliance with legal requirements.
- Review clinical history provided, relative to requested procedure, and address discrepancies.
- Conduct interviews to verify patient identity, obtain information from patients or support persons, identify clinically relevant details, and respond.
- Adapt interactions to enhance communication with patient and support persons.
- Provide complete information about procedures to patient and support persons, and verify understanding.
- Respond to questions from patient and / or support persons, or direct them to appropriate personnel.
- Ensure ongoing, informed consent to procedures.
- Perform patient positioning for common imaging procedures.
- Demonstrate a basic understanding of 12 lead ECG principles, patient preparation, electrode placement and data acquisition.
- Distinguish ECG patterns consistent with normal results and normal variants.
- Distinguish ECG patterns consistent with common pathologies and abnormal results.
- Evaluate ECG results to determine if further action is required.
- Demonstrate novice understanding of paediatric nuclear medicine procedures and specialized techniques.
- Understand basic concepts of correlative imaging and therapy modalities (PET, CT, ultrasound, radiation therapy).
- Discuss the relevance of correlative diagnostic, imaging and radiation therapy procedures to patient care.
- Discuss concepts relevant to inter-professional collaboration, patient-centred care, role clarification, team functioning and inter-professional conflict resolution.
