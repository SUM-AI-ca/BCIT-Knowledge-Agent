### Course Description
This seminar-style course builds on the concepts and frameworks established in HRMG 4200. It focuses on applying human resource principles in complex and sensitive situations. Using situational judgment—assessing, evaluating, and navigating specific circumstances—prepares students to make ethical, informed, and well-reasoned decisions. Through real-world case studies, including managing organizational change, facilitating accommodations, negotiating terms and conditions in unionized settings, resolving workplace disputes, and conducting sensitive conversations, students engage in an environment designed to refine critical thinking, problem-solving, communication, and adaptability—essential skills for tackling today’s complex workplace challenges. This course equips students with practical and adaptable human resource competencies, enabling them to navigate real-world challenges confidently.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Articulate the processes, practices, and approaches that form the foundation of situational judgment, integrating critical thinking to evaluate nuanced workplace scenarios.
- Apply situational judgment to assess and respond to workplace challenges, demonstrating ethical decision-making and cultural awareness for constructive outcomes.
- Apply negotiation concepts to workplace challenges, including interests, positions, and integrative versus distributive approaches, recognizing the roles of interests, rights, and power in managing contentious or potentially contentious matters.
- Utilize effective communication strategies—such as listening, framing, and managing emotions.
- Adapt decision-making methods to emerging human resource trends and respond effectively to evolving issues.
- Explain how to manage organizational changes with strategic, supportive approaches.
- Explain how to handle workplace accommodations, complaints, and return-to-work cases with fairness and compliance.
- Conduct sensitive conversations on performance, compensation, and termination.
- Guide project team through employment relationship complexities.
- Leverage project team strengths to enhance collaboration and productivity.​

### Attendance Requirements
Regular attendance in lectures and labs is seen as integral to student success, therefore, attendance in class is monitored. Unexcused absences in excess of 10% of the time prescribed for this course may result in the assignment of a failing grade. Attendance may be taken at any time during class. A student not present for attendance will be marked absent. A student who leaves class for a period of time deemed excessive by the instructor may be considered absent regardless of whether they are present when attendance is taken.
In case of illness or other unavoidable absence, students must communicate with the instructor, or the appropriate Program Head, as soon as possible (preferably in advance) indicating the reason for the absence. Students who are seeking accommodation for a medical absence must have a BCIT-approved medical certificate ). For other absences, students must be prepared to provide appropriate supporting documentation.
A student who has unexcused absences in excess of 10% of the time prescribed for the course by the Withdrawal Deadline is considered to have ‘Vanished’ and will be assigned a grade of ‘V’. In these cases, no further work will be graded.
These requirements are set out in accordance with BCIT Policy 5101 Student Regulations . More information regarding withdrawals from PTS courses is available at https://www.bcit.ca/pts/pts_withdrawals.shtml.
