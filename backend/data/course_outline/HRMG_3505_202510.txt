## Course Description
A first line management course for people responsible for staff training; members of human resource departments contemplating a training program, or who are involved with on-the-job training will be particularly interested. Develops a solid grounding in current training methodology, techniques and aids. Topics include learning theory, determining training needs, writing objectives, designing training programs using outside resources and evaluation.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
At the end of the course participants will be able to:

- Describe methods for conducting training needs assessments.
- Explain the principles of adult learning.
- Formulate objectives for a training session.
- Select appropriate instructional methods.
- Design a training session.
- Use audiovisual supports.
- Deliver a training session.
- Provide feedback to peers.
- Learn to accept feedback from peers.
- Describe techniques for managing group interactions.
- Describe strategies for creating an inclusive training environment.
- Describe processes for assessing training sessions.

## Learning Resources
Required Text Book: Managing Performance through Training & Development by Alan M. Saks & Robert R. Haccoun, 8th Edition (2019), Ontario: Publisher: Nelson.

## Course Goals
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

Describe the difference between training, learning, and performance

Develop a strategy to assess training, development, and performance needs

Identify the characteristics of a learning organization, and develop strategies to move the organization to achievement of the same

Develop and describe learning strategies that link individual and team learning to organizational goals and objectives

Identify current trends and initiatives in adult education and organizational learning.

Regular presence and participation in on-line courses is seen as integral to student success and is, therefore, monitored. Ethics:

BCIT assumes that all students attending the Institute will follow a high standard of ethics. Any contributions to the course -- assignments, quizzes, discussion posts etc. -- that are not a student's own, original work, must be sourced. The unauthorized use of Artificial Intelligence products (such as ChatGPT) for any part of student work in this course is prohibited, and when identified by your instructor, will result in an academic integrity violation. Refer to policy 5104 - Student Code of Academic Integrity. Students are asked to contact the instructor if there is a question or concern about course contributions. Incidents of cheating or plagiarism may, result in a grade of zero for the assignment, quiz, test, exam, or project for all parties involved and/or expulsion from the course.

Online Discussions:

Online responses to discussion questions are meant to foster similar learning experiences to classroom discussions. Each module will have topic questions and case scenarios for discussion. Students will be required to make at minimum one new post and one response to another student’s post in each of the 8 modules. If the student does not post or respond in the week that is randomly selected for grading, the grade will be negatively impacted. Not all of the responses to the discussion questions will be graded. The instructor will randomly select four of the eight modules and grade the student posts on the following scale. Each module is eligible for 3 points for a total of 12 of the 15 points for participation. 3 additional points will be available to be awarded for consistent outstanding participation, submission of all course deliverables in a timely fashion, responsiveness to communication, and supportive online participation with other students to help create a respectful and inclusive environment. For each graded module discussion, a grade of 0-1 means the module posts did not meet requirements, a grade of 2 means the requirements were met, and a grade of 3 means the posts exceeded expectations (see below for outstanding participation). Posts can be either a student's personal thoughts, examples and/or opinions OR they can be a summary or excerpt from research done on the topic from outside sources. If students consult other sources such as webpages, books, etc., the reference MUST be listed at the bottom of the post to be in compliance with BCIT's Academic Integrity Policy. Students are reminded to create their own posts -- unreferenced work or AI-generated posts may result in a 0 grade for discussions and more serious consequences if there are multiple violations. Posts should not exceed 300 words.

Grading Scale:

Outstanding Participation (13-15): Posted responses to all Discussion Questions, consistently participated and moved class discussions forward with their preparation and well thought out responses. Student clearly distinguished between their own thoughts and ideas and content brought in from other sources. Outside content was clearly and consistently referenced at the bottom of the post. Student volunteered a high quality of analysis and insights or applications of course content. Student assisted other class members to understand course objectives. Student demonstrated leadership and responsibility in online activities and course communication. Student offered additional resources and cited outside resources for all posts where outside sources were accessed. Student shared examples from their own experience.

Acceptable Participation (8 - 12): Student may have missed some posts Discussion Questions but still contributed with good observations and analysis. Postings demonstrated understanding of assigned readings and concepts in the text. Participated in a manner that was not dominating or overwhelming of others. Participated in online activities and responded to emails. Met minimum requirements but posted near the time the module discussion was closing thus limiting the amount of meaningful discussion and engagement with the post or did not have add significantly to the discussion. Outside sources and content were consistently referenced at the bottom of posts.

Unsatisfactory Participation (0 –7): Student did not post responses to Discussion Questions or posts were minimal, vague or irrelevant. Two or more posts were missing. Presented poorly reasoned observations and analysis; was frequently “off task” in responses. Did not contribute to online discussions. Did not adequately participate in course activities and course communications. Posts were simply copied from another source without a reference or created using artificial intelligence e.g. from an internet source, publication or work of others or using software such as ChatGPT (if the source was not listed or a student presented AI generated work as their own, the student may be in violation of Ethics and Academic Integrity policy).

## Course Specific Requirements
Assignments:

All assignments must be completed in accordance with BCIT's academic integrity policy. This includes the expectation that students do not collaborate with others (except if they are working in a pair for Assn 2/3), they do not access past assignments completed by other students, that they do not use AI (such as Chat GPT) to generate content/use outside sources and represent it as their own, etc. If there are any questions or concerns, students should contact their instructor in advance. Assignment 1 is to be done on an individual basis. Assignment 2 and/or 3 can be done in pairs, if students choose. Students are responsible for forming their own pairs should they wish to work with a classmate and each student in a pair is responsible for ensuring that both group members participate fully and fairly. Pairs will not be assigned and students joining a classmate for assignment 2 and/or 3 must be responsible for their participation with one another as well coordinating and communicating as needed for the assignment. The pair assignments will be graded the same way as individual assignments and both students in a pair will be assigned the same grade. All assignments must be loaded to the assignment drop box within the online course site. In the case students have worked in pairs, only one student needs to load the assignment. Both participant names should be included in the message and on page 1 of the assignment. Late assignments will be penalized 10% per day and will not be accepted after 5 calendar days past the due date. In exceptional circumstances flexibility and discretion will be used implementing this policy, provided the instructor is advised of the circumstances before the due date of the assignment. If the online site is down when an assignment is due, students are asked to submit to the instructor's alternate email (leila@bcit.ca) for the time stamp and then reload to the course site when it is accessible.

Assignment 1 focuses on Individual reflections on training in one's own organization or industry and personal goals for the course. It is worth 10% of the overall grade. Assignment 2 is worth 15% of the overall grade and may be done individually or in pairs. It requires students to complete a Needs Assessment assignment for a real organization experiencing a specific performance issue. Assignment 3 includes a recorded presentation outlining a short workshop relating to issue raised in Assn 2. Submission will include a video file as well as a lesson plan for the workshop and handout to be used for the session. The recorded video must include an imbedded slide deck. It is not acceptable to hold a laptop in front of the camera when making a presentation. There is free software available such as Zoom to record the presentation with a slide deck. This assignment can be done individually or in pairs. Students need to be visible in the recorded presentation. This final assignment is worth 20% of the student’s grade.

Exams:

The course has a midterm and a final exam. Each exam makes up 20% of the course grade. The exam is a combination of T/F questions and short answer questions. The T/F portion will cover Module 1-4 (in the midterm) and Modules 4-8 (in the final). The written portion of the midterm exam will cover content from Module 1-4 and the written portion of the final exam will cover all course content. Each student must achieve a minimum of 50% in that final examination, in order to complete the course requirements and be granted course credit. There will be an alternate exam slot available for students to write the exam should the scheduled time not work. Students must contact the instructor to book in for the alternate exam date and time in advance. The deadlines for booking the alternate exam slots are listed in the weekly course schedule. The student will be allowed a 2-hour time frame to complete the exams. Students should ensure they are completing the exam in a quiet area free of any interruptions. It is a student's responsibility to ensure he/she has stable internet access for the time slot chosen.

Makeup Tests, Exams or Quizzes: There will be no makeup tests, exams or quizzes. If a student misses a test, exam or quiz, they will receive zero marks. Exceptions may be made for documented medical reasons or extenuating circumstances. In such a case, it is the responsibility of the student to inform the instructor as soon as possible so options can be discussed.

Illness: A doctor’s note is required for any illness causing students to miss assignments, quizzes, tests, projects, or exam. At the discretion of the instructor, students may complete the work missed or have the work prorated.
