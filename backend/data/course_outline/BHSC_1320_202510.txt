## Course Description
This course further examines the human body: cardiac, respiratory, digestive, renal, neurological (incl special senses), endocrine, and reproductive systems. Fluid and pH homeostasis are studied to exemplify regulation and integration of body systems. In the seminars, case studies and other active learning techniques are used to integrate knowledge across body systems and to develop critical thinking skills essential to clinical practice. Evaluation of learning is conducted through weekly seminar quizzes, exams, as well as in-class practice and participation.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Relate function of body organs and systems to their tissue components
- Relate structure to function in the human body
- Apply knowledge of anatomical and physiological principles to the process of diagnosing and treating selected pathological conditions
- Identify cellular & extracellular components and predict changes in physiology when that component becomes dysfunctional
- Develop skills in problem solving and critical thinking when approaching physiology case studies

## Course Schedule and Assignments
Course Schedule

Week: Lecture Topics
1 (Jan 6): Circulatory System Heart - anatomy of the heart – cardiac function - regulation of cardiac function – cardiac enzymes/markers Peripheral circulation - peripheral hemodynamics -regulation of blood flow and pressure –capillary exchange – albumin and oncotic pressure
2 (Jan 13): Blood Composition and functions – plasma - red blood cell formation and turnover - hemoglobin structure and synthesis - white blood cells - WBC types and functions - WBC production - platelets production and function – hemostasis Lymphatic System Functions of lymph - formation and circulation of lymph - lymph node structure and functions – other lymphoid tissues and organs
3 (Jan 20): Respiratory System Organization of the respiratory system – airway and alveolar epithelia - secretions of the respiratory system, mechanisms of gas exchange
4 (Jan 27): Respiratory System Control of respiration - blood gas transport - oxygen dissociation curves – acid/base balance Jan 31 (Midterm 1)
5 (Feb 3): Digestive System Gross anatomy and histological organization of the gastrointestinal tract – salivary gland structure, secretions and function - GI tract secretions, functions and regulation
6 (Feb 10): Digestive System Liver, gall bladder and pancreatic structure and function - Digestion and nutrient absorption
7 (Feb 17): Digestive System Liver, gall bladder and pancreatic structure and function - Digestion and nutrient absorption (Midterm 2 Feb 21)
8 (Feb 24): Urinary System Urine storage and micturition - Acid-base balance - mechanisms of pH control - chemical buffers - physiological buffers -respiratory and renal compensation
9 (March 3): Reproductive System Gross anatomy – menstrual and ovarian cycles – hormonal control of cycles – male reproductive tract and associated glands – composition and functions of secretions Final Lecture and Labelling Exam March 7

Weekly Lab/Seminar Hand-Ins

Each week, students will submit their lab/seminar worksheet. Each worksheet will be worth 1% and the mark will be assessed based on the following guidlines:

- 0%: The worksheet was not submitted OR no relevant information was included

- 0.5%: The worksheet was submitted but not complete (answers were missing or not relevant for all worksheet components)

- 1%: The worksheet was submitted and an attempt was made for each question, the information pertaining to each question is relevant

Pre-Lecture Quizzes

Each week, there will be a posted module or video to be completed before the first lecture of the week (Tuesday). At the beginning of each class, there will be a short quiz on the Learning Hub relating to the content in the module or video. Students must acheive 100% on a given pre-lecture quiz to earn credit. Students will have unlimited attempts in 10 minutes to complete the quiz. The following criteria will be used to determine the mark for all pre-lecture quizzes:

- 0%: At least 2 out of 9 quizzes completed with 100%

- 0.5%: At least 5 out of 9 quizzes completed with 100%

- 1%: At least 8 out of 9 quizzes completed with 100%
