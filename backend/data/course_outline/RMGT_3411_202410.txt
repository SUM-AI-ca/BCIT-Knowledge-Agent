## Course Description
To understand liability insurance, students must first know something of the law and the Canadian legal system; and the differences between Common Law and the Civil Code of Québec. This is not a law course, but it does introduce students to those legal principles that affect liability insurance. It discusses some of the more common ways corporations and people become liable to others and explains how this liability may be insured or why it cannot be insured.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Explain how liability is imposed by law and why liability insurance exists.
- Discuss the relationship of business liability to personal liability.
- Explain how class actions, limitation periods and interest claimable for damages may or may not be insurable.
- Outline liability to the public with respect to products sold or distributed arising from both contract and tort theories of product liability.
- Analyze how various legal defences might be used in product liability actions.
- Outline and explain the Commercial General Liability (CGL) policy in detail and identify the common endorsements to the CGL policy that relate to garage liability, employers’ liability, and pollution.
- Identify the risk factors an underwriter considers to assess a liability risk including the methods used to learn more about a risk.
