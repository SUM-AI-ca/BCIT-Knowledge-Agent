## Course Description
Introduces additional project management techniques to analyze, design, and improve workplace processes. Projects are unique and uncertain environments, managing this uncertainty and tailoring the project approach is critical to a successful project. The course focuses on application of the techniques to realistic case materials and the student Business Consulting Projects. The course covers the full project lifecycle and provides an opportunity to use a range of management methods necessary to deliver value. The course reinforces the systematic design, problem solving approaches and teamwork skills introduced in first year core courses. Project Management methodologies and approaches presented are consistent with the Project Management Body of Knowledge and the Standard for Project Management.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Describe the role of operations strategy as a competitive strength in a global marketplace.
- Describe a production / service system in terms of inputs, processes outputs, information flows, suppliers, customers and external environment
- Describe common project frameworks such as adaptive (agile) and predictive (waterfall) and their uses.
- Apply course principles to create a Terms of Reference and a Project Plan for an Industry Project
- Use project management tools to define and manage a design or improvement project.
- Identify appropriate approaches to manage client and advisor interactions.
- Use appropriate tools to identify customer requirements and translate into technical project requirements.
- Use appropriate methodologies to develop a data gathering and analysis plan.
- Select, and use appropriately, various tools for understanding, designing, and improving processes.
- Apply risk and quality management techniques.
- Identify the necessary controls to consolidate benefits from improvement projects.
- Describe project closing activities.​

## Course Specific Requirements
Assignments are due at the specified deadline. For every 24-hour period (or part thereof) that an assignment is late, 10% of the total possible grade will be deducted. This means that even if an assignment is submitted just one minute past the deadline, it will incur the full 10% penalty for the first 24-hour period.

This course features team-based work. While all team members typically receive the same mark, exceptions may be made if issues arise that disrupt the team's functioning or fairness. Depending on the circumstances, any of the following actions may be taken:

- Marks may be redistributed among team members.
- Individual contributions may be assessed and marked separately.
- A team member may be removed from the group.

These actions may be considered for various reasons, including but not limited to:

- Frequently absent from team meetings or classes.
- Submitting plagiarized or low-effort work.
- Missing team deadlines or class deadlines.
- Behavior that is disruptive or detrimental to team performance.

As in the workplace, your team will need to have a team charter and document infractions, escalating the issue to your instructor at minimum 2 weeks before the assignment is due. The instructor will then guide your team through the appropriate process. In this process, the team will have three options:

Option 1: Mark Redistribution If all members of the group agree that grades should be reallocated, up to 40% will be taken off one student’s score and redistributed to the other students.

Option 2: Separate Marking If group members do not reach an agreement on how marks should be reallocated, each team member’s contributions will be marked separately.

Option 3: Removal from the Group If all conditions outlined below are met, the team may choose to remove a member from the group. That member will be required to complete the remaining work of the term project individually.

In order to initiate this process, the following conditions must be met:

- The team must have a team charter, signed by all members, that states the desired action can be taken (or references this course outline where this policy is described).
- There must be documented evidence of the team member's non-compliance. This may take the form of records of assigned and submitted work, evidence of quality, effort or plagarism issues, documentation of missed meetings, or other relevant examples. Documentation must be created before or as the issues occur, not after the fact.
- The student must have received at least one verbal and two written warnings, with the final warning clearly stating the action the team will take if another violation occurs.
- All remaining members of the team must agree on the chosen course of action.

If these conditions are met, the team may iniate the process by emailing the instructor with evidence of hte above and booking office hours to discuss.
