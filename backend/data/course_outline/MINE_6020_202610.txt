### Course Description
This is a practical course that involves field studies and application of mining field methods. Emphasis is placed on accurate data collection, analysis, and interpretation. Reports will be produced by students on the work undertaken on the various technical assignments. Aspects of mining engineering study may include but are not limited to: use of various surveying equipment, underground ventilation surveys, review and/or design of rock support systems for different mining applications, stability assessments, and identification of safety issues. This course will include field trips.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Lay out a monitoring/testing program to analyze the given problem [1,2,3].
- Carry out field measurements and tests to analyze the problems [1,2,3,4,5].
- Evaluate and interpret results of the field tests carried out [1,2,3,4,5].
- Design a solution to the problem based on analysis of data gathered [1,4,5].
- Prepare a short field research report [1,2,4,6,7].

Overview:
A field study is designed to immerse students in practical mining evaluation activities, focusing on key aspects of underground mine re-entry and exploration potential. Students will work in teams to assess the tunnel and surrounding conditions, applying skills from geology, environmental science, geotechnical engineering, mine safety, and survey.
Consideration: The Underground mine excavations have been inactive for an extended period and were recently rehabilitated through the removal of loose rock and the installation of back (roof) support systems.
### Course topics
Key Areas of Study and Student Tasks:
Each day, both teams will work on the same assigned tasks, focusing on collecting relevant data in the field. In the evening, after returning to the accommodation, each team will meet with the instructor to present and discuss a summary of their findings for the day.
1. Safety Assessment
- Conduct a detailed inspection of the exploration tunnel.
- Identify any immediate hazards (e.g., ground stability, blocked access, ventilation concerns).
- Evaluate whether the tunnel is safe for exploration activities.
- Develop a list of recommended safety measures.
2. Ventilation & Air Quality
- Assess the current air circulation within the tunnel (qualitative/quantitative).
- Note the presence of gases, airflow restrictions, or ventilation failures.
- Tabulate all airflow and air‑quality measurements and compare them with the threshold values specified in the BC Mining Code.
- Produce a vent survey plan indicating the airflow quantifies and direction.
- Make recommendations for improving tunnel air quality and ventilation design.
3. Survey
- Conduct the survey using selected surveying equipment from BCIT.
- Survey the accessible underground excavations using available tools.
- Survey the mine access routes within a 200 m radius of the portal using available tools.
- Measure and record drift dimensions, orientation, and any notable features.
- Produce a simple sketch or map of the underground and surface layout.
4. Environmental Baseline Study
- Conduct an initial review of surface and subsurface environmental conditions (e.g., water, flora, fauna, and any disturbances).
- Observe signs of contamination, erosion, or habitat disruption.
- Prepare a brief baseline report with recommendations for further environmental monitoring.
- Identify nearby water bodies, vegetation, and signs of wildlife. Collect basic water quality data (pH, turbidity, conductivity, etc.) using portable instruments if available.
- Assess signs of previous disturbance and potential sources of contamination.
5. Geotechnical Assessment
- Observe and document rock mass conditions, support systems, and rehabilitation work.
- Conduct on-site in-situ testing (e.g., Schmidt hammer tests) where possible.
- Identify areas showing signs of instability or degradation.
- Take rock samples. (If further analysis is needed at the Campus Lab)
- Recommend additional geotechnical studies or immediate reinforcement actions.
6. Indigenous Engagement Considerations (Desktop study)
- Discuss and reflect on the importance of Indigenous consultation in exploration and mining projects.
- Identify the traditional territory in which Bear Mountain lies.
- Outline how engagement might be incorporated into future development plans.
- Identify what additional data and studies would be required to complete this assessment.
