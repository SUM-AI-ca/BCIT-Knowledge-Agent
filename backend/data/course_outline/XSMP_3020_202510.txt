## Course Description
​Students will cultivate intercultural competence through reflection and application of core concepts and ideas. They will develop cultural self-awareness and attitudes necessary to study and work in a dynamic and culturally diverse setting abroad. Students will review different communication styles and the influence of societal values and norms. They will use the “Something's Up" method to analyze instances of intercultural misunderstanding and miscommunication and adapt behavior in these situations.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Identify and describe the influence of culture on social and personal values, norms and communication styles.
- Describe how differences in values, norms and communication styles can lead to intercultural miscommunication or misunderstanding.
- Describe the role of history and power in influencing the context surrounding intercultural interactions.
- Apply insights from cultural models to analyze and adjust personal approaches to intercultural situations.​

## Course Goals
The goal of this course is to help prepare you for your study abroad experience, as well as for using intercultural competence to collaborate, communicate and establish meaningful relationships across difference in diverse communities, organizations and teams after returning from your study abroad experience. During the course, you will have an opportunity to develop key intercultual skills such as perspective taking, empathy, openness to difference, tolerace for ambiguity and awareness of your own cultural identity as well as awareness of cultural differences in communitcation.

## Course Specific Requirements
Expectations for engagement

- In order to succeed in the course, plan to spend approximately 3-4 hours per week engaging with the course materials and with each other through online discussions.
- The assignments and discussions require reflection and observation of intercultural skills in your own professional/life context, so in the past students have found it useful to log into the course 2-3 times a week for shorter periods of time rather than trying to complete the readings and assignments in a single session.
- Class discussion was richer and more interesting during the weeks when participants logged in briefly several times and responded to each other’s postings, rather than trying to complete written assignments and participate in forum discussions on the same day.
- In week 3, you will work together in pairs or groups of 3 to test if Generative AI tools can apply intercultural competence. Find someone to work with early, and put your names down on the sign-up sheet.

Use of Generative AI

- You will use a GenAI tools as the basis for a weekly class discussion activity during week 3. A number of GenAI tools (Like Chat GPT) no longer require you to set up an account, so they can be used easily without sharing any personal information.
- As you test the intercultural competence of AI tools please be mindful not to share personal information. This assignment will allow you to further your AI literacy and understand the opportunities and limitations that GenAI tools provide.
- For the graded assignments in class, feel free to use GenAI tools for early brainstorming or to refining your writing, but please reference their use appropriately as indicated by BCIT guidelines. The final assignment needs to be your own work. The Library has some great resources on citing AI here:
- https://libguides.bcit.ca/AI/citing-genai
- The 2 major assignments in the course will ask you to apply the concepts and theories and models we learn in class to your own personal and professional context, and to provide concrete examples of their application. GenAI tools will not be able to do this for you. As of today, they provide very generic vanilla responses, and are not able to create the type of critical analysis and personal examples expected in the course. Please note that AI output may include hallucinations, fabricated authors/dates/publications, and incorrect cultural information based on outdated sources. You are responsible for the accuracy and quality of your final submission.
