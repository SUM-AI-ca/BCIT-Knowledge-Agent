## Course Description
​This course is a continuation of DSON 1001. The emphasis in this course is on commonly seen congenital anomalies, focal pathology, and diffuse disease of the abdomen and male pelvis. Critical thinking skills are developed through the correlation of sonographic findings with clinical history, alternate imaging modalities, and emerging technologies. Sonographic assessment during biopsies or interventional procedures will be discussed as well. The theory taught in this course will be integrated with laboratory experience in DSON 2011 through both live and simulated practice.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- ​Given an image, recognize normal abdominal anatomy in 2D and in colour, anomalous and pathologic conditions, and Doppler profiles as listed in NCP 6.1 Appendix B. Structures of interest include the adrenal gland, aorta and tributaries, biliary tract, gastrointestinal tract, inferior vena cava, liver, male pelvis, pancreas, kidneys and urinary tract, ureters, renal vasculature, spleen, renal arteries and veins.
- Given a patient history, explain how to investigate abnormal findings.
- Given an image, a​​nd clinical presentation, differentiate between acute and chronic pathological processes in the abdomen.
- Using sonographic descriptors, differentiate the sonographic features of pathological conditions to include cysts and solid masses.
- Given an image, clinical scenario or patient history, complete a written technical impression representative of the findings.
- Apply industry standards to measure organs and pathology in the abdominopelvic cavity including length and volumetric calculations.
- Given a patient history, correlate ultrasound findings with other diagnostic imaging modalities including radiography, CT, Nuclear Medicine or MRI. (5.2b)
- Given a patient scenario, relevant imaging and laboratory data, describe the role, preparation and appropriate sterile techniques expected of a Sonographer during interventional procedures.
- Given a patient scenario, apply knowledge of clinical findings and laboratory values, and describe appropriate responses to identified interventional procedure complications or contraindications.
- Given patient symptoms, clinical history, laboratory results, contraindications and sonographic findings, explain how to extend or modify a sonography examination or interventional procedure.
- Identify the role of contrast-enhanced ultrasound or other imaging modalities when assessing pathology. ​​(3.3j)
- Correlate results from laboratory tests, aspirations, and biopsies. (5.2a)
- Understand the role of the Sonographer in interventional procedures. (3.2a)​

## Course Goals
The purpose of this course is to help students;

- Describe normal, anomalous or abnormal findings on an image or cine-loop.
- Provide the most likely or a differential diagnosis in an entry-level sonographic scanning scenario.

## Course topics
Module: Title: Topic: Kawamura Reference
1: Sonographic Assessment & Correlation: Discuss key image optimization techniques, introduce technical report writing & begin correlating to alternate and previous imaging results.: Chapter 1
2: Gastrointestinal Sonography: Normal bowel appearance, inflammation, masses & obstruction.: Chapter 9
3: Midline Vessel Pathology & Variants: Aortic or caval aneurysm, dissection & occlusion.: Chapter 4
4: Pancreatic Pathology & Variants: Pancreatitis, masses & diseases.: Chapter 7
5: Liver Segmentation, Pathology & Variants: Couinaud segments, masses, hepatomegaly, fatty infiltration, hepatitis & cirrhosis.: Chapter 5
6: Biliary Pathology: Biliary or gallbladder dilation, inflammation, sludge, stones, conditions & masses.: Chapter 6
7: Urinary Pathology & Malformations: Renal or bladder variants, anomalies, masses, conditions & inflammation.: Chapters 10 & 11
8: Adrenal & Body Cavity Pathology: Intraperitoneal free fluid & ascites. Retroperitoneal fluid, collections & masses. Adrenal gland pathology.: Chapters 3 & 14
9: Splenic & Immune System Pathology: Functional & passive splenomegaly. Splenic masses. Reactive & malignant lymphadenopathy.: Chapter 8
10: Intervention & Biopsy Considerations: Benefits & complications of an ultrasound-guided fine needle or core biopsy.: Chapter 27
