### Course Description
MLSC 2314 builds on nucleic acid testing (NAT) fundamentals from MLSC 1314 and applies them to clinical molecular work. This course introduces principles, instrumentation, and quality assurance/quality control practices for nucleic acid testing (NAT) in the clinical laboratory. NAT technologies discussed in detail include singleplex and multiplex amplification by end-point PCR. Lab sessions emphasize development of proper NAT technique through manual preparation of genomic DNA samples, testing for clinically relevant DNA sequences by end-point amplification, and interpreting/reporting PCR results. Assessment is based on assignments/ lab reports, and quizzes /exams.​ ​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Prepare patient DNA samples from clinical specimens using solid-phase spin column, and magnetic bead isolation/purification techniques
- Evaluate DNA samples using methods such as fluorometry, and spectrophotometry
- Handle and store clinical specimens, patient and reagent DNA/RNA, and analytical enzymes following established nucleic acid testing (NAT) protocols
- Operate a thermocycler
- Apply molecular principles to assess and determine appropriate PCR amplification conditions
- Perform end-point PCR amplification following established quality assurance and quality control principles and procedures to minimize risk/identify presence of DNA contamination and PCR inhibitors
- Perform agarose gel DNA electrophoresis, from DNA sample loading through to the capturing of a gel image using digital gel documentation
- Identify suspect patient and control PCR results and take appropriate follow-up action as required
- Reports results that meet quality control criteria and identifies unexpected or implausible results and takes appropriate action prior to reporting
- Documents results accurately
- Practices effective verbal, non-verbal and written communication, active listening, and use of technology appropriately to facilitate communication
- Apply the principles of routine practice, utilize personal protective equipment correctly and minimize possible dangers from biological specimens, laboratory supplies and equipment
- Use disinfection and sterilization methods and minimize potential hazards related to disinfection/sterilization methods
- Apply spill containment and clean up procedures for biological and other hazardous materials
- Apply proper ergonomic principles to minimize risk of injury
