## Course Description
Sonography Clinical 1 is the first clinical course and is designed to immerse the student into the clinical environment. Students in this course gain hands-on experience in the settings of abdomen, pelvis, obstetrics, vascular, superficial, and cardiac sonography. Emphasis is placed on patient care, inter-professional collaboration, communication, and safe practice. Students will reflect on typical departmental workflow, sonographer tasks, and the role of other health care professionals in the environment. Course goals will be achieved through guided scanning practice, application of feedback, image review, and job shadowing opportunities in other departments.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- ​Demonstrate professional behaviour at all times and in accordance with the Sonography Canada code of ethics and scope of practice (2.1b).
- Demonstrate effective verbal and non-verbal communication with patients, their support persons, and other healthcare professionals (1.3b).
- Obtain and record accurate and relevant patient history based on clinical indications (1.2b).
- Implement safe practices to include appropriate patient care and own physical safety (6.2c).
- Formulate scanning strategies incorporating relevant clinical information, patient conditions, and knowledge of disease processes while applying problem-solving skills consistent with the level of experience (2.2d).
- Produce accurate and diagnostic images, in a timely manner, demonstrating findings using spatial reasoning and according to departmental protocols of structures listed in NCP 6.1 Appendices A-E.
- Identify and respond to urgent sonographic findings aligned with best practices and site protocols (2.2g).
- Formulate and present physicians with an accurate technical impression based on findings, and limitations of exam quality in a timely manner (5.4b, 5.4f, 5.4g).
- Appraise performance critically and outline goals for self-improvement (2.4a).
- Demonstrate a collaborative interprofessional approach to deliver patient-centred care, using the interprofessional education (IPE) competencies as a guide (2.3c, 2.3d).

## Attendance Requirements
Sick days: For single-option students, a maximum of 4 absences, due to illness, is permissible for the duration of the entire 26 weeks of clinical. For dual-option students, a maximum of 8 absences, due to illness, is permissible for the duration of the entire 39 weeks of clinical. A physician's note is required when 3 consecutive days are missed.

Special Leave Days: For single-option students, a maximum of 3 special leave days are available throughout the 26-week clinical practicum, whereas for dual-option students, 4 special leave days are available throughout the 39-week clinical practicum. Special leave days must meet a specific set of criteria, and advance approval is required from the BCIT Clinical Coordinator.

- Students exceeding the above limits may be required to make up clinical days after the official program end date. This decision is at the discretion of the program.
- This decision is at the discretion of the program.
- Unused sick or special leave is not considered as owing to the student.​

Punctuality: Students must be ready to commence scanning by their assigned start time.

- A pattern of late arrivals will not be tolerated and will be addressed on an individual basis. Extra clinical time may be required.

Clinical days are typically 8 hours including lunch and breaks. Clinical days will vary and may include evening and/or weekend days. Assigned days will be at the discretion of the clinical departments if not previously arranged with BCIT. Overtime may be allowed, and only with program permission.

- All absences and late arrivals must be logged in Comptracker.

## Safety equipment or protective clothing
Please note that infection control and safety procedures must be followed diligently. For example, students are expected to don all required personal protective equipment (PPE) to maintain safety in the clinical environment. This may include such items as;

- Gloves
- Gowns
- Surgical Masks
- Face Shield/Eye protection

Safety equipment will be provided to Sonography students in the clinical environment as mandated by the provincial student practice policies. Please review the specific Health Authority and clinic policies and procedures. Additional SPECO modules and policies may be a requirement of your clinical placement; please complete them as indicated.
