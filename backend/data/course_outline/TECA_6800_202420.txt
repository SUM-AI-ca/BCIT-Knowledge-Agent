## Course Description
In this second of two courses in the team project sequence, students will have further opportunity to experience the types of projects that reflect a technical artist’s day-to-day work environment. They will propose ideas for projects related to visual effects, animation, or games, which should be tools related to dynamics, user interfaces, animation, and/or modeling. Students will form interdisciplinary teams based on their skill sets and interests, and execute collaborative projects that turn their ideas into reality. Teams will plan, track, and troubleshoot each stage of the collaborative project. In this final team project course, students are expected to show advanced technical art and scripting skills. Each week teams will discuss their progress with their instructor and receive feedback through team meetings and online sessions. The instructor will sign off on each milestone as the teams present their work. At the end of the term, students’ must present their finished project to the instructor(s) and an industry panel.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Conceptualize and propose a higher level technical art project, such as a tool, application, or plug-in.
- Define the problem that the project will solve and articulate the value of the project.
- Develop a project plan to deliver the project.
- Identify roles required to complete the proposed project.
- Form an interdisciplinary team, based on the required skill sets for the proposed project.
- Work in an assigned role and deliver the required tasks and assignments to meet the project milestones.
- Develop advanced level scripts and expressions towards creating the elements of the proposed project.
- Conduct tests at different stages of development for the project.
- Regress the fixed issues after each test to ensure the issue is resolved.
- Execute multiple milestones required in the project.
- Present a final project as a team member to the instructor, peers, and an industry panel.

## Guidelines for School of Business + Media
Attendance: Regular attendance in lectures and labs is seen as integral to student success, therefore, attendance in class is monitored. Unexcused absences in excess of 10% of the time prescribed for this course may result in the assignment of a failing grade. Attendance may be taken at any time during class. A student not present for attendance will be marked absent. A student who leaves class for a period of time deemed excessive by the instructor may be considered absent regardless of whether they are present when attendance is taken.

In case of illness or other unavoidable absence, students must communicate with the instructor, or the appropriate Program Head, as soon as possible (preferably in advance) indicating the reason for the absence. Students who are seeking accommodation for a medical absence must have a BCIT-approved medical certificate ). For other absences, students must be prepared to provide appropriate supporting documentation.

A student who has unexcused absences in excess of 10% of the time prescribed for the course by the Withdrawal Deadline is considered to have ‘Vanished’ and will be assigned a grade of ‘V’. In these cases, no further work will be graded.

These requirements are set out in accordance with BCIT Policy 5101 Student Regulations. More information regarding withdrawals from Flexible Learning courses is available at Refunds and Withdrawals
