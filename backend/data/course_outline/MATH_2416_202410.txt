## Course Description
Differential calculus with emphasis on rates of change in problems concerning chemical and environmental technology. Applied maxima and minima problems. Indefinite integrals. The definite integral as a tool to find area under a curve and mean values.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Understand the meaning of the first derivative as the instantaneous rate of change of a function. Example applications:
- Discuss the concept of reaction order in chemical kinetics.
- Determine instantaneous reaction rates for various problems and compare these results to the average rate of change.
- Differentiate functions commonly encountered in chemistry including trigonometric, logarithmic and exponential functions.
- Apply the first and second derivative test in order to find local maxima and minima.
- Apply the maximum and minimum concept to problems in chemical and environmental technology.
- Understand the meaning of the indefinite integral as the inverse operation of differentiation and apply this concept to chemical and environmental problems.
- Integrate functions commonly encountered in chemistry including trigonometric, logarithmic and exponential functions.
- Understand the meaning of the definite integral as the area under a curve and the mean value of a function.
- Apply the definite integral to practical chemical and environmental problems. Example applications:
- Calculate work as related to pressure and volume in thermodynamic systems.
- Determine enthalpy and entropy when molar heat capacity is expressed as a function of absolute temperature.
- Propagate error through algebraic and transcendental formulas (calculus method). Example application:
- General analysis of experimental data and associated calculations.
