## Course Description
This university-transferable course focuses on the psychologist's approach to theories, problems and issues in contemporary psychology. This survey course introduces the student to the following areas of psychology: perspectives in psychology and current research findings on biological aspects, sensory processes, perception, consciousness, learning and memory.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Explain the psychoanalytic, learning, behavioral, cognitive, humanistic, feminist, sociocultural and biological perspectives in psychology.
- Explain and apply the essential elements of basic research methods and understand how the interpretation of findings can be limited depending on the manner in which a study is carried out (e.g., proper sampling procedures, etc.)
- Explain and apply the basic elements of descriptive statistics (e.g., use of graphs, measures of central tendency and variation) as well as those of inferential statistics.
- Explain the difference between conscious and unconscious mental processes and describe the research findings examining altered states of consciousness such as sleep and dreaming, hypnosis, and effects of psychoactive drugs.
- Identify the major structures of a neuron, major brain regions and subdivisions of the nervous and endocrine systems, and describe their roles in basic mental functions, such as maintaining conscious awareness, forming memories, experiencing emotions, and interpreting sensory information.
- Describe the major processes involved in memory with respect to encoding, storage and retrieval of information, and state the factors which have been found to facilitate or impair memory abilities.
- Explain the elements involved in various forms of learning, particularly observational learning, classical and operant conditioning, and their applications.
- Explain the role of genetics and environmental factors in influencing behaviour, traits, and abilities, with a focus on body weight and shapes, and language and intelligence acquisition.
- Explain the major processes involved in the ability to sense stimuli from various sensory organs (visual, auditory, olfactory, gustatory, and skin senses), and identify the basic processes involved in perception, with a focus on perception of pain.

## Course Goals
This course introduces modern psychology’s perspectives on the complexity of human behaviour and interaction. Course topics include: Biological basis of behaviour, sensory processes, perception, states of consciousness, nature vs. nurture, learning, and memory. Course topics in PSYC 1101 are independent of those taught in PSYC 1102.

Throughout this 15-week course, students will develop essential skills in critical thinking, reading comprehension, and research and information evaluation. By the end of the course, students will be able to discuss basic psychological theories and concepts, identify research designs used to examine psychological questions, explain basic brain and nervous system components, understand normal states of consciousness (e.g., sleeping, dreaming) and the impact of altered states (e.g., hypnosis, drugs), describe common sensory and perceptual processes, explain the complex interaction of genes and environment, and understand the basic elements of learning, memory and intelligence. Course instruction will include a combination of lectures, text-based study, and facilitated discussion.

## Course Specific Requirements
Exams (3 Exams: 18% for Exam 1, 22% for Exam 2, and 25% for Exam 3 = 65% of TOTAL MARK): will consist of multiple-choice and written-response questions. All exams will include material from the textbook, our course Learning Hub page, and from our class. Students will be responsible for all material in the readings and on the course Learning Hub page, even if it is not covered during class. Exams 2 and 3 are cumulative and will include material from previous course chapters (10% of material on Exam 2 will be cumulative; 10–15% of material on Exam #3 will be cumulative).

Exams are closed-book. Students will not be permitted to access any supplementary material, notes, or websites during the exam. Failure to take an exam at the scheduled time will result in a grade of 0 for that exam, unless a physician’s note is provided or other arrangements are made prior to the day of the exam. The instructor reserves the right to utilize an alternative assessment method (which may be different from the original) in the case of a missed exam.

Weekly Chapter Quizzes (10%): are short chapter quizzes administered via Learning Hub that are due prior to the start of class each week (excluding, exam and presentation weeks). Students will complete a total of 10 quizzes throughout the term. No late quizzes will be accepted. No re-takes will be offered. All quizzes are open-book; however, students are encouraged to attempt the quizzes without the assistance of course resources to aid in recall and prepare for exams.

Group Presentations (15%): are 10–12 minute pre-recorded presentations completed in groups of 4-5. Groups and presentation topic will be assigned by the instructor. Group presentations will submitted via Learning Hub Video Assignment (recorded on the video assignment platform or uploaded in .mp4 format) for independent viewing by the instructor and classmates. More information will be provided in class.

Presentation evaluations (5%) are an opportunity for students to demonstrate attentiveness to peer presentations and provide constructive feedback and answer discussion questions. Students are required to complete a minimum of three presentation evaluations, and are encouraged to watch more than three group presentations. Presentation evaluations that are complete, provide thoughtful feedback, and demonstrate the presentation was watched in its entirety will receive up to 1% per evaluation (for a maximum of 3% for presentation evaluations). Late presentation evaluations will not be accepted and will receive a 0. Students will also complete an evaluation of their own presentation group, reflecting upon their own contributions to the final product and the contributions of each individual group member (2%).

Class participation (5%) involves actively engaging with the course material, instructor, and classmates. Participation includes attending and actively participating in live weekly classroom sessions. Classroom sessions will include some lecture but will mostly comprise of answering student questions and having group discussions/activities.
