### Course Description
​Earth Science and Soils is an introduction to landform interpretation and soil science. Topics include the geologic cycle; rocks; landforms; soil and surficial materials classifications; soil formation; physical, chemical and biological properties of soils; forest humus; erosion; interpreting soil and terrain maps; soil mapping; soil sampling; land use impacts on soils and watersheds. This course will be taught with a blended approach of synchronous and asynchronous delivery to enhance student learning. There will be a mix of field, lab, classroom and online teaching and learning.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Carry out field description of soils for various applications (e.g., agriculture, silviculture and engineering) and in a variety of ecosystems.
- Describe dominant factors and processes involved in soil formation.
- Demonstrate soil sampling, handling methods and basic soil analyses (pH, bulk density, particle size analysis).
- Discuss soil fertility, nutrient cycling and their importance to plant growth.
- Classify organic forest floor horizons using the Forest Humus Classification System.
- Discuss soil classification in a geographic and ecological context.
- Describe the structure of the Canadian System of Soil Classification (CSSC), including the applicability of its various levels to soil science and to natural resource management.
- Describe the structure of the Unified Soil Classification system.
- Recognize and compare 4 types of rocks including 3 specific examples of each type.
- Identify common, important landforms found in British Columbia using aerial photos and site characteristics.
