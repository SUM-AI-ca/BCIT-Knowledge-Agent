## Course Description
Presents an overview of the design process for creating garden layouts and planning plans with an emphasis on student initiated projects. Of interest to homeowners and gardeners who wish to expand their design fluency and to landscape contractors wishing to expand their range of services.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
- Basic understanding of the methodology and principles of landscape design.

## Course Goals
Course Goals

This is an introductory course which provides the methodology and basic principles for residential landscape design, using a project site supplied by the student. The student applies the process and design principles in a sequential way to his/her selected site, resulting in a Preliminary Landscape Concept Plan for that site.

The student creates a measured drawing of the key existing features of the site and its’ immediate context and uses it to develop the design. The student assesses the site's opportunities and constraints; and defines a program for functional and desired uses. The student determines a preferred organization and placement of these functions as they relate to the existing features of the site. The student then investigates direction and preferences in landscape styles. From this the student applies the principles of design and form composition to create a Preliminary Landscape Concept Plan.

## Course Specific Requirements
Learning Format

In-classroom format:

- This course is offered in person, at the classroom designated.
- This course includes a Lecture component each week. It also includes Studio time exercises to explore landscape design or develop useful skills. Students are to bring tools and equipment to class each week, for work in class according to the course outline or as instructed in class.
- Examples of student assignments may be discussed each week.

Use of Learning Hub course website:

- Lecture and reference materials will be made available as handouts in class each week, and will also be posted to the course’s Learning Hub, Content tab.
- Hand in Assignments, in pdf format, under the Assignments tab. Full sized Prints may be required from time to time, additionally. Those without electronic equipment are welcome to take the class also. Discuss your situation with the instructor if you plan on preparing all work by hand.

Assignment Details

The work done in this class is cumulative: Assignments are components which builds toward the completion of the Preliminary Landscape Concept Plan, the final assignment. Most assignments have more than one part of the work. Skipping portions of assignments will impact the final plan, sometimes severely. A timely completion of assignments will greatly assist you as you proceed through the course. Instructor feedback includes suggestions for adjustments to your work, intended as guidance in shaping the final assignment.

Assignments are prepared as either measured drawings on oversized sheets, sketch drawings on smaller sheets, or images and text on letter sized sheets. Drawing may be done by hand or using CAD. Assignments are typically scanned to pdf format for submittal when due, but oversized prints may also be required from time to time.

Your Preliminary Concept Plan will be an oversize measured drawing (a given length on the drawing represents a measurement on the ground). Sheet size is commonly A1, 23 x 33 inches, or 24 x 36 inches. For those times when a print is required, check for locations and hours of an architectural plans printer near you in order to have your work printed in time for Saturday morning. Most printers are open M - F 9 to 5pm.

All measured drawings to be submitted are to have a bar scale drawn near the title block.

For Studio, BRING to each class:

- Trace paper, straight edge and pens/pencils, architectural or metric scale. We will not be drafting in class.
- In-progress work: The instructor takes time each class to review some students’ work, during studio time. Be prepared to show your in-progress work with any questions you may have. For those working on CAD using BCIT labs, save your work as a pdf or print to bring to class for discussion. Typically the instructor is available after class if you would like Office Hours.
- Note: there are only a very limited number of power outlets in the classroom. Please be prepared.
