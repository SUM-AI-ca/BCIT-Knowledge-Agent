## Course Description
Graphical linear programming with applications from chemical sciences and industry, exponential/logarithmic theory and transformations, common and natural logarithms, logarithmic and semilogarithmic graphs with application to growth and decay functions, exponential and logarithmic equations with various applications from chemical engineering, trigonometric functions of any angle, vectors, solution of triangles, graphs of trigonometric functions, trigonometric identities and equations with applications.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Determine reasonable precision and accuracy in computations involving measured quantities.
- Demonstrate unit conversions involving the Imperial System and Systeme International (SI).
- Simplify algebraic expressions and solve algebraic equations using the basic laws of algebra.
- Find the domain and range of a function and use functional notation to manipulate, combine, and evaluate functions.
- Use substitution, addition/subtraction, matrix algebra, determinants and Cramer's rule to solve linear systems in up to three unknowns.
- Perform the above by hand as well as by using Excel.
- Solve systems of equations using the following methods: substitution, elimination (Gauss), Gauss-Jordan, matrix inversion and Cramer's Rule. Example applications:
- Solve systems resulting from experimental data (e.g., analyze absorption spectra).
- Solve for interpolating polynomials (e.g., instrument calibration, data interpolation).
- Formulate and solve financial, mixture, work and flow problems.
- Solve application problems involving quadratic relationships using factoring, and the quadratic formula.
- Solve inequalities in one and two variables.
- Optimize the use of limited resources in industry through graphical linear programming techniques.
- Solve exponential and logarithmic equations.
- Formulate and solve problems using common and natural logarithms. (e.g., applications to include pH, Beer?s Law, Kinetics, Arrhenius equation, radioactive decay, exponential growth and Newton?s Law of Cooling).
- Convert exponential, power, and inverse data relationships into linear form using appropriate transformations.
- Plot data points using log-log and semi-log graph paper.
- Distinguish between exponential and power function relationships and estimate their coefficients by analyzing a set of data points.
- Solve a variety of problems in chemical science technology and industry using right angle and oblique angle trigonometry, e.g., applications to include determination of bond lengths and angles in molecules and crystals.
- Find the amplitude, period, frequency, and phase shift for sine and cosine waves.
- Graph composite sine and the cosine waves as a function of degrees, radians, or time.
- Demonstrate calculator fluency for all relevant applications.

## Course topics
Review of Basic Algebra Skills

- Precision, accuracy, significant figures and unit conversions
- Review of algebraic operation, equations and functions
- Word problems

Systems of Equations

- Solving systems of linear equations using graphical, algebraic, matrix and determinant techniques

Quadratic Equations

- Solution of quadratic equations
- Derive quadratic equations from word problems

Linear Programming

- Use linear functions and inequalities to analyse problems related to maximizing profits, minimizing costs or the use of materials with certain constraints of production.

Exponential and Logarithmic Functions

- Exponential function, exponential growth and decay problems
- Solving exponential and logarithmic equations
- Linear transformations using logarithms

Trigonometry

- Geometry review including angles, triangles (right and oblique) and the Pythagorean theorem.
- Graphing of trigonometric functions

Graphical Analysis with Logarithmic Paper

- Using log and semi-log graph paper to graph power and exponential functions.
- Use log graphing to estimate exponential and power function coefficiants
