## Course Description
​This course applies the knowledge gained in CIVL 3041 to a variety of geotechnical problems. Material is delivered through a combination of lectures, problem-solving sessions and hands-on labs. Topics include Darcy's law, one-dimensional groundwater flow, analysis of seepage and hydrostatic forces using flow nets, compaction and the Proctor test, the strength concept in soil mechanics, analysis of slope stability, evaluation of forces acting on retaining walls, and the estimation of soil bearing capacity and its effect on the design of shallow foundations.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Estimate effects of groundwater flow on structures and identify methods of failure prevention. [1, 2]
- Apply flow net analysis to estimate seepage and calculate hydraulic forces and gradients. [1, 2]
- Perform laboratory compaction tests, solve related problems and explain how this relates to field QA/QC activities. [2, 3, 8]
- Use Mohr's Circle, Coulomb's Law, and unconfined compression and direct shear tests to analyze and explain the strength concept in soil. [1, 3]
- Describe types and causes of slope failure including effects of soil properties, seepage and ground water on slope stability. [1]
- Use various soil slope analysis methods to evaluate slope stability. [1, 2]
- Apply Rankine and Coulomb theories to determine lateral earth pressure and forces. [1]
- Describe design procedures and considerations for various types of retaining structures. [4]
- Determine the stability of gravity retaining structures. [1]
- Estimate bearing capacity of a foundation soil and its effect on the design of shallow foundations. [1, 3, 4]
- Evaluate the impact of groundwater table and load eccentricity on soil bearing capacity. [1, 3]
- Explain soil hydraulic conductivity, Darcy's law, and complete related laboratory test and problem calculations. [1, 3, 5, 6]​

## Course Goals
Course Description:

This course applies the knowledge gained in CIVL 3041 to a variety of geotechnical problems. Material is delivered through a combination of lectures and problem-solving sessions. Topics include the concept of effective stress, evaluation of forces acting on retaining walls, seepage and hydrostatic forces analysis using flownets, stability analysis of slopes, and shallow foundations.

Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies:

Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Explain and solve problems using the principle of effective stress.
- Calculate forces acting on retaining structures.
- Perform basic seepage analysis.
- Determine the effects of soil properties and pore water upon slope analysis and stability.
- Calculate the factor of safety for soil slopes.
- Analyze the bearing capacity and settlement of shallow foundations.
- Assess bearing capacities using standard penetration tests.
