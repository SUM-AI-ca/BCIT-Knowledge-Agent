### Course Description
​This course examines the relationship between land surveying and law, and how this affects the land surveying profession. Topics include the Canadian legal system (statute, criminal, common and civil law); real property law (interests in land, land registry and title systems, integration, and georeferencing); boundary law (creation, retracement and re-establishment of boundaries, bornage, hierarchy of evidence, and jurisdictional boundaries, including maritime zones, offshore boundaries and the law of the sea); natural boundaries (emphasis on water boundaries, accretion, avulsion, erosion, navigability); professional practice, including the function of Canadian Board of Examiners for Professional Surveyors (CBEPS); jurisdictional associations with a focus on the Association of British Columbia Land Surveyors (ABCLS); and code of ethics, professional standards, negligence, and the disciplinary process. Students will learn these topics through case studies and a term project.​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Describe the Canadian legal system.
- Given case study scenarios, identify the application of common law, Quebec civil law, federal and provincial statutes as they affect property rights, and cadastral surveys.
- Interpret boundary evidence, including the hierarchy of evidence, original monumentation, coordinate-based cadastre, and boundary demarcation.
- Identify when limitations of action and estoppel apply.
- Distinguish between adverse possession and prescription, and the effect of statutes on both.
- Explain the principles, types, and admissibility of evidence and the role of both a witness and expert witness.
- Explain crown grants, exceptions to grants, transfers through deeds, and indefeasible title.
- Define concepts of road dedication, expropriation, rights of way, easements, and bona fide purchaser for value.
- Explain riparian and littoral rights, including the ad medium filum presumption, the doctrines of accretion and erosion, reliction, avulsion, the effect of fill, title to banks, and beds and islands.
- Describe the effect of statute and common law on riparian/littoral rights.
- In a land surveyor capacity, advise a landowner on how to resolve a boundary dispute based on knowledge of case law and appropriate statutes.
- Define the concept of the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea, including maritime zones.
- Explain the role and responsibilities of professional associations and their members.
- Describe modern and historical case law and its application to a cadastral practice.
- Explain the evolution of indigenous rights & title in Canada.
- Identify and provide solutions to complex boundary issues.

(Required Text) - Principles of Property Law (6e), Bruce Ziff, 2014. - Property Law: Cases & Commentary (3e), 2014. - Survey Law for Land Surveyors in Canada (various new chapters available 2011+). Available from CBEPS. - Survey Law in Canada - A Collection of Essays on the Laws Governing the Surveying of Lands in Canada, Carswell, 1989. - The Boundary Point (Monthly Newsletter on Property Title & Boundary Law). - The Law and Practice of Land Surveying in Alberta, (Allred, et al) ALSA, 2007. - The Scrivener, The Society of Notaries Public of British Columbia, Published 4X per year.
