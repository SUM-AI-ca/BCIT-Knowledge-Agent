## Course Description
Cardiac Sonography 1 integrates all of the basic didactic knowledge needed to create diagnostic real-time video clips (cines) and measurements for a routine 2D and M-mode echocardiographic exam. This includes detailed cardiac anatomy and imaging principles from the different scanning views, common ECG rhythms, cardiac cycle basics, as well as normal measurement values for the various structures assessed as part of a routine cardiac examination. Colour and spectral (pulse-wave) ​Doppler are introduced. Theory in this course will be integrated with laboratory practice in DSON 1012.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- ​Identify the anatomy of the heart and major vessels.
- Given an image/cine clip, correlate the relational and cross-sectional anatomy of the heart using spatial reasoning.
- Correlate results from oximetry and/or auscultation (5.2e).
- Correlate valve and chamber movement with the cardiac cycle.
- Given an ECG strip, list the components of normal sinus rhythm, and interpret, and respond to both common and life-threatening arrhythmias.
- Describe optimum system settings, transducer orientation, patient position utilized for each scan plane in a normal echocardiographic routine.
- Given an echocardiographic image/cine clip, label the scan plane & anatomy.
- Given an echocardiographic image/cine clip, differentiate artefact and normal variants from pathology in 2D and M-mode imaging.
- Describe examination protocols for structures of interest in 2D and M-mode, applying sonographic principles and instrumentation techniques as listed in Sonography Canada NCP 6.1 Appendix E. (4.2b).
- Calculate 2D and M-mode measurements.
- Calculate echo parameters and define the normal values.
- Identify normal colour and spectral (pulse-wave) Doppler patterns by integrating knowledge of anatomy, disease processes, artefacts, normal variants, pathological findings, and technical limitations.
- Analyze images and video clips to formulate an impression of findings and possible differentials.
- Describe image orientation and the roles of team members during transesophageal echocardiography. (3.2b).​

## Course Goals
This comprehensive first-year Cardiac Specialty theory course provides an in-depth exploration of cardiovascular anatomy, physiology, and hemodynamics essential for sonographic assessment of the normal heart. As such, a lot of information is covered in a relatively short period of time compared to the accompanying Cardiac Lab course DSON 1012, as learning concepts takes less time than developing psychomotor skills. Strategies that have previously helped students succeed include staying organized, learning content in a timely manner, completing post-module self-assessments, and reaching out for further clarification before starting a new module.
