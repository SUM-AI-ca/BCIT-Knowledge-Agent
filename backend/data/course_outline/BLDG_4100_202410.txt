## Course Description
Building on BLDG 3100, this second course in the progression presents an overview of the design of durable, healthy, and sustainable built enclosures. Based on the applications of fundamental principles, the content is delivered through lectures, problems-solving sessions, and lab sessions. Topics include building envelope assemblies and details, boundary conditions for building envelope analysis, diagnostic techniques, passive solar design and energy performance, and introduction to building integrated energy systems. Major activities include a design assignment and a research project. Students also participate in one or more field trips.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Identify common material components of building envelope assemblies for walls, roofs, foundations, and windows.
- Parse building envelope assemblies of material components into functional elements based on barrier concepts.
- Design interface details between common types of building envelope assemblies for functional continuity.
- Analyse the one-dimensional, steady-state hygrothermal performance of building envelope assemblies.
- Incorporate window, wall, roof, and foundation assemblies into the design of a simple building systems.
- Apply visual and other non-destructive techniques to assess building envelope performance and to diagnose deficiencies.
- Search for building science information from a variety of sources.
- Analyse objective measurements of an aspect of building system performance.
- Evaluate the passive solar performance of a simple building envelope system by first order thermal network analysis.
- Apply computer software to inform the design of building envelope systems.
- Account for characteristics of climate and human occupant behaviour in building envelope design.

## Course Goals
To provide the practical knowledge, skills, experience, and attitudes needed by technologists to participate in the design and construction of new buildings, and the diagnosis, operation, maintenance, and rehabilitation of existing buildings, to provide effective, durable, and healthy building enclosures.

Students are required to participate in a one week (40 hour) industry practicum, and to submit a log of their activities during the practicum, as part of a BLDG 4001 assignment. These reports must be submitted prior to the date of the final exam for BLDG 4100.
