## Course Description
​In this course, students learn about the educational system, technology education and the process of becoming a professional teacher. Observations in a middle or high school acquaint students with current practices in British Columbia's school system.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Demonstrate a willingness to develop personal attitudes and behaviours expected of public school teachers.
- Demonstrate an understanding of how teacher attitudes affect the quality of student learning.
- Actively participate in meaningful class discussions about how to develop appropriate personal attributes of successful and effective teachers.
- Demonstrate high quality oral communication skills.
- Demonstrate the ability to prepare to effectively teach something you know to others.
- Demonstrate the ability to act and respond appropriately when observing, supervising and teaching in a secondary school.
- Describe and demonstrate an understanding of what the law expects of teachers when interaction with students and colleagues is concerned.
- Demonstrate the ability to recognize and describe high quality instructional materials.
- Describe the general goals and objectives of Technology Education.
- Demonstrate a commitment to the development of a personal philosophy which aligns with the BC Ministry of Education's current Technology Education curriculum.
- Describe what constitutes appropriate and effective activities to enable secondary school students to gain technological skills and knowledge.
- Analyze the effectiveness and appropriateness of teaching and classroom activities observed during the school visits.

## Attendance Requirements
Regular attendance in lectures is integral to student success, and will be recorded. Students are expected to be punctual and attend class for the full duration. Late arrivals or early departures may be marked as absences. It is the student’s responsibility to obtain missed course material in a timely manner. Absence from more than 10% of the scheduled course hours may result in the issuance of a warning. If additional scheduled sessions are missed, withdrawal or course failure may be recommended.

· If a class is missed, a written make-up assignment which reflects the missed content will need to be completed.

· If you are absent for one of the school observations, you will need to make arrangements with your host teacher to observe on a day which doesn’t conflict with your TTED classes.
