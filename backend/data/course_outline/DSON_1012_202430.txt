## Course Description
Cardiac Sonography Lab 1 introduces students to sonographic imaging of the heart. The focus of this course is to integrate communication, professional responsibility, patient care, ergonomics and workplace safety with the operation of sonographic equipment in the creation and interpretation of 2D and M-mode cardiac images. Students will use the Picture Archiving and Communication System (PACS) for storage and retrieval of images.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Demonstrate effective oral, written and nonverbal communication to ensure appropriate professional relationships with patients, students and instructors.
- Demonstrate reliability and adaptability. (2.3h, 2.3i)
- Consistently demonstrate initiative, professional conduct and judgment in a respectful and cooperative way.
- Effectively maintain patient dignity, comfort and safety when performing sonographic examinations.
- Set up a 3-lead ECG and recognize components of normal sinus rhythm. (3.3 f)
- Select the optimum system and transducer for examination considering the patient's age and size, structures being examined and specific indications for examination. (4.1a)
- Set up and operate ultrasound equipment appropriately using knowledge of sonographic principles, techniques and instrumentation.
- Perform uncomplicated cardiac sonograms, selecting optimal acoustic windows, varying patient positions, and employing breathing techniques to produce diagnostic quality images, cine clips, and measurements.
- Understand the variables and their relationships within calculations, and use software calculation packages to perform cardiac m-mode and 2D sonographic measurements. (4.2g, 5.4c)
- Select the optimal acoustic window, orient and manipulate the transducer while correctly adjusting instrument controls to optimize the image.
- Understand basic troubleshooting. (4.3c)
- Participate in patient education. (2.3j)
- Assess the following structures with 2D and/or m-mode imaging, and measure on 2D and M-mode images (when applicable): cardiac position, chest and thorax, coronary vessels, hepatic veins, outflow tracts, pulmonary veins, wall layers and segments, all 4 cardiac chambers and appendages, IAS, IVS, great vessels and their branches, all cardiac valves, aortic root, MV/TV annulus, IVC and SVC. (Appendix E)
- Measure the following structures with 2D and/or M-mode imaging: outflow tracts, wall layers, all 4 cardiac chambers, and great vessels. (Appendix E)

## Course Goals
Sonography is a psychomotor skill built on hours of practicing purposeful probe manipulations, critical thinking, problem solving, as well as retention and integration of didactic knowledge. The goals of this course are to apply the theory learned in DSON 1003 with emphasis on patient care, communication, professionalism, scanning ergonomics and 2D imaging.

## Evaluation Criteria
PROFESSIONALISM: In the lab environment, the expectations are identical to those in a clinical setting. Students succeed when they: Complete Pre-Readings and the Pre-Lab Self-Assessment (PLSA) before lab sessions, and arrive on time, appropriately dressed, and prepared to participate as both the sonographer and the patient. Interact with everyone respectfully and maintain confidentiality at all times, just as they would in a clinical setting. Refer to the “BCIT Sonography Lab Professionalism” document for additional details.: 5%
LAB SUBMISSIONS: Over the course of the term, students will have a total of 4 lab submissions to complete. Each submission will be comprised of a set of diagnostic cines/ measurements for the module being practiced in the lab. Students can submit their work anytime during the weeks leading up to the SPEED DATING. The lead instructor will review their images and provide feedback as needed. Below is a list of the modules where submissions are required, and the weeks during which that 1 submission/ module can be handed in. **The penalty for late assignments is 10% per day past the due date. All extensions and penalties will be granted or enforced at the discretion of the instructor. Please reach out immediately if you think you will be missing a deadline.: 20%
Module: Weeks that submissions are accepted
Parasternal Long Axis (PLAX): 2, 3 & 4
Parasternal Short Axis (PSAX): 6 & 7
Apical Views: 9 & 10
2D measurements: 13 & 14
LAB FEEDBACK: Students are given written feedback on the Learning Hub after each guided lab practice session. Areas of STRENGTH are celebrated, and areas of GROWTH are outlined along with the modifications and strategies needed to advance the development of the psychomotor skill(s) in question.
ONLINE DISCUSSION: A discussion feed will be created in the Learning Hub for each module allowing everyone an opportunity to share ideas, tips, tricks, challenges and successes encountered while learning to create diagnostic cines & images. A minimum of one contribution is required per discussion feed, beyond which you can share as little or as much as you like.: 5%
SPEED DATING: There are a total of 3 SPEED DATING modules: PLAX, SAX, & APICAL. Each module is assessed in the lab over a 2-day period during its SPEED DATING week. Students get up to 4 in lab attempts during that week to scan and store the required Cines/ Images for a particular module. They then chose their best attempt, mark it, and submit it to the instructor to review and grade. A mark of 65% or greater is considered a pass. Students that are unsuccessful at passing a SPEED DATING for a particular module are required to do the following to bring their psychomotor scanning skills up to Level 1 standards: Submit to a minimum of 2 instructor-guided sessions. Increase independent scanning time with classmates outside of guided labs, and hand in some of the acquired cines/images from the extra sessions to the lead instructor for feedback and guidance.: 70%
To achieve a Satisfactory (S) grade in the course, students must achieve a minimum overall score of 65%.

## Attendance Requirements
Developing scanning skills requires time and independent practice of techniques learned during guided lab sessions.

Regular attendance and punctuality are essential, as students practice on each other.

If a student cannot attend due to foreseeable circumstances, they must notify the Lab Lead Instructor and arrange for a suitable substitute.

Students are responsible for catching up on any missed content and compensating for lost practice time.

## Course Specific Requirements
Students must act professionally, with integrity as well as dress appropriately while in the sonography laboratory both for safety reasons as well as practice for what is to come in clinical

## Safety equipment or protective clothing
The required dress code for the lab is BCIT Sonography scrubs, a patient gown, two hand towels for modesty and arm support, and closed-toe/heel shoes. Long hair tied back, beards and nails short and neatly trimmed, makeup appropriate for the setting, and no scents or perfume of any kind.

Students will need to purchase a box of gloves for personal use over the term.

***No food is permitted in the lab. Water bottles are permitted but must be kept away from the ultrasound machines.
