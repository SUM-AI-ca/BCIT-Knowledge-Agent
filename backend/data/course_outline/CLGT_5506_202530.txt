## Course Description
A comprehensive introduction to diagnostic molecular techniques for constitutional genetic disease and cancer. This course will include foundational training in the basics of lab safety and human DNA preparation from various sources (blood, cell cultures, and solid tissue). In-class lectures, online resources and hands-on laboratory components of this course will also be focused on techniques such as the polymerase chain reaction (PCR) amplification, gel and capillary electrophoresis, and Sanger sequencing . Students will be introduced to specialty software used for the analysis, interpretation and validation of these assays. The theory behind Next Generation Sequencing (NGS) as a method for detection of pathogenic mutations will also be introduced.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
By the end of this course, learners will be able to:

- Work safely in the laboratory while performing molecular diagnostic assays.
- Describe the transport of dangerous goods regulations relevant for Clinical Genetics Technology.
- Document and prepare specimens for analysis by performing several nucleic acid isolation methods and describing their respective advantages and limitations.
- Use techniques such as PCR amplification, restriction endonuclease digestion, and capillary electrophoresis to diagnose normal, affected, and carrier status in terms of genetic disease.
- Describe genetic diseases such as Hereditary Hemochromatosis and cancer in terms of mode of inheritance, population frequency, symptoms, and affected gene(s).
- Describe basic molecular diagnostic concepts for the constitutional and cancer lab, such as standard operating procedure, accessioning and Mendelian and neoplastic disorders.
- Explain the principles of Sanger and Next Generation sequencing methods.
- Perform and analyze data from Sanger sequencing reactions.
- Perform and document quality control on reagents and media.
- Describe gene mutations using Human Genome Variation Society nomenclature.
- Troubleshoot and solve common technical problems encountered while performing molecular techniques.
- Recognizes CCMG guidelines for specimen and record retention.
- Communicate effectively with the instructor and other students.
- Behave in a professional manner in lecture and lab sessions.
- Demonstrates inventory control skills.

## Course Schedule and Assignments
- Laboratory

Three written technical reports must be submitted in partial fulfillment of the course requirements. These reports — Lab Exercises 1 through 3 (see accompanying schedule) — are based on assays performed and data and results collected during the laboratory sessions. A full description of how these technical reports should be formatted and content they should include will be provided upon commencement of the course.

- Assignments

There will be three take home assignments that must be completed by the given due date. No exceptions are given for assignments that are handed in after the due date and all late assignments will be given a mark of zero. Exceptions may be made for medical reasons or extenuating circumstances. All assignments will be submitted online via the student via the bcit.learn.ca website.

- Quizzes

The closed book 1 hour quizzes will evaluate student progress to date and consists of multiple choice, short answer questions and data interpretation.

- Final Exam

The closed book final exam will be 3 hours long and take place during Exam Week. This will be similar in format to the quiz consisting of multiple choice, problem solving and data interpretation.

- Lab checklist

The labs in this course will strive to mimic the clinical lab environment. There will be a lab checklist for students to complete as a review of their lab skills. Students will be given a marking rubric in the first week of class to explain instructor expectations.

- Use of generative AI tools (eg ChatGPT, etc.)

Generative AI (GenAI) tools may be used in a limited way for completing assignments for this course, according to the following guidelines. It is the student’s responsibility to ensure the quality and accuracy of any GenAI output prior to submitting assignments to the instructor. Note that analysis of lab data is an important part of learning and using GenAI for data analysis does not build adequate skills and understanding. Therefore, use of GenAI for data analysis is strongly discouraged. Any use of GenAI for completion of exams and quizzes is prohibited.

If you use a GenAI tool for any course work, such as assignments, you must acknowledge that you have used it. Follow these guidelines:

- Cite and number each use, like this [1, ChatGPT, (date)]
- Mark all generative AI output, by using “quotes” for using unedited genAI output, and ‘quotes’ for using paraphrased genAI output
- For all generative AI content, student must include primary source verification i.e. original research journal articles or chapters from traditionally published textbooks.
- Undeclared use of GenAI is a violation of academic integrity as it presents work or ideas created by tools or other people as your own. Failure to fully declare the use of GenAI will be considered a violation of BCIT academic policy.
