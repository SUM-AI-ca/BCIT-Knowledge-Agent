## Course Description
As students preparing to enter Technology programs at BCIT, you can look forward to a rewarding but demanding two years of study followed by a prosperous and challenging career. The goal of this course is to prepare you for success at BCIT: to give you the writing, reading, speaking, and listening skills you will need to enter a full-time program at BCIT. Technology Entry [TE] students who pass COMM 0007 with at least 70% meet BCIT’s minimum oral language skill requirement. TE students continuing to follow up programs that require higher oral language requirements need to complete appropriate oral language assessments prior to that program’s acceptance.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
## Writing Skills
When you have successfully completed this course, you should be able to:

- Identify your reader and purpose before you write.
- Organize ideas using pre-writing techniques.
- Use a standard format for letters and memos.
- Write subject lines for your letters, memos, and emails.
- Write routine letters and memos.
- Write in a technical style.
- Use headings and lists in your documents.
- Write sentences that are clear, concise, and grammatically correct.
- Write effective, well-organized effective paragraphs.
- Identify and correct spelling, grammar, and punctuation errors.
- Use MS-WORD to write, edit, and revise documents.
- Prepare a list of questions for instructors and Program Head of your future program for Tech Day.
- Use your communication skills to complete projects assigned in all your Technology Entry courses.

## Speaking Skills
When you have successfully completed this course, you should be able to:

- Participate effectively and confidently in small group discussions and group meetings
- Effectively obtain information from students, instructors, and Program Head on your future program during Tech Day activities.
- Exhibit appropriate language choices, including register (level of formality) and tone for academic and workplace situations.
- Summarize and paraphrase ideas from texts or handouts to the class.
- Plan, organize, and deliver an effective oral report on a technology-related topic.
- Express ideas and opinions clearly, coherently, and logically using Standard English and avoiding idioms.
- Ask for feedback and give effective oral feedback.

## Reading and Study Skills
When you have successfully completed this course, you should be able to:

- Effectively use BCIT library facilities for research.
- Use strategies to manage time effectively.
- Effectively review your notes, handouts, and texts to prepare for exams.
- Follow written instructions for classroom activities, assignments, and exams.
- Research Internet and other sources to prepare oral report.
- Use your reading and study skills in your BCIT courses.
- Give tactful written feedback on group oral reports.

## Listening Skills:
When you have successfully completed this course, you should be able to:

- Follow oral instructions for classroom activities and assignments.
- Listen to oral reports of your classmates in order to obtain information, ask questions, and provide written feedback.
- Listen to lectures in order to take effective notes.

Professionalism & Participation

Your evaluation in this course includes a 7.25% mark for professionalism, which is based on industry’s expectations for BCIT graduates. Your classroom is your workplace while you attend BCIT. To achievet a full 7.25% for professionalism, you will

- complete all lab assignments during the scheduled lab time
- submit all homework, in-class activities, discussion posts, marked assignments etc. on time or obtain an extension before deadlines (as you would at work)
- attend all lectures and labs on time, or email your instructor before your absence (if possible) the same way you would communicate with an employer when you cannot come in to work.
- treat each other and your instructors with courtesy and consideration
- use appropriate language and behave professionally and collegially
- contribute to the success of your course by preparing for and participating actively in every class
- expect respect from and show respect for others.

## Other information
Information for Students

Assignments: Lab attendance is mandatory. Your lab assignments must be done on an individual basis. All lab assignments must be word processed and submitted as WORD documents unless otherwise advised in class. All lab assignments are due at the end of the lab in which they are assigned. If you miss a lab assignment, you will receive a grade of zero. Lab work done outside of class time will not be accepted. Rewrites are not allowed for graded assignments in this course. Marks will be deducted for late assignments, except in exceptional circumstances, such as illness.

Missing Classes/Graded Work: If you miss a lab assignment, test, exam or quiz, you will receive zero marks. Exceptions are made for documented medical reasons or extenuating circumstances. In such a case, it is the responsibility of the student to inform the instructor immediately. See info below to learn more about student rights and responsibilities.

Ethics and Integrity: BCIT assumes that all students attending the Institute will follow a high standard of ethics. First, employ the Golden Rule: treat others as you would like to be treated. Second, Incidents of cheating or plagiarism may result in a grade of zero for the assignment, quiz, test, exam, or project for all parties involved and/or expulsion from the course. You must lift your own weights in the gym to develop your strength; the same applies when learning to plan, draft, and revise writing for business applications. Your reputation is golden—guard it well.

Note: Generative AI writing aids and associated methodologies will be discussed and integrated where appropriate—use them, do not abuse them. When the use of AI tools is permitted, I’ll indicate that in the assignment instructions. You are responsible for ensuring that the content you submit under your name adheres to the course’s expectations. You must also acknowledge use of AI tools at the top or bottom of your assignment, clearly; e.g. I acknowledge the use of [name the tool] in the preparation of my assignment. Using AI tools without authorization is a violation of BCIT’s Student Code of Academic Integrity. I will help you develop your skill and discernment to use chatbots effectively—avoid dependency, maintain your integrity, and build self-confidence by developing your own abilities.

Attendance Policy and Procedure (from the Tech Entry Student Handbook): Your classroom at BCIT is your workplace. As in any other workplace, you’re expected to show up on time for scheduled in-person and synchronous online classes and attend regularly.

Participation: Communication classes are interactive, which means that you are an important participant in your education. You and your classmates, with your wide range of backgrounds, talents, and knowledge, will contribute to the richness of your education. In addition, as you work with other students in small groups, you will be gaining valuable teamwork skills. To participate effectively, you will need to be prepared for each class; manage your time effectively, both in and out of class; read and study the course materials; understand and follow all instructions and course procedures; ensure you have the necessary course prerequisites; and speak to me about any course-related problems you have.

Student Rights and Responsibilities: Please read the section on student rights and responsibilities in your TE Student Handbook.

Punctuality means being respectful of other people’s time, being punctual and on time for class, appointments, and for group meetings.

You are responsible for regular attendance in lectures, seminars, and laboratory periods. BCIT provides reasonable accommodation and makes allowances when you are ill, or otherwise have good reason to be absent from classes.

If your absence causes you to miss graded work, such as a lab, test, quiz, or assignment, supporting documentation, such as a doctor’s note, substantiating the reason for absence may be required. Check your TE Student Handbook to learn more about who you need to contact if you miss graded work.

Standards: Your grades will be based on how well you apply the writing and speaking techniques we’ve discussed and practised in class. The standard used to measure how well you apply these techniques is based on industry’s expectations of BCIT graduates.

Your final mark for this course includes a 5% grade for professionalism and teamwork. This grade is also based on industry’s expectations of BCIT graduates. Use your lab and lecture time wisely.

Teaching Methods:

Your instructor will be using the methods that have proven most effective for teaching communication to students at BCIT:

Types of classes. This term the course will be both in-person and online, in accordance with the hybrid delivery system for all of Tech Entry.

Collaborative Learning and Teamwork: Just as in your other courses at BCIT, you will often be working with others in small groups in the classroom. This will allow you to develop the interpersonal skills you need to succeed in the workplace. You will also learn more in teams as you teach and support each other.

Peer Editing: You’ll work with partners to locate, discuss, and correct errors in each other’s work.

Oral Work: You will be reporting orally to your groups, to the whole class, and to your instructor throughout the course.

Critical Thinking: For each of your assignments in this course, you will be required to practise one or more of the following critical thinking skills:

- Defining your purpose for the communication task, understanding your target-reader, and identifying your assumptions and point of view.
- Deciding what to do or say and evaluating the consequences of your message and tone.

Ground Rules for the Classroom:

BCIT and its management, together with the unions on campus and your Student Association, are committed to providing an environment where the individual differences of all students and employees are valued and respected – an environment free from harassment and discrimination. We are all here to work cooperatively and to learn together.

I am offended by remarks that stereotype people as members of groups and express prejudices against them. I will object to any such remarks that I hear in class, and you are welcome to do the same. If someone objects to a remark you have made, please clarify what you said (if you were misinterpreted) and apologize.

Here are some guidelines for ensuring that your classroom learning environment creates a sense of community of pride and respect --where everyone can learn. You are welcome to offer input or suggestions. I want all of you to be successful in this course.

- Criticize points of view, opinions, statements, and behaviour. Do NOT criticize people. We are all here to learn.
- Do NOT use coarse language in the classroom.Build your professional demeanor.
- Come to all classes, be on time, and stay until the end of the class. If you have to miss a class for any reason, you are responsible for the material covered, announcements made in your absence, and for getting any materials that were distributed in class.
- To ensure that all students can stay focused on the class lecture/discussion, only one person at a time should be speaking.
- Do not take/make personal calls, scroll, post (etc. etc.) or use your cell phone for purposes unrelated to classroom work, as this distracts you and others from the tasks at hand.

Meaning of Grades:

BCIT is noted for its high standards – that’s why you’re here. These high standards are your guarantee that employers will respect your diploma and your grades and will want to hire you. As an instructor at BCIT, I am responsible for assessing your work fairly and accurately according to the standards set by the Institute and by my profession. As a student here, you’re responsible for doing your best work to meet those standards.

For students, the grade on an assignment is sometimes disappointing. For instructors, determining the appropriate grade is not an easy job. The instructor must read each assignment with extreme care and concentration. The grade itself is a judgement made by the instructor that is based on years of acquired expertise and experience. Assignments are seldom all good or all bad; the virtues of an assignment must be balanced against its defects to determine the final grade.

## BCIT Policy
Attempts: As stated in Policy 5103, Evaluation of Students, students must successfully complete a course within a maximum of three (3) attempts at the course. Students with two attempts in a single course will be allowed to repeat the course only upon permission from the Associate Dean. For those courses or programs that have Education Council approval, the number of attempts as stated in the evaluation section of the course outline shall apply.

Any student who needs special assistance in the event of a medical emergency or building evacuation (either because of a disability or for any other reason) should promptly inform their course instructor(s) and Accessibility Services of their personal circumstances.
