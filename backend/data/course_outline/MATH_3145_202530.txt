### Course Description
A second course in statistics, this course builds on the introductory statistics concepts from MATH 2145. This course includes confidence intervals and hypothesis testing for the mean and proportions (from one or more populations); linear regression and correlation techniques; and some experimental design concepts. These methods are applied to examples chosen from the Forest and Natural Areas Management field. Sampling and experimental design will be introduced and discussed as applicable to the students’ technical projects (for FNAM 3231).
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Estimate, with confidence intervals, population parameters (mean of one population, difference between the means of two populations, proportion from one population, difference in proportions for two populations).
- Formulate and test hypotheses for means, proportions, differences between means (assuming equal variances), and differences between proportions.
- Perform chi-squared tests for goodness-of-fit and independence (using contingency tables).
- Perform one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) to test for differences in means between two or more populations.
- Compute and interpret correlation coefficients from a set of data.
- Apply linear regression techniques: compute and interpret linear regression coefficients, and test for a significant relationship between two variables based on a set of data.
- Explain the meaning and importance of the underlying assumptions behind the statistical procedures commonly used in the Sustainable Resource Management field.
- Apply concepts in this course to develop an appropriate sampling or experimental design for the technical project. Determine appropriate hypotheses and corresponding analysis techniques to be used for the technical project.
