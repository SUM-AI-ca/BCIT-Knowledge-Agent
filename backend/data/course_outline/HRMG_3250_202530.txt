### Course Description
This course provides an in-depth study of union-employer relations and the collective bargaining process and equips students with the knowledge and skills to manage unionized workplaces effectively. It examines how unions, as employee representatives, and employers interact to establish terms and conditions of employment, focusing on the negotiation, agreement, and administration of these terms. By exploring the origins, nature, and evolving dynamics of union-employer relationships, students will gain practical insights into strategic approaches for managing unionized settings. Emphasizing legal frameworks, analytical tools, and inclusive practices, the course prepares students to navigate labour relations complexities, manage effectively within unionized workplaces, and develop strategies that support organizational success. ​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Distinguish between employee relations (direct employer-employee relationship) and labour relations (the ongoing relationship with a unionized employee group).
- Articulate the statutory provisions that regulate employment in unionized workplaces, noting the differences between federal and provincial jurisdictions.
- Explain why employees seek union representation, the role of unions, and union impact on organizations.
- Analyze union-employer emergent relationship patterns and their implications for effective workplaces.
- Describe the collective bargaining cycle.
- Apply strategies for collective bargaining preparation.
- Apply strategies to manage disputes during the term of a collective agreement through the grievance procedure.
- Describe strategies to manage disputes during collective bargaining.
- Align human resource management strategies with organizational goals in unionized workplaces, using data analytics to inform decisions.
- Describe how to foster effective union-management collaboration to enhance workplace relations and organizational effectiveness.
- Manage complex labour relations issues, focusing on ethics, communication, and employee voice.

### Course topics
Additional Course Information
This course will introduce students to the concepts of industrial relations and labour relations with an emphasis on Canada and British Columbia. The historical context, processes, activities, issues, and other fundamentals of industrial relations and labour relations will be studied within conflict management and employee voice frameworks. The legal framework of labour-management relations will be examined in addition to the topics of trade unions, management, collective bargaining, collective agreements, collective agreement administration, and grievance and arbitration processes. Additional topics will include labour-management collaboration and the nonunion workplace. Emphasis will be placed on how employers and unions can effectively manage their legal duties and obligations and their workplace relationships with one another. Conceptual frameworks for the course content may include one or more of the following: (1) Albert O. Hirschman’s Exit-Voice-Loyalty Model, (2) the Pluralist Approach and the Unitarist Approach, and (3) the Confrontation Approach and the Collaboration Approach as aids in analysis and decision making in industrial relations, labour relations, employment relations, and human resource management.
