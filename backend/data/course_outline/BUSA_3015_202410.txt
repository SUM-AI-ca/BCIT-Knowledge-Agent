## Course Description
Designed to provide participants with a conceptual framework for preparing and conducting negotiations and to give them hands-on experience in using this framework. After analyzing the negotiation concepts, the major focus of the course will be on negotiation exercises designed to highlight a range of important issues about the negotiation process. This course will be relevant to entrepreneurs, managers, lawyers, human and labour relations practitioners and individuals in government. Excellent supplement to either Certificate program.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
By the end of the course, the student will:

- Improve skills in negotiation and joint problem solving and decision making.
- Be able to analyze negotiations.
- Be able to interpret Past/future negotiating encounters.
- Gain understanding of negotiation type and effectiveness as a negotiator.

## Learning Resources
Required: Roger Fisher, William Ury, et al.Getting to YES, Negotiating Agreement Without Giving In, 3rd Edition. Available at the Bookstore or at Chapters.

## Course Goals
The primary purpose of this course is to provide students with an understanding of the requirements for success as a negotiator. This course will introduce students to the necessary skills to negotiate effectively. The course will also strengthen your ability to listen, ask questions, deal with conflict, problem solve and negotiate effectively. Students will become very familiar with a systematic approach to negotiation. By the end of the course, students should become much better able to analyze negotiations and to interpret past and future negotiating encounters. Valuable experience will be gained from all the negotiation exercises. Students will learn a great deal about themselves from repeated exposure to situations that involve a shifting mix of cooperation and competition. Through formal lectures, role-playing, personal assessment and discussion, students will gain an understanding of their negotiating type and their effectiveness as a negotiator. Students will develop a more reflective, analytical, effective style with respect to becoming a good negotiator.
