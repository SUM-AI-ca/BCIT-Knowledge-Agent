## Course Description
The second of two consecutive courses that cover human anatomy and physiology of relevance to the study and practice of radiation therapy. This course consists of a comprehensive study of the following systems: the cardiovascular system, the lymphatic system, the respiratory system, the digestive system, the urinary system, the female reproductive system and the male reproductive system. This course supplements typical considerations of anatomical structure and function with explorations of relational and sectional anatomy to prepare for the diverse imaging modalities encountered in clinical practice.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Describe the anatomy and physiology as well as the relevant relational and sectional anatomy of the following organ systems:
- Cardiovascular system
- Lymphatic system
- Respiratory system
- Digestive system
- Urinary system
- Female reproductive system
- Male reproductive system
- Gain basic proficiency in the study and use of sectional anatomy, including how to visualize 2D sections of the body along the three cardinal body planes, and how to view and interpret 2D sections of the body that are represented using various imaging modalities (ex. CT, MRI).
- Relate and apply an understanding of anatomical and physiological principles to radiation therapy practice.

## Course topics
I. CARDIOVASCULAR SYSTEM

- ​Anatomy of the chambers of the heart, layers of the heart wall, pericardial serous membranes
- Anatomy of the coronary circulation
- Cardiac muscle physiology
- Whole heart physiology (conduction pathways, ECG, cardiac cycle)
- Histology of blood vessels
- Anatomy of the blood - components of the plasma, blood cells
- Hemostasis
- Hemodynamics

II. LYMPHATIC SYSTEM

- Lymph and lymph vessels
- Anatomy of lymphatic system organs: Thymus, lymph node, spleen, tonsils Distribution of lymph nodes - pathways of lymph drainage
- Thymus, lymph node, spleen, tonsils
- Distribution of lymph nodes - pathways of lymph drainage

III. RESPIRATORY SYSTEM

- Anatomy of the structures of the upper and lower respiratory tracts, lungs and pleural serous membranes
- Physiology of ventilation
- Gas exchange and gas transport in the blood

IV. DIGESTIVE SYSTEM

- Anatomy of the structures of the gastrointestinal tract
- Anatomy of the accessory organs of digestion
- Intraperitoneal/retroperitoneal organs, mesenteries, parietal and visceral peritoneum
- Physiology of GI motility, digestion (mechanical and chemical) and absorption

V. URINARY SYSTEM

- Anatomy of the structures of the urinary system - kidneys, nephron, renal circulation, urinary tract
- Physiology of urine formation (filtration, reabsorption and secretion in the nephron)
- Hormonal control of urine volume
- Control of H+
- Physiology of micturition

VI. FEMALE REPRODUCTIVE SYSTEM

- Bony pelvis and pelvic floor (pelvic diaphragm)
- Anatomy of the female reproductive organs: Breast, ovaries, oviducts, uterus, cervix, vagina
- Breast, ovaries, oviducts, uterus, cervix, vagina
- Supporting structures for the female reproductive organs

VII. MALE REPRODUCTIVE SYSTEM

- Anatomy of the male reproductive organs Testes, spermatic cord, accessory male sex glands
- Testes, spermatic cord, accessory male sex glands
- Inguinal canal and hernias
- Comparison of male and female perineal structures
