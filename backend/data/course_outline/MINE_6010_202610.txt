### Course Description
The course introduce students to the more complex system design tasks, which span disciplines and involve many areas of engineering science and design. This course expands and diversifies on the knowledge acquired during MINE 5000 - Surface Mine Design. Through a series of workshops using selected packages of mining software in current usage, the students will learn and implement different techniques regarding the design, optimization, planning, scheduling, simulation and project management of surface mines. Exercises (and assignments) will include manipulating topographical and geological data, developing a geological database, solids modeling of an ore body shape, mineral resource estimation using sectional and block modeling techniques, open pit design and optimization.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Describe how the use of computer software applications leads to improved efficiency in mine design and operate the software application Dassault Systèmes Geovia SURPAC TM [5].
- Perform all the preliminary tasks/operations leading to generating the block model [3].
- Describe basic block modeling techniques, understand and manipulate all the input requirements [2,3].
- Create a computer block model for different types of ore bodies [2].
- Design a 3D open pit mine [4].
- Design a waste dump development [4].
- Perform a mine road design for given topographic and mining activities [4].
- Generate optimized pit shells while considering physical characteristics of a mineral deposit, cost/price scenarios, and other operational constraints [4].
- Evaluate the economic viability and determine the strategic direction for a surface mine (optimized cut-off grade; mining areas and extraction sequence, push backs, etc.) using GEOVIA Whittle™ software and re-evaluate the mine design in response to changing condition for a mine site [4,11].
- Discuss the distinctions between the results of computer simulation and the realities of operations on the mine site [10].

### Course Goals
1. Develop Proficiency in Strategic Mine Planning :
- Gain hands-on experience using Whittle for strategic mine planning, including pit optimization and strategic scheduling.
- Understand the principles and methodologies behind optimal mining limit determination.
- Develop an understanding of Ore/Waste determination through Cut-Off Grade calculation and application.
2. Enhance CAD-Based Mine Design Skills :
- Learn to use Deswik for detailed mine design, including pit layout, ramp design, and waste dump planning.
- Apply CAD tools to create practical and efficient mine design workflows.
3. Master Production Scheduling Techniques :
- Utilize Minesched or Deswik.Sched for tactical production scheduling and resource allocation.
- Integrate Minesched or Deswik.Sched with supporting software like Surpac and/or Deswik.CAD to streamline scheduling workflows.
4. Integrate Software for Comprehensive Mine Planning :
- Develop the ability to combine outputs from multiple software tools (Whittle, Deswik, Minesched, and Surpac) to support cohesive and effective mine planning.
5. Apply Knowledge to Real-World Scenarios :
- Work on practical exercises and case studies to simulate real-world mining operations and decision-making processes.

### Course topics
Week 1: Course Introduction and Surface Mine Optimization Basics
- Overview of course structure, learning outcomes, and assessments.
- Recap of resource block models and block model attributes.
- Introduction to surface mine optimization concepts.
- Practical session: Introduction to Deswik (Interface and Basic Manipulation).
Week 2: Strategic Mine Planning and Decision-Making
- Key principles of strategic mine planning and levels of mine planning.
- Fundamental data inputs preparation and Block value calculation concepts.
- Decision-making in mining: risk, uncertainty, and trade-offs.
- Practical session: Deswik CAD & Block Model Functions.
Week 3: Lerchs-Grossman Algorithm and Optimization Techniques
- Introduction to the Lerchs-Grossman (LG) algorithm: theory and applications.
- Optimization techniques, inputs, and scenario analysis in Whittle.
- Practical session: Whittle Setup and Running Scenarios
Week 4: Pushbacks, Production Scheduling, and Final Applications
- Optimal/Ultimate Pit Shell Selection Techniques.
- Pushback design and minimum mining width constraints.
- Creating strategic production schedules in Whittle.
- Hands-on workshop: Whittle Optimal Pitshell selection, pushbacks and strategic schedules.
- Quiz #1
Week 5: Deswik and General Design Tools
- Introduction to importing Whittle results into Deswik.
- Practical session: Setting up Deswik projects and importing optimization results.
- Hands-on practice with general design tools in Deswik (navigation, layer management, and basic CAD).
Week 6: Pit and Pushbacks Design
- Practical session: Deswik Comprehensive pit design using imported Whittle results.
- Application of mine design best practices to pitshell design.
Week 7: Waste Rock Dump and Engineered Stockpile Design
- Waste rock dump and stockpile design principles.
- Key considerations for stability, environmental compliance, and operational efficiency.
- Practical session: Designing waste rock dumps and engineered stockpiles in Deswik.
Week 8: Haulage Road Design
- Key principles of haulage road design, including safety, efficiency, and durability.
- Discussion on alignment, gradient, and drainage considerations.
- Practical session: Designing haulage roads in Deswik, integrating with pits and dumps.
- Application of design standards and best practices for haul roads.
Week 9: Design Surface and Solids Creation
- Creating design surfaces and phase solids in Deswik.
- Practical session: Building and refining design surfaces to create solids for different mining phases.
Week 11: Preparing Block Model and Mine Design Data for Scheduling
- Overview of block model and mine design data requirements for scheduling.
- Practical session: Preparing block models in Deswik for scheduling.
- Linking mine design data (pitshells, pushbacks, and surfaces) to scheduling inputs.
- Quality control and validation of data for accurate scheduling results.
- Quiz #2
Week 12: Schedule Setup and Production Resource Configuration
- Introduction to Minesched or Deswik.Sched and its scheduling capabilities.
- Practical session: Setting up a schedule in the chose scheduler, including defining project settings and timelines.
- Configuring production resources: equipment, material flow, and constraints.
- Hands-on exercise: Aligning production targets with resource availability.
Week 13: Running Production Schedule and Exporting Results
- Running a production schedule to simulate the entire mine life.
- Analyzing scheduling outputs and validating results against design constraints.
- Practical session: Exporting schedules to Excel for reporting and further analysis.
- Mineral Reserves reporting according to CIM
Week 14 - 15: Final Project and Integration
- Practical session: Finalizing a complete mine design project, integrating pitshells, pushbacks, dumps, stockpiles, and haul roads.
- Student presentations of their designs with feedback from the instructor.
- Course wrap-up: Summary of key takeaways and discussion of real-world applications.
- Quiz #3
