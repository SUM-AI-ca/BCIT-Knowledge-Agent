## Course Description
This course provides an introduction to earth processes and materials from a civil engineering perspective. Topics include plate tectonics, mineral and rock formation, geological time, rocks structures including faults, surficial processes (glaciers, river, groundwater and shoreline systems), and earthquake causes, wave propagation, magnitudes and intensities. Engineering design implications of geological features such rock structures, surficial material and earthquakes will be discussed.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Discuss the characteristics, origin and form of igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic rocks. [1]
- Describe and classify common minerals and rocks in the laboratory. [1]
- Discriminate between absolute and relative geological time. [1, 7, 9]
- Outline the features of common rock deformation structures such as joints, fractures, faults, and folds. [1]
- Describe the importance of rock structures in engineering design. [1]
- Describe the formation of mineral, coal and oil and gas deposits. [1]
- Explain surficial geological processes (glaciers, river, groundwater and shoreline systems) and the resulting landforms. [1]
- Outline engineering design considerations for different types of surficial material. [1, 7, 9]
- Perform basic identification of geologic structures from aerial photographs. [1]
- Describe the causes of earthquakes (especially in southwestern BC) and classify earthquake intensities and magnitudes. [1]
- Explain how earthquake waves propagate through rock and soil. [1]
- Outline the effects that earthquakes have on the land, on engineered structures and on societies. [1, 7, 9]
