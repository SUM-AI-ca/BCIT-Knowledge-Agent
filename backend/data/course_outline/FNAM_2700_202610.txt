### Course Description
​At nearly 950,000 km2, British Columbia encompasses a diverse range of terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems, each supporting an array of fish, wildlife, and other living organisms. This course explores the practice of ecosystem stewardship and management in BC, providing students with the knowledge and skills needed to interpret and apply best management practices to fish and wildlife habitat for the sustainability of current and future generations. Students will learn practical skills including wildlife species identification, wildlife habitat characteristics, species at risk recovery strategies, and fish-stream classification and management. Students will investigate how frameworks such as provincial legislation, federal legislation, and joint First Nation management agreements guide the management of these areas, including strategies for conservation and sustainability.​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Identify important native fish & wildlife and describe their habitat requirements.
- Evaluate how legislation, policies, and regulations influence management decisions related to fish, wildlife, and their habitat in BC.
- Examine the role of human activities in shaping BC's ecosystems, including alterations to habitats that affect fish, wildlife, and species at risk.
- Apply field measurement techniques and relevant legislation to classify and describe fish-streams in a forestry context.
- Examine various management tools and perspectives of fish and wildlife stewardship, including Indigenous perspectives.​
