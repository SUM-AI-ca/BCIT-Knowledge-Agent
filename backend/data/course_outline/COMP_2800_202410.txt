## Course Description
Students work in teams of four or five to complete an advanced mobile-friendly web application in a real-world domain. Teams will proceed through the majority of the software development lifecycle, from requirements gathering to specification, UI/UX design, to implementation and delivery. The course is focused on developing teamwork and project management skills as well as an understanding of the development lifecycle. The project will simultaneously allow students to apply their previously-developed technical knowledge. Prerequistes: COMP 1510, COMP 1537, COMP 1800 Co-requisite: COMP 2537

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Determine and document project requirements.
- Plan and schedule project work tasks in agile manner.
- Work effectively with a team to meet stated tasks and milestones.
- Design a system that meets the documented requirements.
- Implement a system that has been designed.
- Test and deliver a working system.

*Group marks are subject to adjustment based on individual contribution as evaluated by peer reviews and instructor assessments.

**After the end of week 2, an interim checkpoint will be calculated and provided to students. This interim checkpoint will show students how their grade would be adjusted in future checkpoints. The interim checkpoint will follow the same process as an actual checkpoint (using peer evaluations and instructor assessments), however, no adjustment will be applied for the interim checkpoint. The purpose of the interim checkpoint is to give students early feedback on their individual contribution within their group and to show how grades will be adjusted for the midpoint and final checkpoints.

Example 1: A student who did not contribute as much as their teammates as observed by their peers and instructor and is rated .90 from peers, and 0.85 from instructor. Their team mark of 93% would be adjusted down to 84%.

For more examples of adjustments and possible scenarios see this link: Team Adjustments and Scenarios

Students are encouraged to apply advanced Web Development skills learnt in the partner course (COMP 2537) into the COMP 2800 project.

- Grades for each Checkpoint are assigned by the Faculty Supervisor.
- Group marks may be scaled for individuals, at mid and final checkpoints, based on the individual’s teamwork contribution, project contribution, and professional behaviour.
- An individual’s contribution is determined by Faculty Supervisor observations and Peer Feedback.
- Students who do not participate equally in group projects could have their course marks adjusted downward to the point of failing.
- Students who do not contribute significantly to all aspects of the project will receive a failing grade.
- Students who do not contribute to and participate in all project iterations will receive a failing grade.
- Students who do not participate in a meaningful, non-trivial way in the Final Presentation will receive a failing grade.
- Teams who do not achieve a minimum viable product (MVP) may receive a failing grade.
- Teams who have not handed-off their work to their Client in an appropriate manner may receive a failing grade.
- Final Course Grades are based on the following rubric: 100% Exceptional (Above and beyond expectations; Exemplary quality) 80% Great (Meets all the requirements; High quality) 75% Good (Completes most of the requirements; Some omissions and mistakes) 50% Weak (Numerous omissions; Poor quality) Grades of 80% and above will be reserved for student groups that exceed the requirements and go beyond the expectations.
- The use of generative AI (e.g., ChatGPT, CoPilot, etc.) is encouraged, but students must demonstrate personal understanding of the code that they are using, with citations and acknowledgment of all sources. The faculty team reserves the right to interview individual students about their code or to perform random code walk-throughs to assess understanding.

The grades assigned at the checkpoints (at the midpoint and at the end of the project) are based on team performance with respect to the following aspects of software development and project execution.

Daily:

- Meeting participation, preparedness, and punctuality
- Professional client and supervisor communications
- Teamwork, enthusiasm, helpfulness, consistency, reliability, etc.
- Ability to use project management skills to meet project goals
- Ability to create and prioritize SMART tasks, and complete tasks on schedule
- Track and be able to provide evidence of individual and group progress on a daily basis.

End of Weekly Sprints:

- Sufficient effort has been made towards completion of tasks related to key deliverables
- The weekly deliverables and artifacts must be professional and demonstrate a consistent style and format throughout
- Project is properly scoped such that it involves sufficient complexity, innovation and ambition to meet your minimum viable product (MVP)
- Deliverables and artifacts align with project goals
- Technology is used appropriately to achieve the requirements of the project
- Key deliverables are well tested and the group is able to demonstrate evidence of project testing (unit, system, integration, user acceptance, etc.)
- The team is able to reflect and learn from each iteration.

## Attendance Requirements
Regular attendance is seen as critical to student success, and will be monitored by faculty. Unless otherwise stated, all labs/lectures, scrums, and client meetings are assumed to be mandatory. Consider this course as training for a full-time job. The standard institute policy on attendance applies. Exemptions for health-related situations may be considered with official documentation submitted to the Lead Instructors and Program Head.

Your individual contribution to the group project will, in part, be evaluated on your professional behavior, which includes regular attendance, punctuality, and participation in scrum and client meetings. Being 5 minutes late for a scrum will be considered as an absence.

Failure to attend mandatory meetings may result in your individual final calculated grade being scaled down:

- 1 missed session = no adjustment
- 2 missed sessions = 5% penalty
- 3 missed sessions = 10% penalty
- 4 missed sessions = 20% penalty
- 5 missed sessions = a grade of U
