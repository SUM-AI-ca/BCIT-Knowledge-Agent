### Course Description
The course explores race, gender, and class as intersecting relations that have applicability to academic and working contexts, as well as and applicability to our everyday lives and relationships. Concepts and arguments from the social sciences and humanities dealing with race, gender, and class in inter-related combinations not just as stand-alone concepts will be studied and applied in the course. Course material will focus on but not be limited to Canada and the US. Course material will also have an emphasis on aboriginal issues in Canada. Students will gain opportunities to critically reflect on their own situatedness within these intersections. Additionally, students will learn strategies for critical self-awareness, response, and alliance building that they can take to communities where they work and live.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Examine frameworks for analyzing dominance, privilege, oppressions, and power.
- Identify and describe the ways in which the categories of race, gender, and class as categories are interlocking.
- Explain how these categories structure assumptions, relationships, and practices in both invisible and overt ways.
- Articulate continuities and changes in how these categories are invoked across particular eras, fields of work and study, in Canada and some international contexts.
- Discuss some of the controversies, critiques, advantages, limitations, and ambiguities that accompany looking at race, class, and gender in intersectional ways.
- Examine how different authors, theorists, and practitioners from various fields explain the relationships between race, gender, and class.
- Critically reflect on the student?s own situatedness in the intersections of race, gender, and class.
- Apply ideas drawn from academic and artistic works to contemporary professional and social contexts where race, class, and gender operate.
- Analyze frameworks and issues that arise in anti-oppression and alliance-building.
- Apply critical thinking, response, and alliance building strategies about race, gender, and class to communities where students live and work.

### Course Specific Requirements
- Preparation and participation for class : Students should come to class with evidence of prior preparation and completion of the scheduled course materials for that class, and their notes for their responses to Reading Guide questions. They should have scheduled course materials handy in print or digital form for use during class, and contribute actively and thoughtfully for class discussions and activities. Students should regularly use the BCIT Learning Hub for course files, scheduled course activities, and scheduled coursework submissions.
- Coursework submission: Coursework must be submitted in the format, location, and due date/time specified by the instructor. See detailed instructions provided in class and in documents posted in the Learning Hub during the term for the specific course component. For late coursework assignments, there will be a 5% deduction per day, up to a total of four days, after which that coursework assignment may receive a fail mark or zero. For absence or non-submission of a timed exam or presentation coursework by the due day/time a zero mark will be applied. If a student is absent for an exam or presentation, or seeks accommodation for assignment coursework submission outside of the due date/time due to illness, medical, or other extenuating circumstances, they must consult with the instructor before the due date of that coursework component, unless the student provides evidence for circumstances that made it impossible to do so, and in that case no later than the day after the due date. The student must also submit appropriate supporting documentation (ex. BCIT-approved medical certificate ). If corrections are required to a coursework component, the student must make them and submit them by the instructor- specified due date for the grade on that component to be recorded.
- Academic Integrity: Coursework components must follow the format and requirements specified for that component. The coursework submitted must be the student's work, content, and wording, unless cited by the student from course materials or other reference sources that are formally documented using instructor-approved citation and format protocols (APA or MLA style), through in-text citations within the coursework and a list of references at the end of the coursework, per requirements for the coursework. All sources used and cited for summary, paraphrase, or quotes, must be trackable and accessible, with live links in the reference list, and through attachments with the assignment if the source is not available through the BCIT library or behind a paywall. Fabricated or falsified sources (including quotes) are not acceptable for use or citations. Students must not submit AI-generated content for coursework. Students must not submit coursework for one class, either at BCIT or elsewhere, for evaluation or credit in another class, without prior authorization by the instructor. Coursework will be checked for academic integrity and plagiarism. The course will use BCIT Policy 5104, Student Code of Academic Integrity https://www.bcit.ca/files/pdf/policies/5104.pdf particularly section 8 on plagiarism, cheating, falsifying or misrepresenting, and academic advantage, so students should make themselves aware of this policy.
- Recording of lectures, discussions, and classroom activities : students must not record lectures and classroom activities or discussions unless authorized by BCIT policy 4501 (ex. for IAP academic accommodation purposes). Also see BCIT policy 5201 https://www.bcit.ca/files/pdf/policies/5201.pdf
- Copyright, ownership, and distribution of course materials : Course materials are for the student’s individual study and use. Students must not post or distribute course materials outside the course, including to AI sites/applications, or any websites, without the required permissions from the instructor and copyright holders.
