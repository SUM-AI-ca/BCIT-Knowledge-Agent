### Course Description
Examines how the operating environment affects trade and trading relationships. The course of studies covers topics related to the location of markets, the infrastructure necessary for a successful market, current trade patterns and future trends in trading. Emphasis will be placed on explanations of how the operating environments affect production, transportation and marketing.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Articulate the changing demographics of the global economy.
- Clarify and explain Driving factors for globalization.
- Classify political system and the determinants of economic development.
- Evaluate values and ethical systems and workplace cultural change.
- Document ethical issues in international business and philosophical approaches to ethics.
- Define trade theories, instruments of trade policy, development of the world trading system and understand impact of foreign direct investment (FDI) in the world economy.
- Apply theoretical concepts of regional economic integration and functions of the foreign exchange market.
- Demonstrate a knowledge of economic theories of exchange rate determination.
- Investigate different modes to enter a foreign market, brands and market segmentation, global production strategy and logistics.
- Define strategic role of international Human Resources Management.

### Attendance Requirements
Attendance and Participation: Students are expected to complete the assigned readings before attending class and to read and contemplate the cases beforehand. Textbooks are required for all labs. Students are expected to participate actively by critiquing presentations, contributing insights, asking thought-provoking questions, listening to others, and soliciting the input and ideas of classmates. Another way to participate is to clip current business articles from journals or newspapers and note how they relate to the course concepts. This is a mandatory requirement . Students are expected to come to class prepared and actively participate in all class discussions, exercises, and scheduled presentations. Each student will be graded on both the quality of their contributions and the quantity of their participation relative to their classmates. To ensure fairness in assigning class participation marks, attendance will be taken at the end of every class. At the end of the term, class participation grades will be assigned as follows:
Excellent participation (9 – 10): Attends all labs. Consistently participates and moves class discussions forward. Volunteers’ high-quality analysis and insights or applications of course content. Assists other class members in understanding course objectives. Behaves respectfully towards classmates and helps others participate effectively. Punctual and attentive.
Satisfactory participation (6 – 8): Misses no more than one lab, does not consistently volunteer responses, but is prepared with good analysis when called upon. Understands assigned readings and can explain the concepts in the text, but demonstrates varying degrees of application and analysis. Participates in a manner that does not dominate or overwhelm others. Do not distract others.
Unsatisfactory participation (0 – 5): Misses multiple labs. Is not prepared when called upon and does not convey an understanding of the concepts in the assigned readings; presents poorly reasoned observations and analysis; frequently “off-task” in lab activities. Does not contribute to or dominate large class discussions or small group activities. Dominates class or small group discussions and small group activities. Treats others in a way that is intimidating, discriminatory or does not demonstrate an acceptance of diversity. Demonstrates inappropriate workplace behaviours. Is not proactive in involving classmates but participates in a manner that does not dominate, distract, or disrespect others.
