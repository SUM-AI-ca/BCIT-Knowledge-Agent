### Course Description
This course covers the modeling, analysis, and design of real-world dynamic systems composed of mechanical, electrical, and thermal-fluid elements. Emphasis is placed on the of exchange of energy and power between the different system elements. Power flow diagrams known as bond graphs are developed and used to generate a set of state-based equations, which are solved using Matlab. Students learn to develop, verify, analyze, and design multi-component models of electro-mechanical systems, culminating in a group design project.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Develop constitutive equations for basic system components. [1, 2]
- Develop bond representations of basic system components. [1, 2]
- Develop bond graph models of simple mechanical/electrical/hydraulic systems. [1, 2]
- Develop dynamic, state-based system models from the bond graphs. [1, 2]
- Refine system models through analysis of associated bond graphs. [3]
- Develop a multi-component models and analyze/validate simulated results. [2, 4]
- Apply design and optimization principles to system models to reach desired performance targets. [4]

### Course Schedule and Assignments
This course is offered in a combination of lecture hrs and tutorial/Labs following the textbook. For a summary of topics covered, please see the Course topics.
### Course topics
Duration (week #) | Topics (included)
week 1 | Scope of dynamical systems modeling, definition of engineering systems, subsystems, and components. definitions of modeling, simulation, design, and optimizations. Analogy principle for Eng. Systems (including mechanical, hydraulic, thermal, electrical), examples.
week 2 | Modeling methods for systems; mathematical (including Lagrangian approach), bond graphs/BG, signal-based, equation-based, physical/icon-based, causal vs. acausal models, objectives of system modeling
week 3&4 | Introduction to 20-Sim system modeling tools, with applications examples.
Complex systems, motivational examples, BG method foundations, flow of energy and power direction, definitions of components’ ports and bonds, efforts and flows , Causality assignments concept.
week 5&6 | Defining 9 basic elements of Bond-Graph method (including storage, resistance, capacitance, sources, transformer, gyro, 0-junction and 1-junction ), modulated elements
week 7&8 | Elements and modeling of mechanical and electrical systems (resistance, capacitance, inertance) with examples including BG
week 9 | Exam1, TBA during the course/term
week 9&10 | Elements and modeling of hydraulic (resistance, capacitance, inertance) with examples including BG
week 11 | Causality assignments for bond-graph elements and applications, examples for mechanical, hydraulic, and electrical systems
week 12 | Derivation of systems governing equations from Bond Graphs, examples.
week 13-15 | Examples of model constructions for complex multi-component systems with multi-energy domain and their simulations, design and optimization.
