## Course Description
​DSON 5017 prepares the student for entry into the clinical environment. This course is a lab course with an emphasis on patient care and performing full echo examinations. In the patient care portion of the course, the students will be introduced to health and safety measures, learn how to complete a variety of basic healthcare procedures, and develop competency in the use of equipment commonly used in various healthcare settings. In the cardiac scan lab, students will complete full echocardiograms, including patient histories, modifications required to assess for pathology, and documentation of findings. ​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of the Sonography Lab component, the student will be able to:

- Complete a full echocardiogram including 3D imaging, in a timely manner, including assessment of medical history and health status, formulation of scanning strategies, and modifications based on history and findings. (5.1b, 5.1c, 5.1d, 4.2h)
- Recognize and modify the examination based on sonographic evidence, abnormal findings, clinical information, resource implications, and other contextual factors. (5.3k, 5.3j)
- Identify and respond to urgent sonographic findings. (2.2h)
- While scanning in the lab, use appropriate body language, respond to non-verbal behaviours, and apply conflict resolution strategies. (1.3a, 1.3b, 1.1l)
- Verify patient identification and ensure the requested procedure correlates with the patient's clinical history. (2.2a, 2.2b)
- Evaluate patient preparation for the exam, patient's ability to tolerate the exam, and contraindications to the procedure. (2.2c, 2.2f, 3.1b)
- Ensure all applicable components of the exam are complete, diagnostic, and accurately documented. (5.3l,5.4a)
- Ensure that documentation is timely, accurate, concise, and complete. (1.2d)
- Use medical terminology and standard abbreviations in oral communication. (1.1k)
- Provide and receive feedback in a professional manner. (2.3f)

Upon successful completion of the Patient Care Lab component, the student will be able to:

- Measure blood pressure. (3.3a)
- Using proper body mechanics, ensure safe and comfortable patient transporting, transferring, and/or positioning. (6.2a, 3.1a)
- Engage in practices to promote own physical and environmental safety. (6.2c)
- Recognize, respond to, and disclose adverse events and emergency situations. (2.3l, 3.1j)
- Provide care for a patient in a manner that is respectful of individual diversity. (2.3b)
- Maintain awareness of patient's accessory equipment and act as required. (3.1i)
- Employ sterile technique. (3.1c)
- Perform IV starts on a mannequin arm. (3.3a)
- Follow standardized procedures for handling and disposing of sharps and contaminated and biohazardous materials.
- Demonstrate knowledge of Basic Life Support training or equivalent. (3.1K)
- Perform all required images and measurements of a cardiac ultrasound examination on simple, technically straightforward patients with some problem-solving, minimal guidance, critical thinking and time management. In addition to all structures learned prior to this course, students will now also understand how to assess abdominal situs, cardiac position, the chest and thorax (Appendix E).​

## Course Goals
The emphasis of this course will be on developing critical thinking and problem-solving skills to satisfactorily answer the clinical indication of echo examinations. Students will learn how to:

- Determine the goal of an echo examination
- Anticipate potential findings based on patient requisition and clinical history
- Plan scan strategies to target exam goal and potential findings
- Apply exam modifications to routine imaging to address clinical indication/s
- Adapt scan techniques as required to acquire relevant information
- By the end of the term, students will need to be able to satisfactorily perform a complete routine echo exam with supervision & guidance to BCIT's level 2 clinical standards as stated below:

- Demonstrates strong relational anatomical awareness
- Locates good windows and produces diagnostic images &/or cine clips independently on routine, uncomplicated cases
- Performs acceptable assessment of organs and structures on routine cases; guidance with changes in patient position and respiration may be required
- Recognition of abnormal findings is developing; assistance with measurements and logical sequencing of documentation may be required
- Anticipation of exam outcomes is developing
- Patient outcomes may be compromised without direct oversight

## Evaluation Criteria
CATEGORY: SPECIFICS: PERCENTAGE
Learning Hub Submissions: In an effort to build up integration of pathology, critical thinking, psychomotor skills and scanning proficiency, students will submit a weekly full echo exam that they have completed during independent labs for evaluation. There are a total of 7 submissions, with each one due Sunday by 10 pm. The best 6 out of the 7 submissions will be equally weighted and used towards the final grade. Each submission will be worth 6.7%: 40 %
Professionalism: Our lab environment and expectations are the same as those in clinical. Students succeed when they: Complete Pre-Readings before lab in preparation for the didactic knowledge activities, arrive on time, dressed appropriately and ready to participate as both sonographer & patient. Interact with everyone respectfully and maintain confidentiality throughout as one would in a clinical setting. See the “Sonography Professionalism” document for further details.: 5%
Lab Skills Tracker: Students are given written feedback on the Learning Hub using a Lab Skills Tracker (LST) after each guided lab practice session. The LST is not for marks. Its sole purpose is to guide the lab sessions helping both students and instructors keep track of skill development milestones. Areas of STRENGTH are celebrated, and areas of GROWTH are outlined along with the modifications and strategies needed to advance the development of the psychomotor skill(s) in question.
Comptracker Self Reflection: There is 1 Self reflections that will be completed using Comptracke midway through the term.: 2%
Patient Care: 1 Simulation reflection - 8% Pre-lab quizzes 15% - unlimited attempts * must achieve 80% or greater to continue to next quiz: 23%
Final: The final exam will consist of a Complete Routine Echo exam with a randomly chosen pathology for assessment.: 30%
A mark of 65% or greater is required for an overall Satisfactory (S) in the course.

## Attendance Requirements
Building scanning skills takes time and practice. Regular attendance and prompt arrivals to lab is required as a courtesy to all students.

In cases where a student is unable to attend due to foreseable circumstances, they must inform the Lab Lead Instructor and find a suitable substitute to attend the lab session on their behalf.

Students are responsible for the content they missed and for making up lost practice times.

## Course Specific Requirements
Students must act professionally, with integrity as well as dress appropriately while in the sonography laboratory both for safety reasons as well as practice for what is to come in clinical.

## Safety equipment or protective clothing
The required dress code for the lab is, BCIT Sonography scrubs, a patient gown, two hand towels for modesty and arm support, closed-toe/heel shoes. Long hair tied back, beards and nails short and neatly trimmed, makeup appropriate for setting, and no scents or perfume of any kind.

Students will need to purchase a box of gloves for personal use over the term.

***No food is permitted in the lab. Water bottles are permitted but must be kept away from the ultrasound machines.
