## Course Description
In this course, you will be introduced to the clinical procedures and tests commonly performed in medical settings. You will learn relevant theory about clinical procedures and develop a beginning competency in selected clinical skills. You will gain knowledge of the basic instruments, equipment, supplies and procedures used in medical settings. This course will help you develop the skills needed to assist physicians, prepare patients for tests and examinations and keep accurate records. You will learn how to set up an examination room with the appropriate equipment and supplies for examinations. You will also learn the correct procedures for sterilizing medical equipment, properly disposing of bio-hazardous materials as well as storing, maintaining and ordering supplies. In addition, you will be introduced to the basic role of health services, WorkSafe organizations, medical service plans and other medical organizations across Canada.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Describe how to prepare an examination room.
- Explain the infection process cycle, basic infection control and sterilization techniques used in examination rooms.
- Describe the differences between non-pathogens and pathogens.
- Demonstrate how to safely apply and remove clean disposal gloves.
- Demonstrate asepsis handwashing techniques.
- Interpret the results of different sterilization indicators.
- List examples of medical waste and their disposal processes.
- Explain how to assist with examinations (e.g. eye examinations using the Snellen Chart, gynecological examinations, initial prenatal examinations).
- Explain how to prepare patients for examinations and different clinical and diagnostic tests.
- Describe various x-ray and diagnostic procedures.
- State the three components of a complete physical examination.
- Describe each vital sign and how to accurately measure these.
- Describe how to complete urine dips and pregnancy tests.
- Measure, record, chart and document patient data (e.g. urine/pregnancy tests, height, weight, vital signs).
- Identify how to protect patient confidentiality.
- Plan supply orders (e.g. clinical, laboratory, Pap).
- Explain the purpose, functions, operation and maintenance of a Steam Autoclave.
- Explain how to safely prepare, maintain and store medical/surgical instruments for sanitization, disinfection and autoclaving according to the College of BC regulations and guidelines.
- Describe the role of Public Health for immunizations and other health issues.
- Navigate websites to obtain information, forms and requisitions for tests and examinations.

## Course Goals
This program has been designed to provide students the skills and resources necessary to be employed in the clinical setting in the healthcare field.

Students will gain the confidence to prepare and support patients on the phone and in person. This course will enable the Medical Office Assistant to perform basic clinical procedures including the use and management of medical equipment. The student will learn how to assist the physician with specific in office examinations and procedures. Emphasis is placed on establishing and maintaining medical and surgical asepsis.

Graduates of the program will have a practical understanding of the clinical procedures performed in a medical office or clinic setting.

## Course topics
Sanitization and Disinfection

- Define a microorganism and give examples of types of microorganisms.
- Explain the difference between a nonpathogen and a pathogen.
- Define medical asepsis.
- List the six basic requirements for growth and multiplication of microorganisms.
- Outline the infection process cycle, including the following:
- List and explain the protective mechanisms the body uses to prevent the entrance of microorganisms.
- Explain the difference between resident flora and transient flora.
- State when each of the following is performed: handwashing, antiseptic handwashing, and use of an alcohol-based hand rub.
- Identify medical aseptic practices that should be followed in the medical office.
- Explain how proper handwashing helps prevent the transmission of microorganisms.
- Demonstrate via video proper application and removal of clean disposable gloves.
- Explain the purpose of the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA).
- Identify the guidelines for use of personal protective equipment.

- State the purpose of sanitization.
- State the advantages of using an ultrasonic cleaner to clean instruments.
- List and describe the guidelines that should be followed when sanitizing instruments.
- State the uses of the three levels of disinfection: high, intermediate, and low.
- Explain the differences among the following: critical item, semicritical item, and noncritical item.
- List and describe the primary use of disinfectants in the medical office.

Sterilization

- Explain how the autoclave functions to sterilize articles.
- List and describe types of sterilization indicators.
- Identify the advantages and disadvantages of each of the following types of wraps: sterilization paper, sterilization pouches, and muslin.
- List the guidelines that should be followed when the autoclave is loaded.
- Identify the sterilization times for each of the following categories: unwrapped articles, wrapped articles, liquids, and large wrapped packs.
- Describe the method for storing wrapped articles.
- Describe the daily, weekly, and monthly maintenance of the autoclave.
- State the primary use of steam sterilization method.

Vitals

Temperature

- Define a vital sign.
- Practice proper use of a stethoscope and sphygmomanometer.
- Explain the reasons for taking vital signs.
- Explain how body temperature is maintained.
- List examples of how heat is produced in the body.
- List examples of how heat is lost from the body.
- State the normal body temperature range and the average body temperature.
- List and explain factors that can cause variation in the body temperature.
- List and describe the three stages of a fever.
- List the sites for taking body temperature, and explain why these sites are used.

Pulse

- Explain the mechanism of pulse.
- Practice and perform how to properly take and chart pulse rates.
- List and explain the factors that affect the pulse rate.
- Identify a specific use for each of the eight pulse sites.
- State the normal range of pulse rate for each age group.
- Explain the difference between pulse rhythm and pulse volume.

Respiration and Pulse Oximetry

- Explain the purpose of respiration.
- State what occurs during inhalation and exhalation.
- State the normal respiratory rate for each age group.
- List and explain the factors that affect the respiratory rate.
- State the normal oxygen saturation level of a healthy individual.

Blood Pressure

- Define blood pressure.
- State the normal range of blood pressure for an adult.
- List and describe factors that affect the blood pressure.
- Identify the different parts of a stethoscope and a sphygmomanometer.
- Identify the Korotkoff sounds.
- State the advantages and disadvantages of an automatic blood pressure monitor.
- Explain how to prevent errors in blood pressure measurement.

Preparation for the Physical Examination and Measuring Height and Weight

- Identify the three components of a complete patient examination.
- List the guidelines that should be followed in preparing the examining room.
- Identify equipment and instruments used during the physical examination.
- Explain the purpose of measuring weight and height.
- List the guidelines that should be followed when measuring weight and height.

Assessing the Patient and Assisting the Physician

- List and define the four techniques of examining the patient.
- Describe the responsibilities of the medical assistant during the physical examination.

The Eye

- Define visual acuity.
- Explain the differences among an ophthalmologist, an optometrist, and an optician.
- Explain the significance of the top and bottom numbers next to each line of letters on the Snellen eye chart, the distance for performing eye exams.

The Ear

- List and describe the ways in which hearing acuity can be tested.
- List the reasons & how to perform ear irrigation and an ear instillation.

Gynecologic and Breast Examinations

- State the purpose of the gynecologic examination.
- Prepare and perform competency evaluation for patient and equipment set-up.
- Identify the components of the gynecologic examination.
- Explain the purpose of a breast examination.
- Identify the breast cancer screening guidelines recommended by the BC Cancer Agency.

Pelvic Examination and Vaginal Infections

- Explain the purpose of a pelvic examination.
- List and describe the four parts of the pelvic examination.
- State the purpose of a Pap test.
- List and describe each category on a cytology request for a Pap test. Practice and complete a cytology form.

Prenatal and Postpartum Visits

- Explain the purpose of each part of the prenatal record.
- List and explain the purpose of each procedure included in the initial prenatal examination.
- Perform urinalysis for prenatal patients.
- List and explain the purpose of each prenatal laboratory test.
- Explain the purpose of return prenatal visits.
- Explain the purpose of the six weeks postpartum visit.

Pediatric Office Visits and Measurements

- List the components of the well-child visit.
- State the usual schedule for well-child visits.
- Explain the purpose of the sick-child visit.
- List the procedures performed by the medical assistant during pediatric office visits.
- Explain why it is important to develop a rapport with the pediatric patient.
- State the importance of measuring the child’s weight, height (or length), and head circumference during each office visit.
- State the functions served by a growth chart.
- State the importance of measuring a child’s blood pressure.

Radiology

- State the function of radiographs in medicine.
- Explain the importance of proper patient preparation for a radiographic examination.
- Explain the difference between conventional radiology and digital radiology.
- State the advantages of digital radiology.
- Explain the function of a contrast medium.

Diagnostic Imaging and Digital Radiology

- Explain the purpose of each of the following diagnostic imaging procedures:

- Ultrasonography
- Computer tomography (CT)
- Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)
- Nuclear medicine
- Mammography

Clinical Laboratory

- Explain the general purpose of a laboratory test.
- List and explain specific uses of laboratory test results.
- Identify the purpose of a laboratory request form. List and explain the function of each type of information included on the form.
- Identify the purpose of the laboratory report form, and list the information included on it.
- Describe the advantages of using a computer to send and receive laboratory documents.
- Explain how an electronic medical record (EMR) program can be used to prepare forms for tests performed on a routine basis.

Laboratory Testing in the Medical Office

- Explain the purpose of advance patient preparation for the collection of a laboratory specimen.
- List examples of specimens.
- Explain why specimens must be handled and stored properly.

Collection of Urine

- Explain why a first-voided morning specimen is often preferred for urinalysis.
- Explain the purpose of collecting a clean-catch midstream specimen.
- Explain the purpose of a 24-hour urine collection.

Testing and Analysis of Urine

- List changes that may occur if urine is allowed to remain standing for longer than 1 hour.
- List factors that may cause urine to have an unusual color or become cloudy.
- Identify the various tests that are included in the physical and chemical examination of urine.
- Explain the basis for urine pregnancy tests.
