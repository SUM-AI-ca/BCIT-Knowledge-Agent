## Course Description
Introduces the external forces in the business environment and the resulting implications for organizations. Examines the place of business in BC and Canada. Focus is on the issues arising from government policies, economics, ethics, social environment, forms of businesses, and the basic components of business operations.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
The participants will gain an understanding of the interdependence between businesses and organizations and their environment (ex. political, economic, and social). The participants will understand the basic forms of business and the benefits of the different structures. A general overview will be gained of the basic components of most organizations and their functions.

## Learning Resources
Understanding Canadian Business, 11th Edition, Nickels McHugh - McGraw Hill

## Course Goals
Course Description

A study of the basic concepts of the management process: planning, organizing, staffing, directing and controlling. Integrated with the concurrent first-term courses and using the case study method, it creates opportunities for the students to develop analytical, problem solving, group work, and communications skills, by analyzing and presenting solutions to typical business problems. Topics covered include: structuring organizations, decision-making and an introduction to production, controlling, strategic and tactical planning.

## Course topics
Course Overview

This course provides an introduction to business enterprises and to the key aspects of the contemporary business environment. The purpose of this course is to provide participants with an understanding of the interdependence between organizations and their (political, economic, social) environment.

Participants will also understand the basic components and functions of most organizations, including the different forms of business structures and their respective benefits. BUSA1005 will help each participant to identify areas of interest for future educational opportunities in the BCIT School of Business.
