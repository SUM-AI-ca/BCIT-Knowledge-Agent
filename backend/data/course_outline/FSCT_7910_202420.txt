## Course Description
This course is intended to develop advanced research and analytical skills. It will focus on the theory of inquiry; the logic, reality, and structure of investigative enquiry; and data analysis, methodologies of measurement models, and reporting of results. Emphasis will be placed on data-gathering and statistical techniques. You will be introduced to multivariate statistical analysis techniques, using SPSS for application in the law enforcement and forensics fields. Various models of measurement, including quantitative, qualitative and data-mining techniques with hands on data sets, will be covered. Groups will be required to prepare a mini research project using all the statistical applications covered in class.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
At the end of this course, the student will be able to:

- Explain the strengths and limitations of applied investigative research and research methodologies
- Examine the significance of conceptualization and measurement models and analysis
- Identify the differences between qualitative and quantitative research, and assess the suitability of each type of research for different applications
- Identify the methodological issues when using public safety data
- Articulate research findings in the law enforcement context
- Apply statistical crime research methods and advanced data analysis techniques using SPSS and predictive analytics
- Identify a suitable research topic to be explored in the capstone research course work

## Other information
MINI PROJECT COMPLETED IN APA FORMAT TOTAL ASSIGNABLE MARK = 40 % è(10 %) FOR IN CLASS PRESENTATION, (30 %) FULL WRITTEN

- Specify the research topic / question in well-defined, clear, concise form and forensics related. Your topic should be grounded in good literature review based on ideally peer reviewed journal articles and reliable sources.
- Explain how to operationalize research constructs and how you will codify and measure. What scales of measurement you will use and complete VALIDITY and RELIABILITY of items, scales, and the OVERALL MEASUREMENT INSTRUMENT (survey, questionnaire, experimental protocol)? Provide evidence of reliability and validity studies (face, content, construct, and criteria validities).
- Provide and justify the use of a particular MEASUREMENT MODEL (statistical, non-statistical, text based, interview & focus group-based measurement model) and clearly show / justify why this model was used and HOW FITTING your data is for the chosen model (model assumptions, data selection criteria, sampling frame).
- Describe the different types of variables (operational definitions) as agreed upon by researchers, as justified in literature review and past research measurement models.
- DV’s? Multiple outcomes (specify), consequences, clearly defined IV’s (predictor variables)?
- Define the underlying theory behind your research question, link the theories, show they will help you to solve your research problem, show and illustrate how the researcher on this subject matter agree and disagree on their methods, key research findings, where the agreements and disagreements are, evolve the theories over an archival time frame, show any potential deficiencies theories have, show where the knowledge GAPS are and how you propose these GAPS through your design (incremental knowledge build up)
- Formulate the various types of hypotheses and test them with results shown (Ho and Ha), stated in clear, precise, testable forms.
- Create a visualization of a research question & study (MIND MAP: C MAP: CONTENT MAP: FLOW AND OR SYSTEM DIAGRAM). Prepare a flow diagram, visual map, experimental design protocol and a process diagram (if applicable). If your research is experimental that, is you will conduct a real experiment such as data extraction from factory reset cell phones, the experimental protocol (how the experiment will be conducted, is well laid out like a program diagram, outlining each step. For this, you may use the computer science convention of decision and process matrices.
- Make sure that sub-scales, scales (Alpha scores for each sub-scale are shown and met the acceptable measurement criteria. Make sure that independent variables (IV’s) are pointing at the right DV’s (outcomes, results, consequences) and that the inter-item relationships between the IV’s are shown with a double headed arrows and multicollinearity issues (IV’s overlapping and issues of redundancy are dealt with).
- Write the FULL MINI PROJECT by using the APA style writing and citation criteria, provide a complete set of references used. Watch for good flow, logical, cohesive, critical synthesis, good flow, watch for transitions and precise results, with supporting charts, tables, graphs.
- Writing must be academic, all references provided in good and precise form in APA format.
