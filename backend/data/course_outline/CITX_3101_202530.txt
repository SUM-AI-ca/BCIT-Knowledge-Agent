### Course Description
​BCIT is an Instructor Training Centre for Cisco Networking Academy and this training is only open to sponsored instructors who are affiliated with an institution that has an active Cisco Networking Academy. Introduction to Networks (ITN) Instructor training introduces the architectures, models, protocols, and networking elements used as a foundation for corporate networks. Instructors will have the opportunity to build simple local area networks (LANs) and will develop a working knowledge of IP addressing schemes. Foundational network security will be covered and instructors will also be able to perform basic configurations for routers and switches. Networking Academy badges are available for completing this course. The course is delivered online with students studying the materials and completing the chapter quizzes at their own pace. Upon completion of the self-study component, there are scheduled sessions with an instructor that concentrate on instructional techniques, technical clarification, and challenge lab activities. Students must complete the course within 12 weeks of registration. Note: This course requires a three hour in-person skills-based assessment to be performed in a lab environment.​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Build simple Local Area Networks
- Perform basic configuration for routers and switches
- Configure routers, switches and end devices to access local and remote networks
- Configure end-to-end connectivity between remote devices
- Implement IPv4 and IPv6 addressing schemes
- Configure and troubleshoot a small network using IPv4
- Implement a small network using security best practices

### Course Schedule and Assignments
Week | Module | Topics | Assignments/Activities
Week 1 | Networking Today | Introduction to networks, network types, network components, career opportunities in networking | Readings, introductory discussion, personal network mapping assignment
Week 2 | Basic Switch and End Device Configuration | Switches, device roles, initial configuration steps, CLI basics | Lab: Configure a basic switch and end device, quiz on device roles
Week 3 | Protocols and Models | OSI & TCP/IP models, protocol layers, encapsulation, communication standards | Diagram assignment: OSI layers, protocol comparison worksheet
Week 4 | Physical Layer | Media types, signalling, bandwidth, cabling standards, physical topology | Lab: Identify and compare cable types, assignment on physical topologies
Week 5 | Number Systems | Binary, decimal, hexadecimal, conversions, relevance in networking | Number conversion exercises, quiz on number systems
Week 6 | Data Link Layer | Purpose, MAC addressing, frame structure, error detection, flow control | Lab: Frame analysis, worksheet on MAC addressing
Week 7 | Ethernet Switching | Ethernet standards, switching operation, collision domains, VLANs | Lab: Configure VLANs, assignment on switch operation scenarios
Week 8 | Network Layer | Routing concepts, packet forwarding, logical addressing, IPv4 basics | Lab: Packet tracing, assignment on routing concepts
Week 9 | Address Resolution | ARP, address mapping, DNS basics, troubleshooting address resolution | Lab: ARP and DNS simulation, worksheet on address resolution
Week 10 | Basic Router Configuration | Router roles, initial configuration, CLI commands, troubleshooting routers | Lab: Configure a basic router, assignment on router troubleshooting
Week 11 | IPv4 Addressing | IPv4 address structure, subnetting, public vs. private addressing, network planning | Subnetting exercises, quiz on IPv4 concepts, group activity: design a small network
Week 12 | IPv6 Addressing | IPv6 basics, address types, configuration, transition from IPv4 | Lab: IPv6 address configuration, comparison assignment: IPv4 vs. IPv6
