## Course Description
Applies critical research skills in examining sources and uses of epidemiologic data for public health field work and health services planning, evaluation and administration. Provides practical experience in outbreak investigation using case studies. Explores data collection methods, designs for applied research studies, techniques for data analysis, and scientific communication. Provides the skills to conduct field investigations under supervision, and to assess the validity of scientific reports. Enhances problem solving, critical thinking, reading and communication skills within the context of epidemiology and biostatistics.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Utilize preferred terminology and recognize the synonyms of contemporary epidemiology.
- Outline the evolution of contemporary epidemiology.
- Appreciate the applications of epidemiology.
- Assess the health status of populations using disease distribution rates and risks.
- Examine the measures of mortality and morbidity.
- Calculate the measures of disease rates, such as incidence, prevalence, incidence density rate, and cumulative incidence risk.
- Estimate risk to an exposed group using, for example, relative risk, attributable risk, preventable fraction and odds ratio.
- Investigate outbreaks by determining the distribution of disease in a population with respect to time, place, and person.
- Explain how to estimate the characteristics of a population.
- Describe a variety of sampling techniques.
- Identify sources of community health information.
- Apply Geographic Information Systems (GIS).
- Examine the interrelated roles of epidemiology and statistics by discussing methods to assess threats to validity such as bias, chance, and confounding (including effect modification).
- Calculate appropriate summary statistics.
- Formulate and apply logical statements of causation.
- Assess the relevance and limitations of various research approaches to the study of disease causation, the effectiveness of interventions, the distribution and general impact of health services.
- Describe the common types of epidemiological studies.
- Interpret the characteristics, advantages, and disadvantages of each type of study.
- Critically evaluate published research.
- Formulate an approach to health research questions based upon sound epidemiologic principles and methods.

It is the student’s responsibility to ensure the quality, accuracy and appropriateness of any work submitted.
