### Course Description
This introductory two-part course is designed to acquaint geomatics students with the fundamental concepts of earth and ocean sciences and will demonstrate the importance of these topics to their future practice of geomatics. The first half of the course focuses on earth sciences. Topics include earth basics (evolution, structure, gravity and magnetic fields), forces within the earth (tectonics, earthquakes, volcanoes), sculpturing of the earth’s surface (weathering and glaciers, resulting landforms), and earth materials (minerals, rocks). Where relevant, the central focus will be on the province of British Columbia. This would include earthquakes, rocks and minerals, and landforms. The second half of the course focuses on the ocean environment—the largest feature on the planet Earth. Ocean science is an interdisciplinary science; thus, its study will involve learning how geology, physics, chemistry, biology, meteorology, and astronomy inter-connect in the ocean environment. Topics will include the oceans and the processes that have shaped them, their composition and movement, waves, tides, and beaches, as well as interactions with the atmosphere.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
### Earth Science Outcomes:
- Describe the evolution of the earth and other planets in our solar system.
- Describe geologic history of the earth, the structure of the earth, the earth’s gravity and magnetic fields and their importance to the practice of geomatics.
- Summarize the earth/sun geometrical relationship.
- Describe the components of plate tectonics, with emphasis on mineral and rocks in British Columbia.
- Describe the nature and results of various forces within the earth and how technology (i.e., geophysical methods) is used to measure and monitor them.
- Describe the history of earthquakes, volcanoes and mountain-building, especially in British Columbia.
- Explain the forces that sculpt the earth’s surface (weathering, running water, and glaciers), the resulting landforms in British Columbia, and how technology is used to assess hazards and monitor deformation.
### Ocean Science Outcomes:
- Describe the relevance of ocean science to individuals/society and the influence on the environment.
- Recognize the complex links among processes on land, ocean, atmosphere, and sediments.
- Explain major geological, physical, chemical, and biological features and processes in the oceans.
- Describe the relationship between the motion of the earth, moon and sun, which produce tidal variations, and their effects on associated surveying and mapping measurements.
- Differentiate between different ocean and coastal zones and the factors that impact the work undertaken by geomatics professionals.
- Explain how interactions between the ocean and atmosphere influence earth’s climate.
