## Course Description
Covers general principles of sales management. Emphasis is given to the human resource with stress placed on selection, assimilation, training and supervision. Examination of sales, planning, and organization.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Develop effective public speaking skills by delivering oral presentations (individual and team) to audiences of diverse sizes.
- Distinguish between transactional selling and trust based relationship selling.
- Describe the three primary roles fulfilled by consultative salespeople.
- Explain the critical role of recruitment and selection in building and maintaining a productive sales force.
- Describe how recruitment and selection affect salesforce socialization and performance.
- Analyze the role of sales training in salesforce socialization.
- Explain the importance of sales training and the sales manager's role in sales training.
- Differentiate between salesforce leadership and supervision.
- Describe the five bases of power that affect leadership.
- Explain five influence strategies used in leadership.
- Apply coaching techniques in the sales management situations.
- Apply the 'situational leadership model' to lead your sales reps to their highest potential.
- Identify some of the problems encountered in leading and supervising the sales force.
- Describe salary, commission, and bonus and combination pay plans in terms of their advantages and disadvantages.
- Explain the fundamental concepts of sales-expense reimbursement.
- Differentiate between sales organization effectiveness and salesperson performance.
- Define a sale organization audit and discuss how it should be conducted.
- Define benchmarking and how it should be conducted.
- Discuss the different purposes of salesperson performance evaluation.
- Differentiate between an outcome-based and a behaviour-based perspective for evaluation and controlling salesperson performance.
- Describe the measurement and importance of salesperson job satisfaction.

## Course Specific Requirements
Preparation for Class: This course is composed of a lecture and lab each week where attendance and participation are expected by all students. For each session, students are expected to be prepared. It is each student's responsibility to understand what is required and to complete the readings and other preparation before each lab. Preparation will usually involve readings each on perusall.com and this will be discussed further in class.

Professional Behavior: In order to create a productive and professional learning environment this course follows these guidelines:

- Cell phones should be turned off during lectures and lab.
- Students will refrain from general, non-class related surfing of the net, playing computer games or reading material not related to the session; e-mail, chat or instant messaging. This is an expected professional courtesy to colleagues and instructors
- While casual attire is generally appropriate, students are expected to wear “business casual” attire when we have guest speakers; no shorts or T-shirts. More formal attire is expected for major team presentations unless other apparel is employed for dramatic effect.
- Students are expected to be prepared for each class, which means being able to add value to discussions, general knowledge or opinion-based input will not be sufficient. Students are encouraged to volunteer to speak during class, the instructor will also call on students at random to ask questions or invite opinions.
- Punctuality is extremely important as students who arrive late disturb all others.
- All students are expected to contribute during class discussions in order to enrich the learning experience for everyone. However, individual student should not monopolize or dominate discussions, precluding or intimidating others from contributing. When someone is talking is expected that everyone will listen and not hold parallel conversations
- In general, students are expected to behave as professionals during lecture and lab time.
- Students are encouraged to bring laptops to class. In some cases, students will be asked to go online in small groups to undertake research and prepare for discussion.
- Students are expected to act ethically and with academic integrity at all times.

Completion of All Assignments, Quizzes, Final Exam and Course Work

- Assignments must be submitted on time. There will be a 10% mark deduction for every day a submission is late. After 10 days, you will receive a zero.
- All components of this course must be completed in order to receive a final grade. Students who do not complete assessments will receive a final grade of INC (incomplete) until these are submitted and marked.
