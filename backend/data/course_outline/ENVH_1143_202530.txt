### Course Description
Introduces the health concerns associated with swimming pools and bathing beaches. Examines the operational characteristics and requirements of different pool types in order to gain the necessary knowledge and skills to protect the public health. The section on recreational bathing beaches focuses on assessment and monitoring of the beach water and its surrounding environment. Examines the various provincial regulations and national standards governing these topics.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Explain the public health and safety concerns relating to recreational waters.
- Recognize that a health concern or issue exists.
- Describe how bathers are at risk of disease, injury, or death in recreational aquatic facilities.
- Describe illnesses commonly associated with recreational aquatic facilities, infections of concern, modes of transmission, and prevention measures.
- List and explain the general inspection and evaluation criteria of the pool and its operation.
- Describe the wide range of pool facilities, including new and innovative designs, the PHI/EHO may encounter in practice.
- Describe the operation and requirements of recreational aquatic facilities and associated equipment from a public health protection perspective.
- Describe the overall purpose of the recirculation system.
- Describe the organizational pattern and function of the various recirculation system components. (This will include surface skimmer, gutters, main drain, balance or surge tank, hair and lint strainers, pump, filtration systems, disinfection equipment, heaters, chemical feeders, inlets and outlets, flow meters, and supply headers.)
- Describe and explain the inspection and evaluation criteria for pool components as related to health protection and regulatory requirements.
- Define and calculate pool “turnover" and “design flow rate".
- Discuss and describe the sampling of swimming pool waters for chlorine residuals, alkalinity, cyanuric acid, bacteriological quality, and pH, quoting the accepted criteria for each.
- Describe water chemistry and temperature effects in relation to the expected ranges and management in recreational aquatic facilities.
- Explain the role and use of disinfectants in recreational aquatic facilities.
- Describe free chlorine, total chlorine, chlorine demand, chloramines, breakpoint chlorination, shock chlorination, and super-chlorination and their public health significance.
- Explain chlorine chemistry and its relation to contaminants, bather load, oxidation-reduction potential (ORP) and the effects of pH
- Discuss chlorination, bromination, and ozonation programs for various pool types.
- Describe the various unstabilized and stabilized chlorine disinfectants and their appropriate uses.
- Describe the use of cyanuric acid with emphasis on its effects and related concerns.
- Describe the use of secondary disinfection (including Ozone and Ultraviolet light) with emphasis on safety issues associated with installation and operation.
- Explain automated delivery systems with emphasis on considerations in their operation and maintenance.
- Explain bio-film development with emphasis on associated risks
- Outline new and innovative pool water disinfection systems.
- Describe other chemicals commonly used in pools including degreasers, defoamers, flocculants, algaecides, sequestering agents, clarifiers, and non-chlorine-based oxidizers
- Use field test kits for the examination of pool water parameters.
- Describe the parameters used to “balance pool water." Calculate the Langelier Saturation Index.
- Describe the problems associated with indoor air quality in a recreational aquatic facility.
- Interpret microbiological laboratory reports for swimming pool and spa waters.
- Discuss the use of disinfectants in the spa/whirlpool environment.
- Discuss the scope, application, and interpretation of swimming pool regulations.
- Explain the legislation and/or guidelines that are applicable to recreational aquatic facilities.
- Use swimming pool regulations to identify and recommend corrective action for potential health or safety hazards in swimming pools.
- Describe the requirements for proper maintenance of recreational aquatic facilities including decks, hallways, showers, washrooms, and sauna.
- Describe the operation of wave pools and water slides with emphasis on associated problems
- Explain the public health significance associated with blood, bodily fluid, vomit, and fecal accidents in recreational aquatic facilities.
- Describe the response procedures for fecal accident /vomit/blood and bodily fluid spills.
- Explain the significance of CT values in the treatment of water in recreational aquatic facilities.
- Assess swimming pool/spa blueprints using current legislation and guidelines.
- Demonstrate effective implementation of practice guidelines
- Discuss and describe monitoring and enforcement activities in recreational aquatic facilities.
- Demonstrate the inspection process for a recreational aquatic facility.
- Explain the reasons that would warrant a re-inspection of recreational aquatic facilities.
- Explain the reasons for closing recreational aquatic facilities.
- Demonstrate procedures, techniques and equipment used for water testing in recreational aquatic facilities.
- Explain the chemical tests and the frequency of testing required to monitor recreational aquatic facilities.
- Identify factors that influence the accuracy of the chemical test results and reagents for recreational aquatic facilities.
- Explain the purpose of record keeping of all chemical test results and site observations at recreational aquatic facilities.
- Describe the procedures for the collection of water samples from recreational aquatic facilities for laboratory analysis.
- Explain benefits of a bacteriological sampling program
- Interpret the results of a laboratory water analysis and explain decisions regarding pool operation based on those results.
- Complete an inspection report based on the requirements of the Board of Certification
- Describe methods and tools used in enforcement to achieve compliance.
- Set and follow priorities to maximize outcomes based on available resources
- Apply critical thinking and problem-solving skills to case studies involving outbreak investigations associated with recreational waters.
- Describe and discuss the safety equipment and emergency procedures in recreational aquatic facilities.
- Describe emergency and lifesaving equipment required at recreational aquatic facilities.
- Describe the risks associated with suction and entrapment and the need for maintenance of protective devices and equipment.
- Describe signage requirements for recreational aquatic facilities.
- Describe safety features in a spa/hot tub including the maximum temperature.
- Describe electrical hazards associated with recreational aquatic facilities.
- Describe safe chemical handling procedures with respect to pool patrons
- Describe safe chemical storage and potential chemical interactions if improperly stored and handled, with reference to Occupational Health and Safety Standards, Building Code, Fire Code and Workplace Hazardous Materials Information System
- Describe the development and components of a recreational facility Safety Plan (Pool Safety Plan) including lifeguard qualifications where applicable.
- Discuss the assessment and monitoring of recreational bathing beaches including sanitary surveys, microbiological sampling programs, and results interpretation.
- Use regulations and/or accepted guidelines to identify and recommend corrective action for potential health or safety hazards in outdoor recreational waters.
- Describe some sampling techniques and standard analysis of recreational waters as used in Standard Methods for the Examination of Water and Wastewater, latest edition.
- Interpret microbiological laboratory reports for outdoor recreational waters
