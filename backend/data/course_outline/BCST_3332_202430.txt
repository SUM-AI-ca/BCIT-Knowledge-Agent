## Course Description
In this hands-on course, students produce live and recorded content for a student run radio station. Assignments include reporting from the field as well as hosting daily news and sportscasts.). Evaluation is based on performance, writing/content, professionalism, and production of original news stories. Students with a minimum average of 70% have the opportunity to participate in an industry placement as part of this course.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Produce content to deadline.
- Generate original stories.
- Use critical thinking skills to interpret news and current events.
- Analyze the news value of a story based on the news determinants.
- Demonstrate effective sound gathering and field recording techniques.
- Demonstrate effective audio editing techniques.
- Use industry writing standards and structure.
- Demonstrate proper vocal technique for professional broadcasting.
- Conduct interviews to be used as part of Radio News coverage.

Professionalism: marks are determined by the following criteria:

- Attendance and punctuality
- Adherence to deadlines*
- Timely, professional communication with classmates and instructors
- Journalistic integrity**
- Reviewing feedback and seeking clarification when needed
- Appropriate attire in the field and newsroom
- Understanding of and adherence to the BCIT Student Code of Academic Integrity (Policy 5104)
- Understanding of and adherence to BCIT Student Code of Conduct (Non-Academic) (Policy 5102) and Harassment and Discrimination (Policy 7507)

*Adherence to deadlines: Late submissions, without instructor approval, will be docked 5% for each school day submitted past the due date, as per program policy. In addition, students with two unapproved late assignments in a course, will receive zero as a professionalism mark. Late assignments *may* be accepted without penalty, on a case-by-case basis with prior communication (and permission) in advance of the due date/time.

**Journalistic integrity: In addition to BCIT’s policy, academic integrity violations within the journalism program may also be defined as…

- Deliberately inaccurate or misleading reporting
- Misrepresentation of facts or individuals
- Conflicts of interest (including but not limited to interviewing family, friends, roommates, or individuals at places you work or accepting a gift or monetary compensation for coverage, excluding exchanges as part of cultural traditions)
- Falsifying audio, images, or video including the use of artificial intelligence***

Note on academic integrity and use of AI:

“The unauthorized use of Artificial Intelligence products (such as ChatGPT) for any part of student work in this course is prohibited, and when identified by your instructor, will result in an academic integrity violation. Refer to policy 5104 - Student Code of Academic Integrity.”

Violation of any academic or journalistic integrity policy, as outlined above, could result in one or more of the following penalties:

- A written warning
- Required resubmission of the work in question for equal or lower grade value
- A lower grade on the work submitted (i.e. a penalty applied)
- A failing grade on the work submitted
- An academic misconduct letter being placed on the student’s record.

Additional note regarding Lab evaluation:

Evaluation includes a compilation of all work a student submits for vetting or marking while in a specific role. Therefore, penalties for violations of academic or journalistic integrity will be deducted from a student’s overall mark in that role.

IMPORTANT: A lack of familiarity with the regulations cannot be used as a defence by those accused of academic misconduct. It is the student's responsibility to become familiar with the regulations.

Attendance is required for all class and lab times. Students who have three late arrivals or absences without valid notification (to the course instructor) will automatically lose 100% of their professionalism mark.
