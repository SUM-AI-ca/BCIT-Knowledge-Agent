### Course Description
Emphasizes the integration of problem solving strategies with mathematical and calculator skills in the context of relevant nuclear medicine applications. Topics include unit conversions (Ci to Bq), ratio/proportion (radiation), logarithms, exponential growth and decay (physical, effective, biological half-lives, transmission of shielded radiation), graphing techniques (logarithmic), appropriate curve fitting (least squares), curve stripping, introduction to differential and integral calculus and first order differential equations (decay formulae) and an introduction to descriptive statistics including data presentation, observational and randomized-controlled studies, paired, retrospective and prospective studies.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
### Unit 1: Review of Algebra Skills and Applications of Linear Functions, Cartesian Graphing, Least Squares.
- Define the rules governing precision and accuracy.
- Use significant figures correctly.
- Manipulate literal equations.
- Perform given algebraic operations synthesizing algebraic skills.
- Calculate percentage from given information.
- Formulate and solve simple word problems involving percentages.
- Recall the theory related to fluid flow and mixture problems and the methods for formulating and solving equations for one or two unknowns.
- Recall the rules for radicals, quadratic equations, exponents and scientific notation and apply these in algebraic expressions.
- Review linear functions graphically and algebraically.
- Construct two linear equations and solve them from the context of a word problem.
- Construct a line of fit from experimental data.
- Explain linear regression and the construction of the line of best fit.
- Perform the necessary computations for the above with the aid of a calculator with and without double entry procedures.
- Describe other methods of polynomial curve fitting.
### Unit 2: Ratio and Proportion
- Review the definitions and formulae related to ratios, direct and indirect variation, and constants of proportionality.
- Demonstrate and explain the relationship between area and linear dimensions in similar figures.
- Demonstrate the inverse square law in terms of intensity of light and radiation from a point source.
- Apply the knowledge of ratios and proportions to the solution of relevant applied problems.
### Unit 3: Review of Functions (Logarithmic and Exponential)
- Identify the properties of common and natural logarithms and practice them to a state of mastery.
- Discuss models of radioactive decay, formula equivalency, and physical half-lives.
- Formulate and solve, using logarithms, word problems which require the solution of one or more variables.
### Unit 4: Physical, Biological, and Effective Half-Lives and Half-Value Layers
- Define external and internal dosimetry.
- Derive the formula relating biological to physical and effective half-life.
- Explain mean-life and intuitively develop its formula.
- Explain the formula for exposure rate shielded and unshielded.
- Demonstrate the parallel in formulae between intensity of radiation, and half-value thickness to those for radioactive decay.
- Explain the difference between mass and linear attenuation coefficient formulae.
- Apply the knowledge and construction of these formulae to typical problems involving dose, half-life, and half-value thickness calculations.
### Unit 5: Graphical Analysis Utilizing Logarithmic Graph Papers
- Demonstrate how exponential functions are linearized on semi-log paper.
- Demonstrate how power functions are linearized on log-log paper.
- Explain and practice the determination of appropriate numbers of cycles to be utilized on logarithmic paper.
- Identify a function type and select appropriate graph paper to linearize this function.
- Utilize a variety of graph papers and variables in determining requested information from the graph (e.g., half-lives, doses).
- Demonstrate and practice the method of least squares by appropriately transforming the variables.
### Unit 6: Curve Stripping
- Relate the ideas behind tracer kinetics in terms of a steady state system and tracer methodology.
- Graph, on semi-log paper, the activity over two compartments over time as determined by a single probe.
- Describe and employ the method of curve stripping to resolve the information into the individual rates of change and ultimately the two exponential formulae governing the overall input.
### Unit 7: Calculus and Its Place in Nuclear Medicine
- Relate intuitively, the idea of a derivative, graphically in terms of instantaneous rate of change.
- Explain and practice the product, quotient, and chain rule methods for differentiating logarithmic and exponential functions.
- Apply these methods to rate of metabolism and illuminance.
- Develop the application of differential calculus (instantaneous rates of change, maxima and minima) to a variety of topics including:
- Velocity of a protein band in electrophoresis.
- Velocity of blood in an artery (Poiseuille's Law).
- Maximum concentration of drug in the blood stream.
- Drug sensitivity.
- Measuring blood flow to limb.
- Maximum daughter nuclei.
- Develop intuitively the ideas of integral calculus including anti-differentiation of algebraic, logarithmic, and exponential functions.
- Apply the techniques of integral calculus to determine when a patient will be drug free given the rate of clearance of the drug.
- Discuss first-order differential equations as they apply to the following applications:
- Derivation of the decay formulae.
- Model for distribution of drug in tissues.
- Estimation of activity of waste in storage.
- Practice solutions to simple first-order differential equations.
- Discuss intuitively the idea of a definite integral and its relationship to curve area and mean value.
- Show how definite integrals can be used to derive the formula for cumulated activity from an absorbed dose over time.
### Unit 8: Introduction to Statistics
- Define statistics, population, sample.
- Examine random versus other types of sampling.
- Discuss reasons for sampling.
- Relate and discuss, using actual health care studies, the concepts and implications of: randomized controlled vs. observational studies, retrospective vs. prospective studies, placebo and placebo effect, confounding factors and double blind experiments.
- Define and contrast descriptive and inferential statistics.
- Identify appropriate questions to ask when initially judging the validity of a study.
- Evaluate health care examples which encourage critical thinking.
### Unit 9: Descriptive Statistics
- Define and contrast the measures of central tendency: mean, median and mode.
- Calculate each of the above for both raw and grouped data.
- Define and contrast the measures of dispersion, range and standard deviation.
- Calculate each of the above from raw and grouped data.
- Describe Tchebysheff's (aka Chebyshev's) Theorem and the Empirical Rule and their importance to the practical significance of the standard deviation - calculate a weighted mean and its relationship to the mean for grouped data.
***Any statistics topics above not covered in MATH 1751 will be covered in MATH 2751.

### Course Goals
The goals of this course are to:
- integrate problem solving strategies with mathematical and calculator skills in the context of relevant nuclear medicine applications.
- develop the problem solving/mathematical/calculator skills required for success in other courses within the Nuclear Medicine Program.
- be able to read medical literature such as texts, reports, journals (e.g., Journal of Nuclear Medicine Technology) which involve mathematical discussions or formula derivations with increased understanding.
- appreciate problem solutions rather than answers alone as an important communication tool.
