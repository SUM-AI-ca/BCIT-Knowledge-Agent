### Course Description
​This course introduces principles and procedures of standard research methodologies in the context of an interdisciplinary computing environment. It introduces the students to theories, paradigms and frameworks in computing, and the role and importance of models, theories and conceptual frameworks. The course also exposes students to quantitative and qualitative research methods such as surveys, interviews, experiments, case studies, grounded theory analysis and ethnography. Students also practice using inference as well as deductive and inductive reasoning, assessing the reliability and validity of data, and examining critically the assumptions, claims and methodology of existing literature. Students will also learn how to write an applied research proposal, including data collection and analysis.​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Analyze and assess problems and issues underlying applied research methods in the context of computing.
- Locate current and relevant primary source research materials using the Internet, periodicals, professional journals, and other sources of research.
- Critically analyze and evaluate published literature in major discipline areas related to computing.
- Investigate and evaluate the interdisciplinary application of new computing technologies.
- Identify and appraise opportunities and needs for innovative research in an applied computing environment.
- Propose solutions to pitfalls inherent in research methodologies, specifically addressing issues related to reliability and validity.
- Apply the principles of various research methods such as experiment, survey, field study and case study.
- Prepare and present a computing related research proposal according to generally accepted standards.
- Demonstrate a basic understanding of Indigenous and Indigenist research methodologies.​
