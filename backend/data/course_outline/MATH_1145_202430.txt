### Course Description
This course covers those topics in technical mathematics that are important for success in the Forest and Natural Areas Management field. Topics include measurement accuracy and precision; unit conversions; ratios, proportions, and percentages; right and oblique trigonometry; mensuration including applications of geometry; linear functions and graphs; logarithms and exponents. Applications related to the Forest and Natural Areas Management field will be emphasized throughout the course.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Determine the accuracy and precision of measured values in problems, and use this information in appropriately rounding off calculated results.
- Perform unit conversions with and between the SI and Imperial units of measurement.
- Use ratios, proportions, and percentages to solve scale, mixture and dilution, and change problems.
- Use right triangle trigonometric ratios to solve typical problems of slope and horizontal distances (including slopes in percent units), latitudes and departures (using compass bearings), tree heights, etc.
- Apply Sine and Cosine Laws to determine angles and distances in oblique triangles, and relate these results back to standard measurement systems of slope and bearing measurements.
- Apply a variety of geometrical models to approximate the areas and volumes of physical objects, including cylinders, cones and regular solids.
- Determine and graph linear functions to solve relevant problems.
- Use logarithms to solve for exponents in exponential applications.

### Course Goals
The goals of this course are to provide the student with the mathematical skills needed to solve problems which will be encountered in the Forestry and Natural Areas Management program, and in future employment in related industrial settings.
The student will use writing and diagrams to present organized solutions to these problems, and to draw relevant conclusions from these solutions.

Policy on Late Assignments:
WeBWorK homework assignments will be available after the lecture each week. You will have some time in the labs Wednesday and the tutorial the following Tuesday to work on your homework and ask questions, and the assignment will be due the following Tuesday by 11:59pm (before the following week’s lab).
It is important to keep up with the material in the course, so students should aim to get every homework assignment done by the due date (especially if the material will be on a quiz or test soon after the assignment is due!). However, it may be difficult to achieve this when something suddenly comes up (such as illness, family commitments, or just time management challenges). With this in mind, each student can have two (2) extensions on homework assignments this term (with no reason required). A sample email is provided on the Learning Hub (under Content → Course Logistics) that you are welcome to use to request a free, no-reason-needed extension for an assignment. These extensions will give you 5 extra days to complete the assignment (due Sunday by 11:59pm instead of Tuesday).
Students may also request extensions under extenuating circumstances with documentation by emailing your instructor, which will be handled on a case-by-case basis. These are separate from the 2 free extensions.
