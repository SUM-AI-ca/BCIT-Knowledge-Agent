## Course Description
An introductory course that deals with the basic concepts of microbiology with emphasis on those with environmental and public health significance. Topics discussed include the basic characteristics of microbial agents, relationship between microbes, humans and their environment, laboratory methods for microbiological analyses, and immunology. Special emphasis will be placed on the application of these concepts in the field of Environmental/Public Health.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Outline the significance of microbiology and immunology to the Environmental/Public Health field.
- Discuss the basic and special characteristics of microbes including prions, viruses, bacteria, fungi, protozoa and parasites that are of public health significance.
- Relate the characteristics including environmental growth requirements of microbes to the control measures commonly applied to them.
- Describe virulence factors in the context of pathogenicity and signs and symptoms of infectious diseases.
- Explain the application of laboratory methods in microbiological analyses including their basic principles and limitations as well as the interpretation and significance of their results.
- Describe the roles of the human immune system in health and diseases.
- Apply the knowledge of microbiology and immunology to Environmental/Public Health especially in the area of food hygiene and control of communicable diseases.

## Course Schedule and Assignments
Topics
Unit 1: Introduction to Environmental Health Microbiology Introduction to Microbiology and its relevance to Environmental Health.
Unit 2: Characteristics and Classification of Microbes Classification and taxonomy of microbial groups. Distinguish commensal microbes from pathogens and discuss microbial factors associated with virulence.
Unit 3: Basic Characteristics of Bacteria Describe bacterial cell structure. Outline strategies to classify bacteria based on cellular characteristics. Discuss virulence factors and lab tests for bacterial pathogens.
Unit 4:Basic Characteristics of Viruses and Prions Describe structure, diversity and infectious diseases associated with viruses and prions. Outline virus attachment, life cycle, evolution and transmission.
Unit 5: Basic Characteristics of Fungi, Protozoa and Helminth Worms Descriminate eukaryotes from prokaryotes. Outline the life cycles of fungi, protozoa and helminth. Present examples of relevant infectious diseases associated with each group.
Unit 6: Role of Human Immune System in Health and Disease Describe innate immune system, including barriers, complement and immune cells. Describe cytokine-mediated coordination of immune events, such as chemotaxis, fever and inflammation. Describe antigens and the process of antigen presentation. Explain how the adaptive immune response emerges and the adaptive responses that contribute to long term immunity.
Unit 7: Growth Requirements and Control Measures for Microbes Define the microbial reservoir and outline key microenvironmental factors that influence survival and growth Discuss microbial control, including: (1) Asepsis or Aseptic techniques, (2) Sterility and Sterilization, (3) Disinfection, Antisepsis & Sanitization, (4) Hand Hygiene Explain how microbial control is applied to critical, semi-critical and non-critical equipment.
Unit 8: Laboratory Methods in Environmental Health Microbiology Describe the basic principles and application methods of common laboratory methods Apply this knowledge in critical interpretation of lab results Define the limitations of lab tests

Please note that: 1. Some of the topics listed in the schedule above will be discussed further in BHSC 7423 Communicable Disease Control 2. Specific textbook readings, assignments, deadlines and other relevant supplemental information will be clearly communicated on the Learning Hub.
