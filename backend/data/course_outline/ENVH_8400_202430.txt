## Course Description
Examines the components of the research process. Under the guidance of faculty advisors, students work independently to select a research topic in public health and develop a detailed research proposal for the project to be conducted in ENVH 8410 (Applied Research Project). In addition to developing core competencies in the research process, students will enhance their communication, problem-solving, critical thinking, reading, evidence-based decision making and statistical skills. Requires an off-campus practicum component.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Evaluate an article from a scientific journal.
- Select an appropriate research topic for completion in the subsequent course.
- Define the research problem.
- Conduct a literature review.
- Formulate meaningful research questions.
- Select an appropriate research method and statistical analysis for the problem.
- Define the scope and depth of the project.
- Estimate the time, cost, and effort required to complete the project.
- Justify the benefits of completing the project.
- Prepare and deliver an oral presentation of the research proposal to faculty and students.
- Relate the research process to evidence-based decision making and knowledge translation.

## Course topics
Assignment Details

- RESEARCH TOPIC

Submit a one-paragraph (approximately half page) description of the project you propose to carry out Jan – May 2024. Include:

- Reason for doing study and its relevance to public health. For instance, a problem was identified to you during summer work or from a discussion with a public health practitioner (e.g., EHO / NCCEH / BCCDC)
- Research question(s) to be answered (e.g., can sound-measuring apps on cell phones be used in place of sound level meters?),
- Proposed methodology (e.g., survey, physical/chemical analysis, microbiological sampling) and,
- Potential for knowledge translation (e.g., BCCDC Food Issue Notes or guidelines etc.)

This is an integration of previous research (adequate in scope and depth) that critically explores and addresses your current research question. DO NOT just summarize what others have previously written, e.g. Jones et al 2017 found this, Smith et al 2013 showed this and Lu et al 2015 identified this. Synthesize the findings from multiple research studies (both positive and negative findings), studies that used different methodologies, were performed at different times, in different locations. By doing this a “gap” in the knowledge will emerge and a justification for the study you are proposing can be made. Knowledge Translation (KT) as it pertains to your study should be discussed. How do you anticipate “translating” your findings into policy, practice, procedure, guidelines, innovation? For example, can your findings be incorporated into guidelines, e.g., Sous Vide Guidelines, or Policy Changes, e.g., FOODSAFE expiry date, or incorporated into Fact Sheets, e.g.Sushi Safety pH change?

NOTE: Do NOT summarize different articles separately but integrate them into your literature review. Please refer to the guest lecture by Dr. Angela Eykelbosh for additional details.

- Complete description of materials used.
- Description of standard methods (e.g., water quality analysis test like MPN and membrane filtration)

- Alternate methods (e.g., in-person survey instead of mailed questionnaire)
- Justification for methods selected (e.g., issues relating to financial and human resources, availability of /accessibility to subjects and/or instrument(s))
- Reliability and validity of measures, including a brief discussion of potential alpha and beta errors.
- Calibration of instruments (use established protocol – factory calibration and/or routine calibration)
- Inclusion and exclusion criteria (e.g., “any individual who applied tattoos to the human body in a publicly advertised tattoo business, anywhere within the province of British Columbia was eligible to participate”, J Bratt, 2005)
- Ethical considerations (required for all survey and human based projects)
- Description of pilot study to be done before execution of actual study. This includes the proposed sample size and time frame (e.g., time and temperature measurements for 5 samples of sous vide salmon will be taken during the first week of January).
- For surveys, include the following: cover letter, scripts used, and the survey questions to be asked.
- For non-surveys, include image(s) of equipment, or set up, or location of study (e.g., long-term facility or daycare toys to be swabbed).

- Description of the kind of data you have (e.g., nominal or numerical)

- Descriptive statistics and how they will be presented (e.g., means, modes, medians, ranges, standard deviations and/or percentages or counts that will be displayed in tables, bar charts or pie charts)
- Inferential statistics (e.g. chi-square tests, correlation/regression analysis, t- tests, ANOVA, etc.)
- Statistical package intended to be used (e.g., NCSS, SPSS, Excel, Real- Statistics).
- Trial run-through of statistical analysis using made up, but sensible, data submitted including the following:
- Proposed data entry sheet
- Results print-out from statistical package used
- Interpretation of “made up” results and reasoning for whether parametric or non-parametric statistical test was used.
