### Course Description
The goals of this course are to enable the learner to demonstrate a level of knowledge and comprehension of communicable diseases and communicable disease control such that he/she will be able to apply, and to collaborate with as well as to instruct others to apply, the procedures and practices relevant to communicable disease control in general and to each specific communicable disease discussed in the course. Following a brief review of the basic concepts such as microbial reservoirs, modes and routes of transmission, communicability, signs and symptoms as well as virulence factors, the course systematically deals with control measures for foodborne/enteric, waterborne, zoonotic, vector-borne, vaccine-preventable as well as sexually transmitted diseases. Emphasis is placed on communicable diseases of public health significance, especially those that are more likely to be encountered by Public Health Inspectors/Environmental Health Officers (PHIs/EHOs) in their practice.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
Differentiate between:
- Infection and intoxication.
- Communicability, communicable period, and incubation period.
- Signs and symptoms.
Analyze the relationship between humans, microorganisms, and their environment.
- Differentiate the survival potential of prions, viruses, bacteria and parasites in the environment as well as their control by cleaning, disinfection and sterilization.
- Relate microbial virulence factors to signs and symptoms.
- Describe the roles of exotoxins in the pathogenesis of communicable diseases including foodborne intoxication.
- Outline the body defense mechanisms of the human body.
- Explain the current immunoprophylactic measures for communicable diseases.
- Categorize microbial reservoirs.
- Differentiate between a route of transmission and a mode of transmission.
- Contrast direct and indirect modes of transmission.
### Describe the control measures used for living reservoirs.
- Differentiate between:
- Cases and contacts.
- Case definitions and contact definitions.
- Describe the purpose and nature of isolation, quarantine and social distancing as control measures.
- Apply contact tracing in the control of communicable disease.
- Outline the significance of disease surveillance.
- Discuss the purpose and nature of reporting procedures as related to communicable diseases.
- Describe the roles and responsibilities of PHIs/EHOs in the reporting of communicable diseases.
Demonstrate professional preparedness to take part in the control of outbreaks of health-care associated infections.
- Define health-care associated infections (HAIs).
- Describe the components of an institutional infection control program, e.g. in a long-term care facility (LTCF) and hospital.
- Assess the significance of institutional outbreaks. Identify institutional personnel responsible for infection control.
- Define an outbreak including a gastroenteritis outbreak in a LTCF.
- Describe the roles and responsibilities of a PHI/EHO as a member of the outbreak prevention and management team.
- Explain the steps involved in the investigation of an institutional outbreak.
- Discuss infection control measures that may be implemented in an institution, especially during an outbreak.
Summarize and evaluate the effectiveness of control measures for each of the communicable diseases listed in the course outline:
- Etiologic agent.
- Specimens and/or samples required for laboratory confirmation.
- Common laboratory identification methods, their limitations and interpretation of results.
- Major reservoirs.
- Common modes of transmission.
- Risk factors and settings.
- Control measures including the roles of public health personnel and agencies.
- Notable and/or distinguishing signs and symptoms, incubation periods and period of communicability.
- Propose, justify and assess evidence-based control measures for emerging communicable disease.
