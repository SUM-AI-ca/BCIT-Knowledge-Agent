## Course Description
This course aims to introduce students to theoretical and applied aspects of orthotic management below the knee. Theoretical and practical components of the course run in parallel throughout the semester. The orthotic treatments are performed on a variety of patient volunteers.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Perform patient assessments of the lower extremity (below the knee) (include interpreting subjective and objective information).
- Determine an appropriate orthotic treatment plan (including device specifications) based upon patient assessment findings.
- Measure (cast) fabricate and evaluate the following lower extremity orthotic devices:
- Ankle Foot Orthoses (flexible, rigid and articulated AFO’s, thermoplastic and conventional design)
- Foot Orthoses (functional and accommodative)
- Evaluate the effectiveness of an AFO and/or FO in meeting the treatment criteria and anticipated outcome. Be able to justify all decisions within the treatment plan and rationalize the result of the treatment outcome.

Assignments/Activities / Clinical Orthotic Treatment documentation: 40%: Any work submitted late will be penalized at 10% per day; No work will marked if more than 5 days past the due date. Any extensions will be on a case-by-case basis with the instructor but must be identified 2 days prior to the due date.
Lab Etiquette: 10%: P&O technical instructor will assign lab mark.
Exams: 50%: Written midterm and final multiple choice exams based on course content. Practical OSCE style exam will be based on student's oral communication and physical demonstraion of examination content. Content for final exam and OSCE will be cumulative.
Final Mark: 100%

Complete orthotic treatment reports include the following: patient assessment form, clinical documentation, reflective self-critique and digital pictures.

Evaluation of orthotic reports should include but not limited to reflection upon: Patient assessment (subjective and objective evaluation of the patient); Orthotic Treatment plan (selection and justification of the most appropriate orthotic prescription for the clinical presentation and the evaluation process and device outcome); Students will be assessed on accuracy; appropriateness of information presented and use of appropriate terminology.

Video Gait analysis is performed individually and discussed throughout the term in small groups. One gait analysis report will be submitted for individual grading.

Pathology assignment will be an assigned to student pairs. The students will present a pathology seminar to teach relevant information about the pathology, as well as an oral case study facilitation to create dialogue with the class regarding treatment options and selection criteria. The final written case study portion will be an individual submission.

Multiple-choice written exams will be written on Learning Hub and practical OSCE exams will be based on student’s oral communication and physical demonstration of examination content. Final written exam and practical OSCE exams are cumulative for the term.

Any work submitted late will be penalized at 10% per day; No work will marked if more than 5 days past the due date. Any extensions will be on a case-by-case basis with the instructor but must be identified 2 days prior to the due date.
