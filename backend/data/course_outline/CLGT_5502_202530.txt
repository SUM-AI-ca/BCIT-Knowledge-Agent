## Course Description
Through an extensive collection of normal and abnormal G-banded prints, CytoVision® reprints, and slides, the student is exposed to the many morphological and band characteristic variations of human metaphase chromosomes. Abnormal/normal karyotypes are described according to the ISCN (2024) criteria. The correlation between the chromosome outcomes and phenotype expression is stressed. A system for the analysis of human metaphase chromosomes in print or under the microscope is established. This allows for the consistent analysis of each metaphase to be studied and reduces errors. The student also does critical chromosome analysis on experimental tissue culture preparations (products of conception).

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
By the end of this course, learners will be able to:

- Recognize chromosome morphology and intensity (grey, dark, refractile, etc.) under phase contrast microscopy and apply banding pre-treatments to optimize G-banding.
- Understand the structure and function of chromosomes re banding.
- Understand the mechanism of banding (G-b).
- Detect low to high band resolution chromosome abnormalities in slides/prints.
- Accurately document genetic results. Use the ISCN (2020) System to describe chromosome analysis results. Be aware of limitations.
- Analyze metaphase chromosomes, karyotypes, partial karyotypes both G-banded and special stained chromosomes.
- Correlate the phase contrast appearance of chromosomes to pre-treatment needs for high quality banding results.
- Apply the highest level of integrity and honesty in analyzing human chromosomes.
- Understand the relationship between number of metaphases examined and the exclusion of mosaicism (Quality Assurance).
- Utilize variable Romanowsky stains (G-b) to enhance the positive G-banding pattern after trypsinization.

## Course Goals
To establish and use a systematic protocol for selecting and analyzing G-banded metaphase chromosomes from 5×7 prints, CytoVision® reprints, slides, and CytoVision® images.

Cite and number each use, like this [1, ChatGPT, (date)]
