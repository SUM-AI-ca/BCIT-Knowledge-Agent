## Course Description
​This hands-on course allows the learner to work in a team, to design and execute a live experiential event. Learners will conduct research, consult with stakeholders, develop a proposal, and operate the event. Topics include event design, supplier negotiations, operations, and reporting. The course emphasizes experience design and event management principles to develop an effective program that meets the marketing objectives of the client. Learners will produce a marketing event for the School of Business + Media. ​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Develop effective public speaking skills by delivering oral presentations (individual and team) to audiences of diverse sizes.
- Use current, practical, and technical terminology in reference to experience design.
- Design and carry out an experiential event to meet the marketing objectives of a client.
- Prepare a strategy, budget, and operational plan for a live event.
- Select web-based tools that can be used in experience design.
- Prepare a post event evaluation report
- Describe current trends in experience design.​

## Course Goals
Building on the foundations of experience design, this hands-on course immerses students in the strategic and creative process of crafting impactful experiential marketing initiatives. Students will explore key topics such as attendee engagement, the role of AI in personalized experiences, designing immersive event spaces, measuring event ROI, and planning large-scale events. Through practical applications, collaborative projects, and real-world case studies, students will develop the skills needed to create compelling, results-driven experiences that leave a lasting impression.

## Course Specific Requirements
Preparation for Class: This course is composed of a lecture and lab each week where attendance and participation is expected by all students. For each session students are expected to be prepared. It is each student's responsibility to understand what is required and to complete the readings and other preparation before each lecture or lab. Preparation will usually involve some readings and questions. Students are encouraged to go beyond the supplied resources and find additional research on the topic at hand. There is no textbook for this course and students will need to read from electronic sources in order to understand class content and complete assignments. Participation and reflection will be encouraged and evaluated as part of this course.

Professional Behavior: In order to create a productive and professional learning environment this course follows these guidelines:

- Students are expected to attend all classes and labs, except in the event of illness or some other very good reason for being absent. In such cases the students must e-mail the instructor as soon as possible to advise the extent and reason for the absence.
- Students are encouraged to change their seats from time to time during the term to interact with different people.
- Students should place their name card in front of them before every class/lab for the first month, and when we have guest speakers. This is a courtesy to instructors and allow students to quickly become known to instructors and guest speakers.
- Cell phones should be turned off during lectures and lab.
- Students will refrain from general, non-class related surfing of the net, playing computer games or reading material not related to the session; e-mail, chat or instant messaging. This is an expected professional courtesy to colleagues and instructors. Students who violate this policy may lose the use of laptops or other technology in the classroom.
- While casual attire is generally appropriate, students are expected to wear “business casual” attire when we have guest speakers; no shorts or T-shirts. More formal attire is expected for major team presentations, unless other apparel is employed for dramatic effect.
- Students are expected to be prepared for each class, which means being able to add value to discussions, general knowledge or opinion-based input will not be sufficient. Students are encouraged to volunteer to speak during class, the instructor will also call on students at random to ask questions or invite opinions.
- Punctuality is extremely important as students who arrive late disturb all others.
- All students are expected to contribute during class discussions in order to enrich the learning experience for everyone. However, individual student should not monopolize or dominate discussions, precluding or intimidating others from contributing. When someone is talking is expected that everyone will listen and not hold parallel conversations
- In general students are expected to behave as professionals during lecture and lab time.
- Students are encouraged to bring laptops to class. In some cases, students will be asked to go online in small groups to undertake research and prepare for discussion.
- Students are expected to act ethically and with academic integrity at all times.
