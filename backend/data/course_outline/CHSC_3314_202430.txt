## Course Description
This is the first course of a two course series on the theory and practice of mineral processing. Minerals and ores are defined along with their formation and dispersion in earth's crust. Distinction is made between mineral processing and extractive metallurgy; methods by which metals are extracted from minerals are mentioned. Mass balancing methods, drawing and interpretation of mineral process flowsheets explained. Sampling techniques, particle size measurement methods, size distribution analysis, comminution theory, size reduction by crushing and grinding, industrial screening and factors affecting screen performance are introduced. Students will carry out laboratory work and produce reports in the above areas.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to competently cover the following topics;

- Define minerals, rocks and ores [1].
- Distinguish between mineral processing & extractive metallurgy methods [1].
- List mineral properties used in separation of gangue and economic minerals [1].
- Carry out mass balances, interpret and prepare simple flowsheets of mineral processing operations [1,2,7].
- Explain the significance of sampling & prepare samples for analysis using simple sampling techniques [1,3].
- Describe methods of particle size mesurements and perform size distribution analysis [1,2].
- Describe comminution theory and recognise various forces acting upon mineral particles during comminution [1].
- Explain grinding mechanism, define critical speed in tumbling mills & utilise formulas to obtain critical speed [1,7].
- Describe charcteristics of rod and ball mills, pebble mills and SAG mills [1,7].
- Perform experiments to calculate work index, crushing & grinding rates and screen efficiencies [1,2,3].
