## Course Description
Cell biology is the study of structure-function relationships at the sub-cellular level. This course examines the inner workings of the animal (mammalian) cell and the application of this knowledge in biotechnology. Includes lecture topics on membranes, intracellular compartments, cell signalling, and cell division. Laboratory topics include: in vitro cell culture, gene transfer and expression analysis, cell-based assays, immunostaining and fluorescence microscopy. (UBC BIOT 310)

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

Lecture Learning Outcomes/Competencies

- Discuss the evolutionary origins and compartments of the eukaryotic cell.
- Discuss the major investigative methods of cell biology.
- Discuss the cytoskeleton, and the dynamics of its assembly and re-organization.
- Discuss the mechanics of the cell cycle and its regulation.
- Discuss the structure of cell membranes.
- Discuss membrane transport.
- Describe intracellular compartments and protein sorting.
- Describe vesicular traffic in the secretory and endocytic pathways.
- Describe the mitochondria.
- Discuss cell signalling.
- Describe cell junctions, cell adhesion, and the extracellular matrix.

Lab Learning Outcomes/Competencies

- Successfully grow and aseptically maintain both adherent and suspension cell line cultures in vitro.
- Successfully cryopreserve and reconstitute cultured animal cells.
- Produce transgenic cells in culture via gene transfer.
- Perform immunostaining and fluorescent microscopy.
- Perform Western blots.
- Analyze raw data and write effective data reports.

## Course Goals
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will appreciate the complexity of the living cell and be able to:

· Understand the types of molecular interactions that take place within an animal cell, where they occur and how proteins are trafficked to the correct cell compartment.

· Understand many of the regulatory mechanisms governing cell signalling, cell division and movement.

· Perform and apply advanced techniques in animal cell culture.

BCIT Biotechnology Program Attendance Policy:

Students are expected to attend all lecture and lab sessions unless they have a serious illness or other medical condition. In the Biotechnology Program, laboratory attendance is of the highest priority, because lab experience is what sets this program apart from other undergraduate programs and prepares students for co-op work terms, where it is expected that they will have acquired specific competencies. Furthermore, lab work is typically done in pairs or small teams and students are expected to share the work equally and support their lab partners.

For some lab-based courses, participation marks will be given for laboratory activities, and these marks will be worth approximately 5% of the student’s final course grade. A student must be on time for start of the lab period to avoid losing participation marks. These lab activities involve generating lab specific materials and/or data, learning and/or practicing new skills and/or procedures. To submit a lab report or assignment, a student must participate in 80% of the lab periods associated with the lab report or assignment. In the case of illness or other unavoidable causes of absence, the missed lab period(s) will not be counted towards the 80% participation marks required to submit a lab report or assignment.

For this course, lab attendance, punctuality and teamwork are part of the student’s “Professional Practice” grade (see “Evaluation”).

Please notify your lab partners(s) and instructors (joan_shellard@bcit.ca, myra_howell@bcit.ca) of any absences. If exams, quizzes, or in-class assignments are missed for reasons of illness, medical notes will be required to reschedule. Instructors are not able to re-schedule missed lab periods.

Students who are experiencing ongoing challenges resulting from medical conditions are encouraged to seek the support of the Disability Resource Centre (DRC).

Use of Generative AI:

The ability to analyze and interpret lab data is an important part of learning and an essential skill sought by future employers. The type of data generated in this course is not amenable to GenAI analysis and the output may contain incorrect or misleading information, faked work and/or fabricated authors/dates/publications. Therefore, the use of GenAI for data analysis in this course is not appropriate and strongly discouraged.

If you use ChatGPT (or a similar tool) to get ideas and/or partial answers for an assignment and/or to generate any text for a draft or final version of any part of an assignment, you must acknowledge that you have used it. Follow these guidelines:

- Cite and number each use, like this [1, ChatGPT, (date)]
- Denote generative AI output, by using “quotes” for unedited GenAI output, and ‘quotes’ for using paraphrased GenAI output.
- For all generative AI content, student must include primary source verification (i.e. original research journal articles or chapters from traditionally published textbooks).

Material directly copied from any source (e.g. websites, books, AI tools) must be cited. Undeclared copying of these materials is a violation of academic integrity, because it presents work or ideas created by tools or other people as your own. Failure to fully declare the copying of these materials will be considered a violation of BCIT academic policy.

Any use of GenAI for completion of exams or quizzes is strictly prohibited.

## Course topics
Lecture Learning Outcomes/Competencies

Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

· discuss the evolutionary origins and compartments of the eukaryotic cell

· discuss the structure of cellular membranes

· discuss membrane transport

· describe protein sorting into intracellular compartments including: mitochondria, nucleus, lysosomes, endoplasmic reticulum, golgi apparatus, endosomes etc

· describe vesicular trafficking (i.e. via the secretory and endocytic pathways)

· discuss cell signalling

· discuss the mechanisms and regulation of the cell cycle

· discuss the dynamics of cytoskeleton assembly and re-organization

· discuss the mechanics of cell division

Lab Learning Outcomes/Competencies

Upon successful completion of this course’s lab activities, the student will be able to:

· successfully grow and aseptically maintain both adherent and suspension animal cell cultures in vitro for an extended period of time

· successfully cryopreserve and reconstitute animal cell lines

· Perform cell transfections and assess optimal conditions for reporter gene expression

· culture, label and analyze cells on microscope slides (histochemical analysis)

· perform cell differentiation and metabolic assays

· perform a Western blot
