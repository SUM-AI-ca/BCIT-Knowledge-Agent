### Course Description
Directed Studies enables the students to propose an area of study that would lend itself to increased learning through the exploration of study in Selected Topics areas. This Technical Elective course of study may vary from term to term and may include subjects such as: technology and design; environmental ethics; Studies abroad, architectural workshops; Evaluation, Communication and Monitoring of Project deliveries. The student must submit a Statement of Intent which is to be reviewed and approved by the Program Head prior to the student's ability to register for this elective.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Ability to chose a selected area of study currently unavailable in the program.
Enhances the diversity of learning experiences.
Teaches the students effective specific management skills and critical thinking that would apply to their area of interest.
Emphasizes breadth of learning: encouraging cross-disciplinary approaches, ideas, and solutions, as well as discussing connections, comparisons, and continuities between and among fields of knowledge and technologies.
The course requires that students research a specific topic area outside of the required course materials, in order to cultivate research methodology skills. Students may also be required to present their ideas and research findings in an accepted academic format (e.g. research paper/report) which includes proper citation of sources.
The student may explore and participate in diverse socio-cultural learning opportunities related to their area of study (Studies Abroad).

### Course Goals
Through site visits, observation, and consultation with teachers, school staff and students, our students will develop a user-centred design proposal for an outdoor classroom.
- User-Centred and Context-Based Design: Analyze the developmental, physical, sensory, and social needs of elementary-school children in outdoor learning environments and translate them into spatial and programmatic design criteria.
- Client and Stakeholder Engagement: Collaborate professionally with a partner elementary school to identify project goals, constraints, safety concerns, and operational requirements. Integrate client and stakeholder feedback into iterative design development.
- Spatial and Human-Scale Design: Design outdoor spaces and structures at the scale of the child, using accurate anthropometric, ergonomic, and movement-based considerations. Demonstrate how users will occupy, move through, and learn within the proposed environments.
- Constructability and Technical Resolution: Develop outdoor learning structures using appropriate materials, structural systems, and weather-resistant assemblies. Demonstrate that the proposal is technically feasible, durable, and buildable.
- Integration of Landscape, Play, and Learning: Design environments that support multiple modes of learning including exploration, collaboration, physical activity, and quiet study. Integrate landscape, planting, terrain, and small-scale architectural elements into a cohesive outdoor learning setting.
- Codes, Safety, and Accessibility: Apply basic building, accessibility, and safety principles relevant to children’s outdoor environments. Incorporate risk management, supervision, and durability into the design.
- Technical and Graphic Communication: Produce a complete and coordinated set of scaled drawings including site plans, layouts, sections, elevations, and construction details. Communicate design intent, materiality, and construction logic through clear architectural representation.
- Professional Collaboration: Work effectively in small design teams, managing scope, time, and responsibilities in a real-project context. Participate constructively in design reviews and client presentations.
- Reflective and Ethical Practice: Evaluate the role of architecture in supporting child well-being, learning, and community life. Articulate the professional and ethical responsibilities of designing for public educational environments.
