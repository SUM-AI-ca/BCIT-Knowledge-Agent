## Course Description
This course builds on nucleic acid testing (NAT) fundamentals from MLSC 1314 & MLSC 2314 and applies them to clinical molecular analysis of human genomic sequences. NAT technologies discussed in detail include singleplex and multiplex amplification by real-time PCR using dye and probe detection chemistries. Human cellular processes of transcription and translation will be overviewed, as will basic human genetics, and standard nomenclature describing genomic DNA sequence variants. The molecular basis of select disease phenotypes will be discussed, with focus on the advantages and limitations of NAT relative to phenotypic testing. Select in vitro molecular testing methodologies used in the clinical lab to identify DNA polymorphisms and mutations will be discussed and practiced.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Apply molecular diagnostic principles to identify nucleic acid sequences, and Assess results, identify sources of interference/error, initiate corrective action and/or follow up testing
- Prepare patient DNA samples from clinical specimens using solid-phase spin column isolation/purification techniques
- Apply molecular principles to assess and determine appropriate PCR amplification conditions for real time assays
- Program and operate an end-point and real-time PCR thermal cycler
- Perform real-time PCR amplification following established quality assurance and quality control principles and procedures to minimize risk/identify presence of DNA contamination and PCR inhibitors
- Identify suspect patient and control PCR results and take appropriate follow-up action as required
