## Course Description
​This field course is designed to familiarize students with practical field skills and experience in plant and tree identification, recognizing soil components, identifying and surveying wildlife, and field navigation. Students will gain experience measuring, recording, and relating environmental variables to different habitats to complete forest stand assessments (i.e., site diagnosis). Students will also receive instruction in, and hands‐on experience with, GPS equipment. Field sessions with Indigenous communities, stakeholders and/or professionals working in the field will provide students with multiple perspectives of relevant and contemporary issues in natural resource management and the ecological, economic, and social factors influencing habitat stewardship.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Demonstrate the ability to work effectively and travel safely in field conditions.
- Identify birds and mammals using sight, vocalizations, tracks, and/or other signs.
- Identify tree, shrub, herb, and soil components of a site to support ecosystem classification.
- Describe site history as it relates to abiotic and biotic disturbances and human influence.
- Demonstrate use of a range of tools and technologies for navigating to and accessing a site and record observations.
- Demonstrate safe, respectful, and professional behaviour that meets industry standards and expectations​.

## Course Schedule and Assignments
This course is an intensive 1-week field excursion with long days off campus.

Day 1 Field orientation, practice and familiarization with sampling techniques.

Day 2 Wildlife sweeps, ARU's, quadrat establishment and utilization surveys

Day 3 Passive sampling and timed surveys, quadrat classification

Day 4 Recreational site assessments, wetland and acoustic sampling

Day 5 Safety exercise, gear cleaning and maintenance
