### Course Description
​This course provides a foundational understanding of field measurement and sampling techniques essential to resource-based forestry. Students will gain hands-on experience in various inventory and measurement procedures applicable to disciplines such as timber cruising and vegetation resources inventory (VRI), utilizing digital and analog field equipment. The course emphasizes accurate measurement, comprehensive data recording, and data interpretation. Standards and guidelines, such as those prepared by the British Columbia Provincial Resource Information Standards Committee (RISC), will be followed so that students develop fundamental skills and understanding of principles in forestry. Through a combination of lectures, hands-on exercises, and field trips, students will gain practical experience in various sampling methods, data collection, and statistical analysis.​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Describe the purpose of sampling and differentiate between simple random, systematic, and stratified sampling.
- Apply appropriate sampling techniques, including fixed-area plots, variable radius plots, and linear transects, for different forest types and management objectives.
- Assess the quantity and quality of trees and forest stands using diameter tapes, clinometers, nylon chains, and forest health indicators.
- Collect and record field data following a sampling plan using procedures and guidelines such as those provided by the British Columbia Provincial Resource Information Standards Committee (RISC).
- Compile, analyze, and interpret field data using basic statistical methods to draw conclusions about forest stand characteristics, such as volume, density, and species composition.
- Demonstrate professional conduct during field exercises, including following safety protocols, proper equipment handling, collaborative teamwork, and respect for the natural environment.

### Course Specific Requirements
Assignments (FNAM guidelines): Marks will be reduced by 10% for each 24-hour period (or fraction thereof) that an assignment is submitted beyond the due date. For instance, an assignment that would have earned 82% if submitted on time would earn 72% if submitted up to 24 hours late. If this assignment was submitted 25 hours later, it would earn 62%. The application of this penalty or alternate approaches to late submissions will be at the discretion of the course instructor.
Group Work (FNAM guidelines): Group work is a critical part of natural resource professions, and you will need to be able to work as a team effectively. Your learning in this course and program will be impacted if you are not able to collaborate and communicate effectively and respectfully with peers and instructors. Group assignments make up a significant portion of many courses, and it is expected that everyone contributes their fair share. If a student does not contribute their fair share to group work, instructors reserve the right to penalize their evaluations within reason. Repeated offences (lack of contributions to field or written work, inappropriate communications, distracted or inattentive conduct, etc.) will be treated more strongly.
