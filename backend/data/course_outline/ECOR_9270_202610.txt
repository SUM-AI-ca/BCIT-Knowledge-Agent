### Course Description
​In ECOR 9270 students will finalize their research proposal. The full proposal should include all details of the research, experimental design, statistics used (if appropriate), and milestones and deliverables. During the course students will reinforce many of the concepts presented in level one and two of the program, including experimental design, communicating with Indigenous peoples, the concepts of planning and monitoring in ER, project management, and public engagement. In addition to the proposal, students must develop a detailed safety plan, budget, and apply for all permits needed for their research.​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Develop a detailed, scientifically defendable research proposal
- Submit for approval a detailed safety plan for a research proposal
- Submit for approval a detailed budget
- Develop and implement a detailed communication plan with project partners, supervisor, faculty, and cohort about their proposed research​

### Course Schedule and Assignments
Research Proposal : This term, you will finalize your thesis proposal, expanding upon the conceptual proposal developed last term. Your comprehensive proposal must include standard components: Introduction, Objectives, Methods, Budget, Schedule, and References. If you are planning on applying for funding, your proposal will likely need to adhere to the template and timeline specified by the funding agency (e.g. MITACs). *IMPORTANT: Find information about the specific grant you are interested in and talk to your supervisor early on to ensure the proposal can be submitted on time to both the funding agency and BCIT VP Academic. The course schedule includes some timelines based on MITACs.
Your final proposal must be approved by your Supervisor and Thesis Advisory Committee.
Permits and Approvals: Developing your thesis proposal is an iterative process where different components are elaborated and refined in tandem. Your experimental design and methods will naturally evolve as you work on your proposal and refine the research question and objectives. These methods, in turn, will inform your timeline and budget, and highlight the need for any permits or approvals. You are responsible for preparing and submitting any permit applications required for your research.
Safety Plan: Considerations such as your research location and the nature of your research activities will be incorporated into a comprehensive safety plan, ensuring you conduct your research safely – as safety is paramount. The safety plan should be revised and approved by your supervisor and research partners before you can commence any field work.
Communications Plan: Throughout the research planning and implementation process, you will actively communicate your ideas with your supervisor, advisory committee, research partners, funders, authorities, indigenous and community members, etc., establishing clear commitments and deliverables (e.g., proposal to funding agency, thesis report, presentations, publications). This iterative refinement and communication will ultimately culminate in a robust and well-defined thesis plan. The communications plan will largely be embedded as part of your proposal and safety plan. However, you will prepare a separate, simple document, to help you keep track of relevant communications to be addressed.
The Thesis Research Proposal, safety plan and communications plan should be emailed to your supervisor and uploaded to the Learning Hub on the last day of class.
