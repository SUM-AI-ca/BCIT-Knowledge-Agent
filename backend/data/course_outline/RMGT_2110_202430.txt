## Course Description
This course provides a foundation of knowledge of risk management and the insurance industry. No prior experience or knowledge of insurance is required as this course covers the general principles that underlie the operation of insurance. Students are introduced to the concept of risk and shown how insurance deals with it. Students will study the basic concepts and language of insurance; the legal context of insurance; and the participants in the industry and their relationships to each other. Although specific types of insurance are not covered, the principles students learn underlie the situations encountered in the industry and every policy and course students will study throughout the program.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Explain the process of risk management, how it deals with the problems of risk.
- Identify the major categories of insurance and who provides each.
- Explain the process of ratemaking.
- Compare different types of insurers.
- Use common risk management and insurance terminology.
- Discuss the concept of insurable interest, indemnity, utmost good faith.
- Identify the elements of an insurance contract in a practical application.
- Analyze case study with respect to principles of risk and factors influencing insurance product cost.

Course description

This course introduces students to (just like the name says) Principles and Practice of Insurance. Starting with discovering risk, what it is, how it can be managed and ultimately financed through insurance or other means. The course then reviews key insurance industry functions; distribution, selling, underwriting, insurance policies, adjusting losses and reinsurance. Insurance policies are contracts, to understand contracts we look at the 2 legal systems in Canada, how they operate relating to contracts and how the Supreme Court of Canada deals with appeals from both systems. Finally the course reviews insurance industry regulations regarding policy wordings, licensing of insurance professionals, ethics and the role of industry organizations.

Course learning outcomes / competencies

Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Analyze what ‘risk’ means and how it relates to society.
- Describe the functions of insurance
- Discuss insurance company types, operation and organization.
- Explain how insurance is distributed to consumers, the legal relationship with clients and government regulation for insurance distribution
- Examine legal doctrines regarding general and insurance contract requirements including the differences in the 2 Canadian legal systems
- Differentiate between insurance rates and insurance premiums and describe how they are calculated
- Outline the claims process, how a claims department is organized and review claims terminology
