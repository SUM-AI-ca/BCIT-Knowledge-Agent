### Course Description
This course introduces students to basic organic chemistry. Major topics include hydrocarbons, alcohols, phenols, organohalogen compounds, amines, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, stereochemistry, carbohydrates, lipids, amino acids, and proteins. Laboratory exercises consist of quantitative and qualitative analyses, separation of organic compounds, and isolation and identification of natural products.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Draw complete and condensed structural formulas of organic compounds.
- Differentiate between polar, semipolar, and nonpolar compounds.
- Identify the functional groups in organic compounds.
- Explain differences in physical properties (solubility, melting point, boiling point) of compounds based on the types of intermolecular attractive forces.
- Explain whether vitamins are water-soluble or fat-soluble from their structures.
- Discuss the use of organic solvents and chemically active solvents in extractions.
- Identify and name the major classes of organic compounds (alkanes, alkenes, aromatics, alcohols, phenols, ethers, alkyl halides, amines, aldehydes, ketones, and carboxylic acids and derivatives).
- Describe the physical and chemical properties of the major classes of organic compounds.
- Determine the absolute configuration at chiral carbons.
- Recognize the significance of chemical structures where isomerism can exist in organic molecules.
- Identify compounds as stereoisomers, enantiomers, diastereomers, and meso compounds.
- Draw structures of lipids, carbohydrates, amino acids, and proteins.
- Explain the process of mutarotation of sugars.
- Describe how PAH's and other pollutants enter the food chain
- Discuss the effect of hydrogenation on the amount of saturated fats and trans fats
- Discuss the relationship between iodine number and the degree of unsaturation of fats
- Describe how soaps and detergents work to emulsify fats and oils.
- Describe the application of specific organic compounds to food technology.
