## Course Description
Directed Studies enables the students to propose an area of study that would lend itself to increased learning through the exploration of study in Selected Topics areas. This Technical Elective course of study may vary from term to term and may include subjects such as: technology and design; environmental ethics; Studies abroad, architectural workshops; Evaluation, Communication and Monitoring of Project deliveries. The student must submit a Statement of Intent which is to be reviewed and approved by the Program Head prior to the student's ability to register for this elective.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Ability to chose a selected area of study currently unavailable in the program.

Enhances the diversity of learning experiences.

Teaches the students effective specific management skills and critical thinking that would apply to their area of interest.

Emphasizes breadth of learning: encouraging cross-disciplinary approaches, ideas, and solutions, as well as discussing connections, comparisons, and continuities between and among fields of knowledge and technologies.

The course requires that students research a specific topic area outside of the required course materials, in order to cultivate research methodology skills. Students may also be required to present their ideas and research findings in an accepted academic format (e.g. research paper/report) which includes proper citation of sources.

The student may explore and participate in diverse socio-cultural learning opportunities related to their area of study (Studies Abroad).

## Course Goals
ARSC 7040 DIRECTED STUDIES 2025

Universal Design: Architecture and the senses

Statement of Purpose:

A directed studies course gives students the opportunity to propose a topic of investigation that is of particular interest to them. Whether the focus is on technology and design; ethics and the environment; culture and society; research-based or experiential learning – students can use this course to delve more deeply and examine more critically, an aspect of architecture for which they have a personal passion.

The student must submit a statement of intent in the form of a Research Proposal which is to be reviewed and approved by the Program Head, prior to the student registering for this course. This statement of intent should include a brief description of intermediate and final deliverables in accordance with the course outline and schedule. Evaluation will be on a pass/fail basis, rather than with a numerical grade.

Course Overview

More than 6 million Canadians aged 15 and over (22% of the population) identify as having a disability.1 Many of these disabilities can affects people’s level of independence and/or quality of life. This course will focus on a key aspect to independence - the ability to navigate through the built environment freely and safely. As upcoming practitioners that will be responsible for the future built environment this course is designed to encourage social responsibility and promote critical thinking on issues of equality in students.

The selected area of research for this course is architecture of the senses with the aim of providing end-users with interventions that support their safe and independent navigation through our course site.

The framework for the course revolves around three main modules. The first is Universal Design fundamentals that will provide the base of the research projects. Followed by the Lived Experience and Communities module where students get a chance to learn from community members and the last Module is the Site Intervention where the students will design, fabricate and install their interventions and hear feedback from the community.

## Course topics
The focus for 2025, an often neglected, architectural sensory palette.

“Typically considered as separate entities acting individually, the senses tend to work in synergy to inform each other of a cohesive experience. The distinguished contribution of each sense resulting in holistic immersion is a concept known as Phenomenology. Sensory cues induce emotive characteristics in a space. These can be visual, auditory, olfactory, environmental or haptic cues of any nature.”

- Rushika H.P.

Potential research topics that involve the senses in understanding the built environment could include but is not limited to the following:

- The following topics serve as a departure point for further investigation include:
- Using colour coding for navigation
- Using sound for navigation
- Tactile paving / Guidance paths
- Tactile signs and braille signage
- Tactile maps - handheld
- Tactile maps - built in
- Contrast markings
