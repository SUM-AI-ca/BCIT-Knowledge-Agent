## Course Description
​This course exposes students to technical mathematics with emphasis on its application to surveying. Topics include trigonometric functions of any angle, solution of triangles, identities and trigonometric equations, spherical trigonometry, systems of linear equations, and analytic geometry. Students learn the topics through lectures and hands-on labs.​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Use basic algebraic rules and operations.
- Rearrange formulae used in geomatics.
- Solve line intersection problems.
- Formulate and solve elementary geometric properties of circles.
- Solve right-angle triangles using trigonometry. Use right-angle trigonometry to solve applied surv
- Use radian measure in the solution of surveying problems involving circular arcs.
- Use the sine and cosine laws for solving oblique triangles.
- Apply the sine and cosine laws to elevation problems and traverses.
- Carry out the simplification of trigonometric expressions using trigonometric identities.
- Solve simple trigonometric equations.
- Use statistical parameters in reducing surveying measurements.
- Apply area formulae to problems such as finding the area contained in a traverse and the area contained within a lot with an irregular boundary.
- Apply volume formulae to problems such as cut and fill.
- Solve right-angled spherical triangles.
- Solve oblique spherical triangles.
- Use spherical triangles for determining azimuth and bearing.
- Produce histograms and other statistical plots from raw, observed survey data.
- Calculate measures of central tendency and variation such as mean, median, variance and standard deviation from geomatics measurement data.
- Define the basic laws of probability.
- Connect probability to the randomness of error in measurement.
- Define continuous and discrete probability distributions, such as the normal, binomial, student's-t, chi-square and F-distribution, and calculate their mean and variance.
- Use the normal distribution to determine probabilities of observing defined ranges of measurement in geomatics projects.
- Estimate confidence intervals for large and small samples and apply them to engineering and cadastral surveys and the analysis of error in least-squares adjustments.
- Conduct various hypothesis tests, including mean, variance, and correlation, and recognize the associated Type I and II errors.
- Investigate error propagation in geomatics calculations.
- Calculate the principal parameters of bivariate data, that is, the correlation coefficient and variance-covariance matrices, and relate them to adjustment of parameters.

## Learning Resources
Course notes and lab exercises will be provided in class and through the Learning Hub. Students will require a scientific calculator (for instance, a SHARP EL-520X, or TI-30Xa).

There is no required textbook. A recommended (optional) textbook is Basic Technical Mathematics with Calculus by Allyn Washington. There are various editions, but you will find the 10th edition (SI version) on reserve in the library.

## Course Schedule and Assignments
Week 1: Introduction; Using Formulae; Order of Operations; Using Scientific Calculators
Week 2: Significant Digits; Perimeter, Area, Volume; Pythagoras; Units; Linear Equations
Week 3: Degrees, Minutes, Seconds; Trig Ratios; Solving Right Triangles; Vectors
Week 4: Ratios and Proportions; Geometric Similarity
Week 5: Trig Equations; Radians; Functions; Linear Systems
Week 6: Matrix Methods; Exponents; Complex Numbers
Week 7: Functions; Factoring; Quadratic Equations
Week 8: Conics; Applications of Conics
Week 9: Circles; Trig Identities; Trig Graphs
Week 10: More Trig Identities; Oblique Triangles
Week 11: Spherical Trigonometry
Week 12: Spherical Trig; Irregular Areas
Week 13: Statistics and Parameters; Confidence Intervals/Exponential and Log Equations
Week 14: Final Exam
