## Course Description
Apply theory and laboratory practices to correlate knowledge of clinical chemistry to patient test results and determine the validity of testing data based on sample acceptability criteria, quality assurance/ quality control criteria, and the correlation of test results with patient symptoms, diagnosis, and other related analyte concentrations. Gain knowledge and skills in handling priority testing and critical alert values. New concepts, topics and methods of analysis emphasized in the lectures are practiced in the laboratory sessions. Through various simulation activities, both self-directed and cooperative learning will be used to evaluate clinically relevant case studies and laboratory scenarios. Evaluation of learning is conducted through a range of theory based written assessments, and practical laboratory and skills demonstrations. ​

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Demonstrate further safe work practices by applying standard precautions, laboratory hygiene, infection control practices and use of personal protective equipment and laboratory safety devices.
- Build on analytical processes utilizing light measuring, electrochemical, osmometry, and point of care systems.
- Describe and perform analytical techniques utilizing reflectance and immunoassay methods.
- Maintain and operate automated chemistry instrumentation according to standard operating procedures.
- Illustrate proficiency in dilution techniques with various laboratory pipettes and solution preparation techniques.
- Perform and interpret macroscopic and microscopic urinalysis
- Evaluate and discuss analytical quality requirements to ensure accuracy of laboratory results by applying pre-examination, examination, and post-examination checks.
- Identify potential sources of analytical error or limitations and employ corrective action.
- Apply analytical method principles that assess additional organ system functions learned in term 1
- Conduct appropriate action according to established protocols when patient results are identified as critical, implausible, out of analytical range, and/or priority testing is required.
- Validate, document, interpret, and correlate patient test results with clinical conditions and initiate any follow-up testing prior to reporting.
- Interact using effective communication, interpersonal skills, and team skills to maintain professional relationships.

## Attendance Requirements
Note: The following policies are specific to the Medical Laboratory Science Program. Basic Health Science and Communication courses may have different policies as they are under a different program.

Due to the unique nature of how each course is assessed within the Medical Laboratory Science Program, the details of the policy may be different from discipline to discipline. Please make sure you review each Course Outlines in detail.

Definitions:

Evaluation – any academic event that results in a mark being given. This includes but is not limited to: labs, presentations, quizzes, midterms, skills assessments, and final exams.

Avoidable, non-medical reason – examples include but are not limited to; sleeping in, missing public transit, traffic. There may be specific scenarios where these reasons may be unavoidable, these will be reviewed on a case by case basis.

Missing an evaluation or lab due to a medical or unavoidable reason:

a. The student must contact the instructor as soon as possible, ideally prior to the evaluation or lab time.

b. If it is a medical reason, the student will be expected to submit a BCIT Student Medical Certificate form (attached) to the Instructor for review.

c. For last minute medical issues, a doctor’s note can be initially submitted to the instructor. However, a complete BCIT medical certificate must follow and be presented as soon possible.

d. If a BCIT Medical Certificate is not submitted then the mark on the evaluation may be changed to a 0.

e. For other unavoidable reasons, supporting documentation is expected. Students that meet these criteria will have the opportunity to make up the missed evaluation or lab.

Missing or late to an evaluation or lab due to a non-medical, or avoidable reason and no valid documentation provided to support absence from the evaluation or lab:

a. The student is not eligible to make up the missed evaluation and will be given a mark of 0.

b. The student is not eligible to make up a missed lab.

c. The student will not be given extra time to complete the evaluation if they are late.

In certain circumstances, the instructor may at their own discretion allow for an excused absence from an evaluation due to non-medical reason but the discussion between the student and the instructor must occur as soon as possible, preferably before the evaluation date. The decision on the parameters of the re-evaluation must be documented and acknowledged by both parties. The student is still expected to make up the missed evaluation. Note that this does not guarantee that an absence will be excused.
