## Course Description
This course teaches students the best practices for design fundamentals across media, producing results suitable for web, mobile applications, and other interactive environments. Students will learn how to present the client’s message or objective in a manner that resonates with the target audience and enhances overall communication, human interaction, and usability. The design fundamental course provides a solid foundation for delivering interactive and engaging content for web and mobile applications. This course also includes screen-design principles, digital colour theory; typography; web and mobile page layout and design; correct use of white space; web and mobile graphics, images, and interface design. Students will have hands-on practice developing a series of conceptual projects that demonstrate their work towards creating a website in collaboration with other courses in this program.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:

- Discuss the importance of fundamental design principles for web and mobile applications (screen size, colour, typography, and responsiveness).
- Identify key design messages.
- Compare design principles for digital and print projects.
- Develop digital projects for web and mobile interfaces by applying basic design principles.
- Perform a range of research approaches required for establishing a web and mobile project goals.
- Apply communications theory (rhetoric, nostalgic, semiotics, etc.) when creating graphic assets for multiplatform applications.
- Create a design prototype for desktop, web, or mobile application.
- Design layout and create visual assets for a collaborative web or mobile project.

## Attendance Requirements
1. Submission of Assignments and Projects

a. All assignments and projects are expected to be submitted by the designated deadline, which is typically at the beginning of the class unless otherwise specified. Submission methods may include electronic uploads or physical hand-ins, depending on the nature of the assignment.

b. No late work will be accepted, except under specific circumstances such as in cases of severe illness or a documented injury requiring medical aid

3. Advanced Notification and Extensions

a. If a student anticipates an absence or foresees needing extra time to complete an assignment, it is their responsibility to communicate with the instructor at least 1 week prior to the assignment's due date. Such notification does not guarantee an extension but allows for discussion and evaluation of the situation.

b. No extension requests will be granted on the day of the deadline or within 48 hours before the deadline unless substantial progress on the assignment has been demonstrated. It is recommended that students plan their work schedules and commitments accordingly to meet assignment deadlines.

4. Artificial Intelligence, Self-Plagiarism and Originality

a. Students are expected to produce original work for all assignments and projects. Reusing work previously submitted in other classes is strictly prohibited, constituting self-plagiarism. Any instance of self-plagiarism will result in a zero for the entire project and a penalty for unprofessional conduct.

b. In cases where students utilize AI tools or resources to aid in their assignments, proper referencing of the AI-generated content's sources is crucial. If AI-generated elements are incorporated into an assignment, students must acknowledge the AI tools used and provide appropriate attribution to the sources.

c. It is encouraged for students to always seek clarification and guidance from the instructor when in doubt about the ethical use of AI, proper citation, or the incorporation of AI-generated content. Open communication is essential to maintain the integrity of their work and uphold academic honesty.

d. Any use of AI-generated content, whether in part or in full, that is presented as the student's original creation without proper attribution will be considered a case of plagiarism. Such instances will result in a zero for the entire project and a penalty for unprofessional conduct.

5. Participation and Group Critiques

a. Active engagement during group critiques is an essential aspect of collaborative learning. Students are expected to contribute meaningfully and thoughtfully during these sessions. Non-participation in group critiques, without valid reasons, will result in deductions from professionalism and participation marks.

b. Furthermore, if a student is absent when an assignment is due, it will impact their overall participation and professionalism assessment. Deductions will be made from these marks to reflect the importance of being present during assignment submissions and participating fully in the course activities.

6. Early Departures and Unexcused Absences

a. Students who anticipate leaving a class early should notify the instructor in advance to make necessary arrangements.

b. If a student's unexcused absences surpass 10% of the overall course duration by the Withdrawal Deadline, the instructor will issue a cautionary email. This email serves as a notification to the student, outlining the potential consequences of continued unaccounted non-attendance. Failure to reply or take action within 2 days of receiving the email will lead to the assignment of a 'V' grade and the cessation of further assignment grading.

c. In case of illness or other unavoidable absence, you must communicate with your instructor, as soon as possible (preferably in advance) indicating the reason for your absence. In instances where you are missing an exam or important assessment, or for absences more than 10 days, you must provide documentation: either a BCIT-approved medical certificate (https://www.bcit.ca/files/healthservices/pdf/studentmedicalcertificate.pdf) in the case of illness; or reach out to your instructor regarding other appropriate supporting documentation for other emergencies.
