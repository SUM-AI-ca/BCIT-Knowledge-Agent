### Course Description
​Forest engineering is a complex discipline that requires critical thinking and problem solving to sustainably manage our forests. Within BC is a vast network of resource roads and trails which provide access to a range of commercial and public users. This course provides a comprehensive overview of resource road and trail planning, design, construction, and management and will also introduce students to the principles of forest operations such as harvest planning, harvesting systems, harvesting equipment, and access planning. Students will gain practical skills in surveying, mapping, and construction techniques, while also exploring the economic, social, and regulatory context of resource road development and access in British Columbia. ​ ​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Analyze terrain, assess user needs, and apply sustainable design principles for resource roads and trails, considering environmental impacts and best management practices.
- Perform field surveys using standard equipment and techniques, and process collected data to produce accurate maps, profiles, and cross-sections of resource roads and trails.
- Compare and contrast benefits and impacts of resource roads and trails in BC.
- Summarize common resource road regulations in B.C., including communication methods used to inform commercial and public users.
- Describe the key principles of forest operations, including harvest planning, different harvesting systems and their associated equipment, and the importance of access planning for sustainable forest management.
