## Course Description
​Sonography Clinical 2 is a continuation of DSON 2004 Sonography Clinical 1. Students in this course gain hands-on experience in the settings of abdomen, pelvis, obstetrics, vascular, and/or cardiac sonography. Students who successfully complete this course will be able to perform skills satisfactorily in more difficult or complex cases with assistance. Essential core competencies are emphasized and assessed through this course. Examples of these include effective communication, safe practice, patient care, and integration of academic knowledge. For a complete list of core competencies and clinical proficiency levels see the associated clinical course shell in the Learning Hub.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- ​Demonstrate professional behaviour at all times and in accordance with Sonography Canada's code of ethics and scope of practice. (2.1f, 2.1e, 2.1c, 2.2d, 2.2e, 2.2i, 2.3a, 2.3b, 2.3h)
- Demonstrate effective verbal and non-verbal communication with patients, their support persons, and other healthcare professionals. (1.1a, 1.1b, 1.1d, 1.1e, 1.1g, 1.1h, 1.1i, 1.1j, 1.1k, 1.3a,1.3b, 2.3f)
- Obtain and record accurate and relevant patient history based on clinical indications. (1.2b, 2.2b, 2.2c, 4.1c, 5.1a)
- Implement safe practice to include appropriate patient care and own physical safety. (2.2a, 2.1e, 2.2f, 2.2h, 3.1c, 3.1d, 3.1e, 3,1f, 4.2d, 6.2a, 6.2b, 6.2c)
- Formulate scanning strategies incorporating relevant clinical information, patient conditions, and knowledge of disease processes while applying problem-solving skills consistent with the level of experience. (2.2c, 2.2d, 2.2e, 2.2f, 2.2g, 2.2h, 2.2i, 3.1b, 5.1c, 5.1d, 5.1e, 5.3b, 5.3c, 5.3k)
- Produce accurate and diagnostic images, in a timely manner, demonstrating findings using spatial reasoning and according to departmental protocols of structures listed in NCP 6.1 appendices A to E. (4.1a, 4.1b, 4.1c, 4.2a, 4.2b,4.2d, 4.2g, 4.2e, 4.2f, 4.2g, 5.3a, 5.3b, 5.3c, 5.3d, 5.3e, 5.3f, 5.3h, 5.3i, 5.3l, 5.4a, 5.4d)
- Identify and respond to urgent sonographic findings aligned with best practices and site protocols. (2.2g)
- Formulate and present to physicians an accurate technical impression based on findings, and/or limitations of exam quality in a timely manner. (1.2c, 1.2d, 1.2e, 2.3g, 5.4b, 5.4f, 5.4g)
- Appraise performance and critically outline goals for self-improvement. (2.4a)
- Demonstrate a collaborative interprofessional approach to deliver patient-centred care, using the IPE competencies as a guide. (2.3c, 2.3d)
- Correlate exam indication with given laboratory and diagnostic imaging results, and patient history or findings. (5.1a, 5.1b, 5.1c)

## Evaluation Criteria
Criteria: Grading: Comments
Progress Forms: Formative: Completed on Comptracker by student and Clinical Liaison. Reviewed by faculty. The schedule for forms is outlined in the Learning Hub course shell and is dependent on the length of each clinical rotation.
End Summary Evaluations: Summative: The BCIT DMS program will determine if a student is meeting expectations based on feedback solicited from the assigned clinical sites. BCIT faculty visits may be arranged to further assess student progress. Students must consistently meet all outlined criteria, defined in the clinical proficiency levels and core competencies, to successfully meet expectations. Refer to the shared, and individual student Performance Target Summary for clinical expectations. Students not meeting expectations for a rotation will be placed on a performance contract for the next rotation Students who are approaching the outlined performance expectations may be provided with an opportunity for a clinical extension that will be contingent on site availability Students who are unable to demonstrate consistent practice will be deemed unsafe to practice and may be removed from the program.
Registry Preparation Quizzes: S/U: Multiple choice quizzes are based on all Level 1-3 DMS coursework. A minimum grade of 80% must be achieved over a maximum of 2 attempts to pass each quiz. Quizzes must be passed to receive a satisfactory grade
HSIP 0400: S/U: Students must achieve a minimum of 65% in the workshop to receive a Satisfactory grade
Final Grade: S/U: Course grading is S/U (Satisfactory / Unsatisfactory). Students must meet targets on End Summary evaluations, quizzes, and HSIP 0400 to pass this course

## Attendance Requirements
Sick days: A maximum of 8 absences, due to illness, is permissible for the duration of the entire 39-week clinical practicum. A physician's note is required when 3 consecutive days are missed.

Special Leave Days: A maximum of 4 special leave days are available for the entire 39-week clinical practicum. Special leave days must meet a specific set of criteria, and advance approval is required from the BCIT Clinical Coordinator.

- Students exceeding the above limits may be required to make up clinical days after the official program end date. This decision is at the discretion of the program.
- This decision is at the discretion of the program.
- Unused sick or special leave is not considered as owing to the student.​

Punctuality: Students must be ready to commence scanning by their assigned start time.

- A pattern of late arrivals will not be tolerated and will be addressed individually. Extra clinical time may be required.

Poor attendance and punctuality demonstrate a lack of professionalism which can lead to removal from the program.

Clinical days are typically 8 hours including lunch and breaks. Clinical days will vary and may include evening and/or weekend days. Assigned days will be at the discretion of the clinical departments if not previously arranged with BCIT. Overtime may be allowed, and only with program permission.

- All absences and late arrivals must be logged in Comptracker.

## Safety equipment or protective clothing
Students must follow departmental policies and procedures diligently. Students are expected to don all required personal protective equipment (PPE) to maintain safety in the clinical environment during such as;

- Gloves
- Gowns
- Surgical Masks
- Face Shield/Eye protection

Safety equipment will be provided to Sonography students in the clinical environment as per the provincial student practice guidelines.
