### Course Description
Visual composition and layout focuses on the process of creating ideas and learning how to effectively prepare balanced, creative, design solutions. Students will apply the principles and elements of composition and layout in order to produce balanced creative design solutions for print, web, mobile, interface applications. Through analysis, group critiques, presentations, teamwork, and verbal deconstruction of design, students will learn how to present their work in a manner that resonates with the target audience—enhances overall communication, human interaction and usability. This course provides a solid foundation for delivering interactive and engaging content for web and mobile
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Compare design principles and elements for use in various media.
- Describe and apply visual composition principles and terminology to reinforce communication and concept.
- Design digital layouts with a clear, focused message, following set criteria.
- Organize visual elements by applying principles of alignment and a grid system.
- Direct the viewer's eye through the layout via page shape/orientation, client content and negative space.
- Discuss the importance of fundamental design principles for web and mobile communications.
- Perform a range of research approaches required for establishing web and mobile project goals.
- Apply communications theory (rhetoric, nostalgic and semiotic, etc) when creating graphic assets for multiplatform applications.
- Create a design prototype for desktop, web or mobile application.
- Create visual and typographic hierarchy within a design layout.
- Design and create visual assets for a collaborative web or mobile project.
- Describe typographic terms.
- Analyze layouts and define how they work in terms of compositional and organizational principles.

### Course Specific Requirements
Participation A critical factor for success in this class is being actively engaged in all aspects of the course. Design, in part, is an attitude, not a technical exercise. It is an attitude towards how one sees, approaches, and interacts with their surroundings. This means asking questions, being curious, taking chances, being rigorous, working with others, embracing criticism, accepting anxiety and ambiguity, and always trying to push your design a little further than you thought possible. Participation comprises an important part of your final grade. Working on projects from other courses in class time is strictly prohibited.
Communication You can contact me through e-mail, immediate replies to evening, weekend, or holiday e-mails should not be assumed. Please allow 24-48 hours for a response. E-mail correspondence should be used to alert me of any expected absences or lateness although it will not excuse the absence. Be aware that I could use BCIT E-mails accounts or Brightspace to provide additional source information related to a project or perhaps changes or guidance should an unforeseen circumstance occur so check your mail and Brightspace regularly.
Deadlines All assignments and projects are due at the specific date set out by the instructor. Late assignments will be graded with a penalty of 10% when late on due date, and 20% when turned in the following day. After that, assignments and/or projects will be marked zero.
Submission Cleanness and organization are important part of graphic design work; students are expected to present projects in a clean, organized way. Every project should follow the specific requirements described in the project brief like format and technical aspects of files. A percentage of each project is considered for quality and presentation.
All files must be submitted as requested in the brief. Be sure to name your files using the class convention: “Last name_First name_Project##.pdf” e.g. Brown_Jack_Project01.pdf
