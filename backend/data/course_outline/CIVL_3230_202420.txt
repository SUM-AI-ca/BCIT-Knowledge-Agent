## Course Description
Covers survey fundamentals and quantity take-off of civil engineering projects pertaining to roadwork, water, sewer and building construction. Topics include quantity take-off, productivity rates, labour and material costing, construction equipment and machinery, and project cost control. .

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Read and interpret typical civil construction drawings.
- Identify common construction equipment and their applications.
- Identify fundamental construction activities for a typical construction project.
- Prepare portions of a contractor's estimate.
- Use basic project cost control on a project.

## Learning Resources
Please attend class with the following:

- Scientific Calculator (mobile phone calculator can work, though I find them less efficient)

- Ruler (scale is better but not necessary) or software that can scale measure, Pencil

- 11 x 17 (tabloid size) copy of the LGCA drawings, set of 4 available on the BCIT Learn course page file folder

- Download the course notes, for the upcoming class, to follow on your computer, or print out if you prefer.

Other resources you do not need for class though may be useful in your career as an estimator:

- RS Means Heavy Construction. This book has North American standard estimating costs.

- Caterpillar Equipment Handbook. This book is a good resource to identify typical equipment capacities/productions/fuel economy

- Construction Equipment Management, John Schaulfelberger. I believe other programs at BCIT have required this book, so you may already have it.

## Course Goals
By the end of this course you will develop the following skills:

- Ability to read and interpret civil drawings and associated tender information

- Identify equipment required for a typical civil project

- Develop basic first principle estimates

- Have a general understanding of most costs that will apply to a typical civil tender

## Course Specific Requirements
Please ensure you have awareness with typical geometry, such as area, volume of shapes common in the construction industry. In addition be able to convert from metric to imperial and vice versa as well as use specific gravity for establishing material weights.

Math and basic financial understanding is key to being an excellent estimator.
