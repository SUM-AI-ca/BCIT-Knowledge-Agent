## Course Description
This course is focused on developing the student’s understanding of Cadastral Surveying and Land Use Planning issues that affect the Land Surveying profession. The basic concepts cadastral surveying including Canadian governance and the legal system is introduced so that students gain an appreciation for the laws and rules under which Land Surveyors must practice. The effect of legislation, court decisions and bylaws are illustrated. The history of survey systems and practice is covered to provide an understanding of how historical records influence the modern day cadastral fabric and land title systems. Students will be introduced to planning issues including: legislation; long term planning as associated with official community plans and community visions; land use choices, site analysis; development potential, and the intensity of land use; environmental issues; public involvement; the planning process and costing

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Recognize the types of law in the Canadian legal system.
- Identify sources of law.
- Describe the process of creating a law in various Canadian jurisdictions.
- Describe the various estates in land.
- Describe the differences between covenants, statutory rights of ways and easements.
- Identify the components of the Dominion Land Survey System (DLS).
- Re-state the concept of convergence of meridians in the DLS system.
- Illustrate a township system to the level of a legal subdivision.
- Illustrate the concept of ranges and townships.
- Identify types of boundaries.
- Apply the principle of pro-rationing to re-establish boundaries.
- Determine the acceptance or rejection of legal evidence when determining boundaries.
- Recognize legal monumentation for boundary demarcation.
- Research and report on Land Registry Systems and various types of Multipurpose Cadastres.
- Recognize various terms used in Land Descriptions.
- Create a Land Description using metes and bounds.
- Re-create a parcel from a metes and bounds description.
- Re-state concepts of Aboriginal Land ownership.
- Apply ethics to solve a variety of cadastral dilemmas.
- Understand planning concepts including sustainable development, land use and development potential, floor space ratio, units per acre, gross and net lot area.
- Describe land use regulations and the role of government in land development.
- Describe the role of surveyors in the planning and development process.
