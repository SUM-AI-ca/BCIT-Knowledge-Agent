## Course Description
This course provides an overview of the contemporary Canadian business environment. The political, legal, regulatory and trading environment in which firms operate will be examined. Students will explore the operation of industries in the global economy and assess challenges and opportunities presented by environmental factors.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Evaluate potential sources of competitive intelligence.
- Select ethical approaches to gathering and using competitive intelligence.
- Interpret data and information relating to an organization and its business activities.
- Assess impact of various components of the Canadian and global business environments using PESTLE (Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal and Environmental) analysis.
- Evaluate the level of competition in an industry.
- Conclude whether an organization’s corporate social responsibility programs are appropriate and effective.
- Assess the business climate and culture of Canada’s major trading partners.
- Assess the impact of international trade agreements on an industry.
- Plan a response to the opportunities and threats presented by the business environment.
- Propose implementation plans for strategic and operational initiatives.

## Other information
- In this course, the student will acquire an overview of the contemporary Canadian and International business environment along with the research and analytical skills required to identify and assess environmental factors.
- Students will conduct extensive research for their presentations (individual and team).
- Presentation skills are developed as each student will deliver at least two presentations. Students receive feedback after their presentations from the instructor and the other students.
- Each student will be individually evaluated on their classroom participation and contribution. The evaluation criteria are frequency and quality of contributions to the discussion. Active listening is encouraged such that all students have a chance to contribute. Your voice must be heard to achieve a good evaluation.

The first half of the course will focus on specific industries chosen to reflect a cross section of industries important to the BC and Canadian economies.

To understand Canada's competitiveness amongst other nations, the second half of the course focuses on Canada's largest and important trading partners.

## Guidelines for School of Business + Media
Attendance: Regular attendance in lectures and labs is seen as integral to student success, therefore, attendance in class is monitored. Unexcused absences in excess of 10% of the time prescribed for this course may result in the assignment of a failing grade. Attendance may be taken at any time during class. A student not present for attendance will be marked absent. A student who leaves class for a period of time deemed excessive by the instructor may be considered absent regardless of whether they are present when attendance is taken.
