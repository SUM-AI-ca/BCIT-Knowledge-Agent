### Course Description
This is the second of four clinical education courses. The course focuses on attaining competencies on procedures of the entire skeleton (excluding cranium and thoracic cage), chest, abdomen, gastrointestinal system, fluoroscopic and operating room procedures. Students will be scheduled in all areas related to patient imaging practice such as general imaging department, acute/trauma areas, operating room, patient wards, and outpatient clinics. In addition, students will be scheduled on various shifts including days, afternoons, nights and weekends to experience a variety of procedures. Patient competency evaluations are performed on patients with varying medical acuity. Students will continue working on their patient care and positioning skills providing them with opportunities to become both competent and confident in a different imaging department.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Adhere to legal and regulatory requirements, apply ethical principles, and demonstrate professional integrity.
- Demonstrate social responsibility and cultural awareness.
- Establish and maintain effective communication.
- Obtain, evaluate, and share information.
- Employ effective team processes to coordinate patient care in low and medium acuity settings.
- Manage conflict in collaboration with others with assistance.
- Practice in a manner that contributes to safe care.
- Conduct relevant patient assessment in partnership with patients and families.
- Provide compassionate care for patient’s physiological, cognitive and psychological needs, including adaptations to positioning and/or protocol in response to patient condition and clinical environment.
- Comply with appropriate infection prevention and control standards and/or regulations.
- Provide education and support to patients and families/support persons.
- Identify factors in the clinical environment that may impact delivery of care with assistance.
- Prioritize workflow to optimize patient care.
- Contribute to limited quality assurance initiatives as directed.
- Demonstrate self-awareness.
- Integrate safe work principles and procedures into practice.
- Manage a variety of imaging systems.
- Integrate clinical principles into practice for procedures mentioned below.
- Perform diagnostic and therapeutic procedures.
- chest, abdomen, extremities, spine, pelvis, cranium, thorax, and fluoroscopic and specialized procedures
- Administer substances required for clinical procedures
- Analyze image and data quality and respond for low and medium acuity levels and common skeletal pathologies, anomalies, and conditions.

### Evaluation Criteria
This course is graded on a Satisfactory (S) / Unsatisfactory (U) basis. The final grade is determined by the summative evaluation, which integrates evidence from formative assessments, the midterm assessment, and completed portfolio requirements. Successful completion of all assessments, portfolio requirements, and a Satisfactory summative evaluation are required to earn an overall grade of Satisfactory (S); failure to meet any of these criteria will result in a grade of Unsatisfactory (U) and failure of the course.

### Attendance Requirements
In case of illness or other unavoidable cause of absence, the student must communicate as soon as possible with his/her Clinical Instructor and/or designate via the appropriate procedures outlined in clinical and indicating the reason for the absence. Students who are seeking accommodation for an extended medical absence must have a BCIT approved medical certificate submitted to the department Program Head and Clinical Coordinator(s), substantiating the reason for absence. For other absences, the student should be prepared to provide appropriate supporting documentation. Unapproved absences in excess of the prescribed regulations within this outline may result in forced withdrawal or failure in the course or program. Please see Policy 5101 - Student Regulations and accompanying procedures.
Clinical Tardiness
Tardiness is considered unprofessional and will be documented on the attendance register. Any instance of tardiness in Clinical must be reported to the Clinical Instructor and documented appropriately and reported by email to the Clinical Coordinator(s) or Program Head as soon as possible.
- Tardiness is not tolerated in the clinical area. Tardiness of 30 minutes or less will require two hours to be made up.
- Tardiness of 30–60 minutes will be considered a ½ day (4 hours) absence and must be made up.
- Tardiness in excess of 60 minutes will be managed on a case-by-case basis through collaboration between the BCIT CC and Site CI.
Make-up time in clinical may not begin without the prior approval of the Clinical Instructor. If make-up time is required, it will be necessary for students to extend the workday by a maximum of two hours each day until the required time is made up. The time must be made up prior to the end of the term and arranged with the Clinical Instructor/Clinical Coordinator. If it is not fulfilled, the term will be considered incomplete and will result in failure
