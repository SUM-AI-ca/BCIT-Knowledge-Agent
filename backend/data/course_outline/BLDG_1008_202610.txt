### Course Description
Presents an overview of the design process for creating garden layouts and planning plans with an emphasis on student initiated projects. Of interest to homeowners and gardeners who wish to expand their design fluency and to landscape contractors wishing to expand their range of services.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
- Basic understanding of the methodology and principles of landscape design.

### Course Goals
Course Goals
This is an introductory course which provides the methodology and basic principles for residential landscape design, using a project site supplied by the student. The student applies the process and design principles in a sequential way to his/her selected site, resulting in a Preliminary Landscape Concept Plan for that site.
The student takes measurements and draws plans to scale of the key existing features of the site and its’ immediate context. The student assesses the site's opportunities and constraints; and defines a program for functional and desired uses. The student determines a preferred organization and placement of these functions as they relate to the plan of existing features of the site. The student then investigates direction and preferences in landscape styles. From this the student applies the principles of design and form composition to create a Preliminary Landscape Concept Plan. Site measurement, sketching, image selection and drawing plans to scale are skills developed in this course.

### Course Specific Requirements
Learning Format
· In-classroom format :
- This course is offered in person, at the classroom designated.
- This course includes a Lecture component each week. It also includes Studio exercises to explore landscape design or develop useful skills.
- Examples of student assignments will be discussed each week.
· Use of Learning Hub course website : Course URL: https://learn.bcit.ca/d2l/home/1186871.
- Lecture and reference tools will be made available as handouts in class each week, and will also be posted to the course’s Learning Hub, Content tab. Additional reference content is available to download.
- Hand in assignments , in pdf format, under the Assignments tab. Full sized Prints are also required from time to time, additionally.
· Those without electronic equipment are welcome to take the class also. Discuss your situation with the instructor if you plan on preparing all work by hand.
Assignment Details
The work done in this class is cumulative: Assignments and exercises are components which build toward the completion of the Preliminary Landscape Concept Plan, the final assignment. Skipping portions of assignments will impact the final plan, sometimes severely. Instructor feedback includes suggestions for adjustments to your work, intended as guidance in shaping the final assignment.
· Assignments are prepared as either scale drawings on oversized sheets, sketch drawings on smaller sheets, or images and text on letter sized sheets. Drawing may be done by hand or using CAD. Assignments are typically scanned to pdf format for submittal when due, and oversized prints are also be required as noted in Weekly Outline.
· Your Plans will be oversized scale drawings (a given length on the drawing represents a measurement on the ground). Sheet size is commonly A1, 23 x 33 inches, or 24 x 36 inches.
· For those times when a print is required, check for locations and hours of an architectural plans printer near you in order to have your work printed in time for Saturday morning. Most printers are open M - F 9 to 5pm.
· Ensure your work is titled and dated or numbered. All measured drawings to be submitted are to have a bar scale drawn near the title block. Don't forget to include the north arrow on your plans.
· Please ensure to review assignment details carefully to avoid doing unnecessary or inapplicable work.
For Studio, be prepared to BRING to each class:
· Trace paper, straight edge and pens/pencils. Sometimes your scale will be needed as well. We will not be drafting in class.
· In-progress work: The instructor takes time each class to review some students’ work, during studio time. Be prepared to show your in-progress work with any questions you may have. For those working on CAD using BCIT labs, save your work as a pdf or print to bring to class for discussion.
· Typically the instructor is available after class if you would like Office Hours for a longer discussion.
· Note: there are only a very limited number of power outlets in the classroom. Please be prepared.
Academic Integrity, and the use of AI:
The student is responsible to independently produce work submitted under their own name, and to acknowledge all contributions and sources including use of generative AI tools. Judicious use of generative AI tools may be used for brainstorming idea generation and text editing, but the student is responsible to evaluate and edit what is generated to ensure the work submitted is appropriate and demonstrates understanding of the project and assignment goals.
