### Course Description
This course will introduce learners to the fundamental structure of the Canadian broadcast media system. Students will explore various factors including historical developments that have led to the creation of both the public and private regulatory structure in Canada. Media plays an important role in the social and political life of Canadians. This course is designed to underscore and enhance the students’ understanding of that role. Various perspectives will be discussed, including issues surrounding culture, ethics, gender and media bias. More complex issues around media regulation, policy and ethics will be examined in Policy, Regulation and Ethics in Society 2 in term 3.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
At the end of this course the student will be able to:
- Discuss key historical socio-political events by analyzing their influence on the development of media policy and regulation.
- Describe the development of Canadian content regulation.
- Identify the regulatory structure of the Canadian broadcast industry and related operations.
- Critically analyze regulatory and ethical issues concerning mass media.
- Explain the role media plays in society.
- Identify and explain media bias.
- Discuss the media approaches to gender roles, ethnic and cultural perspectives.
- Compare and contrast public and private broadcasting.
- Discuss a workable definition of “objectivity” in media.

### Course topics
## Assignment #1 (25% of course grade):
In this assignment, you draw on chapters 1-3 of your text to explore a personal family story. As you reflect on your own family’s narrative, you will be better armed to put your feet into the shoes of your great-grandparents, and – with a little imagination and luck – gain their perspective on the world… and even their place in it. Below you will find the format to follow for your paper. Please ensure you include the section numbers and headings.
Family History Storytelling (1-2 pages)
Where on this planet did your grandparents and their parents walk? You’ve got several sets to choose from – pick the family members you find most intriguing (or can find out the most detail about). Explain a very quick overview of their life and what you find most interesting.
Explore how your great-grandparents used communication technologies vs. how you use them (2 pages)
Give some thought to the methods they used to communicate in everyday life. What would they have encountered in an average day? How did they keep in touch with friends and relatives near and far? What lenses could they look through to see – and interpret – the world? Now contrast all this to your average day.
Exploration of how this difference may have affected your personal identity (1-2 pages)
Let’s extend the previous section one step further: given the tools they had to work with versus what you have now, it’s a fairly safe bet to say they likely saw the world – and their place in it – very differently than you. Here are some interesting questions you could explore:
- How do you think this difference may have affected their personal identity ?
- Do you think they would see themselves as a more significant participant in society or less? Did they have any activist tendencies? Did they imagine the world as safer or more dangerous?
- What about their perception of community? Where do you think they had a sense of belonging ? Do you think this had an impact on their view of time or space?
- Do you believe they might ‘otherize’ foreign communities more or less?
Exactly how you explore this is up to you, but push hard to articulate some clear differences between them and you. DUE DATE : Friday, Nov. 7 by 11:59 pm.

## Assignment #2 (20% of course grade):
In this assignment, you'll reflect on the lens through which you view the world and yourself within it as you progress through this term. Since your personal bias exploration across the next few months is largely monitored and scrutinized by you, it would only seem natural that it is you who would best judge your understanding and development. Accordingly, you will be given the tools to grade yourself with this evaluative instrument. You choose the grade you want on this assignment using the key on the next page (yes, you read that correctly – you grade yourself). Provided you follow through on what it is you promised, I will give you the mark you requested.
I simply require you to write down reflections on your learning while it’s in process. Try to target writing 250 words for each weekly entry. You are to write these thoughts on your computer and post them to the Learning Hub before the completion of the course. Please type reflections following each assigned chapter read for a total of 11 journal entries. Please put the entry number and date at the top of each chapter’s reflection. For best results, it’s probably best that you write each weekly entry as you read the recommended chapters before each lecture.
Use the following key to decide what your mark will be out of 20 (matching the mark with the corresponding amount of energy you have to dedicate to this assignment). For page format , please use a 10-point font and double-space along with standard margins.
Section 1: Chapter Reflections (1 page/assigned chapter = 11-page total)
What struck you as the most interesting ideas presented? Were you surprised or shocked by any concepts? Did you strongly disagree with anything? Did something shift your worldview? Why?
Section 2: Identify your personal biases (1-2 pages)
Let’s extend the previous section one step further: you are developing the critical thinking tools to analyze how you see the world and reflect on your cognitive and emotional processing. Here are some interesting issues you could explore:
- Compile a list of any known biases you possess that might affect the lens you view any particular event – or the world in general – through.
- How has your perception of ‘common sense’ changed?
- What shifted the most in you between your first and your final day engaged in this course?
Exactly how you explore this is up to you, but push hard to articulate some clear changes. DUE DATE : Tuesday, Dec.9th by 11:59 pm.
