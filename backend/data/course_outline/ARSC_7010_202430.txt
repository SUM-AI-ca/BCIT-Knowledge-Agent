### Course Description
This course explores the history of building materials, technology and structural systems, following a roughly chronological path from the emergence of First Civilizations to the present day. These explorations take place within the context of the prevailing social, political and economic forces. Students will research and write one term paper on a topic of their choice, focusing on the materials and technology used in a particular building type, geographic or cultural context or at a particular time in history. One mid-term and one final slide exam will test the students’ knowledge of building practices at different periods in history, and their ability to identify and describe significant built examples.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
- Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to
- Identify the most important building materials and systems in use from pre-Classical times to the present day.
- Outline the history, evolution and application of these materials and building systems.
- Explain the most important historical influences (whether structural, geographic or cultural) on the choice of materials and building systems.
- In the contemporary context, understand the relationship between material choice and sustainability.

### Course topics
The purpose of this course is to explore the global history of architecture with a post-colonial lens. The course follows a rough chronological arc from the emergence of First Civilizations to the twentieth century. Evolutions of building typologies, materials, systems and methods are examined within the context of the prevailing social, political and economic forces of particular times in history, in cultures around the world. In addition to course lectures and discussions, students will conduct individual research into subject areas of interest and share findings with the class. The topics covered are:
Vernacular Buildings
First Civilizations
The Classical Age
Sacred Spaces
Shadows of the Silk Route
Gothic Architecture
Renaissance and Beyond
Industrial Age
Art, Craft and Technology
Modernism
New Pluralism
Canada Comes of Age
On completion of this course, students will be able to:
- Discuss the historical development of architecture and building from First Civilizations to the twentieth century.
- Explain the primary influences (geographic, vernacular, cultural, technological etc) informing architectural decision making at key points in history.
- Adopt a post-colonial lens in discussion of First Civilizations and global Indigenous cultural and building traditions.
- Demonstrate a cross-cultural perspective in comparing / contrasting architecture and building examples from around the world.
- Outline the evolution and application of major building typologies, materials and systems.
- Explain how vernacular architecture performs in its geographic, cultural and ecological context.
- Describe the impacts of colonial settlement on Indigenous building traditions.
- Discuss the revival of Indigenous architecture in Canada.
