## Course Description
This course provides an introduction to the concepts and methods used in hydrogeology that are most pertinent to the needs of the EHO/PHI. The concepts and methods of hydrogeology are used to assess risk to both health and the environment with a focus on preventing and solving groundwater contamination problems. The course also introduces the concepts of identifying and assessing drinking water sources including both ground water and surface water sources.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Articulate the behavior of water in soils.
- Describe the water molecule and the forces of adhesion and cohesion that help soil solids retain water and control its movement.
- Describe the energy gradients that arise from matric, osmotic, and gravitational forces in soil and their relationships as soil water content changes.
- Describe saturated and unsaturated flow through soils.
- Relate hydraulic conductivity to percolation rates.
- Explain terms relating to groundwater and the hydrologic cycle.
- Use the hydrologic equation to explain the interrelation between groundwater and surface water.
- Apply concepts of surficial geology to assess the presence and movement of groundwater.
- Develop geological correlations using water well records, geological maps, and hydrogeological maps.
- Determine rate and direction of groundwater flow using piezometer information and Darcy’s Law.
- Determine methods for investigating known and unknown sources of groundwater contamination e.g.: agricultural sources, commercial/industrial sources, domestic sewage.
- Differentiate between preliminary and detailed hydrogeological investigations.
- Explain the options for groundwater remediation.
- Identify the major biological, chemical, and physical hazards and illnesses associated with drinking water in Canada
- Identify the sources of drinking water and potential hazards associated with each source
- Outline the biological indicators used in drinking water analysis
- Describe the limitations of using biological indicators for drinking water analysis
- Explain the aesthetic objectives associated with drinking water
- Identify common chemical parameters monitored in drinking water
- Identify common maximum acceptable concentrations (MAC) associated with drinking water
- Interpret the results for bacterial and chemical parameters from a laboratory report based on Guidelines for Canadian Drinking Water, with emphasis on the public health significance of these results
- Demonstrate the procedure for taking a drinking water sample
- Describe the components of source water protection
- Explain the types of guidelines, standards, regulations and objectives associated with drinking water in Canada
- Describe key characteristics of the main types of wells (dug, drilled, driven, pit)
- Explain how construction differs for the main types of wells (e.g., rationale, method, depth, equipment used)
- Describe potential sources of well contamination and the importance of proper well siting.
- Explain the concept of groundwater under direct influence (GUDI)
- Identify national, provincial and municipal legislation that oversees surface, ground and coastal water qualities
- Describe the natural hydrologic, major nutrient and major metal cycles
- Explain the significance from an environmental health perspective of the physical, chemical and biological parameters used to characterize surface, coastal and ground water quality
- Describe the lab- and field-based analytical methods used to quantify water quality parameters
- Describe the factors influencing the fate and transport of contaminants through aquifers and watersheds
- Identify natural and human sources of ground and surface water contamination in urban, suburban, and rural watershed settings
- Identify the physical, chemical (including nutrients), and biological hazards typically associated with natural and human contamination sources and activities
- Describe diseases that can be transmitted through contaminated surface water
- Describe adverse public health effects that can be acquired through ingestion and exposure to various surface water contaminants
- Explain how humans can be exposed to contaminants moving through surface, ground, and coastal waters
