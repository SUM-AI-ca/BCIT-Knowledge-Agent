### Course Description
Students are required to submit their thesis as partial fulfillment of the Master of Applied Science degree program requirements. Their thesis should be based on original investigation and must demonstrate scholarship and critical judgement, as well as familiarity with methods of research and relevant literature in the chosen building science topic. The thesis should be at a high level of originality and examine a problem in depth. Elements of the thesis that are considered to constitute original scholarship and an advancement of knowledge must be clearly indicated.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Define research problems through analysis of literature surveys.
- Develop suitable research plans.
- Apply research techniques and principles to create knowledge.
- Collect and evaluate information, critically analyze theories.
- Develop lines of argument, and make sound judgements independently.
- Produce research reports and scientific papers.
- Communicate research project findings at a graduate seminar level.
### Learning Resources
There are no mandatory textbooks for this course. Students are expected to have their own laptops and to co-ordinate with their research supervisors on obtaining the necessary materials and resources to complete their research.

### Evaluation Criteria

The thesis is evaluated by the Thesis Defence Committee. The thesis evaluation process consists of two parts: the reading and examination of the master’s thesis; the thesis defence which is an oral examination conducted by a Thesis Defence Committee. It consists of an oral presentation by the student that lasts approximately half an hour, followed by one or several rounds of questions by the committee members that could last about one hour. The decision of the Thesis Defence Committee is based both on the thesis and on the candidate's ability to defend it. All members of the examination committee are to provide a vote (accepted or rejected). The Thesis Defence Committee can render one of four decisions, subject to a vote of majority. The thesis can be: 1. Accepted as submitted, which may include corrections that do not require the supervisor’s approval. 2. Accepted with minor modification s defined as corrections which can be made within no more than 2 weeks and to the satisfaction of the supervisor. 3. Accepted with major modifications . The Examiner’s Report will include a precise description of the modifications along with a date (normally six months) for their completion. It is then the responsibility of the student’s supervisor to confirm in writing to the Thesis Defence Committee and the Building Science Department that the required modifications have been made and approved. It is not necessary for the Examining Committee to reconvene. 4. Rejected. Such a thesis may be re-submitted only once, in revised form. Such re-submission can only be made six (6) months or more from the date of the original defence. Formal re-submission of a thesis follows the same procedure as an initial submission. If the Thesis Defence Committee is unable to reach a decision concerning the thesis at the time of the defence, it is the responsibility of its chair to determine what is required by the Committee to reach a decision; to make the necessary arrangements to fulfill any requirements of the Committee; and to promptly call another meeting and inform the student that the Committee’s decision is pending. The student is not normally required to be present at the second meeting of the Committee. The Graduate Program renders a decision on whether the student has fulfilled the requirements of the master’s degree based on the Thesis Defence Committee Examination Report and its own records of the student’s academic progress. The final version must contain any revisions required by the Thesis Defence Committee, as well as any formatting revisions. ### Attendance Requirements
The Research Thesis should be executed within a 48-week time frame for full-time students. To ensure that the Research Thesis is built on a sound theoretical and practical framework and that it progresses in the right direction, regular meetings need to be scheduled with the supervisor.
### Course Specific Requirements
The applied research process leading to the thesis is the culmination of the student’s journey through the Master of Applied Science in Building Engineering/Building Science degree. It is also a test of the student’s capacity to conduct research independently. As such, the student is expected to take the initiative in driving the project under the guidance of the supervisor. Taking the initiative involves surveying the relevant literature, identifying knowledge gaps, developing specific research goals, plans, and methods, and being resourceful enough to devise ways to find tools or information that may not be readily available.
BCIT Policy 6600 Integrity in Research provides guidelines for researchers in maintaining the highest ethical standards in conducting and reporting research work. In general, research integrity involves: 1) the use of honest and verifiable methods in proposing, performing, and evaluating research, 2) reporting research results with particular attention to adherence to rules, regulations, guidelines, and 3) following commonly accepted professional codes or norms.
APPLIED RESEARCH PROCESS
The process starts with an observed problem in practice that is deemed to be relevant enough to justify the research efforts. The practical significance is therefore the main motivator for the research, and therefore a key research task will be to measure the practical implications. As a point of departure the research has to consider previous work addressing the problem to help learn from the knowledge/mistakes of others, and identify practical and/or theoretical knowledge gaps more precisely. Furthermore, reviewing previous work enables stating the research question or hypothesis to attempt to address the knowledge gaps. A research plan is then produced with goals to test the hypothesis and help close or narrow the knowledge gaps. The research results are then interpreted and analyzed to help assess their value, in light of the research question and the soundness and rigour of the whole research plan. Finally, the research contributions are evaluated as well as the practical significance of the project outcomes.
The success of the applied research process depends on having a clear focus from the beginning of the project that will permit maintaining consistency throughout the process to achieve the project goals. Project goals should be specific, measurable, and achievable given the inherent time and resource constraints of an applied research project.
FINAL THESIS SUBMISSION TO ELECTRONIC REPOSITORIES
All approved theses are required to be submitted electronically to the BCIT Institutional Repository, per BCIT Policy 5101 - Student Regulations (revised May 4, 2016), Section 1.2.2: Theses in the BCIT Institutional Repository at the BCIT Library. Effective December 2015, all approved theses are required to be submitted electronically to the BCIT Institutional Repository. Refer to Procedure 5101-PR1, Implementation of Student Regulations, for submission instructions and listing of relevant permission forms.
All approved theses are also required to be submitted electronically to the national thesis archive held by Library and Archives Canada. The final deposited electronic copy of the thesis must contain an unsigned signature sheet with the names of the examiners. Submission to be made on the Theses Non Exclusive License Form (Library and Archives Canada).

Building Science Graduate Program Policy on Repeating Courses
This policy supercedes the School policy below : No more than two courses may be repeated with no course being repeated more than once. Where a failing grade is obtained in a core course, the student will be required to repeat the course and obtain a passing grade. If a student fails to obtain a passing grade in the repeated course, the student may apply, on the recommendation of the Graduate Program Director, to the Dean of the school for permission to take the course a third time. If permission is not granted, the student may be requested to withdraw from the program. Where a failing grade is obtained in an elective course, the student may repeat the course once or take an alternate course.
