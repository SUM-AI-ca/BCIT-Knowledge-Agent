## Course Description
Introduces fundamental physics and mathematical concepts important to the therapeutic use of ionizing radiation. Begins with a review of some basic physics concepts and expands into a discussion of topics specific to radioactivity, production of x-rays and their interactions in different media. Identifies applications of physics theory to radiation therapy treatment. Provides problem based laboratory work, and reinforces lecture topics with numerical examples requiring solution of numerical problems where appropriate.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Differentiate between x-rays and -rays.
- Compare x-rays and -rays and understand their position within the electromagnetic spectrum.
- Analyze the production of x-rays. Categorize x-rays based on their origin.
- Analyze the construction of a basic x-ray tube. Describe and identify the basic components on a simplified circuit diagram of an x-ray unit.
- Describe the fundamentals of nuclear structure. Understand the Bohr model of the atom.
- Differentiate the differing modes of radioactive decay.
- Categorize photon beam interactions within a medium. Differentiate between photoelectric effect, Compton effect, coherent scattering and pair production.
- Analyze photon attenuation.
- Define such concepts as exposure, dose and kerma.
- Describe and identify radiation detectors.
- Solve quantitative problems related to course material.
