## Course Description
Focuses on the experience of the individual on dialysis. Provides an understanding of the principles of hemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis, the complications of dialysis and the lived experience of various individuals to develop the nursing role in the management of dialysis.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
As a healthcare practitioner, you are expected to follow professional standards of practice throughout all SN programs and courses. In this course, you will have opportunities to use the processes of critical thinking, systematic inquiry, communication, collaboration, leadership, professionalism, and clinical decision-making. Upon successful completion of this course, you should be able to:

- Reflect on various approaches to health care in a dialysis setting and the building of patient-nurse relationships using the principles of transition theory.
- Utilize critical thinking and systematic inquiry skills to create a comprehensive assessment tool for a renal client experiencing common health challenges.
- Describe the principles of hemodialysis and the underlying physiological impacts.
- Describe the principles of peritoneal dialysis and the underlying physiological impacts.
- Identify psychological and social impacts of dialysis as it varies over the life span and with individuals.
- Explain the efficacy of dialysis using clinical data.
- Interpret clinical data to recognize complications of dialysis.
- Apply knowledge of dialysis to develop care plans for renal patients.
- Create a personal action plan together with a renal patient, using the principles of adult education and self-care management.

## Course topics
This course is focused on the principles of hemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis; the application of the concepts of chronicity and transition to the context and theory of dialysis; comprehensive assessments of clients on dialysis; and lastly, the principles of case management and adult education: all of which will provide you with a foundation for dialysis nursing. The basic theoretical concepts introduced in this course will be further applied in subsequent clinical courses that follow NSNN 7400.

Some of the content in this course will be familiar to you. Specifically, we will revisit concepts of chronicity, transition, and client or patient-centered care, as they relate to relationships between nephrology clients, families, and health care providers. Similarly, we will briefly revisit healthy kidney function and the effects of kidney dysfunction, in order for you to consolidate this theory into your dialysis assessments. However, this review, as well as the material that will be new to you, will be directly applied to the dialysis setting.

In this module, we revisit the concept of chronicity and transition theory as they pertain to clients who have commenced dialysis. Emphasis will be placed on critical reflection of varying individual and clinical goals of dialysis treatment, exploration of the concept of compliance, patient-centered care, and examining dialysis experiences. The focus in this module is on collaboration and partnerships in the dialysis setting.

In this module, we review healthy kidney function and the associated pathology and uremic syndrome as a basis for performing comprehensive assessments in the dialysis setting. The emphasis in this module is on the loss of regulatory, excretory, and metabolic functions of the kidney, assessment of physiological and psychological implications of clients living with dialysis, and nursing considerations for treatment. The learning outcome of this module is a consolidation of theory about living with renal disease, in order to develop an assessment framework for the dialysis setting.

The focus in this module is on hemodialysis theory, including principles of hemodialysis, related equipment, and vascular access. Psychological and physiological considerations and complications are explored via a case-based discussion that emphasizes both the ways in which hemodialysis may be experienced, as well as the basic hemodialysis processes and potential treatment outcomes and implications.

In this module, we explore the theory surrounding the principles of peritoneal dialysis, related equipment, and PD-dialysis access. The psychological and physiological issues and complications that arise in the context of peritoneal dialysis are also explored. As in the previous module, a case‑based approach will be used to emphasize how to live with peritoneal dialysis and the related treatment consequences.

In the last module, the basic principles and concepts of self-care management and adult teaching/learning in a home dialysis setting will be introduced. Integration of self-care management and support into nephrology nursing practice will be explored, using case-based scenarios. The emphasis will be on empowering clients on dialysis to self-manage through collaborative goal setting.
