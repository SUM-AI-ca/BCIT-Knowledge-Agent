## Course Description
This second course in pathophysiology presents neurological and neuromuscular orthopaedic disorders relevant to the practice of prosthetics and orthotics. Essential brain, spinal cord, nerve and muscle anatomy and physiology underlying the initiation, execution, and control of movement are discussed in depth. The selected pathologies include myopathies, neuromuscular junction defects, peripheral neuropathies, plexopathies, radiculopathy, disorders of the spinal cord, basal ganglia, cerebellum, and cortex. Case studies will be employed to develop diagnostic skill. Systematic and comprehensive understanding of neurological and neuromuscular orthpaedic disorders will improve clinician ability to assess and treat patients, predict the likely progression of pathology, and enable the clinician to time interventions and advise patients.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Describe the components involved in the initiation and control of normal voluntary movement, including cortical regions, basal ganglia, thalamus, cerebellum, brainstem motor nuclei, brain and spinal cord tracts, peripheral sensory inputs, and muscle.
- Describe the initiation, control, and feedback essential for generating normal gait.
- Demonstrate the assessment of normal motor function and describe characteristic abnormalities.
- Describe the observations made in EMG and note the electromyographic findings characteristic of normal or disordered muscle.
- Describe the development of the human central nervous system and give examples of developmental abnormalities that affect movement.
- Describe course and extent of selected myopathies and explain pathophysiology.
- Describe selected pathologies affecting the peripheral nerves including entrapment neuropathies and the potential responses to injury of peripheral nerves.
- Identify disorders of movement, sensation, joint position, or integrity that arise principally in the spinal cord and describe the problems commonly associated with each.
- Review the normal contributions of the cortex, basal ganglia, and cerebellum to movement.
- Describe specific motor/cognitive disorders characteristic of altered function in both the basal ganglia (including Huntington's disease, Parkinson's disease, dystonia, dyskinesia) and the cerebellum.
- Describe the nature, typical courses, and pathophysiology underlying multiple sclerosis.
- Describe, localize, and explain movement, sensory, and cognitive problems which may be experienced by those affected by cerebral palsy; describe/model appropriate support and movement which minimizes dysfunctional reflex activity.
- Describe and explain motor/sensory problems associated with stroke.
- Review and apply information in the processing and management of sensory disorders, including pain.
