## Course Description
A comprehensive continuation to the training in diagnostic molecular techniques begun in CLGT 5506. In-class lectures, online resources and hands-on laboratory components of this course will focus on advanced techniques such as Next Generation sequencing, real time PCR, RNA based diagnostic assays and Multiplex Ligation Dependant Probe amplification (MLPA). Students will be trained in speciality software for the analysis, interpretation and validation of these assays. Analysis of data from Next Generation Sequencing (NGS) assays as a method for detection of pathogenic mutations will also be introduced.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
By the end of this course, learners will be able to:

- Communicate effectively with the instructor and other students.
- Behave in a professional manner in lecture and lab sessions.
- Prepare specimens for analysis by isolating RNA.
- Use techniques such as real time PCR amplification, reverse transcriptase PCR amplification, MLPA, and capillary electrophoresis to diagnose normal, affected, and carrier status in terms of genetic disease.
- Performs Next Generation sequencing under instructor supervision
- Analyze data from Next Generation sequencing reactions.
- Explain why and when different PCR techniques are used in clinical laboratories.
- Explain how molecular technology can be used to diagnose genetic diseases.
- Adapts effectively to a changing work environment and troubleshoots to solve problems in the molecular laboratory.
- Perform and document lab procedures, observations, technical information, results and quality control on reagents and media.
- Describe emergent molecular diagnostic concepts for the constitutional and cancer lab, such as molecular barcodes, digital droplet PCR and cell free DNA.
- Work safely in the laboratory while performing molecular diagnostic assays.

## Course Goals
Upon completion of the Course, the student will be able to successfully:

- Build on the theory and procedures introduced in CLGT 5506
- Perform NGS library prep under supervision
- Describe NGS sequencing results and how they are used to make variant calls
- Utilize American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics classification and tools to interpret NGS variant calls
- Perform Real time PCR and MLPA
- Isolate human genomic RNA
- Explain the importance of quality control and quality assurance in a clinical lab
- Explain the role of emergent technology in the clinical diagnostic lab
- Describe the importance of personalized medicine
- Validate the results of lab diagnostic assays
- Prepare laboratory reports based on diagnostic assay results

## Course Schedule and Assignments
Assignment Details

- Use of generative AI tools (eg ChatGPT, etc.)

Student analysis of lab data is an important part of student learning. Instructional staff do not believe that generative AI tools like ChatGPT are an adequate substitute for student judgement in analyzing data. Reports to be submitted for marking are modelled after the format used at clinical sites, so there is little opportunity to use generative AI to write these reports. However, if students do choose to use one of these tools, please follow these guidelines:

Cite and number each use, like this [1, ChatGPT, (date)]

Mark all generative AI output, by using “quotes” for using unedited genAI output, and ‘quotes’ for using paraphrased genAI output

For all generative AI content, student must include primary source verification i.e. original research journal articles or chapters from traditionally published textbooks.

- Laboratory

Four written technical reports must be submitted in partial fulfillment of the course requirements. These reports —Lab Exercises 1 through 4 — are based on assays performed in the laboratory and/or analysis of laboratory data and results. A full description of how these technical reports should be formatted and what content they should include will be provided upon commencement of the courses.

Please note that there are four exercises to be completed in Term 2 as compared to three exercises in Term 1. The reason for this is that students will have had more experience and be at a higher level of competency in Term 2.

- Quizzes Constitutional and Cancer
- Constitutional and Cancer

The 1 hour quizzes will evaluate student progress to date and consists of short answer questions and data interpretation.

- Final Exam

The cumulative written final exam will be 3 hours long and take place during Exam Week. This will be an exam similar in format to the quizzes consisting of multiple choice, definitions, problem solving and data interpretations.

A practical lab exam, focussed on lab procedures and data analysis will take place during weeks 6 through 8.

- Competency evaluation checklist

The labs in this course will strive to mimic the clinical lab environment. There will be a lab checklist for students to complete as a review of their lab skills. Students will be given a marking rubric in the first week of class to explain instructor expectations.
