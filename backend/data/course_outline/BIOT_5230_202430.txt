## Course Description
(UBC BIOT 309) Plant biotechnologists can genetically modify gene functions to introduce desirable plant traits, giving scientists more tools to create new crops and plant products. Students will be introduced to modern techniques used to genetically engineer plants and analyze gene expression. You will learn various skills in molecular biology, genetics, bioinformatics, microscopy and plant tissue culture.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Genetically transform plants.
- Use CRISPR technology to delete selected genes.
- Use GAL4-UAS systems to mis-express genes.
- Identify and characterize altered phenotypes.
- Perform genetic crosses.
- Examine gene expression using various markers.
- Utilize bioinformatics resources.
- Section and stain paraffin-embedded tissues.

## Attendance Requirements
Students are expected to attend all lecture and lab sessions unless you have a serious illness or other medical condition. In the Biotechnology Program, laboratory attendance is of the highest priority since lab experience is what sets this program apart from other undergraduate programs and what prepares students for co-op work terms. Furthermore, lab work is typically done in pairs and students are therefore expected to be present to support their partners. Students must attend all laboratory sessions.

Please notify your instructor and the Biotechnology program head of any absences.

If exams, quizzes, or in-class assignments are missed for reasons of illness, medical notes will be required to reschedule
