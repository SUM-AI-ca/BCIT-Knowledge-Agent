### Course Description
​This course will introduce basic concepts related to software design as it relates to human computer interaction and social contexts in which software is used. Students will explore a brief history and foundational concepts in computing, software and algorithm design, and human computer interaction. In addition, through readings, case studies, and project work, students will learn to incorporate non-technical considerations in computing, such as social structures and ethical and other issues (e.g., social inequalities) that impact use, when designing software systems. Specifically, this will include considering the impact of software systems on individual and collective consciousness, designing to maximize agency of users, consciously avoiding amplifying social inequalities, considering environmental impacts, and taking into account the impact of values embedded in software design especially across different cultures. A secondary aim is to learn how to consciously effect constructive change through software systems.​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Use basic principles of human computer interaction to design software systems.
- Apply a historical understanding of computing when designing software and algorithms.
- Incorporate ethical and other non-technical considerations that impact users and the environment when designing software systems.
- Analyze values embedded in software (and especially algorithm) design, and how they would impact users with potentially different values.
- Assess the impact of software design decisions on individual and collective consciousness.
- Assess the impact of software design decisions on user choice.​

During the first three modules, classes will consist of lectures, class discussions and a seminar-style discussion of an assigned paper on the topic.
During the final module, students will present on one of the following topics:
- Cross-cultural considerations in computing
- Environmental issues
- Privacy and security
- Social media
- AI & robotics
- Smart devices and ubiquitous computing
- Gaming
- Remote work
The presentations will be a culmination of groups of students working throughout the term to find relevant published works, idenifying and analyzing social issues related to computing and presenting alternative designs and/or design processes to address those issues.
During the presentations, each student is expected to provide peer feedback on presentations by other students. This will be a key factor in the class participation mark, in addition to participating in class activities throughout the course.
