### Course Description
This course expands on the histological principles and instrumentation required for the clinical Anatomical Pathology laboratory mastered in Histology 1. Topics of study include human microscopic tissue recognition of tissue and organ systems (microanatomy), microtomy, stain theory, routine stains, special stains, immunohistochemistry and in-situ hybridization. In addition to continued grossing, embedding and microtomy, laboratory activities include microscopic evaluation of digital tissue sections, reagent preparation, performing special stains, IHC, special histology procedures, troubleshooting and improving the quality and quantity of microtomy and staining results.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Operate and maintain a rotary microtome to correctly produce 20 diagnostically acceptable sections from 20 different blocks in one hour.
- Assess the quality of fixation, tissue processing and microtomy and recommend corrective actions for recognized issues.
- Recognize and state the causes and corrections for all the common microtomy artifacts.
- Apply general laboratory safety rules and considerations specific to Anatomical Pathology.
- Correctly recognize gross and microscopic sections, including specific cellular details, of normal basic tissue systems, including the urinary system, endocrine system, gastrointestinal tract and organs, male reproductive, and female reproductive tissues and immune tissues and organs
- Explain the theory of the H&E stain, Papanicolaou stain, Romanowsky stains, microbiological tissue stains, carbohydrate stains, connective tissue stains, pigment stains, amyloid stains, bone stains, lipid stains, nucleic acids stains, immunohistochemistry, preparation of tissue for in-situ hybridization methods, and frozen section cryotomy.
- Explain the principle and chemical mechanism for each of the staining methods performed.
- Correctly perform a Hematoxylin and Eosin stain (H&E) and other common tissue special stains performed in a diagnostic Anatomical Pathology laboratory (see above).
- Correctly coverslip slides using automatic coverslippers and manual methods.
- Assess the quality of staining, troubleshoot, and recommend corrective action as needed.
- Predict, based on chemical structure and physical properties, the possible tissue sites with which a staining method may react.
- Summarize the clinical application for different types of special stains and diagnostic procedures.
