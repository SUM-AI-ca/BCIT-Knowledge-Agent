## Course Description
An overview of occupational diseases and their causes, prevalence and prevention. Occupational health is a multi-disciplinary approach to the recognition, diagnosis, treatment, prevention and control of work-related diseases and injuries. Introduces methods to set up and lead an occupational health program. Discusses respiratory, skin, liver and kidney disorders. Explores occupational diseases of the nervous system and reproductive tract, as well as those related to biological and physical agent exposures. A section on occupational cancer addresses this timely and controversial workplace concern. (This course is equivalent to OCHS 4360.)

## Course Goals
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

-Define the terms occupational disease and occupational health

-Discuss the professional practice implications of occupational disease prevention programs versus occupation injury prevention programs

-Identify the methods used by Canadian workers compensation boards to recognize occupational diseases and determine the compensability of the disease

-Effectively manage the claims management process for occupational disease claims

-Access and review occupational disease statistics and comment on the limitations of these statistics

-Discuss the primary, secondary and tertiary approaches used in occupational health programs for prevention of occupational diseases

-List the other professionals involved in occupational health programs and discuss their roles as team members with an OHS program

-Discuss the benefits and legal limitations of screening programs for occupational disease

-List and describe the broad-based occupational disease surveillance programs used in Canada

-Differentiate between obstructive and restrictive lung diseases and list examples of occupational respiratory diseases of each type

-Summarize the processes by which occupational asthma and extrinsic allergic alveolitis develop in workers

-Describe the causes and progression of typical workplace pneumoconiosis such as silicosis and asbestosis

-List the worker characteristics that might make a worker more susceptible to occupational diseases of the respiratory tract

-Describe the nature and progression of primary irritant and allergic contact dermatitis

-List common workplace agents that are marginal irritants and absolute irritants, biological agents causing skin disease and hydrocarbons causing skin disease and pigment disorders.

-List and describe the nature, causative agents and progression of occupational diseases of the nervous system

-Discuss the safety implications of occupational diseases of the nervous system

-Discuss the effects of various workplace agents on male and female reproductive function

-List and discuss the importance of workplace agents that are suspected endocrine disruptors

-List and describe occupational diseases of the liver and kidney, including the causative agents and progression

-Describe the nature of those occupational diseases caused by biological agents (bloodborne pathogens and other common bacteriological, fungal and viral agents)

-Describe the nature of temperature extreme-related diseases in the workplace

-Describe the nature of diseases caused by ionizing and nonionizing radiation sources in the workplace.

-Explain carcinogenesis and present arguments that help explain why most researchers believe that there is an under-reporting of occupational cancer in Canada

-Access and interpret occupational cancer information from cancer registries, workers compensation boards and agencies such as IARC and ACGIH.

-Design an effective program for the surveillance, recognition and prevention of occupational diseases in the workplace
