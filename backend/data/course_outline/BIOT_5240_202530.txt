## Course Description
This course explores the structural and functional properties of proteins, and the diverse roles they play in living cells. Key principles of protein biochemistry will be applied to problems in the laboratory. Laboratory topics include the purification, quantification and characterization of proteins and enzymes. (UBC BIOT 311)

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

## Lecture Learning Outcomes
- Discuss the basic principles governing biochemical interactions and the molecules involved.
- Illustrate, identify and describe in detail the structures and properties of the common amino acids
- Describe the relationship between protein structure and function.
- Describe common techniques used to explore protein structure and function.
- Manipulate 3-D computer models of proteins to visualize interactions
- Discuss the molecular basis for haemoglobin function.
- Discuss the molecular basis for hemoglobin function (allostery & cooperativity).
- Discuss the basic features and functions of enzymes.
- Discuss and analyze the kinetics of enzyme action.
- Discuss transition states and common mechanisms in enzyme catalysis.
- Describe the mechanisms of enzyme action of lysozyme and serine proteases.
- Discuss and analyze the kinetics of enzyme activity (Michaelis-Menten).
- Explain common mechanisms for the control of enzyme activity.
- Discuss protein folding, design and modeling of interactions.
- Describe the basic features of carbohydrates.
- Discuss the forces governing protein folding.
- Discuss protein misfolding and aggregation in neurodegenerative diseases.

## Laboratory Learning Outcomes
- Quantify and characterize proteins using the Bradford assay and UV spectrometry.
- Isolate, purify and analyze proteins using the following techniques:
- Sonication and detergent lysis
- Ultra filtration
- Ion exchange and size exclusion chromatography
- SDS-PAGE and 2D-PAGE
- Salt fractionation
- Analyze and characterize enzymes using:
- Enzyme activity assays
- Vmax & KM determinations
- Specific activity determinations
- Perform basic bioinformatics of proteins by using online databases to search for functional information based on protein sequence and other information.
- Analyze raw data and write effective data reports.

## Attendance Requirements

Students are expected to attend all lecture and lab sessions unless they have a serious illness or other medical condition. Attendance and punctuality are part of the student’s “Professional Practice” grade for this course (see “Evaluation”). ## Other information
Use of Generative AI:

The ability to analyze and interpret lab data is an important part of learning and an essential skill sought by future employers. The type of data generated in this course is not amenable to GenAI analysis and the output may contain incorrect or misleading information, faked work and/or fabricated authors/dates/publications. Therefore, the use of GenAI for data analysis in this course is not appropriate and strongly discouraged.

If you use ChatGPT (or a similar tool) to get ideas and/or partial answers for an assignment and/or to generate any text for a draft or final version of any part of an assignment, you must acknowledge that you have used it. Follow these guidelines:

- Cite and number each use, like this [1, ChatGPT, (date)]
- Mark all generative AI output, by using “quotes” for using unedited GenAI output, and ‘quotes’ for using paraphrased GenAI output
- For all generative AI content, student must include primary source verification (i.e. original research journal articles or chapters from traditionally published textbooks).

Material directly copied from any source (e.g. websites, books, AI tools) must be cited. Undeclared copying of these materials is a violation of academic integrity, because it presents work, or ideas created by tools or other people as your own. Failure to fully declare the copying of these materials will be considered a violation of BCIT academic policy.

Any use of GenAI for completion of exams or quizzes is strictly prohibited.

## Course topics
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

Lecture Learning Outcomes

- Discuss the solvent properties of water and its influence on protein structure & function
- Discuss the principles and applications of biological buffers, salts and detergents
- Illustrate, identify and describe in detail the structures and properties of the common amino acids and the polypeptide backbone
- Discuss in detail the forces governing protein folding
- Describe the methods used for the experimental and computed determination of protein primary, secondary & tertiary structures
- Describe the relationship between protein structure and function (protein domains)
- Describe common techniques used to purify proteins
- Discuss the molecular basis for hemoglobin function (allostery & cooperativity)
- Discuss the basic features and functions of enzymes
- Discuss transition states and common mechanisms in enzyme catalysis
- Describe in detail the mechanisms of Lysozyme and Serine Protease enzymes
- Discuss and analyze the kinetics of enzyme activity (Michaelis-Menten)
- Discuss enzyme inhibitors and their mechanisms

Laboratory Learning Outcomes

- Isolation and purification of proteins using the following techniques:

- Animal tissue homogenization
- Sub-cellular fractionation of animal cells using selective detergents and centrifugation
- Differential extraction and/or precipitation of proteins
- Ion exchange, affinity and size exclusion chromatography
- Ultrafiltration

- Detection, quantification and analysis of proteins:

- UV Spectrophotometry
- Total protein (Bradford) assay
- Quantitative enzyme activity assays, specific activity determinations, Vmax & KM determinations

- Bioinformatics of proteins:

- Using amino acid sequence data and/or accession number to search online databases for protein structural and functional information.
- Using ‘protein database’ (PDB) to view and analyze the 3-dimensional structure of proteins.
