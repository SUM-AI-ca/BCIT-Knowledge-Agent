### Course Description
​The course covers the major areas of crime and incident scene examination, in order to give an in-depth understanding in theories of searching and in dealing with the entire range of physical evidence located during the various types of crime and incident scene searches. Students will be exposed to the fundamentals of processing various scenes from start to finish, including in-depth knowledge regarding the theory of scene searching, and in dealing with the entire range of scene specific evidence that can be located and recovered. Students will learn how to forensically photograph, and document scene exhibits and will be exposed to the practice of proper evidence detection, collection and documentation.​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- ​Describe the various types of physical and biological evidence that might be present at both an indoor and outdoor crime or incident scenes.
- Explain the various means of scene documentation available and the strengths and weaknesses of each.
- Demonstrate the proper detection, collection, and preservation of physical and biological evidence at a scene.
- Explain the value of using an alternate light source at a scene and demonstrate their use.
- Demonstrate proper scene security and management techniques
- Explain the personal safety considerations applicable to each technique considered or situation encountered.

### Course Schedule and Assignments
Week | Topic | Readings
1 | Intro week Assignment: Q&A of self introduction- whom am I poster- TCPS2 -CORE assignment | Powerpoint
2 | File management / scene search - major case management checklist - types of search at scenes Assignment- outdoor scene 3-4 small items, utilize 5 search patterns to locate the items in a 20X20 meter forested area. | Powerpoint
3 | Case study - Richard Ramirez case study Assignment- watch 10 mini episode documentary and answer questions pertaining to the file | Powerpoint
4 | Fingerprints- introduction to the uniqueness and persistency of fingerprints Assignment- fingerprint study of family members to determine pattern and consistency through genealogical lines | Powerpoint
5 | Cognitive bias- understanding the subjectiveness of being a crime scene investigator and how it affects their processing of crime scenes Assignment-- watch the documentary about Cognitive bias Dr Itel Dror and his study. Q&A of cognitive biases | Powerpoint
6 | Plan drawing- creating plan drawings and the importance of scene sketches at major crime scenes Assignment- create a plan drawing of a mock crime scene at home with measurement using both methods of scaling | Powerpoint
7 | Intro to photography- understanding the importance of professional photographs to document crime scenes, theory of light and the use of a DSLR camera Assignment- with the words provided, compose and image via connotative meaning | Powerpoint
8 | Bloodstain- introduction to basic bloodstain pattern use of this expertise at blood-related impact scenes Assignment- No assignment | Powerpoint
9 | Forgery and deception- be able to observe if a crime scene is tampered with or forgery has been used at a crime scene Assignment- create a fake fingerprint using household items and determine if that fingerprint can be transferred to a crime scene- report via scientific journal | Powerpoint
10 | Crime Scene photography- understanding the difference between artistic photography and forensic photography. Assignment- create crime scene place evidence in a scene and be able to professionally document a crime scene with proper evidence markers/scales | Powerpoint
11 | 11 Crime scene practical- - crime scene day, students attend class given and crime scene and be able to process the evidence and document/examine it with the proper medium | Powerpoint
