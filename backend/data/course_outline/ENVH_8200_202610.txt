### Course Description
This course examines the role of the Public Health Inspector/Environmental Health Officer (PHI/EHO) in land use planning. The student is introduced to concepts, principles, and terminology employed in land use planning including zoning and Official Community Plans. Relationships between land use planning (and the resulting social and built environments) and population health are examined. Social determinants of health are discussed in terms of how obstacles to healthy living can be removed through well-designed aspects of the social and built environment. Students also utilize research evidence to evaluate the key factors of a healthy built environment and to make recommendations on how to positively affect the health of a community.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Describe steps to identify, reduce, or eliminate a health hazard, using specific examples.
- Recognize that a health concern or issue exists.
- Describe key elements associated with various land use categories (agriculture, residential, commercial, industrial, reserve).
- Assess the role and importance of zoning as it relates to land use.
- Explain the incompatible land uses concept.
- Define key terminology including “site specific", “susceptible population" and “site suitability".
- Explain why the identification of susceptible populations is important in land use planning.
- Analyze the relationship between land use planning and neighbourhood design, transportation systems, food security, housing, natural environments, air quality, and noise.
- Describe the procedure and legislative framework by which land is subdivided and developed.
- Explain the responsibility and duties of the PHI/EHO in land development.
- Identify major themes in land use planning.
- Describe Provincial, Regional, and Municipal land use planning frameworks.
- Differentiate between the development processes involved in various land management frameworks (e.g. municipalities, unincorporated areas, First Nations lands).
- Properly utilize land use and urban planning terms such as gross density, property identifier, variance permits, and land-use designations (zoning codes) so as to enable meaningful inter-professional dialogue.
- Outline how an Official Community Plan is developed and implemented.
- Outline the role of the PHI/EHO in community development and the planning process including Regional Growth Strategies, Official Community Plans (OCPs), other municipal plans, and development permit applications.
- Justify the role of engineering the environment in promoting healthy lifestyles (compared to other strategies such as education and enforcement of legislation).
- Distinguish between health equity/inequity and health equality/inequality.
- Identify opportunities to advocate for structural and policy action to address the social determinants of health and health equity.
- Identify opportunities to influence environmental public health policy and controls. e.g. how can CIPHI be part of the advocacy.
- Evaluate and explain a range of public health science concepts: the health status of populations, inequities in health, the determinants of health and illness, strategies for health promotion, disease and injury prevention and health protection, as well as the factors that influence the delivery and use of health services.
- Use evidence and research to inform health policies and programs.
- Demonstrate how key factors of a built environment can impact population health using research evidence and case studies.
- Identify and collaborate with partners in addressing public health issues.
- Advocate for healthy public policies and services that promote and protect the health and wellbeing of individuals and communities.
- Recognize how the determinants of health (biological, social, cultural, economic and physical) influence the health and well-being of specific population groups.
- Address population diversity when planning, implementing, adapting and evaluating public health programs and policies.
- Contribute to developing key values and a shared vision in planning and implementing public health programs and policies in the community.
- Build community capacity by sharing knowledge, tools, expertise and experience.
- Describe the purpose, process and components of community planning.
- Define key terminology including “built environment".
- Explain the characteristics of a “healthy built environment" (e.g., Complete Streets, Smart Growth, mixed use, healthy housing, access to green space, food environments, transportation options).
- Examine the social environment as a distinct environment with characteristics that impact (and are impacted by) the built environment.
- Describe the health authority's role in and contributions to community planning.
- Articulate the public health rationale and targeted outcomes for planning a healthy built environment.
- Explain a proposed development plan from a public health perspective.
- Identify potential roles of Environmental Health Officers in Injury Prevention.
- Explain the importance in advocating for strong environmental public health practices and controls within the community/public and to government/legislators.
- Identify opportunities to advocate for healthier environments and better controls for population groups most vulnerable to health hazards and least equipped to advocate for themselves.
- Analyze how sustainable development applies to environmental public health.
- Discuss biodiversity, ecosystem services, and natural capital regarding their importance to environmental and human health.
- Discuss the importance of natural spaces regarding human physical, mental, and social health.
- Explain concepts such as food security/insecurity, food deserts, and food swamps.
- Discuss challenges and opportunities in efforts to reduce of food insecurity.
- Integrate best practices for community nutrition, food safety, and food security.
- Discuss how the social determinants of health affect and are affected by the cultural food practices of vulnerable populations, with emphasis on balancing this with environmental public health goals.
- Identify housing conditions which may have an adverse impact on health.
- Examine the vulnerability of population groups to high radon exposure.
- Identify barriers to reducing radon exposure for vulnerable groups.
- Identify ways to build partnerships with government departments/agencies to increase opportunities for collaboration, advocacy, and options to address identified housing issues.​
