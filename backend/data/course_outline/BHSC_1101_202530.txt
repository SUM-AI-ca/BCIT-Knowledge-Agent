## Course Description
Together with BHSC 2201, this course follows a systems approach to study the relationship between structure and function in the human body. BHSC 1101 begins with a discussion of cells and tissues as basic structural/functional units then introduces regulation and integration of body systems and the role of control systems in homeostasis. Nervous, endocrine, and other major major systems will also be covered. Where appropriate, each course gives examples of the uses of biomedical instruments in diagnosis and treatment of human disease.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Describe the overall relationship between structure and function in the human body.
- Relate the function of each body system covered in this course to the common goal of homeostasis.
- Use a broad range of common terms to describe relative positions of structures in the body.
- Illustrate the concept of the body being in a dynamic catabolic/anabolic steady state.
- Explain the interrelationship of the components of human homeostatic control systems.
- Relate the structures of the basic tissues to their functional capacities.
- Describe how the structural components of skin relate to its many functions.
- Describe how ionic distribution relates to the electrical properties of cells.
- Relate neuronal function to the ability of the nervous system to receive, transmit and integrate information.
- Identify basic structures of the central and peripheral nervous systems.
- Outline the nature and effects of hormones secreted by the major endocrine glands.
- Explain how the nervous system regulates endocrine function.
- Discuss how the anatomy and physiology of body systems in this course relate to the design, function and operation of biomedical instruments.
- Demonstrate how the anatomical and physiological principles discussed in this course may be applied to new or theoretical biomedical devices.

## Course Goals
Upon completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Build foundational knowledge by understanding the basic structures and functions of the human body at the cellular, tissue, organ, and system levels.
- Connect structure to function by explaining how anatomical features relate to physiological processes and their importance for human health
- Apply these core anatomy and physiology concepts to biomedical engineering applications, including medical devices and diagnostics
- Develop technical language by using appropriate anatomical and physiological terminology to communicate effectively in academic and professional contexts
- Foster critical thinking through systems-level analysis of how body systems interact and respond to internal and external influences
- Prepare for advanced study by establishing a solid knowledge base to support future coursework in biomedical instrumentation and applied health sciences

## Course Schedule and Assignments
Module: Contents Covered: Description
One: Introduction to Anatomy and Physiology - Body organization - Homeostasis and homeostatic control systems - Descriptive terminology: Establishes the foundation of anatomy and physiology, emphasizing how the human body is organized and regulated.
Cytology and Human Reproduction - Structure of a mammalian cell - Cellular metabolism and cell division - Human development and reproductive technology: Introduces cellular structure, function, and reproduction, linking microscopic processes to human growth and development.
Two: Histology - Basic tissue types: epithelium, connective, muscle, and nerve: Focuses on the microscopic study of tissues and their roles in the human body.
The Integumentary System - The skin - Skin appendages: Examines the structure and function of the skin and related structures in protection and regulation.
The Endocrine System - Hormones - The endocrine glands: Explores the major endocrine glands, hormones, and their roles in regulating physiological processes.
Three: Nervous System - Neurons and support cells - The central nervous system - The peripheral nervous system - Special senses: Provides an overview of the nervous system, including cellular components, system organization, and sensory function.
