### Course Description
This course integrates the science of nuclear medicine technology and molecular imaging with specific clinical applications to prepare students for competent performance in clinical practice. Students will gain knowledge in decision-making based on literature and current best practice guidelines for performing diagnostic nuclear medicine procedures in the following categories: skeletal, inflammation, tumour, lymph, gastrointestinal, and positron emission tomography applications in oncology.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Apply knowledge of anatomy, relational anatomy, physiology, pathologies, anomalies and radiopharmaceutical applications to perform Nuclear Medicine diagnostic procedures in the following categories: skeletal, inflammatory process, tumour, lymph, gastrointestinal, bone mineral density, and positron emission tomography applications in oncology.
- Apply knowledge of the pharmacologic and dietary effects in relation to specific clinical procedures.
- Apply knowledge of radiopharmaceutical preparation, administration, and contraindications in the context of clinical procedures.
- Plan and adapt procedures to optimize patient outcomes in response to patient condition and clinical environment​.
- Evaluate the technical and diagnostic quality of images and data.
- Evaluate results to determine if further images and data are required.
- Distinguish patterns consistent with normal results and normal variants.
- Recognize patterns consistent with common pathologies and abnormal results.
- Review decision options based on best practice evidence.
