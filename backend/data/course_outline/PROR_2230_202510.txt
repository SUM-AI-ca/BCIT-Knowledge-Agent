## Course Description
This practicum provides the student the opportunity to apply knowledge of design principles and evaluation procedures to a variety of patients, under the supervision of a practising prosthetic/orthotic clinician. Specific projects aimed at applying skills learned during PROR1100 (Prosthetic and Orthotic 1) and PROR2200 (Prosthetic and Orthotic 2) courses.

## Learning Resources
Students are required to organize their own 4-week clinical placement in an affiliated OPC Canadian P&O faciltiy and under the supervision of an OPC Certifee in good standing. The students will communicate confirmed facility sites to the clinical coordinator and provide BCIT with a contact person for the student's evaluation. All sites need to have current BCIT affiliation agreements in place. Students must complete one prosthetic and one orthotic clinical placement during the program. Students are to select work placements in facilities that they have not had paid work or volunteered in the past, or have future plans for a residency. This is to optimize the student's learning opportunity in new P&O environments.

Specific Guidelines can be found in the Prosthetic and Orthotic Student Practicum Term 2 Document for April 28-May 23, 2025

## Course Goals
Orthotics Placements • Students should be provided with the opportunity to assess and cast 6 patients • Under supervision, students should assume responsibility for the treatment of at least two patients who require a foot orthosis and one patient who requires an ankle foot orthosis (Three complete projects).

For each of these patients the student should; • Perform an appropriate patient assessment and casting • Independently fabricate the device • Evaluate the device(s) on each patient, demonstrating to the supervisor a systematic fitting process and a final critique as to how the device fits, and what could be changed/done differently next time.

Prosthetics Placements • Students should be provided with the opportunity to assess and cast 4 patients • Under supervision, students should assume total responsibility for the treatment of at least one patient who requires a transtibial prosthesis and, if available, one patient who requires a symes or partial-foot prosthesis (Two complete projects).

For each of these patients the student should; • Perform an appropriate patient assessment and casting • Independently fabricate the device • Fit and align the device to a satisfactory standard • Critique process with the supervisor and identify what could be changed/done differently next time.

## Evaluation Criteria
Criteria: %: Comments
Clinical Placement report (graded as Satisfactory or Unsastisfactory): 50%: Students are expected to attend one virtual class meeting to share their clinical placement experiences with classmates. Clinical supervisors are expected to complete a clinical performance appraisal form in the final week of the student’s practicum with the student. The student is expected to sign that this report was reviewed with them. In addition, the primary clinical supervisor is required to assign the student a SATISFACTORY or UNSATISFACTORY grade and this is to be sent to Yvonne Jeffreys (yvonne_jeffreys@bcit.ca) along with the signed practicum appraisal form. Clinical placement sites are required to have an active affiliation agreement with BCIT to be eligible to host a student for the clinical placement. Students are required to attain a Satisfactory grade on the clinical placement evaluation to pass this course.
Clinical Reflection Journal (graded as Satisfactory or Unsastisfactory) or successful completion of HSIP-0400 IPE Collaborative Leadership Module.: 50%: The Journal should provide a daily reflection of specific, unique experiences and observations. When describing client management, the description may include an indication of pathology, aims of prosthetic/orthotic treatment and a reflection on how this case fit into the student’s clinical education the steps taken to complete the procedure. Journal entries should focus on ‘ahha’ moments during the placement or highlights that are ‘notes to self for future consideration. You may also consider specific technical process or lay-ups for future reference. You may also discuss impact of interprofessional interactions (meetings, clinic rounds etc.) and how this contributed (or not) to the P or O treatment and patient outcomes. Reflections may also include your reactions to your experiences throughout the placement (not limited to: patient safety, new viewpoints, surprises or disappointments).. The Journal is to be electronically documented, and submitted in pdf format to Yvonne Jeffreys, Clinical Coordinator by11:59pm on the first Friday following the last day of the clinical placement.The journal will be marked as Satisfactory or Unsatisfactory. based on reflective content. Late submissions for reflective journals are not eligible for grading if received after the deadline unless a request has been discussed and approved by Clinical Placement Coordinator (Yvonne Jeffreys) at minimum 24 hours prior to the due date. All students must attain a satisfactory grade for this Journal or successfully complete the HSIP-0400 IPE Collaborative Leadership Module to pass this course
100%: All students must attain a satisfactory grade in their clinical placement evaluation AND a a satisfactory grade on either the Reflective journal or the sucessful passing of the HSIP-0400 IPE Collaborative Leadership Module to to pass this course.

## Course Specific Requirements
Academic Misconduct

Violations of academic integrity, including dishonesty in assignments, examinations, or other academic performances are prohibited and will be handled in accordance with the ‘Violations of Standards of Conduct’ section of Policy 5002.

Use of Artificial Intelligence Tools

AI (artificial intelligence) tools may be used in a limited way to complete assignments in this course. See each assignment for details on acceptable use (e.g. brainstorming, editing) or inquire with your instructor in advance of submission. Assignments are designed to help you build skills and understanding. Please note that AI tools cannot learn for you.

AI tools offer benefits when used wisely. You must use AI tools responsibly and ethically and acknowledge use in all work submitted for a grade. Please note that AI output may include incorrect or misleading information, fabricated authors/dates/publications, or faked works. You are always responsible for accuracy and quality. Material directly copied from any source (e.g. websites, books, AI tools) must be cited. Other uses (e.g., ideas, brainstorming, designing, paraphrasing) must be acknowledged including the sources. Undeclared use of AI is a violation of the academic integrity policy as it presents work or ideas created by tools or other people as your own.

Late Submissions

Any work submitted late will be penalized at 10% per day; No work will marked if more than 5 days past the due date. Any extensions will be on a case-by-case basis with the instructor but must be identified 2 days prior to the due date.
