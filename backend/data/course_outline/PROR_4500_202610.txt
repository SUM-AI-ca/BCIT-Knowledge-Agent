### Course Description
Under the guidance of faculty subject matter experts, students complete an applied research project. The project includes selection and development of a relevant topic, planning a detailed research proposal, conduction of a literature review, design of an appropriate study methodology and data collection, analysis and interpretation. The project will be completed in a written thesis format, then presented and defended in a public forum.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Define a relevant and worthwhile research area within the subject area prosthetics and orthotics.
- Apply critical thinking strategies to develop and test hypotheses related to specific clinical subject matter.
- Demonstrate accepted methods of Evidence Based Practice to clinical prosthetics and orthotics.
- Describe, present and defend strategies and methods taken to test hypotheses.
- Articulate the meaning of the project and the relevance of it's findings to the prosthetic and orthotic profession.

### Course Specific Requirements
Attendance
Regular attendance and participation in lectures, seminars, laboratory, and clinical sessions are integral to student success. Similarly, regular participation via prescribed forms of electronic communication may be required in any virtual and/or online aspects of the course. In case of illness or other causes of absence, students must notify their instructor AND program head.
Academic Misconduct
Violations of academic integrity, including dishonesty in assignments, examinations, or other academic performances are prohibited and will be handled in accordance with the ‘Violations of Standards of Conduct’ section of Policy 5002.
Use of Artificial Intelligence Tools
AI (artificial intelligence) tools may be used in a limited way to complete assignments in this course. See each assignment for details on acceptable use (e.g. brainstorming, editing) or inquire with your instructor in advance of submission. Assignments are designed to help you build skills and understanding. Please note that AI tools cannot learn for you.
AI tools offer benefits when used wisely. You must use AI tools responsibly and ethically and acknowledge use in all work submitted for a grade. Please note that AI output may include incorrect or misleading information, fabricated authors/dates/publications, or faked works. You are always responsible for accuracy and quality. Material directly copied from any source (e.g. websites, books, AI tools) must be cited. Other uses (e.g., ideas, brainstorming, designing, paraphrasing) must be acknowledged including the sources. Undeclared use of AI is a violation of the academic integrity policy as it presents work or ideas created by tools or other people as your own.
Late Submissions
Any work submitted late will be penalized at 10% per day; No work will marked if more than 5 days past the due date. Any extensions will be on a case-by-case basis with the instructor but must be identified 2 days prior to the due date.
