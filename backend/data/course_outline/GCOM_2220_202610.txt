### Course Description
Building on skills developed in GCOM 1200 (e.g. cutting, folding), students will gain advanced knowledge of common lithographic finishing methods. Working in teams, students will learn to setup, operate, and troubleshoot the traditional saddle-stitcher and perfect binder. They will learn the design, pre-press, and layout considerations necessary for successful bindery finishing and identify the challenges of more complex bindery projects. They will define the role and responsibilities of a bindery operator including record-keeping, the identification of production standards, and consistent monitoring of work quality. Students will be educated in bindery and warehouse management and be able to determine the required workflow to successfully complete a bindery project. They will be introduced to additional common bindery functions such as collating, tipping, wire-o binding, spiral binding, case-binding, shrink-wrapping, and final packaging.​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Describe complex bindery finishing functions and operations.
- Explain the considerations for a cutting and folding operator for saddle-stitching and perfect binding projects.
- Perform basic setup and maintenance tasks on saddle-stitcher.
- Perform basic setup and maintenance tasks on a perfect binder.
- Identify common bindery challenges and how to address them.
- Plan the necessary work processes and their sequencing for completion of a complex bindery project.
- Explain the design, pre-press, and layout considerations for various bindery methods.
- Produce load tags, carton labels, and basic shipping documents for a completed bindery project.
- Differentiate between common packaging methods.
- Calculate requirements for a completed bindery project.
- Maintain a clean and safe workplace.​

### Safety equipment or protective clothing
The Graphic Communication program utilizes a unique print lab facility, with equipment donated by the generous business owners in our community. Students must always respect the equipment in the lab, and comply with the following rules:
- Loose hair or long beards must be tied back or contained in a hat or hair net.
- Remove jewelry, scarves, coats, or loose clothing.
- Gaming, videos, or loud music that can cause distraction are not permitted.
- Mobile devices are not permitted when working around machinery.
- No food or drink in the lab (closed water bottles are allowed at desks only).
- PPE is required to be worn when working with chemicals.
- No video recording or photography is permitted in the lab.
These rules are for the safety of all students using the equipment in the lab and are overseen at the discretion of all faculty. Failure to comply with lab rules will not be tolerated and may result punitive action including dismissal from class, formal warning, course failure, or withdrawal from the program.
