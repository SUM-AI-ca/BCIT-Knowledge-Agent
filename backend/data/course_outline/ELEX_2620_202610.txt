### Course Description
This course introduces fundamental engineering principles that underpin the physical phenomena encountered by an INC graduate. Building on the concepts developed in the Electrical Systems for Industrial Network Cybersecurity course in term one, this course prepares students for the Industrial Networking course in the following five-week term and the Process Measurement and Control Fundamentals for Industrial Network Cybersecurity course in term three. The concepts taught in this course are closely related to the concepts being developed concurrently in the Programmable Logic Controllers and Calculus for Industrial Network Cybersecurity courses. Topics include: kinematics and dynamics of translational and rotational motion, conservation of energy, simple harmonic motion, electrical and magnetic fields, wave propagation, magnetic circuits, basic thermodynamics (Law of Conservation of Energy) and heat transfer, and basic fluid mechanics (Bernoulli’s equation). ​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Use the principle of conservation of energy to solve problems in work, energy, power, and efficiency (electrical and mechanical). (3)
- Define the variables and discuss the principles used to describe the kinematics and dynamics of translational motion. (2)
- Explain the concepts of kinematics, dynamics, and energy to problems in rotational motion. (2)
- Describe simple harmonic motion principles problem as applied to mechanical systems and the parallel in electrical systems. (2)
- Explain the physical properties of charge, conductors, insulators, semiconductors Gauss’s law and Faraday’s law. (2)
- Explain Lenz’s law, the principles of magnetism and their relationship to electrical voltages, currents and materials. (2)
- Describe the practical applications of magnetic fields and electrical current and voltage (e.g. transformers, motors). (2)
- Summarize Maxwell's electromagnetic theory and explain electromagnetic wave propagation and its application to wireless communications. (2)
- Describe the concepts of temperature, thermal energy, heat transfer, conservation of energy and phase change to address heat transport. (2)
- Apply the principles of fluid flow and the practical applications of Bernoulli’s equation. (3)
