### Course Description
During the course, students will undertake and complete a biomedical engineering project. From the concept developed during BMET 3302, students will develop detailed specifications, a project plan and schedule of the project. The project will be carried out with design methodologies that are consistent with ISO 13485 and QSR (21CFR820.30). Following detailed planning, students will participate in the design and development of the project, and will complete the project independently. These tasks may include circuit design, parts acquisition, printed circuit board layout, circuit building, package design and fabrication, wiring, troubleshooting and final testing. Students will maintain a detailed design history file, a logbook of their work throughout the course, and will prepare a technical report at the completion of their project. The process of design, prototyping, verification, validation and documentation will be introduced at different stages of the course. Guidance documents will specify the criteria for expectations with respect to various aspects of design control, risk management, project management, and documentation.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Prepare a project proposal that meets specified criteria.
- Prepare a detailed project plan that meets specified criteria.
- Prepare high level design documents that meet specified criteria, including:
- Requirement specifications that define objective pass fail criteria for their project.
- Architectural design.
- Initial hazard analysis.
- Carry out detailed design as required to meet the requirement specifications.
- Fabricate a working prototype.
- Verify that the prototype meets established requirements.
- Explain important aspects of the project experience to first year students.
- Prepare a technical report that meets specified criteria.

### Course Goals
- To design, fabricate, verify and document a simple biomedical or assistive device. Devices typically consist of commercial parts (sensors, actuators, circuit boards, etc.) with custom interfaces (electrical and mechanical), along with appropriate software, wiring, enclosure and labeling etc., as required to produce a functional prototype.
- To apply design control, risk management and project management methods during the development of the prototype device.
- Optional: to use advanced prototyping techniques (e.g., 2D/3D cutting, 3D printing, PCB fabrication) during the fabrication of the prototype device.

### Course Schedule and Assignments
The detailed course schedule is subject to change and will be posted on the course website.
Students will work in teams of 2 to 5 (depending on class size). Each team will submit the following documents:
Design Documents:
- Concept : a high level description of the project, including initial requirements (user needs, key performance characteristics) and key technologies that make the project feasible.
- Project plan : a record of agreement between team members that assigns responsibilities to each team member, provides a risk management plan, and identifies key milestone dates.
- Requirement specifications : a list of verifiable features and characteristics that define what it means when we say it works.
- Hazard analysis : a systematic and comprehensive review of the device to ensure that the device is safe and/or to identify risk control features that need to be added to the requirement specifications.
- Architectural design : a high level design document that identifies all commercial modules and custom parts (e.g., interface electronics, enclosures, labeling) and provides the constraints for all detailed design.
Design documents are due according to the schedule that will be posted on the Learning Hub. Each document will be reviewed by the instructor for review and feedback. Students may revise these documents throuout the term and are expected to incorporate all feedback in their final version, which is due one weel before the final presentation. Only the final versions will be graded.
Technical Report :
The final technical report provides a summary overview of all project activities, presents the results and provides a critical review and self-assessment. The content of this report forms the basis for the final presentation. Any feedback received during the presentation may be reflected in the final submission of this report.
Guidance documents for all deliverables will be posted on the course website. Concurrently with BMET4402, students are expected to take COMM3478, Technical Communication 3 for BMET, which supports students throughout the term in writing these documents.
### Course topics
Proposals for student projects must be pre-approved by the instructor prior to commencement of the course. Students may choose between the following options: ​
- Structured design project: The instructor will define project objectives and constraints. Focus will be on design quality and craftsmanship.
- Innovative design project : Students will define their own product concept and initial requirements, with input from instructors and relevant stakeholders. Focus will be on innovation and suitability for clinical use.
- Improvement design project: Students may build on previous students' work to solve specific problems. Focus will be on quality and suitablity for clinical use.
During the course, students will follow the following process:
- Design Concept: In the second week of the course, each team will submit a document outlining the design concept as the basis for project planning.
- Planning: Each team will define the plan for managing their own project. The plan will include an equitable breakdown of each student's contribution, which will form the basis for final evaluations.
- Risk Management: As part of the high-level design, students will carry out and document an initial hazard analysis to identify safety related requirements. Risk management according to ISO14971 will be applied throughout the project as appropriate.
- Design Process: A structured design process will be applied, similar to quality managment system requirements (ISO13485, 21CFR 820). Design Input: As part of the high-level design, each team will define and document the requirement specifications that will be subject to verification. Design : As part of the high-level design, each team will document the architectural design, prior to completing the detailed hardware and software design. Design Review: Each team will be subjected to a formal design review by the rest of the class, based on their documented architectural design. Implementation : Each team will fabricate a functioning prototype. Verification: Each team will verify their prototype against their documented requirement specifications. Validation: If appropriate, each team will work with a client to carry out initial validation of the device.
- Documentation : Each team will document important steps of the design process to facilitate review and input from others. Each team will submit a formal report, which will document the design of the finished prototype and which will provide a thoughtful analysis of the team's own performance during the project.
- Final Presentation : Each team will demonstrate and explain their finished prototype to students in next year's class, program instructors and invited guests. Students will explain their individual contributions to the project and answer questions.
