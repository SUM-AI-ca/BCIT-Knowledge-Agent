### Course Description
​Students are required to submit their research project as partial fulfillment of the Master of Engineering degree program requirements. Their research projects must demonstrate application of existing knowledge to solve new industry problems or advancement of knowledge in the chosen topic, as well as familiarity with methods of research and relevant literature in the chosen building science topic.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to;
- Apply analytical, numerical and/or experimental methods to evaluate building materials, systems, and whole buildings performances.
- Identify research problems through analysis of industry practices and literatures.
- Develop suitable research methods.
- Implement research methods, perform analyses and synthesize findings.
- Independently draw conclusions.
- Produce research reports and scientific papers.
- Communicate research project findings at a graduate seminar level.

### Course Specific Requirements
## APPLIED RESEARCH REQUIREMENTS IN THE INDUSTRY/RESEARCH PROJECT
The goal of the Industry/Research Project is to provide students with the opportunity to explore systematically and at a deeper level a practical industry problem. The Industry/Research Project is a unique opportunity for students to apply and expand the knowledge gained from the Master of Engineering (MEng) courses for solving a meaningful problem in a building science area of interest. The success of the Industry/Research Project depends on the selection of a relevant topic of genuine interest to the student, the supervisor, and the industry. The Industry/Research Project will give the student the research skill that can be used in the future whenever confronted with an applied research problem during the course of her/his career.
The Industry/Research Project involves the application of knowledge, rather than the creation of new knowledge. However, the main motivation in applying knowledge to solve an industry problem is to gain new insights into the behaviour of a product or system that will shed new light leading to a solution. Therefore, a reasonable intellectual scope and level of originality are expected, and should be agreed upon by the supervisor and the student. Nevertheless, the expectations of in depth investigation are greatly reduced in MEng research projects and the validation of findings is not required. It is expected, however, that MEng students follow the organization and methodology presented in the Building Science Graduate Program Handbook to demonstrate a minimum required level of research competency to produce sound results.
The Industry/Research Project is the culmination of a student’s journey through the MEng program. The Industry/Research Project is expected to originate and evolve from the student’s own initiative. Part-time students working in the industry are encouraged to bring meaningful problems from their own practice. The role of the supervisor is to guide the student. The supervisor can also act as a bridge to facilitate contacts within industry, if necessary, and give ideas on meaningful problems to investigate based on his/her own interests. The supervisor will always be ready to discuss ideas and issues with students and to challenge them to increase their knowledge. However, the applied research project remains the student’s sole responsibility: the student is expected to take the initiative in developing specific project goals, plans, methods, analyses, or conclusions. Being able to conduct independent work is part of the learning process of a student within the MEng program.
## PROCEDURAL STEPS
Select a topic of Study and Prepare a Research Plan
In the first month or two, students will meet with their supervisor and agree on an area of research based on mutual interests, significance, resources (equipment, facilities, software, time-frame, etc.), methods, and viaibility. The topic of research as well as the research plan are subject to the supervisor's approval. Students prepare a 150-500 word abstract of their chosen topic and complete a draft version of the research plan. A good plan will make sure that the study goals are relevant and achievable.
Execute the Research and Track Its Progress
Following the approved research plan, students execute the research. Research progress will be tracked at the student and supervisor meetings based on achieving the following milestones:
- Literature review
- Research design
- Implementation
- Data collection
- Data analysis
- Findings
- Report writing
Submit the Industry/Research Project
At least two months should be assigned for writing the project report. Report-writing is an iterative process with supervisor feedback. A complete draft version of the Industry/Research Project should be submitted to the supervisor at least one month before the end of the term. A final version of the Industry/Research Project should be submitted at least two weeks before the end of the term. Two printed copies of the final report will be submittted to the supervisor; one copy will be for the supervisor and another for the Department. Students should refer to the Building Science Graduate Program Handbook for formatting and printing requirements for the report.
