### Course Description
This course introduces the fundamentals of writing for video production. The course provides students with an introductory understanding of storytelling before helping them develop writing skills for various types of video. Through a combination of lectures and group work in labs, students learn writing techniques for various promotional, informational, and corporate video formats. The course examines principles of audience, attitude, and appeal as they relate to commercials and promotions. It also introduces advertising rules and regulations at the cursory level. Throughout, concepts of mood, production design, photographic composition, screen direction, and more, from other courses inform the visual aesthetic contained in students’ writing.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Describe and discuss the basic elements of narrative storytelling.
- Express the requirements for an instructional, corporate or promotional video, public service announcement or commercial.
- Evaluate copy written by peers and industry writers to uncover cause-and-effect relationship of a variety techniques.
- Identify persuasive or manipulative messages within visual media.
- Explain how and why advertising works.
- Explain the various rules and regulations governing advertising in Canada.
- Write copy using a variety of styles, appeals, and formats for a variety of target audiences (demographics and psychographics).
- Produce copy that uses video production technique to creatively and visually communicate ideas.
- Incorporate elements of storytelling into their writing.
- Recognize various script formats and apply the appropriate format for the type of video being written.

1. BCST 1124 Policy:
Instructors will issue marks deductions in order to address student attendance issues.
These deductions will apply to the final grade after all other marks have been calculated.
Students are issued a 2% deduction from their overall final grade per occurrence as an indication they have been late for scheduled class activities without notifying the course instructor(s) prior to the start of the class via email or text message and obtaining instructor approval for the lateness. Students are issued a 4% deduction from their overall final grade per occurrence as an indication they have been absent from scheduled class activities without having been excused in writing by the course instructor(s). Advance permission to be absent or late may be granted at the discretion of the course instructor(s) in conjunction with the Television & Video Production Faculty, based on a written request submitted by email to all course instructor(s) a minimum of 7 calendar days prior to the absence.
Deductions for absences may be rescinded if medical documentation is provided within 7 calendar days of the student’s return. Repeated absences for any reason may result in additional course-related activities being assigned to ensure relevant student competence.
