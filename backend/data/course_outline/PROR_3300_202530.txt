## Course Description
This course aims to introduce students to theoretical and applied aspects of lower extremity prosthetic and orthotic management (at the knee and above). Theoretical and practical components of the course run in parallel throughout the semester. During the practical sessions the students’ examines design, construction and fit of knee orthoses, knee ankle foot orthoses and transfemoral prostheses. Volunteer patients are assessed and fitted for a variety of these devices.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Make appropriate prosthetic and orthotic treatment plans based on subjective and objective information (including patient history, patient physical assessment, gait analysis and patient goals). Be able to justify (verbal) treatment plans and expected outcome measures.
- Discuss fitting procedures for KAFO and KO.
- Fabricate, fit, evaluate and align the following lower extremity devices:
- Prosthetic check sockets for a transfemoral amputee using conventional methods,
- Endoskeletal trans-femoral prosthesis – Quad socket,
- Endoskeletal trans-femoral prosthesis - Ischial containment socket,
- Thermoplastic KAFO to patient model.

## Course topics
Detailed Course Description : The course will cover the following aspects: Lower Extremity Orthotics II (KO and KAFO) Observational gait analysis and apply OGA concepts as applied to KO and KAFO orthotic treatments. Commonly observed pathologies (CVA, CP, SCI, MS, Spina Bifida, MD, polio, Arthritis) and their implications for KO and KAFO treatments. Challenges facing the Orthotist in the orthotic management of patients using KO and KAFO orthoses. Identifying clinical challenges that may include the intervention of KOs or KAFOs. Biomechanical principles and appropriate alignment procedures for Kos and KAFOs. Indications and contraindications of various KO and KAFO design specifications. Componentry used in the fabrication of KOs and KAFOs. Development and evaluation of prescription criteria for conventional metal, thermoplastic, thermosetting and hybrid KAFOs and KOs. Principles involved with various stance control KAFO designs. Practice of the systematic physical examination of an orthotic patient requiring a KAFO. This shall include palpation, passive and active range of motion, manual muscle strength testing, basic neurological examination, and joint range of motion and skin condition. This should include appropriate positioning of patients and appropriate patient/clinician communication. Shape capture (casting/digitizing), model rectification, fabrication and evaluation of KAFOs. Discussion of the evaluation process of a KO. Professional demeanor as per the OPC Codes of Ethical Conduct. Communication (in written and oral formats) of orthotic treatments and concepts with colleagues, patients and other health professionals.
Transfemoral, Knee Disarticulation and Hip Disarticulation Prosthetics Describe different types of transfemoral prosthetic socket design including Quadrilateral socket design and Ischial containment socket design and flexible versus rigid designs. Describe indications and contraindications for specific prosthetic socket designs. Discuss biomechanical implications for different trans-femoral socket designs. Describe the desirable functions of a transfemoral suspension system. Describe suspension options for management of transfemoral and knee disarticulation amputees. Describe indications and contraindications for specific suspension options. Discuss biomechanical implications of different designs. Demonstrate a complete understanding of voluntary control, alignment and mechanical stability and the implication of these factors on energy consumption of transfemoral amputees. Recognize the features of stabilizing mechanisms utilized in commercially available knee units and describe indications and contraindications for their use. Describe different types of knee joints including indications and contraindications for their use. Discuss appropriate bench alignment configurations for transfemoral prostheses and recognize how this may change according to various patient characteristics. Recognize common gait deviations, their underlying cause and possible prosthetic solutions. Discuss major characteristics of transfemoral amputee gait, including temporospatial, kinetic and kinematic parameters. Demonstrate knowledge of amputation trends and major causes of lower extremity amputation. Demonstrate a basic understanding of surgical procedures for knee disarticulation, transfemoral, and hip disarticulation amputations. Describe casting, fabrication and evaluation procedures for prosthetic management of knee disarticulation amputees and hip disarticulation amputees. Discuss challenges that may arise in management of these groups of amputees. Discuss the role of the physiotherapist in team management of transfemoral amputees. Knowledge of gait training procedures and specific exercises that may be used for balance training and gait training of transfemoral amputees.
