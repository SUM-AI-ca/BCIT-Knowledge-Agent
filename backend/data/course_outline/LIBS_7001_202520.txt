## Course Description
This is a course in advanced composition and rhetoric, in which students will develop skills in complex critical analysis and interpretation by analyzing and evaluating materials from a variety of discourses or genres, including visual, online, and print; developing and writing essays, including critiques and research papers; applying and discussing principles of rhetoric and critical theory; examining and using methods of interpretation and analysis from the humanities and social sciences; evaluating the credibility of primary and secondary sources, including as it applies to media literacy, and for the purposes of academic research; situating discourses within their historical context and relevant to rhetorical theories of different periods (for example, Aristotle in the ancient world and Bakhtin in the twentieth century). The course format will include lecture, discussion, and both individual and group activities.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Analyze and use complex levels of style and tone suited to a given purpose (GA 7)
- Identify and assess logical and organizational patterns within complex texts. (GA 7)
- Recognize written, oral, and visual discourse conventions within and outside of specific disciplines. (GA 7)
- Compose documents for audiences with different backgrounds and specializations. (GA 7)
- Evaluate rhetorical strategies in a variety of written and digital media (GA 7, 12)
- Develop and apply interpretative and analytical skills. (GA 7, 12)
- Demonstrate the social and ethical implications of rhetoric in historical development and contemporary practice. (GA 7)
- Propose, research, organize, and write a research paper that uses the rhetorical concepts and strategies discussed in the course. (GA 7)​

## Course Specific Requirements
- All assignments must be completed in order to pass/complete the course.

- Academic Honesty and Integrity for Coursework: Coursework components must follow the format and requirements specified for that component. All coursework must be the work and words of the individual student, unless otherwise specified by the instructor, or documented through instructor-approved citation and format protocols (MLA or APA style). The student must consult with the instructor before anyone can modify or edit a student’s assignment or other coursework component in any way. Students cannot submit work for one class, either at BCIT or elsewhere, for evaluation or credit in another class, without prior authorization by the instructor. Coursework will be checked for academic integrity and plagiarism. The course will use BCIT Policy 5104, Student Code of Academic Integrity https://www.bcit.ca/files/pdf/policies/5104.pdf particularly section 8 on plagiarism, cheating, falsifying or misrepresenting, and academic advantage, so students should make themselves aware of this policy.
- You cannot use AI-generated content/computer generated text. In short, if it is not written by you or by sources which can be cited and found, you cannot use it. For the final paper you will be required to submit your sources and note where in each source (by page and paragraph) the quote or what was paraphrased can be found
- Recording, Copyright, Ownership, and Distribution of Course Materials: All course material is for the student’s individual study and use. Students must not record lectures and classroom activities or discussions, nor must they post or distribute course material outside the course, including to any websites, without the required permissions from the instructor and copyright holders.
