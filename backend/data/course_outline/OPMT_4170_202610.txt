### Course Description
This course prepares the student for managing complex projects using a structured project management methodology. Five project management process groups (initiating, planning, executing, monitoring/controlling, and closing) and the Project Management Institute’s PMBOK (Project Management Body of Knowledge) provide a framework for advanced project management. This course prepares the student for the PMI’s Certified Associate in Project Management (CAPM) certification exam.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
At the end of this course the student will be able to:
- Initiate a given project by developing a project charter and identifying stakeholders.
- Manage the competing demands of project time, cost, scope, risk, quality, and requirements.
- Maintain project quality through quality assurance and quality control processes.
- Manage project teams and project communication in order to meet project goals.
- Perform project risk analysis and effectively control risks.
- Plan and conduct project procurement.
- Interpret the project manager’s code of ethics.

In every lecture, students will get a handout that they will complete during the lecture which will be graded for completeness and effort. The worksheets on are not graded based on right/wrong answers.
Lab Participation
Every lab, students will be graded based on the following:
- Time: Are you late? Do you take extra long breaks? Do you leave early?
- Attention: Are you on your phone? Wearing earbuds? Working on unrelated things?
- Engagement: Are you trying to do the assigned work? Are you asking questions or volunteering information?
- Disruption: Are disrupting or distracting other students?
### Course Specific Requirements
Team Based Project
This course features a major team-based project. All team members usually receive the same mark. However, marks can be redistributed if one or more team members contribute less than their fair share. This may include one or more of the following:
- frequently absent from team meetings or classes
- submitting plagiarized or low effort work
- missing team deadlines or class deadlines
- disruptive behavior during team meetings.
Your team will need to have a team charter and document infractions, escalating the issue to your instructor before the assignment is due. The instructor will then guide your team through the mark redistribution process. In this process, the team will have two options:
- Option 1: If all members of the group agree that grades should be reallocated, up to 40% will be taken off one student’s score and redistributed to the other students.
- Option 2: If group members do not reach agreement on how marks should be reallocated, each team member’s contributions will be marked separately.
