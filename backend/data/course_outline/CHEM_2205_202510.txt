## Course Description
Continues from CHEM 1105 and covers topics of inorganic chemistry including solubility equilibria, buffer solutions, oxidation and reduction reactions, electrochemistry, physical and chemical properties of gases, liquids and solids, phase diagrams and intermolecular forces. Electrochemistry includes electrochemical cells, applications of electrolysis, electrometallurgy, and corrosion. Lab work covers a variety of topics including electrochemical cells, ion exchange, spectrophotometric analysis, water hardness and alkalinity indices.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Describe how buffer solutions operate in controlling pH.[1,3,6,7]
- Predict the pH of solutions of strong acids, strong bases, weak acids, weak bases, acidic salts, and basic salts. [1,2,3,4]
- Use Ksp values to determine the solubility of salts and to predict if a precipitate will form when solutions of different salts are combined.[1,2]
- Write balanced chemical equations for oxidation-reduction reactions.[1,2]
- Analyze the construction of electrochemical cells to determine the voltage which would be produced under standard and nonstandard conditions.[1,2,4,6,7]
- Calculate the minimum voltage required to cause electrolysis and the amount of substance produced in electroplating processes.[1,2,4,6,7]
- Describe applications of electrochemistry, such as electroplating, electrorefining, corrosion, and the Hall process.[1,2,4,6,7]
- Calculate variables related to the gas laws.[1,2,3,6,7]
- Describe how the relative rates of effusion and diffusion of two gases depend on their molar masses.[1,2,3,6,7]
- Predict the intermolecular forces found in molecules and the effect that these intermolecular forces have on physical properties (boiling point, melting point, viscosity, surface tension).[1,2,3,6,7]
- Draw a phase diagram of a substance given appropriate data, and use a phase diagram to predict which phases are present at any given temperature and pressure.[1,2,3,6,7]
- Predict the type of solid (molecular, ionic, covalent-network, or metallic) formed by a substance, and describe its general properties.[1,2,3,6,7]
- Calculate quantities related to colligative properties of a solution.[1,2,3,6,7]
- Recognize major classes of organic compounds.[1,2,3,6,7]
- Use the apparatus available in the lab to perform a variety of qualitative and quantitative analytical determinations.[1,2,3,6,7]

Engineering Accreditation

The Canadian Engineering Accreditation Board (CEAB) accredits bachelor of engineering programs across Canada. To ensure programs graduate well-rounded engineers who are prepared to enter engineering practice, the CEAB requires graduates to possess 12 specific attributes (see below for descriptions). The learning outcomes for each course have been mapped to relevant CEAB graduate attributes. The numbers in [ ] after each learning outcome indicate the graduate attribute to which the learning outcome is mapped.

CEAB Graduate Attributes

1. A knowledge base for engineering: Demonstrated competence in university level mathematics, natural sciences, engineering fundamentals, and specialized engineering knowledge appropriate to the program.

2. Problem analysis: An ability to use appropriate knowledge and skills to identify, formulate, analyze, and solve complex engineering problems in order to reach substantiated conclusions.

3. Investigation: An ability to conduct investigations of complex problems by methods that include appropriate experiments, analysis and interpretation of data, and synthesis of information in order to reach valid conclusions.

4. Design: An ability to design solutions for complex, open-ended engineering problems and to design systems, components or processes that meet specified needs with appropriate attention to health and safety risks, applicable standards, and economic, environmental, cultural and societal considerations.

5. Use of engineering tools: An ability to create, select, apply, adapt, and extend appropriate techniques, resources, and modern engineering tools to a range of engineering activities, from simple to complex, with an understanding of the associated limitations.

6. Individual and team work: An ability to work effectively as a member and leader in teams, preferably in a multi-disciplinary setting.

7. Communication skills: An ability to communicate complex engineering concepts within the profession and with society at large. Such ability includes reading, writing, speaking and listening, and the ability to comprehend and write effective reports and design documentation, and to give and effectively respond to clear instructions.

8. Professionalism: An understanding of the roles and responsibilities of the professional engineer in society, especially the primary role of protection of the public and the public interest.

9. Impact of engineering on society and the environment: An ability to analyze social and environmental aspects of engineering activities. Such ability includes an understanding of the interactions that engineering has with the economic, social, health, safety, legal, and cultural aspects of society, the uncertainties in the prediction of such interactions; and the concepts of sustainable design and development and environmental stewardship.

10. Ethics and equity: An ability to apply professional ethics, accountability, and equity.

11. Economics and project management: An ability to appropriately incorporate economics and business practices including project, risk, and change management into the practice of engineering and to understand their limitations.

12. Life-long learning: An ability to identify and to address their own educational needs in a changing world in ways sufficient to maintain their competence and to allow them to contribute to the advancement of knowledge.

## Course topics
Buffered Solutions - pH of a buffer - buffer capacity - addition of strong acids or bases - pH range

Solubility Equilibria - solubility and Ksp - common-ion effect - solubility and pH - precipitation and separation of ions; criteria for precipitation and dissolution - qualitative and quantitative analysis for metallic elements

Oxidation and Reduction Reactions - oxidation states - oxidation-reduction reactions - balancing redox equations

Electrochemistry - voltaic cells - cell potentials under standard and nonstandard conditions; concentration cells - spontaneity of redox reactions - strengths of oxidizing and reducing agents - commercial voltaic cells - corrosion - electrolysis - electrometallurgy - pyrometallurgy

Gases - characteristics of gases; the gas laws - applications of the ideal-gas equation - gas mixtures and partial pressures - the kinetic molecular theory - molecular effusion and diffusion - real gases: deviations form ideal behaviour

Properties of Liquids and Solids - intermolecular forces - select properties of liquids: viscosity and surface tension - vapor pressure - phase changes; phase diagrams - bonding in solids

Properties of Solutions - the solution process - saturated solutions and solubility - factors affecting solubility - colligative properties
