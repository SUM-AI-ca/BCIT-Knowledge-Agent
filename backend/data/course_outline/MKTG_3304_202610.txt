### Course Description
Through projects, individual research and case studies, students will learn to analyze foreign operating environments to determine market potential, develop market entry strategies and create appropriate marketing mixes. An emphasis will be placed on the role of international marketing in the overall growth and strategies of a domestic firm.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Develop effective public speaking skills by delivering oral presentations (individual and team) to audiences of diverse sizes.
- Identify the strategic risks of foreign operating environments.
- Develop research appropriate for international marketing.
- Identify the technical factors essential for international marketing.
- Develop appropriate market entry strategies.
- Identify tariff and non-tariff barriers.
- Identify the role of transportation, logistics, and customs procedures in international marketing.
- Develop appropriate international marketing mixes.

### Course Goals
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Effectively assess cultural, political, economic, and legal environments influencing international business.
- Identify potential risks associated with global business and organizing for global competition.
- Identify factors that create international business opportunities.
- Follow the steps of international trade research using a macro-to-micro analytical approach.
- Understand the choice of market entry strategies that firms can choose from.
- Apply intelligence to market planning, product adaptation and effective promotional strategies.
- Understand the basics of marketing logistics, exporting, and sourcing.
- Understand international pricing issues.
- Understand international sales management, with a focus on B to B selling
- Understand international agreements, financial terms, and institutions that affect trade.
- Understand the characteristics of emerging markets.

### Attendance Requirements
Attendance in lectures and labs is mandatory as your instructor views each one as a scheduled business 'meeting'. Attendance will be taken at the beginning of each lecture and lab, and s tudents not present at that time will be recorded as absent. It is the students’ responsibility to sign the lecture attendance sheet.
In case of illness or other unavoidable absence, students must communicate with the instructor, or the appropriate Program Head, as soon as possible (preferably in advance) indicating the reason for their absence. Students who are seeking accommodation for a medical absence must have a BCIT-approved medical certificate . For other absences, students must be prepared to provide appropriate supporting documentation.
A student who has unexcused absences in excess of 10% of the time prescribed for the course by the Withdrawal Deadline is considered to have ‘Vanished’ and will be assigned a grade of ‘V’. In these cases, no further work will be graded.
These requirements are set out in accordance with BCIT Policy 5101 Student Regulations . More information regarding withdrawals from PTS courses is available at https://www.bcit.ca/pts/pts_withdrawals.shtml.
### Other information
Use of Artificial Intelligence Products/Services
The unauthorized use of Artificial Intelligence products/services for any part of a student’s work other than idea generation is strictly prohibited. Any work purportedly submitted as a student or student team's original work but that is found to contain work created by an AI tool will receive an automatic grade of 0% and the infraction will be noted on their student record which may impact their ability to further their academic studies. As students work in teams, and they assign their names to a particular deliverable, it is incumbent upon each and student to ensure that the work they are endorsing is clear of any plagiarism, including the use of AI tools. That is, students cannot claim ignorance or non-participation in any work found to have been generated by AI.
