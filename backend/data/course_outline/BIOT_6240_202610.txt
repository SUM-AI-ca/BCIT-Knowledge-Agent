### Course Description
(UBC BIOT 312) Discusses the generation of metabolic energy through an examination of the central pathways involved in the oxidation of fuel molecules. Examines the biosynthesis of macromolecular precursors. Analyzes the process of photosynthesis and its role in providing biological systems with free energy. Studies methods used for the isolation, purification and analysis of simple and complex biopolymers.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Describe, Discuss and Illustrate the central pathways, regulation and molecules involved in metabolism as detailed in the course schedule below.
The goal of this course is to provide students with a theoretical and practical background to allow the students to discuss the central intermediate molecules in key metabolic pathways and the relationships between those intermediates and the macromolecules for which they are precursors. In particular the course focuses on the basics of how cells extract energy and reducing power from their environment, how cells synthesize macromolecular building blocks, and the strategies which tie these processes together. The practical goal is to familiarize the student with some approaches used in the laboratory to examine biologically significant molecules.

### Attendance Requirements
Students are expected to attend all lecture and lab sessions unless they have a serious illness or other medical condition. In the Biotechnology Program, laboratory attendance is of the highest priority since lab experience is what sets this program apart from other undergraduate programs and what prepares students for co-op work terms where it is expected that they will have acquired specific competencies. Furthermore, lab work is typically done in pairs or small teams and students are, therefore, expected to work collaboratively and support their lab partners.
This is a lab-based course: participation marks will be given for completion of laboratory activities and these marks will be worth up to 5 % of the student’s final course grade. A student must be on time for start of the lab period to avoid losing participation marks. These lab activities involve generating lab specific materials and/or data, learning, acquiring and/or practicing new skills and/or procedures. In addition, to submit a lab report or assignment, a student must participate in 80% of the lab periods associated with the lab report or assignment. In the case of illness where a medical note is provided, lab participation marks will not be lost for those missed lab period(s) and will not be counted towards the 80% participation marks required to submit a lab report or assignment.
Please notify your instructor and the Biotechnology program head of any absences. If exams, quizzes, or in-class assignments are missed for reasons of illness, medical notes will be required to reschedule.
### Safety equipment or protective clothing

### Course Schedule and Assignments
Weeks | Outcome/Material Covered
1 | Introduction (Chapter 12, 13 4 th Edition) Describe the regulation of enzymes Discuss the main functions of metabolism.
2-3 | 1. Discuss carbohydrates (Chapter 8) 1.1 Describe monosaccharides and disaccharide structures and reactivity 1.2 Describe the structure of mobilizable and structural polysaccharides 1.3 Describe the structure of glycoproteins and glycolipids with reference to biological membranes (chapter 9, 10, 19) I may also include some additional material on membrane lipids.
3-4 | 2. Discuss glycolysis (Chapter 15) 2.1 Describe key structures and reactions 2.2 Discuss generation of reduced coenzymes and adenosine triphosphate 2.3 Describe the entry of disaccharides into glycolysis and illustrate the toxicity of galactose 2.4 Describe the enzymes involved in the regulation of glycolysis
4-5 | 3. Describe the citric acid cycle (Chapter 17 ) 3.1 Illustrate the formation of acetylcoenzyme A 3.2 Describe the citric acid cycle intermediates and their interconversions 3.3 Discuss the generation of reduced coenzymes and high energy phosphate bonds 3.4 Describe thiamine deficiency 3.5 Discuss the role of the citric acid cycle in the provision of biosynthetic precursors 3.6 Illustrate the glyoxylate cycle in plants and microorganisms 3.7 Discuss control points in the citric acid cycle
6 | 4. Discuss oxidative phosphorylation (Chapter 18) 4.1 Describe the role of the mitochondrion 4.2 Describe the respiratory chain 4.3 Discuss the coupling of oxidation and phosphorylation by a proton-motive force 4.4 Discuss shuttles involved in entry of cytoplasmic NADH into the mitochondrion 4.5 Illustrate the energy yields in the complete oxidation of glucose
7 | 5. Describe the pentose phosphate pathway and gluconeogenesis (Chapter 15, 16) 5.1 Discuss the generation of NADPH and five carbon sugars 5.2 Describe the linkage between glycolysis and pentose phosphate pathway 5.3 Discuss the synthesis of glucose from non-carbohydrate precursors 5.4 Illustrate the reciprocal regulation of glycolysis and gluconeogenesis 5.5 Describe the shift of metabolic load from muscle to liver during intense exercise.
8 | 6. Describe glycogen metabolism (Chapter 16 ) 6.1 Describe the enzymes involved in the cleavage of glycogen 6.2 Describe the enzymes involved in the synthesis of glycogen 6.3 Illustrate the regulation of glycogen synthesis and degradation
9 | 7. Describe fatty acid metabolism (Chapter 20) 7.1 Describe the nomenclature of fatty acids 7.2 Discuss triacylglyceroids as energy stores 7.3 Discuss the regulation of lipases 7.4 Illustrate the degradation of fatty acids 7.5 Discuss the formation of ketone bodies 7.6 Describe the synthesis of fatty acids 7.7 Illustrate the control of fatty acid metabolism
10 | 8. Discuss amino acid degradation and the urea cycle (Chapter 21 ) 8.1 Illustrate the fate of the α-amino group 8.2 Describe the urea cycle 8.3 Discuss the fate of carbon atoms of degraded amino acids 8.4 Illustrate the role of cobalamin in pernicious anemia 8.5 Discuss the consequences of genetically inherited blocks in amino acid metabolism
11 | 10. Describe biosynthesis of membrane lipids and steroid hormones (Chapter 20 ) 10.1 Discuss the synthesis of phosphoglycerides and triacylglycerol 10.2 Discuss the synthesis of cholesterol 10.3 Describe the role of lipoproteins in transport 10.4 Outline the synthesis of steroid hormones from cholesterol 10.5 Describe the synthesis of Vitamin D from cholesterol
12 | 11. Discuss biosynthesis of amino acids and heme (Chapter 21 ) 11.1 Describe the conversion of N2 into NH4 + by microorganisms 11.2 Outline the assimilation of NH4 + into amino acids 11.3 Describe the role of major pathways in providing intermediates for amino acid synthesis 11.4 Illustrate the role of SAM and tetrahydrofolate in providing one carbon unit
13-14 | 12. Describe biosynthesis of nucleotides (Chapter 23) 12.1 Outline the synthesis of purines and pyrimidine 12.2 Describe the control of purines and pyrimidine synthesis 12.3 Discuss the interconvertibility of mono-, di-, and triphosphates 12.4 Describe the formation of CTP from UTP 12.5 Describe the formation of deoxyribonucleotides 12.6 Outline the activities of several anticancer drugs 12.7 Describe the degradation of purines and pyrimidine 12.8 Describe Lesch-Nyhan Syndrome
