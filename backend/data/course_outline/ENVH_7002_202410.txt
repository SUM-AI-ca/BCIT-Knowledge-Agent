## Course Description
This course addresses the public health risks associated with outdoor air quality at the local, regional and global level. Types and sources of air pollutants are described and classified. Climate change, stratospheric ozone depletion and acid deposition are discussed. The health risks associated with outdoor air quality are assessed and the role of the Public Health Inspector/Environmental Health Officer (PHI/EHO) is examined. Standards and regulations regarding outdoor air quality are also evaluated. Students are equipped with the skills and knowledge to interpret air quality monitoring data and recommend measures to protect public health.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Evaluate the public health risks associated with local and regional ambient air quality.
- Compare health risks in urban vs. rural environments
- Describe stratospheric ozone depletion.
- Explain acid deposition.
- Assess the effect of atmosphere and weather on ambient air quality (e.g. horizontal and vertical dispersion, temperature inversion, heat islands)
- Outline the health messaging and advocacy role of the PHI/EHO for outdoor air quality issues.
- Identify hazards and risks related to air pollutants.
- Identify susceptible and vulnerable populations and specific risk factors to be considered (e.g., housing quality and location, transportation methods) when managing air quality risks for those groups
- Describe the sources of ambient air pollution
- Suggest ways in which ambient air quality databases such as the Air Quality Health Index (AQHI) may be used to inform public health interventions
- Describe the various kinds of hazardous air pollutants that threaten public health or may be reported to an EHO (e.g. criteria air contaminants, heavy metals, persistent organic pollutants, toxicants, etc.)
- Identify common sources of air pollutants with emphasis on the health effects associated with major hazardous air pollutants (e.g. Nitrogen oxides, sulphur oxides, ozone, carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide, hydrogen sulphide)
- Explain the implications of key air quality indicators (e.g. PM2.5, sulphur oxides, hydrogen sulphide, nitrogen oxides, ozone, and volatile organic compounds)
- Analyze human health effects of exposure to air quality indicators (e.g. PM2.5, sulphur oxides, hydrogen sulphide, nitrogen oxides, ozone, and volatile organic compounds)
- Explain the atmospheric cycles of oxides of nitrogen and oxides of sulphur and their relationship to acid rain
- Explain the atmospheric cycles of carbon dioxide and methane and their relationship to the greenhouse effect
- Explain the effect of air pollutants, both human made and naturally occurring (i.e. forest fires) on the climate, or more locally on weather conditions and the subsequent impact on health
- Explain the sources and atmospheric cycles of hydrocarbon
- Explain what the Air Quality Health Index (AQHI) is and how it is used to protect health and provide health messages for the at-risk and general populations
- Design a risk communications plan to inform various audiences (e.g. media, general public, vulnerable groups, politicians) on ambient air quality issues
- Explain the concept of dispersion modeling and its use during a public health air quality emergency or when investigating abnormal air emission from point sources
- Describe the data required from an ambient air quality monitoring program
- Explain how to compile ambient air quality standards from literature reviews
- Assess the health risks and make recommendations on point-of-source control measures based on field data
- Assess the health risks and make recommendations on control measures for local area air sheds and/or larger regional zones based on field data
- Assess and predict the effects of various emission gases based on field data
- Describe the types of air testing equipment and sampling methods currently used in the field with emphasis on strengths and limitations
- Describe the variables associated with seasons on air quality
- Describe the sources of atmospheric contaminants from industries, residential communities and transportation systems
