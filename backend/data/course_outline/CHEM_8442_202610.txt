### Course Description
Profiles selected industries in terms of technological processes, environmental pollutants and chemical wastes generated. Examines treatment or detoxification methods employed to reduce the amount of toxic pollutants released into the environment. Assesses physical and chemical properties that determine the transport, fate and effects of hazardous waste substances. Concepts in redox, solution equilibria and organic chemistry are applied.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:
- Examine the pollution problems associated with the chemical industry, wood preservation, pulp and paper industry, mining and aluminum production.
- Identify the various sources and components of industrial waste streams.
- Recognize major organic pollutants. Classify hazardous organic compounds according to their functional groups and characteristics.
- Account for the origin of dioxins and furans in the environment from herbicides, incineration processes and pulp and paper industry.
- Describe the chemicals used as wood preservatives, the properties of these chemicals and the environmental pollutants released when these preservatives are applied or burned.
- Examine the concerns related to the presence of pesticides and other chemicals in the environment (water, air and soil).
- Examine the physical and chemical properties of the toxic waste constituents and their pollution potential.
- Compare the physical and chemical properties and persistence in the environment of selected pesticides which are in common use today.
- Explain the biotransformation of toxic metals (e.g., inorganic mercury) to the more toxic organic species.
- Describe the relationship between the chemical properties of soil (mineralogical characteristics, cation and anion adsorption and exchange capacities, soil acidity and alkalinity) and the movement of pollutants in soil.
- Account for the environmental behaviour of chemicals.
- Explain the significance of neutralization, precipitation and redox reactions in the treatment of wastes.
- Appraise specific air and water pollution control methods employed to reduce pollution of the environment.
- Be aware of the method of approach and outcomes of ecological risk assessment.

### Course topics
Chapter 1 - Technology and the Environment.
Chapter 2 - Pulp and Paper Industry – Kraft and sulphite pulping processes and their environmental effects; pulp bleaching and its environmental effects; toxicity of pulp mill effluents.
Chapter 3 - Wood Preservation – nature of wood; chemicals used to treat wood and their environmental impact; disposal of treated wood; health risks of exposure to chromated copper arsenate; newer wood preservatives.
Chapter 4 - Pesticides – overview of synthetic pesticides; fate of pesticides in the environment; profiles of most common synthetic pesticides; alternatives to the use of synthetic pesticides.
Chapter 5 - The Chemical Industry – the petroleum industry, petroleum processing; oil spill effects and cleanup methods, BTEX; environmental problems of the organic and inorganic chemicals industry; environmental effects of inorganic chemicals - spills of strong acids and leaks of toxic gases such as chlorine and ammonia.
Chapter 6 - Toxic Metals – sources of exposure to mercury; biomethylation; factors affecting mercury toxicity; lead and other toxic metals.
Chapter 7 - Soils and soil pollution – soil composition; cation exchange capacity and importance of soil pH.
Chapter 8 - Mining – extraction of minerals; liquid waste (AMD) and its treatment; advantages and disadvantages of wetlands; pollution from tailing ponds.
Chapter 9 - Aluminum Production – red mud and air pollutants; gaseous fluorides, PAHs and perfluorocarbons; aluminum recyling.
Chapter 10 - Pollution Control Methods – reduction of NOx emissions; environmental and health concerns of ground-level ozone; removal of cyanide and chromium from wastewater; use of ion-exchange resins for wastewater treatment.
