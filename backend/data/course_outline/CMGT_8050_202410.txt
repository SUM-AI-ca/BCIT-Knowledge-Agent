## Course Description
The aim of the course is to enable students to develop knowledge and examination of various methods of conceptual estimating used in commercial and institutional buildings and their significance in the development of projects prior to the design and construction phases. The course will look at why budget/conceptual estimates are necessary, what factors need to be considered when preparing, evaluating and discussing project and operating costs during the early stages of a project when very little information is available and the design is very minimal. The course will also look at a number of different types of conceptual estimate, when to best use each and the limitations of the estimates types. The lectures will be delivered in 3 hour units per week by a qualified Professional Quantity Surveyor. The 3 hour units will be broken down into a 1 hour lecture and a 2 hour lab session allowing the student to work within a group context to assist learning and allowing question and answer time with the lecturer.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:

- Develop an outline of the key elements of the owner’s decision process when considering to initiate a project.
- Generate a site options decision matrix with key elements.
- Examine different types of conceptual estimating techniques.
- Select and apply applicable conceptual estimating techniques.
- Examine and evaluate the role of life cycle costs in the decision making process.
- Evaluate procurement and project delivery strategies.
- Assess and apply contingency strategies.

## Course Goals
A conceptual estimate is necessary to give the building owner a reasonably accurate idea of the cost of a potential project at the very early stages of the development to help him decide whether the work can be undertaken as proposed or needs to be changed or even abandoned. This will depend upon the availability of funds, risk profile and prospective direct and indirect benefits. The evaluation of this type of information is extremely important at the start of each project, during the early stages of discussion and planning, consideration of finance options and feasibility for the project. The capital and life cycle cost implications including the operating and maintenance costs over the life of the building may also determine if the project proceeds or not. The approach and relative accuracy of the estimates are a critical part of the owners business plan to proceed or not and so knowledge of this type of estimating is essential for the cost consultant/quantity surveyor
