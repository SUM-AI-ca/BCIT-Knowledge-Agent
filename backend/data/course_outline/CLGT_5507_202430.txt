## Course Description
Introduction to chromosomal microarray technology (CMA) is an entry-level course that utilizes lectures and tutorials to introduce the principles of microarray technology and its application to the assessment of copy number variants and copy neutral aberrations in clinical genetics.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
By the end of this course, learners will be able to:

- Describe the strengths and weaknesses of the current standard test menu of a clinical cytogenetic laboratory.
- Outline the basic principles of microarray technology.
- Describe the development and evolution of microarray technology (BAC, oligo, SNP, targeted, whole-genome) for copy number assessment.
- Describe how CMAs detect duplications, deletions, and LOH.
- Describe the current standard of care for clinical application of CMAs to post-natal and pre-natal cytogenetic analysis as well as the area of pediatric oncology.
- Utilize reference databases for CNV assessment (DECIPHER, OMIM, DGV, etc).
- Describe the challenges associated with reporting variants of unknown significance (VOUS).
- Use microarray analysis software to view CMA data and summarize the findings.
- Communicate effectively with other students and the instructor.

## Learning Resources
There is no required text for this course. Required readings will be provided as course handouts during the lecture and tutorial sessions

## Course Goals
The goals of this course are to:

· Outline the principles of microarray technology and describe its evolution as a tool for the detection of chromosomal abnormalities.

· Introduce the use of chromosomal microarray analysis software to view and interpret patient data files.

· Describe the procedures established for defining the clinical significance of CNVs, including the use of reference databases for CNV assessment (DECIPHER, OMIM, DGV, in-house, etc).
