### Course Description
​As part of the Technology Entry program, COMM 0016 is taken concurrently with COMM 0007, providing additional tutorial support to strengthen students' English language and communication skills. To help students achieve entry into their desired programs at the end of the program, the instructor will work with students to set their own communication skill development goals and assess progress.​​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
For all of these outcomes, the level of competency required is that of a student entering a first-year technology program at BCIT. By the end of term, learners should be able to do the following:
- Set goals to improve applied communication skills and abilities.
- Reflect on their own performance regularly and effectively.
- Identify and practice strategies for succeeding in a new culture and at BCIT.
- Demonstrate the ability to manage workload by collaborating with peers, communicating with instructors, and building good study habits.
- Accurately define terms, paraphrase, and summarize information from material (cases, samples, course textbooks).
- Acknowledge source(s) correctly and appropriately in both speaking and writing.
- Revise their writing to improve organization, content, word choice, and grammatical accuracy.
- Improve writing tone and style according to typical genre conventions.
- Demonstrate a range of techniques to orally present information, including incorporating visuals and clear pronunciation of key terminology.
- Incorporate technology (e.g. generative AI bots, Grammarly, etc.) appropriately in classwork and assignments.​​​
### Learning Resources
Reference materials, readings, writing exercises, and team activity materials will be provided on the BCIT COMM 0007 Learning Hub . No textbook purchase necessary for this course.
### Course Goals
COMM 0016 will support students' success in COMM 0007 . Specifically, 0016 will help students improve the reading, writing, speaking, and listening skills needed to communicate effectively in 0007 and other TE courses. Therefore, 0016 also assists students achieve their goal of acceptance into their chosen, full-time technology programs at BCIT in subsequent terms.

### Attendance Requirements
COMM 0016 is a support course for COMM 0007 which provides strategic approaches to streamline upgrading. Its purpose is to provide practice, personalized feedback, and the opportunity to strengthen core communication skills, such as reading, writing, interpersonal skills, and presentation skills for the BCIT classroom. This approach not only reinforces understanding but also strengthens critical thinking to help students progress quickly and adapt to more formal technical and business writing contexts. Homework is rarely assigned--practice occurs in class in a workshop format; therefore, attendance is necessary to progress.
For absences, the student should be prepared to provide appropriate supporting documentation. Unapproved absence in excess of the prescribed regulations within this outline may result in failure or forced withdrawal from the course or program. Please see Policy 5101 - Student Regulations, and accompanying procedures.
### Course Specific Requirements
Your evaluation for this course includes 5% for professionalism and participation. To get full marks, you will need to
- complete all in-class assignments and practice exercises during scheduled times
- show up on time for class ready to work
- attend all classes or explain your absence in the same way you would if missing a day at work, not 'just' school
- demonstrate professionalism by treating classmates and your instructor with courtesy and consideration
- submit any (rare) homework assignments, including writing revisions, on time or obtain extension before deadline
- contribute to the success of your course by participating actively in every class.
### Other information
Assignments : Lab attendance is mandatory. Your lab assignments must be completed by you in person. All lab assignments must be completed on BCIT computers and submitted as WORD documents unless otherwise advised in class. All lab assignments are due at the end of the lab in which they are assigned. If you miss a lab assignment, you will receive a grade of zero. Lab work done outside of class time will not be accepted. Rewrites may be allowed for graded assignments in this course. Marks will be deducted for late assignments, except in exceptional circumstances, such as illness.
Missing Classes/Graded Work : If you miss a lab assignment, test, exam or quiz, you will receive zero marks. Exceptions are made for documented medical reasons or extenuating circumstances. In such a case, it is the responsibility of the student to inform the instructor immediately . See info below to learn more about student rights and responsibilities.
Ethics and Integrity: BCIT assumes that all students attending the Institute will follow a high standard of ethics. First, employ the Golden Rule: treat others as you would like to be treated. Second, Incidents of cheating or plagiarism may result in a grade of zero for the assignment, quiz, test, exam, or project for all parties involved and/or expulsion from the course. You must lift your own weights in the gym to develop your strength; the same applies when learning to plan, draft, and revise writing for business applications. Your reputation is golden—guard it well.
Note: Generative AI writing aids and associated methodologies will be discussed and integrated where appropriate—use them, do not abuse them. When the use of AI tools is permitted, I’ll clearly indicate that in the assignment instructions. You are responsible for ensuring that the content you submit under your name adheres to the course’s expectations. You must also acknowledge use of AI tools at the top or bottom of your assignment, clearly; e.g. I acknowledge the use of [name the tool] in the preparation of my assignment. Using AI tools without authorization is a violation of BCIT’s Student Code of Academic Integrity . I will help you develop your skill and discernment to use chatbots effectively—protect yourself, avoid dependency, maintain your integrity, and build self-confidence by developing your own abilities.
Attendance Policy and Procedure (from the Tech Entry Student Handbook): Your classroom at BCIT is your workplace . As in any other workplace, you’re expected to show up on time for scheduled in-person and synchronous online classes and attend regularly.
Participation: Communication classes are interactive , which means that you are an important participant in your education. You and your classmates, with your wide range of backgrounds, talents, and knowledge, will contribute to the richness of your education. In addition, as you work with other students in small groups, you will be gaining valuable teamwork skills. To participate effectively, you will need to be prepared for each class; manage your time effectively, both in and out of class; read and study the course materials; understand and follow all instructions and course procedures; ensure you have the necessary course prerequisites; and speak to me about any course-related problems you have.
Student Rights and Responsibilities: Please read the section on student rights and responsibilities in your TE Student Handbook.
Punctuality means being respectful of other people’s time, being punctual and on time for class, appointments, and for group meetings.
You are responsible for regular attendance in lectures, seminars, and laboratory periods. BCIT provides reasonable accommodation and makes allowances when you are ill, or otherwise have good reason to be absent from classes.
If your absence causes you to miss graded work, such as a lab, test, quiz, or assignment, supporting documentation, such as a doctor’s note, substantiating the reason for absence may be required. Check your TE Student Handbook to learn more about who you need to contact if you miss graded work.
Standards Your grades will be based on how well you apply the writing and speaking techniques we’ve discussed and practised in class. The standard used to measure how well you apply these techniques is based on industry’s expectations of BCIT graduates.
Your final mark for this course includes a 5% grade for professionalism and teamwork. This grade is also based on industry’s expectations of BCIT graduates. Use your lab and lecture time wisely.
Teaching Methods: Your instructor will be using the methods that have proven most effective for teaching communication to students at BCIT . This term the course will be both in-person and online, in accordance with the hybrid delivery system for all of Tech Entry.
Collaborative Learning and Teamwork: Just as in your other courses at BCIT, you will often be working with others in small groups in the classroom. This will allow you to develop the interpersonal skills you need to succeed in the workplace. You will also learn more in teams as you teach and support each other.
Peer Editing: You’ll work with partners to locate, discuss, and correct errors in each other’s work.
Oral Work: You will be reporting orally to your groups, to the whole class, and to your instructor throughout the course.
Critical Thinking : For each of your assignments in this course, you will be required to practise one or more of the following critical thinking skills:
- Defining your purpose for the communication task, understanding your target-reader, and identifying your assumptions and point of view.
- Deciding what to do or say and evaluating the consequences of your message and tone.
Ground Rules for the Classroom: BCIT and its management, together with the unions on campus and your Student Association, are committed to providing an environment where the individual differences of all students and employees are valued and respected – an environment free from harassment and discrimination. We are all here to work cooperatively and to learn together.
I am offended by remarks that stereotype people as members of groups and express prejudices against them. I will object to any such remarks that I hear in class, and you are welcome to do the same. If someone objects to a remark you have made, please clarify what you said (if you were misinterpreted) and apologize when appropriate.
Here are some guidelines for ensuring that your classroom learning environment creates a sense of community and mutual respect where everyone can learn. You are welcome to offer input or suggestions. I want all of you to be successful in this course:
- Criticize points of view, opinions, statements, and behaviour. Do NOT criticize people. We are all here to learn.
- Do NOT use coarse language in the classroom, do not gossip, be collegial. Build your professional demeanor.
- Come to all classes, be on time, and stay until the end of the class. If you have to miss a class for any reason, you are responsible for the material covered, announcements made in your absence, and for getting any materials that were distributed in class.
- To ensure that all students can stay focused on the class lecture/discussion, only one person at a time should be speaking.
- Do not take/make personal calls, scroll, post (etc. etc.) or use your cell phone for purposes unrelated to classroom work, as this distracts you and others from the tasks at hand.
Meaning of Grades: BCIT is noted for its high standards – that’s why you’re here. These high standards are your guarantee that employers will respect your diploma and your grades and will want to hire you. As an instructor at BCIT, I am responsible for assessing your work fairly and accurately according to the standards set by the Institute and by my profession. As a student here, you’re responsible for doing your best work to meet those standards.
For students, the grade on an assignment is sometimes disappointing. For instructors, determining the appropriate grade is not an easy job. The instructor must read each assignment with extreme care and concentration. The grade itself is a judgement made by the instructor that is based on years of acquired expertise and experience. Assignments are seldom all good or all bad; the virtues of an assignment must be balanced against its defects to determine the final grade.

Attempts As stated in BCIT Policy 5103 - Student Evaluation , students must successfully complete a course within a maximum of three (3) attempts at the course. Students with two attempts in a single course will be allowed to repeat the course only upon permission from the Associate Dean. Students who have not successfully completed a course within three attempts will not be eligible to graduate from their respective program. For those courses or programs that have Education Council approval, the number of attempts as stated in the evaluation section of the course outline shall apply.
Academic Integrity It is the responsibility of all students to be familiar with the Student Code of Academic Integrity.
