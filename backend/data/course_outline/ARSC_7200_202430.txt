### Course Description
Through a series of lectures and work sessions presenting learning resources, case studies, green building rating systems and other related materials, students will have the opportunity to develop a thorough understanding of environmentally responsible design in the construction industry. This understanding will consider both the technical performance of individual buildings and the social context within which sustainability is now being framed.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:
- Analyze the environmental context of sustainability as it relates to the construction industry.
- Communicate the importance of environmental responsibility in the construction industry.
- Explain 'green building' approaches to sustainable sites (planning), water efficiency, energy and atmosphere, materials and resources, indoor environmental quality.
- Identify sustainability indicators and apply them to measure sustainability.
- Evaluate various design options using green building rating systems.
- Apply life cycle analysis to design options and make recommendations based on these analyses.
- Perform effective research in the area of green building materials.
- Advocate environmental responsibility in design and construction.
- Integrate green building concepts into the design.
- Participate effectively in an integrated design process.
- Use the vocabulary and tools of sustainable design.

### Course Goals
The goal of this course is to expand and reinforce our understanding of sustainability as embracing not simply the technological ‘hardware’ that underpins quantitative advances in building performance; but also, the evolving social ‘software’ of human values, beliefs and behaviors, that are essential to the cultural changes our society must make in order to achieve our goals.
On completion of this course, students will be able to:
- Analyze the environmental and social context of sustainability as it relates to the construction industry, its local implementation and global supply chains
- Explain the Indigenous worldview; the concept of Doughnut Economics and the imperative for adopting holistic, long-term thinking
- Identify 'green building' approaches to Place; Water, Energy, Health & Happiness; Materials; Equity and Beauty as outlined in the Living Building Challenge
- Identify commonly used sustainability indicators (including mandatory and elective green building rating systems) and how they might be integrated to effectively create a holistic approach to sustainability.
- Explain the connection between community design, city planning and sustainability.
- Conduct research into the attributes that make a building material sustainable; and the design practices and protocols that could bring us closer to circularity in construction
- Compare building case studies to assess the efficacy of various sustainable design strategies, including passive design, living building design, regenerative design, Passive House and Net Zero Energy
- Identify and explain various aspects of a holistic and sustainable approach to water supply and waste disposal for buildings
- Identify and explain various aspects of a holistic, sustainable approach to operating energy for buildings
- Identify and explain various aspects of a holistic approach to occupant health and well-being in buildings
- Advocate environmental responsibility in design and construction, not just in terms of quantitative building performance; but also for the qualitative values of occupant health & wellbeing, equity & inclusion.
