### Course Description
Reviews the formation, characteristics and geometry of crustal deformation structures. The concepts of stress and strain and the mechanisms of deformation provide the background for a descriptive analysis of common deformation structures including joints, veins, faults, folds, planar and linear deformation fabrics and shear zones. The relationships between geological structures, mineral deposits and mining are examined throughout the course, as are the links between deformation and plate tectonics. Laboratory exercises focus on the visualization and graphical solution to three-dimensional structural problems and structural geology map problems. Some labs will involve analysis of structures in the field.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:
- Explain the basic aspects of stress and strain with respect to the deformation of rocks [1].
- Describe principle deformation mechanisms and the effects of changing conditions on deformation mechanisms [1].
- Outline the microscopic features and processes that lead to the development of brittle and ductile deformation structures [1].
- Describe common deformation structures such as fractures, joints, faults, folds, foliations and lineations [1].
- Analyse common deformation structures such as fractures, joints, faults, folds, foliations and lineations [2].
- Demonstrate the important relationships between deformation and the formation and mining of mineral deposits [1].
- Demonstrate the relationship between tectonics and deformation structures. [1].
- Apply structural analysis to field mapping and geological/mining problems [2,3].

### Course Schedule and Assignments
- Lecture and Lab Schedule (Subject to modifications as the course progresses)
Week Starting | Outcome/Material Covered
Sep. 02 | Introduction to structural geology; an overview of the course content Introduction to planes and lines Primary and tectonic structures Note: The laboratory hours for this week will be used for lectures.
Sep. 08 | Stress: normal stress, shear stress, stress in three dimensions, mohr diagram for stress, deviatoric stress, measuring stress. Lab 1 – Outcrop patterns, Structure Contours and 3-Point Problems
Sep. 15 | Strain: deformation and strain, types of strain, Mohr circle for strain, states of strain. Lab 2 – Orthographic Projections (applied to apparent dip problems)
Sep. 22 | Mechanical Behaviour or Rocks (Rheology): elastic, plastic and viscous behaviour, brittle and ductile deformation, effects of temperature, confining pressure, strain rate and pore fluid pressure on rheology and deformation, brittle-ductile transition, brittle and ductile deformation mechanism. Lab 3 – Bree Creek Map and Orientation of Rock Units from Map Data
Sep. 29 | Mechanical Behaviour or Rocks (Rheology): cont’d. Lab 4 – Stratigraphic Thickness and Column
Oct. 06 | Joints – morphology and character, joint arrays and sets, formation and origin, common types, significance to mining and mineralization. Veins – formation, mineralogy and textures, morphology, relationship to mineralization and vein deposits. Lab 5 – Structure Sections
Oct. 13 | Faults – anatomy of faults, fault terminology, Anderson’s classification of faults, recognizing faults and physical characteristics, fault rocks, map representation of faults, thrust faults, normal faults, strike-slip faults, analysis of faults, relationship of faults to mineralization. Lab 6 – Stereonets
Oct. 20 | Faults – cont’d. Midterm Exam (30%): Lecture portion (15%) on Tuesday Oct. 21, Lab portion (15%) on Thursday Oct. 23
Oct. 27 | Folds – anatomy of a fold, cylindrical folds and the fold axis, antiforms and synforms, fold shapes, description and classification, superimposed folds, folds and mineralization. Lab 7 – Fault Analysis
Nov. 03 | Folds – cont’d. Lab 8 – Fold Analysis
Nov. 10 | Fabrics: Foliations and Lineations – types of foliations and their character, cleavage (stylolite, crenulation, pencil, slaty, phyllitic), schistosity, gneissic layering, relationship of cleavage to folding, intersection lineations and fold hinges, boudinage, mineral lineations, significance of lineations to mineral deposits. Lab 9 – Fold Analysis and Cross-Sections
Nov. 17 | Ductile Shear Zones – shear zone deformation processes, mylonites, shear-sense indicators, shear zone patterns, relationship of shear zones to mineralization. Note: The laboratory hours for this week will be used for lectures.
Nov. 24 | Ductile Shear Zones Course review Term project interview Due date for term project – 2:30 pm on Thursday Nov 27 th
Dec. 01 | Lab review Final Lab Exam (25%): Thursday Dec. 4 th
Dec. 08-12 | Final Exam Week (Final Exam, 25%)
