## Course Description
This is an advanced course built on the learning acquired during the course MINE 2105 - Surface Mining and Blasting. Students will further develop their knowledge and skills in surface mine designs and production planning. Topics covered during the course include: an in-depth analysis of the cutoff grade concept for both metallic and coal mines, review of surface mining methods and mining operations, extraction of the rock minerals by methods other than drilling and blasting (rocks of medium and low strength). The course includes the design of surface mine dewatering systems. The fundamentals of open pit slope design incorporating geological, geotechnical and hydrogeological models will be covered. Production capacities of mining equipment including machine availability and utilization will be examined with the emphasis on bucket wheel excavators and draglines. The use of range diagrams and related calculations will be practiced.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Understand and use the cut-off grade concept in the surface mining design and mine planning processes [1,2,3,4,11].
- Describe the fundamentals of slope design for surface mining [2].
- Understand and apply the geological, geotechnical and hydrogeological models for the design of surface mines [2,3].
- Select appropriate mining systems for various types of mineral deposits in varying physical, economic and environmental conditions [2,3].
- Analyze surface mining unit operations and select the equipment used [2,4].
- Determine the correct match of type and size of equipment [2,3].
- Calculate the productivities for each type of equipment [2,4].
- Implement a slope depressurization program [2,3,4,11].
- Design and calculate a dewatering system for a surface mining operation in varying conditions [2,3,4].
- Understand and describe the risk management concept and processes associated with surface mine slope design [3,4,11].
