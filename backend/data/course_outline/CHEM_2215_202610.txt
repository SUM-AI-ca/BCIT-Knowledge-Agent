### Course Description
Offers an applied approach to melding established chemical principles to chemical hazards, their problems and solutions. Terminology encountered in the field is related to principles such as acid-base chemistry, oxidation-reduction, electrochemistry, stoichiometry, equilibrium, chemical bonding, simple thermodynamics, etc., that are linked to potential and real chemical hazards. Laboratory exercises and field trips are designed to complement and integrate lecture material. The language of practical and theoretical applications is emphasized.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
This course is tailored to give the students a good working knowledge of the subject matter with a firm introduction to the ‘language' of chemical safety.
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to
- Apply a variety of established chemical principles to chemical hazard problems in the field of OCHS.
- Relate principles such as acid-base, oxidation and reduction properties and reactions; stoichiometry, equilibrium, simple thermodynamics and chemical bonding to the hazards of chemical substances.
- Rate the potential hazards of chemical substances using published standard physical and chemical properties.
- Relate the potential reactivities of some common hazardous elements to their periodic properties.
- Relate the properties and reactivities of major common chemicals to their safe handling, transportation and storage in the workplace.
- Be able to apply safe practices to the handling of specific chemicals under industrial spillage emergencies.
- Identify the sources and types of corrosive, water-reactive, toxic, explosive or similarly hazardous or potentially hazardous chemicals.
- Direct or handle tasks involving the safe management of common hazardous chemicals.
- Choose the correct type of fire extinguisher for chemical or common fires based on knowledge of the chemistry of fire and fire extinguishing materials.
- Advise on problems dealing with accident prevention and incidents involving hazardous materials.
- Name common inorganic compounds from their formula and vice-versa.
- Advise on and solve storage problems involving various chemicals based on the use of correct support literature, and using a scientific approach.
- Be able to understand and apply terminology used in the labeling and placards covered by the Canadian Transport of Dangerous Goods Act and WHMIS regulations.

### Course topics
1. Introduction to corrosive materials
2. Chemicals in the workplace: definitions, ratings (combustible, fire point etc.), hazard identification systems, threshold limit values (including T.W.A.). lethal doses (LD50, LC50), acute effects, chronic effects, mutagens, teratogens, carcinogens, anemia, methods of exposure, calculations - ppm, mg/m³
3. Physical interpretations: Calculations involving the Gas Laws, rupturing of containers. Dispersion of chemicals: solids, particulate matter - dust, fumes, liquids - mist, fogs, gases and vapor.
4. The Periodic Table: filing of electrons, family groups, oxides, metals, non-metals, gases.
5. Chemical bonding: ionic, covalent, polar covalent, metallic and coordinate bonding.
6. Equilibrium Studies: Le Chatelier's Principle, equilibrium constants, the role of complexing agents, ionization constants (calculations), conjugate acid-base pairs, buffers, and the role of chelates in poisoning.
7. Chemistry of common elements: oxygen, hydrogen, the halogens, carbon, phosphorous, sulfur, corrosive materials (revisited).
8. Chemistry of water-reactive materials: Properties, reaction equations involving metals.
9. Introduction to thermodynamics: ΔH, ΔG and ΔS, exothermic and endothermic reactions.
10. The chemistry of fire and fire extinguishers.
11. Introduction to kinetics: reaction order, chain reaction, factors affecting reactions
12. Explosives and explosions
