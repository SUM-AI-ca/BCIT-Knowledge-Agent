## Course Description
The course covers theoretical and practical basic knowledge of electrical passive components, electrical quantities, their units and relationships. Major topics covered include: DC, simple transient, and AC circuit analysis of RLC circuits, magnetism and magnetic circuits. Each topic is reinforced by practical applications, laboratory exercises, and hands-on instruction on electrical devices and techniques.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of the course the student will be able to:

- Define and describe electrical entities such as: resistance, conductance, voltage, current, power, electric and magnetic field, complex impedance and their associated SI units.
- Describe the circuit characteristics of resistors, capacitors and inductors in DC and AC circuits.
- Use Ohm’s law, voltage and current divider rules, Kirchhoff’s voltage and Kirchhoff’s current law to analyze series and parallel networks.
- Analyze complex networks using: branch-current analysis, mesh analysis or nodal analysis.
- Use superposition, Thevenin’s and or Norton’s theorem to analyze series-parallel networks.
- Explain how to use the maximum power transfer theorem to choose a load that will receive maximum power.
- Determine the transient response of RC and RL networks and plot the resulting voltages and currents.
- Explain the basic equations of electricity and magnetism.
- Describe electric and magnetic fields in terms of flux and flux density.
- Explain what magnetic circuits are and what they are used for.
- Explain the AC fundamentals in terms of voltage and current phasors and impedance.
- Determine impedance, voltage, and current values for a series-parallel AC network.
- Perform relevant power calculations and compute the power factor of an AC network.
- Use the laboratory test equipment (DVM, power supply, function generator, oscilloscope etc) in a safe and proper manner.
- Measure circuit parameters using appropriate test equipment.
- Demonstrate in the laboratory the ability to assemble, test and troubleshoot various circuits.

## Course topics
- Describe the characteristics of conductors, insulators, and semiconductors.
- Define voltage and describe its relation to energy and charge.
- Explain how current is established in a circuit and its relation to charge and time.
- Contrast direct current (DC) and alternating current (AC).
- Compare different battery types and their characteristics.
- Define resistance and conductance and describe the relation between the two.
- List and describe various types of resistors and explain how to identify the value of a resistor.
- Discuss the effects of temperature on the resistance of a material.
- Describe the main characteristics of other devices such as thermistors, photoconductive cells,
- Use proper procedures for measuring current, voltage, and resistance.

- Use Ohm’s law to describe the relationship between voltage, current, and resistance.
- Describe how Ohm’s law can be used to analyze and troubleshoot electrical circuits.
- Read and interpret a graphical plot of voltage versus current.
- Compute power in DC circuits and explain how voltage, current, resistance and power are related.
- Determine the minimum acceptable power rating for a resistor in any circuit.
- Describe the relationship between power and heat and show how to calculate the efficiency of a system.

- Describe the main characteristics of series and parallel circuits.
- Discuss the characteristics of ideal and real voltage and current sources.
- Use Ohm’s law, voltage divider rule, and Kirchhoff’s voltage law to analyze a series network.
- Use Ohm’s law, the current divider rule and Kirchhoff’s current law to analyze a parallel network.
- Determine the total resistance in a series or parallel circuit.
- Solve for the voltage between any two points in a series or parallel circuit.
- Calculate the loading effect of ammeters and voltmeters.
- Use the Multisim program to analyze simple circuits.
- Explain the symptoms associated with an open (or shorted) component in a series or parallel circuit.
- Analyze the operation of the Wheatstone Bridge.

- Convert a voltage (current) source into an equivalent current (voltage) source.
- Solve a given series-parallel circuit for any current, voltage, or power value.
- Analyze complex linear networks using: branch-current analysis, mesh analysis or nodal analysis.
- Apply the superposition theorem to analyze multisource circuits.
- Use Thevenin’s (or Norton’s) theorem to analyze complex circuits.
- Explain how to use the maximum power transfer theorem to choose a load that will receive maximum power.
- State and use the reciprocity and the substitution theorems.

- Describe the basic construction of capacitors; explain how capacitors store charge and describe the electric field of a capacitor.
- Define capacitance and describe what factors affect capacitance.
- List the main characteristics of different types of capacitors and examine various marking schemes used for labeling.
- Compute the capacitance of series and parallel capacitive networks.
- Explain why transients occur in simple RC circuits.
- Determine the transient (time-varying) response of an RC network and plot the resulting voltages and currents.
- Use the Multisim program to solve simple RC-transient problems.

- Describe magnetic fields in terms of flux and flux density.
- Explain what magnetic circuits are and what they are used.
- Describe the relationship between B (magnetic flux density) and H (magnetic field intensity or magnetizing force).
- Use Ampere’s circuital law to solve simple series magnetic circuits.
- Describe what an inductor is and list the factors that affect the strength of the magnetic field established by an inductor.
- Explain the Faraday’s law and Lenz’s law and use Faraday’s law to compute the induced voltage.
- Define inductance and compute the inductance of series and parallel networks.
- Explain why transients occur in simple RL circuits.
- Determine the transient (time-varying) response of an RL network and plot the resulting voltages and currents.
- Use the Multisim program to solve simple RL-transient problems.

- Identify the characteristics of a sinusoidal waveform such as: period, frequency, amplitude, rms and average values etc.
- Describe how ac voltage is generated.
- Use phasors to represent sinusoidal voltages and currents.
- Represent ac voltage and current phasors as complex numbers.
- Describe the response of a resistor, capacitor and inductor to the application of a sinusoidal voltage or current.
- Compute the inductive and capacitive reactance.
- Explain the impedance concept and determine the impedance for R, L and C circuit elements.
- Use Ohm’s law, Kirchhoff’s voltage and current laws, voltage and current divider rules to analyze series and parallel ac circuits.
- Analyze complex networks using: branch-current analysis, mesh analysis or nodal analysis.
- Apply the superposition theorem to analyze multisource circuits.
- Use the Thevenin’s (or Norton’s) theorem to analyze series-parallel and bridge circuits.
- Explain what is meant by active power, reactive power and apparent power.
- Construct and use the power triangle to analyze power to complex loads.
- Compute the power factor.
