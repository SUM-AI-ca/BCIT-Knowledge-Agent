## Course Description
This course further examines the human body: cardiac, respiratory, digestive, renal, neurological (incl special senses), endocrine, and reproductive systems. Fluid and pH homeostasis are studied to exemplify regulation and integration of body systems. In the seminars, case studies and other active learning techniques are used to integrate knowledge across body systems and to develop critical thinking skills essential to clinical practice. Evaluation of learning is conducted through weekly seminar quizzes, exams, as well as in-class practice and participation.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

- Relate function of body organs and systems to their tissue components
- Relate structure to function in the human body
- Apply knowledge of anatomical and physiological principles to the process of diagnosing and treating selected pathological conditions
- Identify cellular & extracellular components and predict changes in physiology when that component becomes dysfunctional
- Develop skills in problem solving and critical thinking when approaching physiology case studies

## Course Schedule and Assignments
Week: Lecture Topics
1: Blood Composition and functions – plasma - red blood cell formation and turnover - hemoglobin structure and synthesis - white blood cells - WBC types and functions - WBC production - platelets production and function – hemostasis Lymphatic System Functions of lymph - formation and circulation of lymph - lymph node structure and functions – other lymphoid tissues and organs
2: Circulatory System Heart - anatomy of the heart – cardiac function - regulation of cardiac function – cardiac enzymes/markers
3: Circulatory System (Cont.) Peripheral circulation - peripheral hemodynamics -regulation of blood flow and pressure – capillary exchange – albumin and oncotic pressure
4: Respiratory System Organization of the respiratory system – airway and alveolar epithelia - secretions of the respiratory system, mechanisms of gas exchange - control of respiration - blood gas transport - oxygen dissociation curves – acid/base balance
5: Digestive System Gross anatomy and histological organization of the gastrointestinal tract – salivary gland structure, secretions and function - GI tract secretions, functions and regulation - Liver, gall bladder and pancreatic structure and function - Digestion and nutrient absorption
6: Urinary System Gross structure - renal blood supply and nephron structure – urine formation – glomerular filtration
7: Urinary System tubular reabsorption and secretion - control of blood volume and composition - urine storage and micturition

8: Fluid, Electrolyte and Acid-Base Balance Fluid compartments - electrolyte distribution - sodium – potassium - other electrolytes - Acid-base balance - mechanisms of pH control - chemical buffers - physiological buffers - respiratory and renal compensation
9: Reproductive System Gross anatomy – menstrual and ovarian cycles – hormonal control of cycles – male reproductive tract and associated glands – composition and functions of secretions
10: Review
