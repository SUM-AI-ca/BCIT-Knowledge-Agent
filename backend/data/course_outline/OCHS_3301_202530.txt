### Course Description
A theory course introducing the recognition and control of physical hazards in occupational hygiene: noise, vibration, temperature extremes, pressure extremes, nonionizing radiation and ionizing radiation. Explores practical applications including assessing and interpretation of physical hazard-related OH&S Legislation and selection of appropriate engineering, administrative, and PPE controls for these physical hazards.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Explain the properties that describe the physical nature of sound.
- Define decibels.
- Given formulae for sound power, pressure and intensity, apply algebraic laws to carry out calculations involving deciebels.
- Define the common noise descriptors and terms used in noise legislation and standards.
- Describe the basic anatomy and physiology of the ear.
- Describe the role of the OH&S professional in the enforcement of, or compliance with, occupational noise legislation.
- Explain the physical nature of human vibration.
- Name and describe the physiological effects of excess exposure of vibration.
- Recommend methods of human vibration control.
- Explain the variables in the net heat exchange equation and relate these variables to hear exhaustion, heat stroke, and hypothermia.
- Given WBGT and ECT measurements apply temperature-related OHS legislation.
- Apply physical gas laws to explain the physiological responses of the body to work in pressure extremes.
- Interpret OHS laws related to work in pressure extremes, including use of dive tables.
- Explain and discuss the forms of nonionizing radiation encountered in the workplace.
- Given an exposure to a nonionizing radiation in the workplace, interpret the appropriate OHS legislation, interpret the relevant OEL and recommend an exposure control program.
- Explain and discuss the forms of ionizing radiation encountered in the workplace.
- Explain the role of agencies such as Health Canada in the protection of radiation exposed workers in Canada.
- Given an exposure to an ionizing radiation in the workplace, interpret the appropriate OHS legislation, interpret the relevant OEL and recommend an exposure control program.
- Demonstrate an ability to prepare occupational hygiene evaluation/assessment technical reports that are acceptable for regulatory compliance purposes.
- Demonstrate an ability to prepare exposure control plans that are acceptable for regulatory compliance purposes.

### Other information
OCHS 3301 is one of 4 occupational hygiene courses the student will complete during second year of the Diploma program. OCHS 3301 and its companion course, OCHS 3311, cover the PHYSICAL occupational hygiene hazards (noise, vibration, temperature extremes, pressure extremes, nonionizing radiation and ionizing radiation).
OCHS 4301 and 4311 cover the BIOLOGICAL and CHEMICAL occupational hygiene hazards.
