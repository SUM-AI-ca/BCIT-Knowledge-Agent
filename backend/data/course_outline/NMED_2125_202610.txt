### Course Description
​NMED 2125 Radiopharmacy 2 builds on NMED 1125 Radiopharmacy and Radiochemistry 1. Students continue practicing core radiopharmacy skills related to knowledge and safety in radiopharmacy operations, handling of biohazardous materials and sharps, chemicals, and radioactivity. They will advance in these areas in terms of independence, efficiency, and safety. Some specific areas of practice include basic indications, mechanism of localization, preparation, and quality-control guidelines for a variety of radiopharmaceuticals used in a nuclear medicine department. Using the manufacturer package insert or best-practice guidelines, ALARA, and CNSC regulations, students will perform generator elution and QC, radiopharmaceutical preparation and QC, dose dispensing, record-keeping, and managing inventory and equipment, as well as other common lab procedures.​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Consistently perform safe handling and disposal of biohazardous substances, chemicals, syringes and sharps according to universal precautions, WHMIS, and general lab safety guidelines. (4.4.3)
- Consistently demonstrate safe work practices according to CNSC regulations and the ALARA principle in a simulated nuclear medicine environment. (NM 1.1, 1.3, 1.5, 1.7, 1.8, 2.3)
- Perform quality control on radiopharmacy lab equipment. (NM 3.3)
- Manage inventory and appropriate storage of radioactive and non-radioactive materials using a radiopharmacy lab management software system. (NM 2.2, 4.1)
- Identify the indications and mechanism of localization for a given radiopharmaceutical. (NM 4.5)
- Plan and perform radiopharmaceutical kit reconstitution to meet simulated patient schedule needs following manufacturer or best-practice guidelines. (NM 4.5)
- Perform complex radiopharmaceutical quality control according to manufacturer or best-practice guidelines. (NM 4.6)
- Analyze radiolabeling results for clinical acceptability and take appropriate action. (NM 4.6)
- Dispense multiple radiopharmaceutical patient doses safely and accurately. (NM 4.9)
- Prepare a standard/stock solution for a given clinical application. (NM 4.10)​

### Attendance Requirements
Mastering the lab skills taught in this course requires full participation in all lab activities. Students who miss a lab for any reason are responsible for arranging a make-up lab during the prescribed Open Lab period on the timetable. Please connect with the Open Lab Instructor a minimum of 24 hours in advance of the Open Lab so they have time to prepare.
### Safety equipment or protective clothing
A full length lab coat with long sleeves and tight-fitting cuffs is required for all labs.To reduce the risk of radioactive contamination to the skin, all skin below the neck needs to remain covered during radiopharmacy labs. Please arrive to all labs wearing long pants with closed-toed/heeled shoes and your lab coat, and keeping any long hair secured away from the face .
A lead apron and gloves will be provided for all labs. Safety goggles are not required and will be provided if necessary.
