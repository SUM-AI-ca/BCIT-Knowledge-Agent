## Course Description
This course introduces students to the scientific principles of digital forensics and provides a good forensic evidence acquisition experience with the most common digital devices found today. Students will learn the fundamental components of a forensic investigation, including proper evidence collection, the chain of custody, and forensic standards for legal evidence. Students will understand how to forensically acquire laptop/desktop computers, mobile phones, Raid arrays and USB drives, as each device type often requires a unique combination of skills, techniques and forensic tools to capture a forensic image that can be used for evidentiary purposes. The course offers a brief introduction to data recovery methods and techniques, which would assist the future investigator in extracting data from non-functioning digital devices.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:

- Describe the structure and components of digital devices related to digital forensics.
- Explain the overall flow and structure of a digital forensic investigation.
- Explain how information is stored and extracted from different types of data storage devices.
- Identify Windows/Mac computer-based artifacts.
- Perform Initial data recovery procedures.
- Select the best method of forensic evidence collection to be implemented for a given device.
- Execute the acquisition of a complete forensic image using different forensic tools on a variety of devices (tablets, mobile phones, Desktop/laptop computers, RAID arrays, Flash-based devices).
- Conduct RAM acquisition.
- Prepare and present detailed, court-acceptable notes outlining every step in the acquisition process.

## Course Goals
Students will learn how to carefully plan the forensic acquisition process while gathering information regarding the scope and the scene of the investigation including considerations for the unique specifics of each digital device to be imaged or cloned, selecting the best approach to each acquisition, and documenting the process.

Students will learn about the different tools and methods involved in the digital evidence collection process and the best ways to utilize them. Various file systems, OS types, and digital media carriers will be analyzed and explained throughout the course to expand the knowledge of the process.

Students will learn about the rules and principles that guide the forensic data acquisition process. A proper note-taking procedure will be discussed and implemented.Students will practice the proper techniques necessary to acquire a sound forensic image from a variety of digital devices. Students will also be exposed to and practice the proper acquisition of evidence from both “live” and “dead” systems.

## Course topics
Part 1 - Introduction to data storage technology:

- Hard drives – Basic concepts, methods of operation, types (SATA/(IDE)/SAS//)
- Flash drives – Basic concepts, the principle of flash technology operation
- Mobile devices
- Partitioning (Windows, Linux/Unix)
- File systems – NTFS, FAT16/32, exFAT – an introduction to each file system’s and operating methods, file storage and unallocated space concepts
- Types of RAID arrays

Part 2 – Introduction to Forensic evidence collection tools and methods

- Determining the Scope of Examination – information and asking the right questions
- Documentation – Work order, ITO, Chain of custody form
- Preparing target media for storing the images – The wiping process
- Cloud storage – Acquiring cloud artifacts
- Controlling the evidence integrity – Hardware/software write blockers
- Faraday bags and cages
- Images clones
- Imaging selected Partitions/Volumes
- Types of image files RAW
- Dedicated Hardware imaging tools and their methods of operation
- Working with software evidence collection tools - Encase imager, Linux tools, .
- Cloning and imaging using Multiboot USB and various Linux distributions
- GPS devices acquisition and analysis
- RAM acquisition methods
- Hashing and image validation
- Working with encrypted images and devices
- Imaging mobile devices – Cellebrite and additional tools capable of mobile extraction
- Preparation of imaging station/laptop
- Proper note-taking

Part 3 – Engaging in Forensic Imaging – Hands-on practice:

- Creating sound forensic images using the learned techniques: Wiping and preparing target drives Physical storage removal and Imaging Flash-based devices Imaging SATA drives Imaging SSD devices Imaging RAID arrays Imaging tablets/mobile devices Validating the images created
- Wiping and preparing target drives
- Physical storage removal and
- Imaging Flash-based devices
- Imaging SATA drives
- Imaging SSD devices
- Imaging RAID arrays
- Imaging tablets/mobile devices
- Validating the images created
