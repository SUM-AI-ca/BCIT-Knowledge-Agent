### Course Description
This course builds on the basic microcomputer skills developed in MATH 1441 and the comprehensive coverage of basic statistical inference methods presented in MATH 2441 to familiarize the student with more sophisticated applications of spreadsheet programs to more sophisticated methods of statistical analysis. The course includes an introduction to experimental design issues, one-way analysis of variance, multiple regression, factorial analysis, by implementing the computations in a spreadsheet from scratch, and using various built-in tools. Emphasis is given to interpretation of results.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will be able to:
- Demonstrate use of the basic statistical functions and charting facilities of the electronic spreadsheet package they are using.
- Explain the difference between experiments and observational studies, define and give examples illustrating the concepts of experimental units, treatments, factors, experimental error, control treatments, replications, blocking, and related terms, concepts, and issues of experimental design and statistical analysis.
- Explain the principle behind the one-way analysis of variance method for inferring whether a difference exists between the means of several statistical populations (i.e., state the basic ANOVA model and state the appropriate null and alternative hypothesis); set up the basic calculations using a spreadsheet; and draw the appropriate conclusion based on the value of the F-Test statistic.
- Explain the difference between pair-wise and experiment-wise levels of significance, and the importance of the issue in the ANOVA experiment.
- Describe and carry out the Fisher Least Significant Difference test, Tukey's test, the Bonferroni test, the Duncan Multiple Range Test, etc., for achieving pair-wise comparisons of the means of the populations under consideration.
- Carry out a one-way analysis of variance calculation using the built-in data analysis tool in Excel, relate the results produced to the basic features of the method using standard notation, and interpret the results.
- Give examples of typical situations relevant to food technology for which the ANOVA/paired comparison procedures are useful in objectively distinguishing features of varieties, brands, production procedures, etc.
- Set up in a computer worksheet a single-variable regression analysis calculation, including the computation of the slope and intercept of the least-squares line, the t-test for the slope, the F-test for existence of a linear relationship, computation of the coefficient of determination and its interpretation, computation and graphing of estimation and prediction bands
- Produce residual and line fit plots, both 'manually' and using existing regression analysis tools in the spreadsheet package, and explain how these relate to the adequacy or the appropriateness of the linear model.
- Give examples of the interpretation of regression analysis results in situations relevant to food technology in which the constants in the regression equation relate to physical, chemical, biological, or other properties of the material under study.
- Describe the extension of the regression model to situations involving two or more independent variables, including methods for handling non-linear terms and use of dummy variables in the regression model, implement such calculations using existing linear regression tools in the spreadsheet package, to produce and interpret the results, including assessment of the adequacy of the regression model (via residual plots).
- Demonstrate the interpretation of results of a multiple regression analysis to distinguish between important and unimportant influences of various variables on some characteristics of an application on food technology.
- Describe the principles of extending single-factor ANOVA methods to experiments involving two distinct factors; set up the required calculations in a computer spreadsheet from the basic formulas, and using existing factor analysis tools in the software package so that the results of the latter calculations can be interpreted in terms of the quantities in the basic description in statistical terms; draw appropriate conclusions from the ANOVA table produced; and carry out an appropriate multiple comparison test (e.g., Duncan Multiple Range Test) with interpretation.

### Course Goals
The two major goals of the course are:
- to introduce the student to a working/understanding knowledge of basic idea of experimental design and some advanced statistical inference methods used to analyze data obtained in typical studies in their technology, and
- to expand the student's use of spreadsheet (Excel) and statistics software for statistical computations.
This course introduces students to statistics and computer skills for planning and implementing final term projects, analyzing the resulting data, and preparing reports.
