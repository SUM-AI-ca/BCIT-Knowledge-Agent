### Course Description
​This course builds on the principles of marketing, sales, and accounting to explore the hotel sales process and revenue management. Learners will explore new business development, account management and current issues in hotel sales. Principles of revenue management, forecasting, inventory pricing strategy, performance metrics, and common reports used will be covered. Role plays, reports, presentations, and a revenue management simulation will be used to evaluate progress. ​
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Develop effective public speaking skills by delivering oral presentations (individual and team) to audiences of diverse sizes.
- Apply marketing and sales principles to the hotel and services industry.
- Discuss sales and revenue opportunities and relative to the accommodation industry.
- Identify typical organisational structures for resorts and hotel sales departments.
- Analyze trends impacting the accommodation sector.
- Outline various revenue management principles used to increase revenue.
- Assess various revenue management strategies through a simulation
- Conduct a simulated sales call with a client.
- Analyze data to develop a strategy to increase revenue​.

### Course Goals
This course focuses on revenue growth strategies and advanced personal selling methods and skills. The course will allow the learner to explore revenue optimization techniques which will allow a company to set and adjust pricing to maximize profitability. Pricing and revenue optimization uses analytical techniques derived from statistics, management science, and machine learning. In addition to revenue optimization strategy and practical applications, this course covers advanced personal selling skills used by tourism professionals. Skills and attitudes for the complex negotiations of multi-year agreements, conducting sales calls overseas, and tactics to close more business will be covered. Additionally, students will prepare for their internship with interview and resume skill-building activities.

### Course Schedule and Assignments
Week 1 January 6, 2026 | Course kickoff & foundations • Course overview, expectations, grading • The business of hotels: location types, product types, key segments • Internship preparation: cover letters & resumes, how to present your “hospitality story” | Readings: Business Book Club Launch • Overview of 3 books and why they matter for hotel careers • Reading for next week: Be Our Guest – Introduction + Chapter 1
Week 2 January 13, 2026 | Ownership, brands & interviews • Ownership vs management vs brands • Brands vs independent hotels: pros/cons • Interviewing skills lab & mock interviews (service-focused questions) | Readings : Disney #1 – Designing Service • Discussion: first impressions of Disney’s approach • Reading for next week: Be Our Guest – Chapters 2–4 (quality service, service standards, on-stage/back-stage)
Week 3 January 20, 2026 | Intro to Revenue Management • Key terms: Occupancy, ADR, RevPAR • How guest experience supports pricing power and demand • Revenue Management game + debrief (linking pricing decisions to guest perception) | Readings: Disney #2 – Guest Experience & Recovery • Discussion on service standards and recovery in Be Our Guest • Reading for next week: Finish Be Our Guest (remaining core chapters on guest satisfaction, service recovery, continuous improvement)
Week 4 January 27, 2026 | Lead qualification & sales development • Lead qualification: finding the “right” business for the hotel • Sales & revenue development: building a simple sales funnel • Practice: discovery questions that explore both logistics & guest experience | Readings : Disney #3 – Applying Disney to Hotels • Wrap-up discussion: what Disney teaches hotels about designing arrivals, stays, and recoveries • Optional article: “You Are Where You Sleep” (if assigned) • Reading for next week: The New Gold Standard – Introduction + Chapters 1–3
Week 5 February 3, 2026 | Lifestyle & niche hotels + F&B basics • Lifestyle brands and niche concepts (who they target, how they feel) • Very light RM for F&B (capacity, dayparts, average check, menu mix) • Lab: selling brands vs independents (positioning and storytelling) | Readings : Ritz #1 – Gold Standards & Brand Promise • Discussion of Ritz-Carlton Gold Standards and service philosophy • Reading for next week: The New Gold Standard – Chapters 4–7 (empowerment, service recovery, leadership/training)
Week 6 February 10, 2026 | Hotel sales & the sales call • Hotel sales process: prospecting, discovery, proposal, negotiation, closing • Expectations of corporate clients & meeting planners • Lab: hotel sales call role-plays (selling both space and service culture) | Readings : Ritz #2 – Empowerment & Culture • Discussion: empowered employees, “wow” stories, service recovery • Reading for next week: Finish The New Gold Standard (selected later chapters) + Unreasonable Hospitality – Chapters 1–4
Week 7 February 17, 2026 | Hotel sales pt. 2 & sales trip planning • Key account management and long-term relationships • Planning a sales trip/mission: where to go, who to see, what your message is • Lab: planning a 3-day sales mission with hospitality-focused client touches | Ritz Wrap & Guidara #1 – Bigger Hospitality • Ritz wrap-up: one practice you’d steal for a hotel sales team • First reactions to Unreasonable Hospitality and the idea of going “beyond reasonable” • Reading for next week: Unreasonable Hospitality – middle section (systems, culture, 95/5 rule, team rituals)
Week 8 February 24, 2026 | AI & the future of hotel work • How AI is used in hotels: chatbots, pricing tools, CRM, upselling, housekeeping optimization • Discussion: where AI can help vs where it risks hurting hospitality • Lab: mapping a “human + AI” guest journey.
