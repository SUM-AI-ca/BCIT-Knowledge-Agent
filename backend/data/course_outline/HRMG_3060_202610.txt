### Course Description
Provides students with a working knowledge of recruitment and selection performance appraisals, job evaluations and job descriptions.
### Learning Resources
This course requires: Schwind, H., Uggerslev, K., Wagar, T., Fassina, N. & Halinski, M., 2025, Canadian Human Resource Management [14th Edition], 9781265688615,
Additional Resources: Publication Manual of the American Psychological Association, APA, 7th edition
### Course Goals
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:
Understand the importance to organizational success of programs and processes associated with attracting, developing and maintaining an effective and efficient workforce.
Students will be able to describe some of the key human resource management issues facing Canadian organizations now and in the foreseeable future. They will also develop a working knowledge of the terminology and generally accepted processes of human resource management activities such as job design, HR planning, recruitment and selection, training, compensation, terminations, etc; understand basic references to B.C. and federal employment legislation; as well as understand some of their own basic employment rights.

### Course Specific Requirements
Participation (10% of the course grade): Active class participation is essential to accomplish the course objectives. Your grade for participation will be determined by the extent to which you are prepared for class, your contribution to class discussions, the extent to which you enable others to participate, and your participation and performance in assigned course activities, exercises, and homework. Your ability to demonstrate appropriate professional workplace behaviours will be considered when determining your grade for participation.
Excellent participation (8-10) is defined as consistently participating and moving ahead in class discussions. Volunteering high-quality analysis and insights or applications of course content; Assisting other class members in reaching course outcomes and understanding course content; Helping others to participate effectively; Being punctual, attentive, and engaged in assigned class activities and exercises; and performing very well in exercises, quizzes, and other assignments.
Satisfactory participation (6-7) is defined as being prepared with good observations and analysis when called upon. Understanding assigned readings and being able to explain or discuss the concepts put forth in classes and readings; Participating satisfactorily in assigned class activities and exercises; Participating in a manner that is not dominating or overwhelming of others; Not distracting others; Performing satisfactorily in exercises, quizzes, and other assignments.
Unsatisfactory participation (0-5) is defined as not being prepared when called upon, missing classes, presenting poorly reasoned observations and analysis, being “off-task” in class activities and exercises, not contributing to large class discussions or small group activities, dominating class or small group discussions, talking with others at inappropriate times, arriving late to classes or leaving early, etc. Performing poorly in or not completing exercises, quizzes, and other assignments.

### Guidelines for School of Business + Media
