### Course Description
This course further examines the human body: cardiac, respiratory, digestive, renal, neurological (incl special senses), endocrine, and reproductive systems. Fluid and pH homeostasis are studied to exemplify regulation and integration of body systems. In the seminars, case studies and other active learning techniques are used to integrate knowledge across body systems and to develop critical thinking skills essential to clinical practice. Evaluation of learning is conducted through weekly seminar quizzes, exams, as well as in-class practice and participation.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- Relate function of body organs and systems to their tissue components
- Relate structure to function in the human body
- Apply knowledge of anatomical and physiological principles to the process of diagnosing and treating selected pathological conditions
- Identify cellular & extracellular components and predict changes in physiology when that component becomes dysfunctional
- Develop skills in problem solving and critical thinking when approaching physiology case studies

### Course Schedule and Assignments
Course Schedule
*Note: Unforeseeable circumstances may require changes to content, sequencing, timing or evaluation; students will be given reasonable notice.*
Week | Lecture Topics
1 | Endocrine System Principles of hormone action – hypothalamus-pituitary connection Endocrine glands; thyroid gland - parathyroid glands - adrenal glands – endocrine pancreas
2 | Circulatory System Heart - anatomy of the heart – cardiac function - regulation of cardiac function – cardiac enzymes/markers - albumin and oncotic pressure
3 | Blood Vessels Peripheral circulation - peripheral hemodynamics -regulation of blood flow and pressure –anatomy of arteries and veins - types of capillaries - capillary exchange Lymphatic System Functions of lymph - formation and circulation of lymph - lymph node structure and functions – other lymphoid tissues and organs
4 | Respiratory System I Organization of the respiratory system – airway and alveolar epithelia - secretions of the respiratory system, mechanisms of gas exchange
5 | Respiratory System II Control of respiration - blood gas transport - oxygen dissociation curves – acid/base balance February 3 (Midterm 1)
6 | Digestive System I Gross anatomy and histological organization of the gastrointestinal tract – salivary gland structure, secretions and function - GI tract secretions, functions and regulation
7 | Digestive System II Liver, gall bladder and pancreatic structure and function - Digestion and nutrient absorption February 18 (Midterm 2)
8 | Urinary System Urine storage and micturition - Acid-base balance - mechanisms of pH control - chemical buffers - physiological buffers -respiratory and renal compensation Reproductive System Gross anatomy – menstrual and ovarian cycles – hormonal control of cycles – male reproductive tract and associated glands – composition and functions of secretions
9 | Review Final Lecture and Labelling Exam (March 6)
