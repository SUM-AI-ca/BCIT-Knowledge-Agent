### Course Description
The practicum term is 30 weeks in length and begins with a four-week Clinical Readiness session on campus. The remaining twenty-six-weeks is divided between cytogenetics/FISH and microarray technology (CLGT 6607) and molecular genetics technology (CLGT 6608) training at one or more of our affiliated clinical sites. The cytogenetics/FISH and microarray portion of the practicum is 16 weeks in length and deals with the theoretical and technical content for courses CLGT 5501/6601, 5502/6602, 5505/6605, and 5507/6609, as well as methods not covered at BCIT due to time/equipment constraints. It may be necessary to locate some students to Alberta, Saskatchewan, or Manitoba due to a limited number of cytogenetics sites in the Lower Mainland.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
By the end of this course, learners will be able to:
- Review and enhance practical/theoretical skills in Terms 1 and 2 at BCIT.
- Note and appreciate differences in site lab procedures with those studied at BCIT.
- Become familiar and comfortable with the operation/dynamics of the practicum site for processing, analyzing, and reporting results of patient specimens.
- Process/report cases and projects for review with senior technologist - use duplicates and compare to those run by staff, in accordance with lab SOP.
- Review cases being investigated cytogenetically and the implications of the result on patient prognosis.
- Be familiar with the role and responsibilities of the genetic technologist in the process of specimen culture, processing, analysis, and reporting.
- Communicate and work cooperatively with fellow technologists, supervisor, and under the direction of the lab director.
- Appreciate the need for keeping supplies, solutions, and reagents from running out.
- Ensure that the students’ handbook is kept up to date
- Use laboratory notebook re care and use of equipment and procedures.
- Review Quality Management System’s Quality Control standards in the laboratory.
- Process newly arrived specimens in accordance with lab SOP.
- Performs laboratory procedures safely, in accordance with lab SOP.
- Prepare specimens for analytical processing, in accordance with lab SOP.
- Operate clinical practicum equipment, in accordance with lab SOP.
- Performs and analyzes the results of clinical diagnostics assays, in accordance with lab SOP.
- Perform clinical laboratory quality control and assurance procedures, in accordance with lab SOP.
- Demonstrate laboratory management skills.
- Conduct themselves in a professional manner in the clinical lab.

In the diagnostic laboratory, cytogenetics can be performed on cell samples taken from blood, bone marrow, solid tissue (CVS/tissues/solid tumors), or amniotic fluid. To successfully complete the cytogenetics practicum, students will be required to complete the following minimum sample numbers from one or more of the specimen types mentioned above.

### Course Schedule and Assignments
- Four G-banding tests (consisting of 5 metaphase prints each) derived from images supplied by the Program.
- Use of generative AI tools (eg ChatGPT, etc.)
Student analysis of lab data is an important part of student learning. Instructional staff do not believe that generative AI tools like ChatGPT are an adequate substitute for student judgement in analyzing data. Reports to be submitted for marking are modelled after the format used at clinical sites, so there is little opportunity to use generative AI to write these reports. However, if students do choose to use one of these tools, please follow these guidelines:
Cite and number each use, like this [1, ChatGPT, (date)]
Mark all generative AI output, by using “quotes” for using unedited genAI output, and ‘quotes’ for using paraphrased genAI output
For all generative AI content, student must include primary source verification i.e. original research journal articles or chapters from traditionally published textbooks.
