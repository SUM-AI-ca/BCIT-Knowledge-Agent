## Course Description
Presents an introductory analysis of the fundamental issues and facts of labour-management relations. Special emphasis is given to collective agreement content and interpretation, bargaining and basic labour economics.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:

- Understand the principles and obligations of the Labour Code of BC.
- Appreciate the different roles employers, unions and OH&S specialists discharge in the workplace.
- Characterize the steps in the collective bargaining process including mediation.
- Be familiar with the grievance and arbitration process with emphasis on OH&S disputes.
- Appreciate the impact of relationships in determining the success of the enterprise.
- Have a strong appreciation of how OH&S issues are managed in a unionized environment.

## Course topics
Additional Course Information

This course will introduce students to the concepts of industrial relations and labour relations with an emphasis on Canada and British Columbia. The historical context, processes, activities, issues, and other fundamentals of industrial relations will be studied within conflict management and employee voice frameworks. The legal framework of labour-management relations will be examined in addition to the topics of trade unions, management, collective bargaining, collective agreements, collective agreement administration, and grievance and arbitration processes. Additional topics will include labour-management collaboration and the nonunion workplace. Emphasis will be placed on how employers and unions can effectively manage their legal duties and obligations and their workplace relationships with one another. Conceptual frameworks for the course content may include one or more of the following: (1) Albert O. Hirschman’s Exit-Voice-Loyalty Model, (2) the Pluralist Approach and the Unitarist Approach, and (3) the Confrontation Approach and the Collaboration Approach as aids in analysis and decision making in industrial relations, labour relations, employment relations, and human resource management.
