## Course Description
​​Operations management may be defined as the design, operation and improvement of the systems that create the firm's products or services. Demand for quality, time-based competition and international production has demonstrated the importance of operations management to the survival of the firm. This course will develop an appreciation for the nature and role of operations in a firm, and the strategic importance of operations to provide a competitive advantage in the marketplace. More specifically the course will examine operations strategy; quality management; product and service design; process and facilities planning, analysis and reengineering; Materials Management, and supply chain management.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion of the course, the student will be able to:

## Introduction
- Define Operations Management and the role of the Operations Manager
- Identify operations strategies in a global environment.
- Define productivity and productivity measurement.
- Distinguish between manufacturing strategies and service strategies in operations
- Compare and contrast service and manufacturing operations in the areas of output, customer contact, labour content, inputs and outputs and productivity measurement

## Design of Operations
- Describe the goods and service design process and identify process choices
- Differentiate various process strategies and their application.
- Relate layout strategies to process strategies.

## Quality
- Demonstrate appreciation for the different perceptions and definitions of quality such as conformance, value, customer satisfaction and product features
- Explain the philosophy of Quality Management
- Describe a problem solving strategy and process improvement tools and techniques to improve operations.
- Describe the dimensions and costs of quality

## Operating Productive Systems
- Develop a project charter to initiate and manage a project
- Apply inventory control techniques to manage materials
- Identify lean techniques as applied to industry.
- Explain the elements of and potential issues in the supply-chain
- Describe the major business processes involved in supply chain management

This course features a major team-based project. All team members usually receive the same mark. However, marks can be redistributed if one or more team members contribute less than their fair share. This may include one or more of the following:

- frequently absent from team meetings or classes

- submitting plagiarized or low-effort work

- missing team deadlines or class deadlines

- disruptive behavior during team meetings

As in the workplace, your team will need to have a team charter and document infractions, escalating the issue to your instructor before the assignment is due. The instructor will then guide your team through the mark redistribution process. In this process, the team will have two options:

Option 1: If all members of the group agree that grades should be reallocated, up to 40% will be taken off one student’s score and redistributed to the other students.

Option 2: If group members do not reach an agreement on how marks should be reallocated, each team member’s contributions will be marked separately.
