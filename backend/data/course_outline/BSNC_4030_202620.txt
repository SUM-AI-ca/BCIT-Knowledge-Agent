### Course Description
This course builds on Practice of Nursing 3 and consists of two rotations of six weeks each, one on an acute mental health care unit and the other in a Public Health unit. This course explores evidence based approaches when completing focused mental health and public health nursing assessments. Learners will follow up with effective, efficient and relevant nursing interventions. Life span factors, cultural variables and legal aspects of care frame the ethical decision-making employed in client choices for care within the acute mental health and public health settings. The aim of the mental health rotation is to prepare learners to provide competent mental health and addiction care in any health care setting, by providing a foundation for practice, at the beginning level, in a specialized mental health setting. In the public health context, learners examine the nursing process and how public health nurses promote health and prevent disease within a variety of populations. During practicum experiences, learners participate in and explore nurses' roles in public health settings. In addition, epidemiology, population health, communicable disease control, health promotion and working with specific at-risk populations are explored in relation to specific local and global health issues.
### Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
Upon successful completion, the student will meet the following competencies:
Advocate
- Collaborates with clients to facilitate navigation through health care services effectively
- Uses knowledge of relational practice to impact health outcomes of individuals, families, communities and populations
- Examines and promotes the equitable allocation of healthcare resources considering the health disparities of at-risk population
- Incorporates knowledge of social justice, population health, environmental and global health issues in the nursing care of clients
- Advocates for informed decision making and preservation of dignity on behalf of clients
- Incorporates knowledge of the unique needs of specific populations into nursing care
Communicator
- Communicates effectively with clients and healthcare team members to support safe, high quality, client-centred health care in diverse situations and contexts
- Demonstrates a strengths-based approach to developing and maintaining therapeutic partnerships
- Engages in relational practice through a variety of approaches that demonstrate caring behaviours appropriate for clients
- Assists clients to identify and access health and other resources in their communities
- Demonstrates effective public speaking
- Educates clients to maintain and promote health
Collaborator
- Identifies hierarchical structures and power dynamics within the healthcare system
- Solicits and accommodates multiple perspectives in any given situation to support safe, high quality, client-centered care
- Implements a strength-based approach to collaboration in defined contexts and situations
- Facilitates linkages between resources and clients
Decision Maker
- Uses critical Inquiry and relational practice to incorporate knowledge of a particular client, client patterns of responses; personal clinical knowledge of similar clients, and empirical knowledge, in the planning and provision of client care
- In relation to specific clients, identifies credible, accurate, appropriate and current information, resources, services and/or relevant technologies
- Aligns care with quality criteria, legal frameworks, and economic and ecological principles
Global Citizen
- Uses critical perspectives to analyze social justice and equity issues in health care
- Incorporates global awareness in nursing practice
- Incorporates cultural humility and social consciousness in nursing practice
- Plans care for clients that incorporates consideration of determinants of health and social justice
- Provides care to clients that promotes social justice and equity in healthcare
- Uses moral questioning to identify the socio-political, economic and historical factors that lead to inequalities among individuals, communities and nations
Leader
- Demonstrates appropriate use of expertise/influence/credibility to positively influence client care outcomes
- Explores and develops a range of possible alternatives and approaches for care with clients
Lifelong Learner
- Uses evidence-based resources to promote better practices in client care
- Incorporates interprofessional perspectives so as to optimize client outcomes
Professional
- Uses knowledge of the nursing role and the roles of other healthcare professions to consult with the client, family and the health care team to develop a plan of care to meet the client’s needs
- With guidance, assesses own practice and undertakes activities to improve practice and meet identified learning goals
- Respects and preserves clients’ rights based on the ethical standards of the nursing profession

### Attendance Requirements
Attendance: As student success is tied to regular attendance of classes, the BSN program strongly recommends that students attend class regularly. Attendance at all nursing theory classes is an expectation of the BSN program.
In all learning environments, students are socialized into their professional role and are thus required to demonstrate knowledge and application of professional nursing standards and ethics. Multiple absences may jeopardize a student’s development of the knowledge base required for competent professional nursing practice and successful completion of course requirements. As adult learners, students are ultimately responsible for their own learning; however, it is also recognized that repeated absences have a negative impact on collaborative learning experiences.
### Course Specific Requirements
Students may be required to complete a minimum of one hour of preparation prior to the clinical shift to be considered prepared for clinical practice. This preparation is an evaluated course requirement but does not constitute course credit hours.
Students who do not arrive with sufficient time to complete the required preparation will be evaluated as unprepared rather than late. In such circumstances, the instructor may exercise discretion to withhold the start of clinical practice until the student is adequately prepared, which may result in the student being late to clinical.
Course Outline Changes: The material or schedule specified in this course outline may be changed by the instructor. If changes are required, they will be announced in class.
