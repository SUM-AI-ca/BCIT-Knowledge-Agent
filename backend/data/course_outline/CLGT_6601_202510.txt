## Course Description
A continuation of CLGT 5501 but with increased emphasis on the quality of preparations and troubleshooting. Slide making techniques and theory are reviewed and are applied to blood cultures/suspensions. High resolution chromosomes are derived from specialized protocols using PHA, FUdR, and thymidine. Various G­banding methods are studied and applied. Special banding procedures are reviewed. The need for an organized, professional approach to laboratory procedures/experiments is strongly emphasized. Students study workflow in blood culturing from accessioning to harvest and slide making. This is also strongly emphasized. It is a CSMLS competency and is given maximum time and resources.

## Course Learning Outcomes/Competencies
By the end of this course, learners will be able to:

- Relate to the technologist’s role in the culturing of cytogenetic specimens and identification of chromosome abnormalities and the importance of these results to patient care.
- Correlate the selection of specific cytogenetic tests with the clinical indication of the patient.
- Provides information to the client on specimen collection, transportation, and storage.
- Follow standard laboratory procedures by correctly labeling culture tubes, flasks, plates, slides, etc., and understand the consequences of incorrect labeling and/or documentation.
- Maintain a high degree of integrity and professionalism in maintaining patient confidentiality.
- Use and explain the need for specific, defined selection criteria for choosing metaphases to be counted/analyzed, including the use of metaphase finders.
- Troubleshoot technical problems with cytogenetic procedures and equipment.
- Perform stain procedures, both routine G-b and special stains such as High-Resolution Chromosomes (HRC), C-b, NOR, DAPI.
- Prepares and prioritizes specimens for further testing as required.
- Follows established protocols for entering and retrieving data from the lab information system as defined in policy and procedure manuals.
- Explain the relationship between neoplasia and chromosome abnormalities.
- Explain the need for timely, accurate record keeping and change management strategies.
- Establish blood cultures for chromosome analysis and consistently follow workflow procedures from setup to harvest, slide making, and cell pellet banking.
- Develop an individualistic approach as well as a group approach to solving chromosome and banding preparations.
- Demonstrates professional practice needed to be successful at the clinical sites.
- Understands the purpose of quality assurance programs as it relates to the setup, culture and harvesting protocols for peripheral blood, both regular and high-resolution.
- Work safely in the laboratory while performing Cytogenetics.

## Course Goals
The goals of this course are to:

Perform current/updated cytogenetic techniques that are being used in the various cytogenetic laboratories.

Explain the theory behind culture, harvest, and banding procedures.

Troubleshoot procedures that produce unsatisfactory results.

Follow a comprehensive system for establishing blood culture specimens for chromosome analysis from accessioning to harvest and slide making.

Develop strategies for slide making and banding.

Assignment Details

- Use of generative AI tools (eg ChatGPT, etc.)

Student analysis of lab data is an important part of student learning. Instructional staff do not believe that generative AI tools like ChatGPT are an adequate substitute for student judgement in analyzing data. Reports to be submitted for marking are modelled after the format used at clinical sites, so there is little opportunity to use generative AI to write these reports. However, if students do choose to use one of these tools, please follow these guidelines:

Cite and number each use, like this [1, ChatGPT, (date)]

Mark all generative AI output, by using “quotes” for using unedited genAI output, and ‘quotes’ for using paraphrased genAI output

For all generative AI content, student must include primary source verification i.e. original research journal articles or chapters from traditionally published textbooks.

- Laboratory

Four written technical reports must be submitted in partial fulfillment of the course requirements. These reports —Lab Exercises 1 through 4 — are based on assays performed in the laboratory and/or analysis of laboratory data and results. A full description of how these technical reports should be formatted and what content they should include will be provided upon commencement of the courses.

Please note that there are four exercises to be completed in Term 2 as compared to three exercises in Term 1. The reason for this is that students will have had more experience and be at a higher level of competency in Term 2.
